# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: RHOBTB2 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

RHOBTB2 is an atypical Rho GTPase subfamily member that functions as a substrate adaptor for the Cullin3-based ubiquitin ligase complex, mediating proteasomal degradation of target proteins. The gene is recognized as a tumor suppressor in cancer contexts and plays critical roles in cell cycle regulation, apoptosis, and cellular signaling pathways including the Hippo-YAP1 pathway.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of Studies and Patient Cohort:**
Nine independent publications were identified, comprising approximately 56 individual patients with RHOBTB2-associated neurodevelopmental disorders. The largest case series included 22 patients with genotype-phenotype correlations (PMID: 37165955), followed by 11 patients with alternating hemiplegia phenotypes (PMID: 33504645), 10 patients with early-onset epilepsy (PMID: 29276004), and 7 patients in a developmental encephalopathy case series (PMID: 37982109). The remaining studies comprised 1-3 patients each.

**Most Common Phenotypes:**
The clinical presentation is remarkably consistent across studies, with the following core features:
- Early-onset seizures (typically 4-12 months of age, median 4 months)
- Severe to profound intellectual disability
- Developmental delay
- Movement disorders (stereotypies, choreodystonia, ataxia, dystonia)
- Postnatal microcephaly
- Paroxysmal movement disorders responsive to carbamazepine/oxcarbazepine
- Brain structural abnormalities (myelination delay, thin corpus callosum, brain atrophy, hippocampal atrophy)

Additional phenotypic features include acute encephalopathic episodes (sometimes triggered by febrile status epilepticus or head trauma), hemiplegic episodes, dysautonomia, gastrointestinal symptoms, and episodes of skin color change. One study expanded the phenotypic spectrum to include hemiplegic migraine responsive to flunarizine (PMID: 40007339).

**Predominant Variant Type:**
All identified variants are heterozygous de novo missense mutations. No loss-of-function variants (frameshift, nonsense, or splice site mutations) were reported. Variants cluster predominantly in the BTB (BTB/POZ) domain-encoding region, with several hotspot positions identified: Arg511 (p.Arg511Trp in PMID: 37090824; p.Arg511Gln in PMID: 39831600), and other BTB domain positions (R461H, R485C, R489Q noted in PMID: 41478322). A subset of variants occur in the GTPase domain, which show variable neurodevelopmental phenotypes without impaired proteasomal degradation (PMID: 37165955).

**Cohort Source:**
Patients were identified through international collaborations and specialist pediatric centers (movement disorder clinics, neurodevelopmental centers). No patients were explicitly stated to be from established research cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL), though international collaboration suggests potential overlap with such networks.

**Strength of Clinical Evidence: STRONG**

The clinical evidence is strong based on: (1) consistent phenotypic presentation across 56 patients and 9 independent studies; (2) de novo inheritance pattern in the majority of cases (confirmed de novo in 9/11 cases in PMID: 33504645); (3) clear genotype-phenotype correlation showing BTB domain variants cause severe EIEE64 with impaired proteasomal degradation, while GTPase domain variants cause variable phenotypes (PMID: 37165955); (4) identification of recurrent hotspot variants (Arg511 position); (5) functional validation showing mutant RHOBTB2 accumulation due to impaired degradation; and (6) expansion of recognized phenotypic spectrum beyond the original EIEE64 designation to include alternating hemiplegia and hemiplegic migraine.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms Studied:**
Drosophila melanogaster was the primary model organism employed.

**Key Phenotypes from Model Studies:**
In Drosophila, elevated RhoBTB levels (modeling the accumulation seen with human BTB domain variants) cause seizure susceptibility and severe locomotor defects (PMID: 29276004). Conversely, RhoBTB knockdown in dendritic arborization neurons decreases dendrite number, suggesting a critical role in dendritic development and neural circuit formation. These findings directly parallel the human phenotype of developmental delay, movement disorders, and seizure susceptibility.

**Consistency with Human Phenotype:**
The Drosophila model demonstrates that altered RHOBTB2 levels (both elevation and reduction) cause neurological dysfunction, supporting the hypothesis that precise regulation of RHOBTB2 protein levels is essential for normal neural development and function. The seizure susceptibility phenotype in flies with elevated RhoBTB directly mirrors the early-onset seizures observed in human patients with BTB domain variants that impair proteasomal degradation.

**Strength of Model Organism Evidence: MODERATE**

While Drosophila studies provide functional validation of the pathogenic mechanism (protein accumulation leading to seizure susceptibility and motor defects), the evidence is limited by: (1) use of a single model organism; (2) lack of mammalian (mouse) knockout or knockin studies; (3) absence of data on Mendelian ratio deviations or lethality in model organisms; and (4) limited characterization of developmental encephalopathy-specific features beyond seizures and locomotor defects.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known Pathways and Molecular Function:**
RHOBTB2 functions as a substrate adaptor for Cullin3-based E3 ubiquitin ligase complexes, recruiting target proteins for proteasomal degradation (PMID: 15107402). The protein binds to Cullin3 via its BTB domain and is itself a substrate of the Cul3 ubiquitin ligase complex. Assembly of RHOBTB2-Cullin3-COP9 E3 ligase complexes is Hsp90-dependent, revealing a chaperone-modulated assembly mechanism (PMID: 24608665). RHOBTB2 also modulates the Hippo-YAP1 signaling pathway by stabilizing KLHL13 and inhibiting its proteasomal degradation (PMID: 41107424). Additionally, RHOBTB2 is a direct transcriptional target of E2F1 with roles in cell cycle progression and apoptosis regulation (PMID: 18039672).

**Expression Pattern:**
While specific brain expression data are not detailed in the provided evidence, the consistent neurodevelopmental phenotype across all patients and the Drosophila neurological findings indicate critical expression and function in neural tissues. The gene's role in dendritic development (demonstrated by RhoBTB knockdown effects on dendrite number) suggests particular importance in neural circuit formation during development.

**Key Experimental Findings:**

*Proteasomal Degradation Mechanism:*
Disease-associated BTB domain variants (e.g., R461H, R485C, R489Q, Arg511Trp/Gln) result in impaired proteasomal degradation and accumulation of RHOBTB2 protein (PMID: 29276004, PMID: 37982109, PMID: 39831600). Variants in the GTPase domain do not impair degradation and cause variable neurodevelopmental phenotypes without the severe EIEE64 presentation (PMID: 37165955).

*Ion Homeostasis Disruption:*
Mutant RHOBTB2 reduces Na+/K+-ATPase levels via lysosome-dependent degradation and impairs mitochondrial respiration, providing a mechanistic link between RHOBTB2 dysfunction and seizure susceptibility through disruption of neuronal ion homeostasis (PMID: 41478322). BTB domain hotspot mutants accumulate in nucleus and mitochondria, while GTPase domain mutants show normal protein localization.

*Protein Interactions:*
RHOBTB2 directly interacts with KLHL13 and stabilizes it by inhibiting proteasomal degradation (PMID: 41107424). The protein is a client of the Hsp90 chaperone machine, with GTP binding modulated by the Hsp90 ATPase cycle (PMID: 24608665).

**Strength of Biological Evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is strong based on: (1) well-characterized molecular function as a Cullin3 substrate adaptor with clear mechanistic links to proteasomal degradation; (2) identification of specific functional consequences of disease-associated variants (impaired degradation, protein accumulation, altered subcellular localization); (3) demonstration of downstream effects on ion homeostasis and mitochondrial function relevant to seizure pathogenesis; (4) Hsp90-dependent assembly mechanisms providing additional regulatory complexity; and (5) consistent correlation between variant location (BTB vs. GTPase domain) and functional consequences.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall Evidence Level: STRONG**

RHOBTB2 is a well-established neurodevelopmental disorder gene with compelling evidence across clinical, biological, and model organism domains.

**Key Strengths of the Evidence:**

1. **Consistent Clinical Phenotype:** 56 patients across 9 independent studies show remarkably uniform presentation of early-onset seizures, severe intellectual disability, movement disorders, and microcephaly, with clear de novo inheritance pattern.

2. **Clear Genotype-Phenotype Correlation:** BTB domain variants cause severe EIEE64 with impaired proteasomal degradation, while GTPase domain variants cause variable phenotypes without degradation impairment (PMID: 37165955), demonstrating structure-function relationships.

3. **Mechanistic Understanding:** The pathogenic mechanism is well-characterized—missense variants in the BTB domain impair binding to Cullin3 or alter protein stability, leading to RHOBTB2 accumulation. This accumulation disrupts ion homeostasis (Na+/K+-ATPase) and mitochondrial function, directly linking molecular dysfunction to seizure susceptibility.

4. **Recurrent Hotspot Variants:** Multiple independent patients carry variants at the same positions (e.g., Arg511), supporting pathogenicity and suggesting these are true disease hotspots.

5. **Functional Validation:** Drosophila models demonstrate that elevated RhoBTB levels cause seizure susceptibility and motor defects, directly validating the human phenotype.

6. **Expanding Phenotypic Spectrum:** Recent studies have expanded recognized phenotypes beyond EIEE64 to include alternating hemiplegia and hemiplegic migraine, indicating broader clinical relevance.

**Key Gaps and Limitations:**

1. **Lack of Mammalian Models:** No mouse knockout or knockin studies are reported. Conditional knockout models would strengthen understanding of tissue-specific and developmental stage-specific requirements.

2. **Limited Mechanistic Detail on Dendritic Development:** While Drosophila studies show RhoBTB knockdown reduces dendrite number, the molecular mechanisms linking RHOBTB2 dysfunction to impaired dendritic development in mammals remain unclear.

3. **Incomplete Variant Characterization:** Some case reports do not specify exact variant sequences, limiting comprehensive variant-level analysis.

4. **Brain Expression Data:** Specific quantification of RHOBTB2 expression in developing brain and neural tissues is not provided in the available evidence.

5. **Therapeutic Insights:** While some patients respond to carbamazepine/oxcarbazepine for movement disorders, systematic therapeutic trials and mechanistic understanding of drug responsiveness are lacking.

**Conclusion:**

RHOBTB2 is a high-confidence neurodevelopmental disorder gene in which de novo heterozygous missense variants, particularly in the BTB domain, cause developmental and epileptic encephalopathy type 64 (EIEE64) and related neurodevelopmental phenotypes through a gain-of-function mechanism involving impaired proteasomal degradation and subsequent protein accumulation. The consistent clinical presentation across 56 patients, clear genotype-phenotype correlations, well-characterized molecular mechanism, and functional validation in model organisms provide strong evidence for pathogenicity. Future studies employing mammalian models and systematic therapeutic trials will further elucidate disease mechanisms and identify targeted interventions.
