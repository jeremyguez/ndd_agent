# SHANK2 Evidence Report: Autism Spectrum Disorder (Autosomal Dominant / Heterozygous)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

SHANK2 (SH3 and multiple ankyrin repeat domains protein 2; also known as ProSAP1) encodes a master scaffolding protein of the postsynaptic density at excitatory glutamatergic synapses, where it organizes and stabilizes the postsynaptic signaling complex by interacting with glutamate receptors, actin-binding proteins, and other scaffolding molecules. Beyond its canonical synaptic role, SHANK2 has emerging functions in non-neuronal tissues including bone, kidney, and cochlear hair cells, and participates in regulation of the Hippo signaling pathway.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports/series:** Ten independent case reports or case series were identified, spanning peer-reviewed publications and conference abstracts (ASHG 2010, 2011, 2013, 2015, 2018).

**Total approximate number of patients:** Across studies with reported patient counts, approximately 56 patients carry pathogenic or likely pathogenic SHANK2 variants. This is a minimum estimate, as two studies did not report patient numbers.

**Most common phenotypes observed:** The predominant clinical presentation is autism spectrum disorder (ASD), reported across virtually all case series. Intellectual disability (mild to moderate) and speech/language delay are the next most consistently reported features (PMID: 32987185; PMID: 40307697). Additional phenotypes include developmental delay, ADHD, hypotonia, repetitive behaviors, adaptive behavior delays, and communication delays (PMID: 40307697). One conference report described associated features of mild obesity, synophrys, and delayed walking in a patient with a paternally inherited 11q13.4 deletion disrupting SHANK2 (ASHG 2015 - Grisolia et al.). Functional studies using patient-derived iPSCs have also implicated impaired dopamine neuron differentiation (PMID: 38704506) and reduced osteoblast differentiation potential (PMID: 36751416) as potential systemic manifestations.

**Predominant variant type:** Loss-of-function (LoF) variants predominate, including protein-truncating variants, nonsense mutations, and copy number variants (deletions). De novo LoF variants are well-represented (PMID: 32987185; ASHG 2010 - Berkel et al.; ASHG 2011 - Leblond et al.). Rare inherited missense variants have also been reported, though their individual pathogenicity is less certain; functional studies showed that a majority of tested missense variants reduced synapse density at dendrites (ASHG 2011 - Leblond et al.; ASHG 2010 - Berkel et al.), supporting a "multiple hit" model in some cases. One synonymous variant of uncertain significance was identified in a Brazilian cohort (ASHG 2018 - Rosan et al.).

**Known cohorts:** None of the patients were explicitly identified as belonging to established research cohorts such as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL. Studies were conducted across multiple countries including Germany, France, Italy, Canada, Brazil, and the UK (multi-center studies reported at ASHG 2011 and ASHG 2010).

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**
The evidence is supported by multiple independent reports, de novo LoF variants in ASD/ID patients, and functional corroboration. However, individual cohorts are small (most series report fewer than 15 patients), specific variant notations are often not provided, and formal case-control statistical analyses are limited in the peer-reviewed literature reviewed here.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*) and zebrafish (*Danio rerio*) models have both been characterized.

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

In mice, multiple independent Shank2 knockout lines consistently demonstrate autistic-like behavioral phenotypes. These include deficits in social interaction and sociability (PMID: 30072871; PMID: 33126897), altered ultrasonic vocalizations (PMID: 23994547; PMID: 27903723), repetitive and stereotyped behaviors, and hyperactivity (PMID: 30072871; PMID: 27903723). One line displayed a prominent mania-like phenotype with increased locomotor activity, abnormal reward-seeking, anhedonia, and circadian rhythm perturbations that were normalized by lithium and valproate treatment (PMID: 29046483), suggesting relevance to a broader neuropsychiatric spectrum. Cerebellar-specific knockout mice showed reduced excitatory synapse density in Purkinje cells, impaired motor coordination, and anxiety-like behavior (PMID: 27903723). Shank2-deficient mice also exhibit altered nociception, with mechanical allodynia and pain hypersensitivity linked to dysfunction of glycinergic inhibitory interneurons in the dorsal spinal cord (PMID: 37316943). Non-neurological phenotypes include decreased bone mass due to reduced osteoblastogenesis (PMID: 36751416) and proteinuria due to dysregulated renal albumin trafficking (PMID: 26333830). Dietary zinc supplementation reversed hyperactivity and reduced social preference but did not rescue working memory deficits or NMDA/AMPA receptor transmission abnormalities in knockout mice (PMID: 38853567).

In zebrafish, shank2-deficient animals showed reduced social and kin preference, enhanced behavioral stereotypy, hypersensitivity to auditory stimuli, and greater susceptibility to drug-induced seizures, with reduced GABAergic receptor expression as a molecular correlate (PMID: 36768529).

**Lethality/Mendelian ratio:** All knockout models reported are viable, with no evidence of lethality or Mendelian ratio deviations reported across studies.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The mouse and zebrafish models are highly consistent with the core human phenotype of ASD, intellectual disability, and communication deficits. The mania-like phenotype in one mouse line (PMID: 29046483) aligns with the broader neuropsychiatric spectrum observed in human SHANK2 variant carriers (PMID: 30072871). The non-neurological findings (bone, kidney) have not yet been systematically characterized in human patients but are supported by patient-derived iPSC data (PMID: 36751416).

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent mouse lines and a zebrafish model consistently recapitulate core ASD-relevant behavioral phenotypes, with molecular and electrophysiological correlates. The convergence across species and laboratories substantially strengthens the gene-disease relationship.

---

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:** SHANK2 functions as a postsynaptic density scaffold at excitatory glutamatergic synapses, organizing NMDA and AMPA receptor complexes. Loss of SHANK2 leads to altered NMDA and AMPA receptor composition and function (PMID: 29046483; PMID: 38853567). SHANK2 also regulates the Hippo signaling pathway through competitive binding for a LATS1 activator, with implications for cell proliferation and oncogenesis (PMID: 32661924). Additionally, SHANK2 physically interacts with CFTR via its PDZ domain and negatively regulates CFTR-mediated ion transport in epithelial cells (PMID: 14679195).

**Expression pattern:** SHANK2 is highly expressed in the brain, with particular relevance to synaptic function in the hippocampus, prefrontal cortex, striatum, and cerebellum. Transcriptomic analysis of Shank2 mutant mice revealed brain region-specific and gene dosage-dependent changes in synaptic, ribosomal, mitochondrial, and extracellular matrix gene expression, with the prefrontal cortex showing the strongest effects (PMID: 36311025). Shank2 is also expressed in glycinergic interneurons of the dorsal spinal cord (PMID: 37316943), cochlear hair cells (PMID: 40627398), bone (PMID: 36751416), and kidney podocytes (PMID: 26333830), indicating broad tissue expression beyond the CNS.

**Key experimental findings:**
- Heterozygous LoF mutations in SHANK2 derived from ASD patients cause hyperconnectivity of human neurons in iPSC-derived models, with increased dendrite length, complexity, synapse number, and spontaneous excitatory postsynaptic current frequency; these phenotypes were rescued by gene correction (PMID: 30911184).
- The patient-derived SHANK2B Y29X nonsense mutation impairs dopamine neuron differentiation from iPSCs, rescued by isogenic correction, and knock-in mice show autistic-like behavior and reduced dopamine release (PMID: 38704506).
- CRISPR/Cas9-generated SHANK2 frameshift mutations in SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cells impaired neurite outgrowth, reduced apoptosis, increased proliferation, and elevated p-AKT signaling, with reduced APP expression (PMID: 33483523).
- miR-137 directly represses SHANK2 protein translation via its 3'-UTR, and approximately 29% of validated miR-137 target genes including SHANK2 show significant expression differences in schizophrenia dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (ASHG 2017 - Berkel et al.), linking SHANK2 dysregulation to broader neurodevelopmental and neuropsychiatric conditions.
- Hair cell-specific ablation of Shank2 disrupts outer hair cell bundle geometry and causes progressive hearing loss, revealing a non-synaptic structural role (PMID: 40627398).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
Convergent evidence from iPSC-derived human neurons, patient-derived cell lines, isogenic rescue experiments, protein interaction studies, and transcriptomic analyses robustly supports SHANK2's role in neuronal development and synaptic function, with direct mechanistic links to ASD-relevant cellular phenotypes.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths:**
1. Multiple independent clinical reports across different countries and centers document de novo and inherited LoF variants (protein-truncating variants, nonsense mutations, deletions) in individuals with ASD and intellectual disability, with a consistent core phenotype.
2. Multiple independent mouse knockout lines and a zebrafish model consistently recapitulate ASD-relevant behavioral phenotypes (social deficits, repetitive behaviors, communication abnormalities, hyperactivity), with no lethality, strongly supporting haploinsufficiency as a disease mechanism.
3. Human iPSC-based functional studies directly demonstrate that patient-derived heterozygous LoF variants cause neuronal hyperconnectivity and impaired dopamine neuron differentiation, with phenotypic rescue upon gene correction, providing high-quality mechanistic evidence (PMID: 30911184; PMID: 38704506).

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. Individual clinical cohorts are small (most series report 1–15 patients), specific variant-level data are often not provided in the available abstracts, and formal case-control statistical analyses with adequate power are limited in the reviewed literature.
2. The contribution of inherited missense variants to disease risk remains incompletely resolved; a "multiple hit" model has been proposed for some cases (ASHG 2011 - Leblond et al.), complicating straightforward autosomal dominant classification for this variant class.
3. Patients have not been systematically ascertained through large-scale standardized cohorts (e.g., DDD, GEL), and genotype-phenotype correlations across the full allelic spectrum are not yet well-defined.

**Conclusion:** SHANK2 is a well-supported ASD gene with converging clinical, model organism, and functional biological evidence. Heterozygous LoF variants, particularly de novo protein-truncating variants and deletions, are associated with a neurodevelopmental syndrome characterized primarily by ASD, intellectual disability, and speech delay. The gene-disease relationship is supported by consistent phenotypic recapitulation in multiple animal models and by direct mechanistic evidence from patient-derived human cell studies, meeting criteria for a strong overall evidence classification for autosomal dominant ASD.
