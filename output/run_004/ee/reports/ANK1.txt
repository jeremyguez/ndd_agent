ANK1 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: ANK1 AS AN EPILEPTIC ENCEPHALOPATHY GENE (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
ANK1 encodes ankyrin-1 (ankyrin-R), an adaptor protein that tethers integral membrane proteins (e.g., band 3/SLC4A1) to the spectrin-actin cytoskeleton. It is best known for its role in red cell membrane stability, and ANK1 loss of function is the most common cause of autosomal dominant hereditary spherocytosis (HS). A small muscle isoform (sAnk1) also exists. No established link to epileptic encephalopathy was identified in the evidence provided.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent case reports or case series: 0. The clinical evidence set was empty.
- Total patients: none reported with epilepsy, epileptic encephalopathy, or any neurodevelopmental phenotype.
- Common phenotypes: none for this indication. Hematologic HS phenotypes appear only in the biological evidence, not as clinical neurological cases.
- Variant type: no clinical data for this indication. HS-related ANK1 variants described elsewhere in the evidence are predominantly loss-of-function (nonsense, frameshift, splice), e.g., R281X, Y216X, E142X (PMID: 31016877) and c.1994C>A nonsense (PMID: 39521890).
- Cohort membership (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organism: mouse only.
- Erythroid phenotypes:
  - An ENU-induced null allele causes profound in utero anemia, perinatal lethality, and severe spherocytosis in surviving pups, with loss of ANK-1 and reduced beta-spectrin. Heterozygotes have normal red cell survival but are resistant to malaria (PMID: 19179303).
  - The truncating Ank1 E924X allele gives mild HS in heterozygotes and severe hemolytic HS in surviving homozygotes (PMID: 21193012).
  - The hypomorphic nb (normoblastosis) frameshift allele is viable and causes ankyrin-deficient hemolytic anemia, with pathology reported in non-erythroid tissues (PMID: 14671619).
- Other tissues:
  - sAnk1 knockout mice are viable, with reduced sarcoplasmic reticulum volume and impaired Ca2+ release in skeletal muscle (PMID: 31323924).
  - Cochlear hair cell conditional knockouts (single Ank1 and triple Ank1/2/3) show preserved mechanotransduction (PMID: 40024092).
  - Inducible fibroblast knockout increases fibrosis in a heart failure model (PMID: 39125973).
- Neurological phenotypes: no seizures, encephalopathy, or brain phenotypes were reported. Mendelian ratios were not reported.
- Consistency with human phenotype: the mouse models are consistent with human HS, but not with epileptic encephalopathy. Heterozygous mouse phenotypes (mild HS) match autosomal dominant HS, not a neurological disease.
- Strength of model organism evidence for epileptic encephalopathy: NONE to LIMITED. The models are strong for HS but contribute nothing for epilepsy.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: ANK1 links membrane proteins (band 3, SPTB) to the spectrin cytoskeleton. In vitro, ANK1 loss-of-function variants reduce protein stability, block plasma membrane localization, abolish SPTB and SLC4A1 interaction, and increase osmotic fragility (PMID: 31016877). Intronic variants cause aberrant splicing in minigene assays (ASHG 2018 - Defective gene splicing with mutant alleles of the ankyrin 1 gene isolated from patients with hereditary spherocytosis).
- Mechanism in HS: de novo loss of expression of one ANK1 allele is common. Of 19 informative heterozygous children, 12 expressed cDNA from only one allele (PMID: 9470011).
- Expression: an Ank-1-related fetal 165 kD protein is present in mouse reticulocytes (PMID: 8471772). Ank1 is expressed in cochlear hair cells (PMID: 40024092). No brain or neurodevelopmental expression data were provided.
- Other associations:
  - Regulatory variants at ANK1 are linked to spleen iron and red cell traits (ASHG 2022 - Analysis of MRI-derived spleen iron in the UK Biobank).
  - ANK1 is a candidate effector gene at a type 2 diabetes locus (PMID: 32499647).
  - Rare variants are enriched in steroid-induced osteonecrosis (PMID: 41920036).
  - A SNP is associated with metabolic syndrome (PMID: 35631165).
  - Haplotypes are associated with yak meat traits (PMID: 33655403).
- None of these associations concerns epilepsy or brain disease.
- Strength of biological evidence for epileptic encephalopathy: NONE. The evidence is strong for erythroid and HS mechanisms only.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT for ANK1 as an epileptic encephalopathy gene.
- Strengths: ANK1's role in autosomal dominant HS is well supported by multiple mouse models (PMIDs: 19179303, 21193012, 14671619) and functional studies (PMIDs: 31016877, 9470011). Heterozygous loss of function is a recognized disease mechanism, but for a hematologic disease.
- Gaps and limitations:
  - There are no clinical cases of epilepsy or neurodevelopmental disease with ANK1 variants.
  - No mouse model shows seizures or a brain phenotype.
  - No neuronal expression or function data were provided.
  - The non-erythroid associations (muscle, heart, metabolic, bone) are unrelated to epileptic encephalopathy.
  - Neuronal ankyrins (ANK2, ANK3) are the more plausible neurological paralogs, but they are outside this evidence set.
- Conclusion: The available evidence supports ANK1 as a dominant HS gene but provides no support for an epileptic encephalopathy association. Without human neurological cases, a neural phenotype in models, or neuronal functional data, this gene-disease relationship cannot be supported at present. It should be classified as having insufficient evidence for epileptic encephalopathy.
