FBN1 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: FBN1 AS AN EPILEPTIC ENCEPHALOPATHY GENE (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
FBN1 encodes fibrillin-1, a large extracellular matrix glycoprotein that is the main component of connective tissue microfibrils. It also provides the scaffold that regulates TGF-β/LTBP bioavailability, and its C-terminal cleavage product is the hormone asprosin (PMID: 32080995). Heterozygous FBN1 variants cause Marfan syndrome and related fibrillinopathies, including isolated ectopia lentis and Weill-Marchesani-like phenotypes. They are not an established cause of epileptic encephalopathy.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: 5 were found, each a single patient (total about 5 patients). All are incidental, weakly described, or of uncertain causality.
- Phenotypes:
  - Epilepsy-related: SUDEP in a family with a rare FBN1 variant that segregated completely, proposed as a candidate gene only (PMID: 26423924). One infantile spasms patient had a likely pathogenic, probably de novo FBN1 variant alongside a pathogenic CACNA1E variant, and the FBN1 variant was reported as an incidental finding with cardiovascular implications (PMID: 41492575). The seizures are better explained by CACNA1E.
  - Non-epilepsy: an adult with a known pathogenic variant and a presumed Marfan/FBN1 disorder (PMID: 36265913); a Taiwanese child with a severe neurological/neuromuscular disorder, not clearly epileptic, with an unspecified FBN1 variant (PMID: 33333793); an aortic aneurysm case with a reported FBN1 nonsense variant that was not confirmed on a 23-gene panel or exome, so causality is uncertain (ASHG 2019 - PgmNr 2056: Drug metabolism anomalies co-segregating with familial prostate cancers and aortic aneurysms).
- Variant types: mostly unspecified ("other"). One nonsense (LoF) variant was reported, with uncertain causality. No recurrent variants or variant-level functional data in a neurological context.
- Cohorts: none of UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL are identified. The only country noted is Taiwan.
- Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED, leaning toward NONE for epileptic encephalopathy specifically. No case shows a convincing phenotype-genotype link to epileptic encephalopathy.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse (the majority) and zebrafish.
- Zebrafish:
  - fbn1 loss-of-function mutants showed peri-aortic and cranial hemorrhage, cardiomegaly, reduced aortic diameter and severe scoliosis by 6 dpf (PMID: 35052463).
  - A separate systematic fibrillin knockout study found no comparable cardiovascular phenotype in the fbn1 mutant, while fbn3 mutants did (PMID: 42025244).
- Mouse:
  - Knock-in models include Fbn1 C1039G/+ and C1041G/+ (Marfan models with aortic dilation, mitochondrial dysfunction, myopia and strain-dependent severity), plus the homozygous G234D knock-in, which died within 5 weeks from aortic rupture (PMID: 40365676, PMID: 33709773, PMID: 38177536, ASHG 2023 - Atropine treatment rescues myopia in a mouse model of Marfan syndrome, ASHG 2017 - Functional characterization of modifier loci for Marfan syndrome reveals novel therapeutic strategies).
  - The mgΔlpn dominant-negative model showed ectopia lentis (PMID: 33516761).
  - Heterozygous models are viable. No Mendelian ratio data were reported.
- Neurological phenotypes: none reported. No seizures, EEG abnormalities or brain phenotypes were described in any model. The phenotypes are cardiovascular, ocular and skeletal, which matches Marfan syndrome but not epileptic encephalopathy.
- Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG for Marfan/fibrillinopathy phenotypes, but NONE for epileptic encephalopathy.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function:
  - Structural microfibril component and regulator of TGF-β/SMAD and ERK signaling (PMID: 32698686, PMID: 39585648). It also acts as a binding partner for ADAMTSL proteins (PMID: 22242013).
  - Contributes to mechanosignaling through FGF12/AngII/RhoA (PMID: 41540272).
  - Affects mitochondrial respiration in VSMCs (PMID: 33709773).
  - Appears in cancer and kidney contexts: Wnt/β-catenin in DLBCL (PMID: 33312371), VEGFR2/STAT2 in ovarian cancer (PMID: 35234370), and ZEB1/ET-1/β-catenin in CKD (PMID: 42328432).
  - Asprosin acts on the liver via cAMP-PKA (PMID: 32080995).
- Expression: the data focus on aorta, eye, kidney and adipose tissue. No brain or neurodevelopmental expression data were provided.
- Variant-level function: an intron 11 variant causes in-frame exon 11 skipping and isolated ectopia lentis (ASHG 2023 - A novel FBN1 intron variant causing isolated ectopia lentis via an in-frame exon skipping). This is ocular, not neurological.
- Strength of biological evidence: STRONG for connective tissue and vascular biology, but LIMITED/NONE for any role in epilepsy or brain development.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT for FBN1 as an autosomal dominant epileptic encephalopathy gene.
- Key strengths: FBN1 is well established as the cause of Marfan syndrome, with extensive mouse and zebrafish models and detailed mechanistic data.
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - The five single-patient reports are incidental, unspecified or uncertain, and the FBN1 variants are not described.
  - The one infantile spasms case has a co-occurring pathogenic CACNA1E variant that is the more likely cause.
  - The SUDEP report is only a candidate-gene proposal (PMID: 26423924).
  - No model shows seizures or neurological phenotypes.
  - No brain expression data and no recurrent variants or de novo enrichment were found.
- Conclusion: There is no meaningful evidence that heterozygous FBN1 variants cause epileptic encephalopathy. The few epilepsy-related observations are most likely incidental findings of a gene that is otherwise well established as a cause of Marfan syndrome. FBN1 should not be classed as an epileptic encephalopathy gene without new, well-phenotyped cases and supporting neurological functional data.
