ATP5F1A (ATP synthase F1 subunit alpha) encodes the catalytic α-subunit of the mitochondrial F1Fo ATP synthase (complex V), which makes ATP by oxidative phosphorylation. Heterozygous variants are linked to autosomal dominant mitochondrial complex V deficiency, nuclear type 4A (MC5DN4A), and to movement and neurodevelopmental phenotypes. The extracted evidence does not support a clear "epileptic encephalopathy" designation (see below).

1. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

Independent reports: Three clinical reports were extracted. They are a multigenerational family with the recurrent p.Arg207His variant (PMID: 42520343), a case series on movement disorders (PMID: 40276935), and a single-patient report (PMID: 41743395). The biological evidence also describes a de novo p.Gly418Arg trio (PMID: 41053757) and a neonatal series of three infants with p.Arg207His (PMID: 34483339). The infants in PMID: 34483339 may overlap with cases cited in PMID: 42520343, so counting them as independent is uncertain.

Patient numbers: About 9 patients are listed in the clinical reports (4 + 4 + 1). Adding the neonatal series and the Han Chinese trio gives roughly 13 or more. Some overlap is possible, since PMID: 41743395 may be the same patient as, or a relative of, the c.1404del case in PMID: 40276935, or an additional case of the same variant.

Variants:
- c.620G>A, p.Arg207His is recurrent and missense. It was previously reported as de novo and has now been shown to be transmitted from a mother to her three children (PMID: 42520343).
- c.1404del, p.Glu469Serfs*3 is a frameshift LoF variant. Patient fibroblasts show reduced mRNA, reduced α-subunit protein and compromised ATPase activity (PMID: 40276935; PMID: 41743395).
- c.1252G>A, p.Gly418Arg is missense. It segregated in three patients from two families with hereditary spastic paraplegia (PMID: 40276935) and was reported de novo in a trio with developmental delay and motor dysfunction (PMID: 41053757).
- Missense variants predominate, with a smaller number of LoF cases.

Common phenotypes:
- p.Arg207His: growth faltering or failure to thrive, hyperlactatemia, hyperammonemia or urea cycle dysfunction, and developmental delay. Seizures and autism spectrum disorder were also reported in one family. Some children had congenital heart disease, sideroblastic anemia and hypogammaglobulinemia, and the two younger children showed spontaneous clinical resolution. The mother was reportedly asymptomatic, which suggests variable expressivity or reduced penetrance. An NDUFA12 deletion in the two younger children is a possible modifier (PMID: 42520343; PMID: 34483339).
- c.1404del: developmental delay or neurodevelopmental disorder, myoclonic dystonia and spasticity. One patient was misdiagnosed lifelong as cerebral palsy (PMID: 40276935; PMID: 41743395).
- p.Gly418Arg: hereditary spastic paraplegia, or developmental delay with motor dysfunction.
- Seizures appear in only one family (PMID: 42520343). Epileptic encephalopathy is not a defining or consistent feature. The core phenotype is a neurodevelopmental and movement disorder, with or without neonatal metabolic features.

Cohorts: No patients are identified as coming from UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL.

Strength of clinical evidence for ATP5F1A as a heterozygous disease gene: MODERATE. For an epileptic encephalopathy phenotype specifically it is LIMITED, because seizures are reported in only one family.

2. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

Organisms: Only zebrafish has been studied. This was an atp5fa1 knockdown, not a stable knockout (PMID: 41053757).

Key phenotypes: Knockdown caused growth retardation, motor dysfunction and impaired motor neuron axon development in Tg(mnx1:eGFP) larvae. It also caused multi-system defects and 2,261 differentially expressed genes, enriched in neurotransmission and apelin signaling. Autophagy was inhibited (apln, becn1 and map1lc3b were downregulated, P62 increased and Lc3b-II decreased). Human wild-type ATP5F1A mRNA partially rescued motor neuron morphology. Mendelian ratios, lethality and seizure-like phenotypes were not reported.

Consistency: The motor and developmental phenotypes match the human developmental delay and motor dysfunction. The partial rescue supports specificity. No seizure phenotype was assessed, and there are no mouse data.

Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED. It comes from a single knockdown study with no stable knockout and no seizure assessment.

3. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

Pathways and function: ATP5F1A is the α-subunit of the F1 catalytic head of ATP synthase, central to oxidative phosphorylation. Structural modelling of p.Arg207His places it at the α/β subunit interface near the β active site (PMID: 34483339). The p.Gly418Arg variant maps to an intersubunit communication site (PMID: 40276935).

Expression: Supporting data on expression are mostly non-neural. ATP5F1A is a hub gene in oxidative phosphorylation, downregulated in sarcopenia and obesity (PMID: 41053312). It is reduced in aged epidermis (PMID: 36851889), and it is altered in colon and prostate cancer. It is also reduced in type 2 diabetes muscle, where splice variants differ (PMID: 41563348), and it is increased in cardiomyocytes during reverse remodeling (PMID: 38910562). Brain or neurodevelopmental expression data are not provided in the extracted evidence.

Key experimental findings:
- Patient fibroblasts with p.Arg207His show deficits in complex V function and expression (PMID: 34483339).
- Frameshift fibroblasts show reduced ATPase activity (PMID: 40276935).
- In HEK293T cells, p.Gly418Arg reduced protein stability and expression while mitochondrial localization stayed intact (PMID: 41053757).
- Post-translational modifications, namely K531 lactylation (PMID: 40800583) and K239 β-hydroxybutyrylation (PMID: 41213123), regulate ATP synthase activity. These findings are in non-neurological disease contexts.

Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE. The functional evidence for variant effects is good, but the evidence on brain expression and disease mechanism is limited.

4. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

Overall evidence level: MODERATE for ATP5F1A as a heterozygous neurodevelopmental, movement and mitochondrial disease gene. For an epileptic encephalopathy classification, the evidence is LIMITED.

Strengths:
- A recurrent variant (p.Arg207His) has been seen de novo and with transmission.
- Several independent variants are reported.
- Functional data show reduced protein and ATPase activity.
- Structural modelling is consistent with the variants' effects.
- A zebrafish knockdown with partial rescue supports a motor and developmental phenotype.

Gaps:
- The patient numbers are small, with possible overlap between reports.
- Seizures are reported in only one family, so an epileptic encephalopathy phenotype is not established.
- The phenotypes are heterogeneous and show variable expressivity and reduced penetrance, with an asymptomatic mother and resolution in some children.
- The mechanism (dominant-negative vs haploinsufficiency) is unresolved.
- There is no mouse model, and the zebrafish data are knockdown only.
- No cohort-level data are available.

Conclusion: ATP5F1A has moderate support as an autosomal dominant gene for a neurodevelopmental, movement and complex V deficiency disorder, led by the recurrent p.Arg207His variant and supported by LoF and missense variants with functional data. Evidence for a specific epileptic encephalopathy association is limited, because seizures are rare in the reported cases. Further cases with detailed epilepsy phenotyping and stronger animal models are needed.
