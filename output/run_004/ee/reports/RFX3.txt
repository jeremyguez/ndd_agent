RFX3 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: EPILEPTIC ENCEPHALOPATHY, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
RFX3 encodes a winged-helix transcription factor of the regulatory factor X (RFX) family. It is a master regulator of ciliogenesis (including via FOXJ1) and of neuronal and endocrine differentiation. It is expressed strongly in the developing and adult brain and is under haploinsufficiency constraint.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: three reports in the clinical extraction, each describing a single patient with epilepsy.
  - An infantile epileptic spasms syndrome (IESS) and autism spectrum disorder (ASD) case with an RFX3 mutation (PMID: 37717291). The exact variant was not available in the extracted text.
  - A complex 9p24 deletion/duplication plus 12q24 duplication CNV in a child with severe epilepsy, ascertained by rapid genomic testing in the UK (PMID: 40399560). The deletion is heterozygous and spans SMARCA2 and RFX3, so RFX3-specific causality is not established.
  - A cohort of 349 epileptic encephalopathy patients analysed by exome CNV calling (PMID: 26068938). One patient had a de novo CNV including RFX3 plus a de novo RFX3 sequence variant on trio exome sequencing. The phenotype was infantile spasms or Lennox-Gastaut syndrome (IS/LGS).
- Total: about 3 patients with epilepsy phenotypes. The 9p24 CNV case is confounded by multigene involvement, so only about 2 cases are potentially RFX3-specific.
- Common phenotypes: infantile spasms or epileptic encephalopathy, with ASD in one case.
- Variant types: the types are poorly defined in the extracted data. They comprise an unspecified RFX3 mutation, a multigene CNV deletion, and a de novo CNV with a de novo sequence variant. Heterozygous loss of function (deletion, haploinsufficiency) is the likely mechanism but is not demonstrated for the epilepsy cases.
- Cohorts: no UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL cohorts were identified. The UK rapid genomic testing cohort is a clinical service cohort, and the 349-patient study is an epileptic encephalopathy cohort.
- Supporting neurodevelopmental data from the biological evidence: de novo RFX3, RFX4 and RFX7 variants in 38 individuals from 33 families with ASD, ID and/or ADHD (PMID: 33658631). An ASHG 2019 abstract reported heterozygous de novo or inherited RFX3 mutations in 17 patients with ASD, ADHD and mild ID, with functional analysis consistent with loss of function (ASHG 2019 - Mutations in RFX family members are a common cause of autism and ADHD). Epilepsy is not highlighted in these series, so they support a broader neurodevelopmental disorder phenotype rather than epileptic encephalopathy specifically.
- Strength of clinical evidence for epileptic encephalopathy: LIMITED.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse (the majority of studies) and Xenopus.
- Key mouse knockout phenotypes:
  - Full knockout causes sub-Mendelian ratios and a high rate of embryonic lethality. Surviving animals show left-right asymmetry defects, including situs inversus, with stunted nodal cilia (PMID: 15121860).
  - Hydrocephalus without aqueduct stenosis, defective choroid plexus differentiation, and agenesis of the subcommissural organ (PMID: 16930429).
  - Corpus callosum agenesis with disorganised midline guidepost neurons and reduced GLI3 repressor ratio (PMID: 22479201). Agenesis was rescued by a GLI3 repressor allele (PMID: 26071364).
  - Impaired motile cilia growth and beating (PMID: 19671664).
  - Pancreatic islet and beta-cell defects with glucose intolerance (PMID: 17229940; PMID: 20413507).
- Xenopus: rfx3 acts downstream of rnf20/rnf40, and its loss impairs cilia motility and left-right patterning (PMID: 31235600).
- Consistency with human phenotype: the brain malformations (corpus callosum agenesis, hydrocephalus) indicate an essential neurodevelopmental role, which is broadly consistent with human neurodevelopmental disease. The reported data are mostly for homozygous knockouts. No seizure or epilepsy phenotype and no heterozygous neurobehavioral phenotype were reported in the extracted data. Human heterozygous disease is not clearly modelled.
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE (strong evidence for a neurodevelopmental role, but limited relevance to heterozygous epileptic encephalopathy).

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function:
  - RFX3 is a ciliogenic transcription factor and a co-activator of FOXJ1 in airway epithelium (PMID: 23822649).
  - Its activity depends on auto S-fatty acylation in the dimerization domain, which affects ciliogenesis and Hedgehog signalling (PMID: 30127002).
  - It regulates beta-cell genes, including glucokinase (PMID: 20413507).
  - It regulates the MAP1A promoter (PMID: 12411430).
- Expression: strongly expressed in developing and adult human brain, and brain-specific per the ASHG abstract. It is also strongly expressed in ependymal cells in mouse (PMID: 16930429). RFX binding motifs are enriched near ASD risk genes (PMID: 33658631).
- Key neuronal findings: in human iPSC-derived neurons and organoids, biallelic RFX3 loss disrupted ciliary genes and delayed differentiation. Monoallelic loss disrupted synaptic gene expression and activity-dependent responses by reducing CREB binding (PMID: 40060598). This is directly relevant to a haploinsufficiency mechanism with altered neuronal activity.
- Other findings, such as islet differentiation (PMID: 40263183), CML (PMID: 31237926) and glioma (PMID: 35416616), are not relevant to epileptic encephalopathy.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: LIMITED for RFX3 as an epileptic encephalopathy gene. The evidence is stronger for a broader neurodevelopmental disorder (ASD, ADHD, ID).
- Strengths:
  - Plausible haploinsufficiency mechanism, with loss of function supported by the ASD/ADHD/ID cohorts.
  - Neurodevelopmental phenotypes in knockout models.
  - Human neuronal data showing synaptic and activity-dependent dysregulation with monoallelic loss.
- Gaps:
  - Only about 3 epilepsy cases, with unspecified variants and one multigene CNV.
  - No segregation, variant-level detail or functional validation for the epilepsy variants.
  - No seizure phenotype in model organisms.
  - Established neurodevelopmental cohorts are not epilepsy-focused.
- Conclusion: RFX3 is a credible haploinsufficiency neurodevelopmental gene, and it may contribute to infantile spasms and epileptic encephalopathy as part of that spectrum. The current epilepsy-specific evidence consists of isolated cases (PMID: 37717291; PMID: 26068938) and one confounded CNV (PMID: 40399560). Additional well-characterised patients with epilepsy and RFX3 loss-of-function variants are needed to establish the gene-disease relationship.
