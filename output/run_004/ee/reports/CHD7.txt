CHD7 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: CHD7 AS AN EPILEPTIC ENCEPHALOPATHY GENE (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
CHD7 encodes chromodomain helicase DNA-binding protein 7, an ATP-dependent chromatin remodeler that opens chromatin and regulates enhancers, particularly during neural crest, neural stem cell and inner ear development. Heterozygous loss-of-function variants cause CHARGE syndrome, a multisystem developmental disorder. Epilepsy is a recognized but non-defining feature.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: Six clinical reports were extracted. Three are case reports or small series with defined variants, and three are cohort or abstract-level reports without variant detail.
- Approximate patient count: At least 8 patients are explicitly counted: 2 (PMID: 35446972), 1 (PMID: 41225453), 2 (PMID: 33775046) and 1 (PMID: 37521304). Two further studies give no patient number (PMID: 42528014; PMID: 33270637), and the total is therefore somewhat higher.
- Phenotypes: CHARGE syndrome is the dominant diagnosis. Epilepsy-related features occur in a minority of reports:
  - Infantile spasms in one patient with a de novo missense variant, c.5050G>A (PMID: 35446972).
  - EEG abnormalities in a preterm infant with a de novo splice variant, c.5405-7G>A (PMID: 41225453).
  - Epilepsy and intellectual disability in a patient whose primary diagnosis was FOXP1-related disorder, so the significance of the CHD7 variant is unclear (PMID: 37521304).
  - Epilepsy, including heat-sensitive seizures, and global developmental delay or intellectual disability in a Chinese chromatin-remodeler cohort (PMID: 42528014).
  - Neonatal encephalopathy with a perinatal hypoxic event in 2 neonates with CHARGE (PMID: 33775046).
  - Other features include cerebellar vermis dysplasia, ventricular enlargement, hypogonadotropic hypogonadism, pituitary stalk interruption (PMID: 33270637), choanal atresia, and cardiac and renal anomalies.
- Variant types: Where specified, variants are LoF (a nonsense/frameshift-type c.4015C>T and a splice variant) or missense. Most variants in the abstracts are unspecified. Two are confirmed de novo by trio WES (PMID: 35446972), and one splice variant is supported by a mini-gene assay (PMID: 41225453).
- Cohorts: None are from UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL. Most reports are from China, with one from Italy.
- Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED for CHD7 as an epileptic encephalopathy gene. Pathogenicity for CHARGE is well supported, but epilepsy is a variable, secondary feature. No report describes a patient with an epileptic encephalopathy as the primary phenotype, and the numbers are small.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: Mouse (conditional knockouts, a heterozygous gene-trap allele, the ENU-induced Looper nonsense allele, and an ATPase-deficient knock-in) and zebrafish (a mutant and morpholino knockdown).
- Key phenotypes:
  - Neural crest-specific deletion causes conotruncal heart defects and perinatal lethality (PMID: 33127760).
  - Heterozygous and Looper mice show hearing loss, ossicle and stapes defects, semicircular canal dysgenesis, vestibular dysfunction, growth retardation, facial asymmetry and hyperactivity (PMID: 21875659; PMID: 24840056).
  - Neuroepithelial deletion causes microcephaly and olfactory bulb agenesis, rescued by EZH2 inhibition (PMID: 40164694).
  - Chd7 is required for the onset of CNS myelination (PMID: 26928066).
  - Cochlear deletion causes morphogenesis and neurosensory patterning defects (PMID: 34004180).
  - Oocyte-specific deletion causes female infertility or sub-fertility (PMID: 35402599).
  - Zebrafish mutants show craniofacial and cardiac defects (PMID: 36568983). Morpholino knockdown impairs neural crest-derived Schwann cells, enteric neurons and the craniofacial skeleton, and Sox10 knockdown rescues the defects (PMID: 27418670).
  - Mendelian ratios and sample sizes are not reported. No model shows spontaneous seizures or an epilepsy phenotype.
- Consistency with humans: The models closely replicate CHARGE features (ear, heart, craniofacial, olfactory and brain anomalies). They give little direct support for epileptic encephalopathy.
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE to STRONG for CHARGE and neurodevelopmental disruption, but LIMITED for epilepsy specifically.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: CHD7 is a chromatin remodeler that acts at enhancers. It interacts with BMP SMAD1/5/8 to regulate Nkx2.5 (PMID: 24293546). It cooperates with Sox10 (PMID: 26928066) and acts downstream of BMP2/Wnt3a to promote FoxD3 expression in neural crest-derived stem cells (PMID: 27579714). It also suppresses PPAR-γ signaling (PMID: 35418650). A pooled CRISPR imaging screen showed that CHD7 compacts chromatin over long range in a CTCF-dependent manner (ASHG 2023 - Perturb-tracing enables scalable high-content discovery of 3D genome regulators). The let-7 miRNAs regulate CHD7 expression (PMID: 32816902).
- Expression: CHD7 is expressed in the developing inner ear, retina and brain (PMID: 32816902). In the ear, Chd7 acts genetically upstream of Sox2, and double heterozygotes die early postnatally (ASHG 2022 - Chd7 and Sox2 cooperate to regulate development of the inner ear). CHD7 is also highly expressed in oocytes (PMID: 35402599). Tissue-specific maternal allele-specific methylation has been reported (PMID: 40069157).
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE. The data support a role in neurodevelopment and neural stem cell regulation, but no study directly links CHD7 to neuronal excitability or seizure mechanisms.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: LIMITED for CHD7 as an epileptic encephalopathy gene. The evidence for CHD7 as a CHARGE syndrome gene is strong.
- Key strengths: Heterozygous, often de novo LoF variants are well established in CHARGE. Several cases show epilepsy-related features, including infantile spasms (PMID: 35446972) and EEG abnormalities (PMID: 41225453). Mouse and zebrafish models consistently show neurodevelopmental, neural crest and sensory defects, and the molecular function is well characterized.
- Key gaps: Epilepsy is an inconsistent, secondary feature, and no case series is centered on an epileptic encephalopathy phenotype. Many reports lack variant-level detail, and the cohort data are indirect (PMID: 42528014). One case has a competing primary diagnosis (PMID: 37521304). Seizure phenotypes have not been assessed in the animal models. There is no UDN, DDD or similar cohort data, and no segregation or functional data link specific variants to seizures.
- Conclusion: CHD7 is a definitive autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental gene (CHARGE), and seizures and infantile spasms occur in a subset of patients. Evidence that CHD7 variants cause a primary epileptic encephalopathy is limited. Further epilepsy-focused cohort data and seizure assessment in animal models are needed before classifying CHD7 as an epileptic encephalopathy gene rather than a gene in which epilepsy is a variable associated feature.
