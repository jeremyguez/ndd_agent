MIDEAS (ELM2- and SANT-domain-containing; also called ELMSAN1) as an epileptic encephalopathy gene: literature evidence report

1. GENE SUMMARY
MIDEAS is a scaffold and corepressor subunit of the mitotic deacetylase complex (MiDAC). MiDAC assembles with HDAC1/2 and DNTTIP1 and is involved in chromatin regulation, cell-cycle progression and neurodevelopmental gene expression (PMID: 25653165; PMID: 32591534).

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- The clinical evidence agent returned no case reports or case series. No cohort-based clinical data were found (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL), and no epilepsy or epileptic encephalopathy patients with MIDEAS variants were identified.
- The only human genetic observation comes from the biological evidence set. A 2025 report (PMID: 41290615) describes 2 unrelated individuals with the same de novo heterozygous missense variant, p.Tyr654Ser. They have a multisystem neurodevelopmental syndrome with delayed speech, joint contractures, dysmorphism and gut dysmotility. Seizures or epilepsy are not described in the available summary. This report is not counted as formal clinical evidence here, but it is the only human disease association.
- The predominant variant type is missense, and it is a single recurrent variant. The proposed mechanism is a gain of function: Y654 sits in an auto-inhibitory loop over the HDAC active site, and the variant is thought to displace the loop and increase deacetylase activity. This does not fit a loss-of-function haploinsufficiency model.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE for epileptic encephalopathy. For a neurodevelopmental disorder generally, the evidence is at most very limited (2 patients).

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Mouse is the only organism studied.
- Full Mideas knockout mice die in late embryogenesis, with heart malformation, haematopoietic failure and perturbed embryonic gene expression. The phenotype is identical to that of Dnttip1 knockout mice (PMID: 32591534). No Mendelian ratios or sample sizes were reported. Heterozygous phenotypes, neurological phenotypes and seizures were not reported.
- Consistency with human phenotype is limited. Embryonic lethality shows that the gene is essential. The human de novo variant appears to act through a different mechanism (increased activity), and the heart and haematopoietic phenotypes do not match the reported neurodevelopmental features.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED. It supports essentiality but not an epilepsy or neurodevelopmental phenotype.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: MIDEAS is part of the MiDAC family, one of six HDAC1/2 complex families (SIN3, NuRD, CoREST, MiDAC, MIER, RERE) (PMID: 40966515). It is a corepressor in a cell-cycle-associated HDAC1/2 complex with DNTTIP1, and DNTTIP1 mediates complex assembly and nucleosome targeting (PMID: 25653165). The cryoEM structure shows a multivalent assembly with four HDAC1 copies at the periphery. MiDAC is also required for chromosome alignment in mitosis in cancer cells (PMID: 32591534).
- Expression: no expression data were provided. The neurodevelopmental relevance comes from functional studies.
- Key findings:
  - MiDAC regulates a neurodevelopmental gene expression program. It upregulates SLIT3 and NTN1, probably via H4K20ac removal, and represses negative regulators of neurogenesis by reducing H3K27ac. Loss of MiDAC causes neurite outgrowth defects that SLIT3 and/or NTN1 supplementation rescues (PMID: 32297854).
  - Patient fibroblasts carrying p.Tyr654Ser show gene expression changes reciprocal to those seen after rapid MiDAC degradation, which is consistent with increased MiDAC activity (PMID: 41290615).
  - MIDEAS is required for Tat-activated HIV expression in cells (PMID: 38781120). This is not relevant to the neurological phenotype.
  - MIDEAS was differentially methylated in blood in late-onset Alzheimer's disease (PMID: 40343304). This is an epigenetic association from public datasets, not genetic evidence, and it is of low relevance.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for a role in neurodevelopmental gene regulation. It is not specific to epilepsy.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level for MIDEAS as an epileptic encephalopathy gene with heterozygous pathogenic variants: INSUFFICIENT.
- Key strengths:
  - MiDAC is mechanistically well characterised, with structural data.
  - MiDAC regulates neurite outgrowth and neurodevelopmental genes.
  - A recurrent de novo missense variant in 2 unrelated individuals has functional support, with a plausible gain-of-function mechanism.
  - The mouse knockout shows the gene is essential.
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - There are no clinical cases of epilepsy or epileptic encephalopathy.
  - Only 2 patients with a neurodevelopmental syndrome are reported, both with the same variant, and no seizure phenotype is described.
  - There are no loss-of-function variants and no cohort data.
  - The mouse knockout is embryonic lethal, with no heterozygous or neurological phenotyping.
  - The proposed disease mechanism is gain of function, which is not typical of the haploinsufficiency model for other epilepsy genes.
- Conclusion: There is currently insufficient evidence to classify MIDEAS as an epileptic encephalopathy gene. The best support is for a rare neurodevelopmental syndrome with multisystem features, based on one recurrent de novo missense variant (PMID: 41290615), and seizures have not been documented. Further patients with MIDEAS variants and epilepsy, together with functional validation, would be needed to support an epilepsy association.
