MFN2 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: EPILEPTIC ENCEPHALOPATHY, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) PATHOGENIC VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
MFN2 encodes mitofusin 2, a mitochondrial outer-membrane GTPase. It mediates mitochondrial fusion and tethers the ER to mitochondria (MAMs). Heterozygous MFN2 variants are the best-established cause of axonal Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2A (CMT2A). The evidence below concerns a different question: whether MFN2 is an epileptic encephalopathy gene.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Number of reports: 8 clinical reports or series were extracted. These include 2 ASHG abstracts, 2 HSP cohort or family reports, 1 CMT2 phenotype study, 1 Indian inherited-neuropathy cohort, 1 single-patient splice-variant report, and 1 large Japanese CMT series.
- Approximate patients: about 130 individuals with MFN2-related findings. The main contributors are the Japanese series of 79 patients (PMID: 28660751) and the 47 patients in ASHG 2016 (Analysis of mitochondria-related gene from clinically suspected Charcot-Marie-Tooth patients). Some of these patients may overlap, because both studies come from Nagoya. The remaining reports contribute 1-2 patients each, or an unspecified number.
- Phenotypes: The dominant phenotype is axonal CMT/CMT2 neuropathy. The Japanese series (PMID: 28660751) also reported optic atrophy, vocal cord paralysis and spasticity in some patients. Mean onset was 12 ± 14 years. 43 cases had an autosomal dominant family history and 36 were sporadic. Other phenotypes were:
  - Hereditary spastic paraplegia in two Iranian reports (PMID: 34353391; PMID: 42120987). One of these was a dual diagnosis with ERLIN2.
  - Severe CMT2 with fatal encephalopathy, reported with a novel splice variant in a single patient (PMID: 20530328). The encephalopathy is not described as epileptic in the available data.
  - Tonic-clonic seizures and developmental delay in one patient with a de novo MFN2 point mutation (ASHG 2015 - The role of combined SNV and CNV burden in patients with distal symmetric polyneuropathy). This patient also carried a de novo PRICKLE1 CNV, a gene linked to epilepsy, so the seizures are confounded.
- Variant type: Predominantly missense, with a few LoF or other types. Missense is mostly assumed from the known MFN2 spectrum rather than stated in the abstracts. Specific variants are largely not reported. Other types include one splice variant (PMID: 20530328) and one compound heterozygous case (PMID: 28660751).
- Cohorts: No UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL cohorts were identified. The ASHG 2015 abstract comes from the Baylor/Lupski group, which is not one of these named cohorts.
- Strength of clinical evidence for epileptic encephalopathy: LIMITED. Clinical evidence for MFN2 in CMT2 is strong, but evidence for epileptic encephalopathy specifically is minimal. It rests on one confounded patient with seizures (ASHG 2015) and one fatal encephalopathy case without documented epilepsy (PMID: 20530328).

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organism: Mouse only.
- Full knockout: Mfn2-null embryos die in midgestation with severe disruption of the placental trophoblast giant cell layer. Null MEFs show fragmented mitochondria (PMID: 12527753).
- Knock-in: The R94W CMT2A knock-in (ASHG 2012 - Characterization of a novel mitofusin 2 knock-in mouse model) causes perinatal lethality in homozygotes, with low birth weight and death within a day of birth. Heterozygotes show no gross behavioral or morphological deficits up to 1 year. MEFs show fragmented mitochondria.
- Conditional knockouts: These models are viable and show non-seizure phenotypes in platelets (PMID: 38156445), liver (PMID: 31051106) and the heart. VIP neuron-specific deletion (PMID: 40590229) disrupts circadian rhythm, sleep-wake architecture and body temperature. Cerebellum-specific deletion causes Purkinje cell degeneration (PMID: 17693261).
- Consistency with human phenotype: Neurodegeneration (Purkinje cells) and the mitochondrial defects are broadly consistent with CMT2A pathophysiology. Seizures and epileptic encephalopathy have not been reported in any model, and heterozygous R94W mice lack an overt phenotype.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED for epileptic encephalopathy. It is moderate to strong for a role in neurodegeneration and mitochondrial function.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Function and pathways: MFN2 mediates mitochondrial fusion, ER-mitochondria tethering and mitochondrial transport. Key findings:
  - It interacts directly with AMPK, and loss of MFN2 reduces MAMs and attenuates autophagy (PMID: 32249716).
  - It binds phosphatidylserine and promotes its transfer to mitochondria (PMID: 31051106).
  - It maintains mitochondrial coenzyme Q levels (PMID: 25688136).
  - It interacts with SERCA2 to regulate Ca2+ handling (PMID: 37738362).
  - It interacts with PERK (PMID: 34988075).
  - It recruits ATAT1 to regulate α-tubulin acetylation and mitochondrial motility. The CMT2A mutations R94W and T105M may disrupt this (PMID: 38883841).
- Expression: Brain and neurodevelopmental expression data were not supplied. Neuronal relevance is shown by the Purkinje cell dependence on Mfn2 for dendritic outgrowth, spine formation and survival (PMID: 17693261).
- Association data: The MFN2 SNP rs1474868 is associated with platelet counts and ARDS mortality (PMID: 38156445). This is not relevant to epileptic encephalopathy.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE. The evidence on mitochondrial and neuronal function is robust, but it does not specifically link MFN2 to epileptogenesis.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level for MFN2 as an epileptic encephalopathy gene: LIMITED (bordering on insufficient).
- Key strengths: MFN2 is a well-validated autosomal dominant neuropathy gene, with a large number of patients (PMID: 28660751). It has a clear role in mitochondrial dynamics, and neuron-specific mouse models show neurodegeneration (PMID: 17693261).
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - Only two reports describe a central nervous system phenotype that overlaps with epileptic encephalopathy. One is a single fatal encephalopathy case with a splice variant (PMID: 20530328). The other is a single patient with seizures and a confounding de novo PRICKLE1 CNV (ASHG 2015).
  - The extracted data are mostly abstract-level, and variant details, segregation, de novo status and ACMG classifications are generally missing.
  - Many variant types were assumed rather than reported.
  - No mouse model reports seizures.
  - No named large rare-disease cohorts are represented.
- Conclusion: MFN2 is an established cause of dominant CMT2A, with occasional CNS involvement (optic atrophy, spasticity, HSP-like features, rare encephalopathy). The available evidence does not establish heterozygous MFN2 variants as a cause of epileptic encephalopathy. Further curation with well-phenotyped patients with seizures and de novo or segregating variants is needed before a gene-disease relationship for epileptic encephalopathy can be supported.
