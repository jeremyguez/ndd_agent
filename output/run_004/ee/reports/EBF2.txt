EBF2 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: EBF2 as a candidate epileptic encephalopathy gene (heterozygous, autosomal dominant)

1. GENE SUMMARY
EBF2 (early B-cell factor 2) encodes a COE-family (collier/Olf/Ebf) transcription factor. It is best characterized as a determinant of brown and beige adipocyte identity, and it also has developmental roles in the cerebellum, peripheral nerve, hypothalamus and spinal motor neurons. No epileptic encephalopathy or other human Mendelian disease association is established in the evidence reviewed.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent case reports or case series: 0 were found.
- Total patients: 0.
- Phenotypes: none reported, so there is no human phenotype to compare with epileptic encephalopathy. Seizures are not described in any of the evidence.
- Variant type: no pathogenic variants (LoF, missense or other) are reported. No heterozygous de novo or inherited variants are documented.
- Cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none identified.
- The only human genetic data are a GWAS for inguinal hernia (PMID: 26686553). It identified the EBF2 locus among four novel susceptibility loci (5,295 cases and 67,510 controls in discovery; 9,701 cases and 82,743 controls in replication). This is a common-variant association with an unrelated phenotype and does not support a neurological disease role.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse (full Ebf2 knockouts) is the only vertebrate model with phenotyping. C. elegans appears only in a rescue context, where mouse Ebf2 rescued unc-3 mutant motor defects (PMID: 30658714).
- Knockout mice are viable. Mendelian ratios and sample sizes are not reported in the extracted data.
- Key neurological and endocrine phenotypes:
  - GnRH neuron migration defects with hypogonadotropic hypogonadism and infertility, plus peripheral neuropathy with hypomyelination, axonal damage and sharply reduced motor nerve conduction velocity (PMID: 12466206).
  - Schwann cell and axonal defects, with severely impaired motor and less affected sensory nerve parameters (PMID: 21220016).
  - Locomotor impairment, poor motor coordination and learning, cerebellar hypotrophy with loss of a Purkinje cell subset, and altered Purkinje cell firing (PMID: 23000673).
  - Selective Purkinje cell death and ectopic zebrin II-positive gene expression (PMID: 18403128). Disrupted Purkinje cell topography also causes secondary abnormal unipolar brush cell distribution (PMID: 19800947).
  - Severely hypocellular fastigial nuclei and defective anterior lobe Purkinje cell development (PMID: 31273610).
  - Decreased orexin neurons with a narcolepsy-cataplexy-like phenotype (PMID: 21435382).
  - Marked reduction of periaqueductal gray dopamine neurons, with substantia nigra and VTA neurons spared (PMID: 25762221).
  - Loss of brown adipocyte identity and thermogenic capacity (PMID: 23499423), and impaired beige adipocyte induction (PMID: 26844207).
- Consistency with the human phenotype: there is no human phenotype to compare with. The mouse phenotypes (ataxia-like motor dysfunction, neuropathy, hypogonadism, sleep disorder) are neurodevelopmental but do not include reported seizures or an encephalopathy. All data are from homozygous nulls; heterozygous phenotypes are not described, and this limits relevance to a dominant disease mechanism.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED for epileptic encephalopathy. The evidence is robust for a role in neural development, but not specific to this disease.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Molecular function: EBF2 is a DNA-binding transcription factor. It recruits PPARγ to brown-selective sites (PMID: 23499423). It binds BAT enhancers and interacts with BRG1 and the BAF complex, with Dpf3 as a direct target (PMID: 28428261). It forms a ribonucleoprotein complex with the lncRNA Blnc1 in a feedforward loop (PMID: 25002143). It interacts with CBP/P300 to deposit H3K27ac (PMID: 39736442). It is stabilized by the deubiquitinase USP2 (PMID: 40189098) and is transcriptionally activated by SOX4 (PMID: 39402212).
- Expression: Ebf2 mRNA is present in all Purkinje cell progenitors, in an early-born subset of anterior-lobe granule cell precursors, and prenatally in projection neurons of all cerebellar nuclei (PMID: 31273610). It is also expressed in medial motor column spinal motor neurons, and Ebf2 knockout mice show defective differentiation of a subset of these neurons (PMID: 30658714).
- Key experimental findings: most mechanistic work concerns adipose biology. The neural findings concern cerebellar, motor neuron and peripheral nerve development, not cortical or epilepsy-related circuits.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for a general developmental transcriptional role, but LIMITED relevance to epileptic encephalopathy.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT for EBF2 as an epileptic encephalopathy gene with heterozygous pathogenic variants.
- Key strengths: well-characterized transcription factor function; consistent mouse knockout data showing roles in cerebellar Purkinje cell development, myelination and motor neuron differentiation, and in hypothalamic neuron development.
- Key gaps:
  - No human case reports or variant data.
  - No reported seizures in any model.
  - Only homozygous null mouse phenotypes are described, with no heterozygote data.
  - Brain expression data focus on the cerebellum rather than cortex.
  - Most mechanistic literature concerns adipose tissue.
  - No evidence on dominant-negative or haploinsufficiency mechanisms.
- Conclusion: there is currently no clinical or genetic evidence linking EBF2 to epileptic encephalopathy. Mouse and biological data show that EBF2 is important for neural development, particularly the cerebellum, peripheral nerves and motor neurons, but they do not point to epilepsy. EBF2 should be regarded as having insufficient evidence for this disease association unless human patients with heterozygous variants and a seizure phenotype are identified.
