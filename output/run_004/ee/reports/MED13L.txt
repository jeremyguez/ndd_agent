MED13L EVIDENCE REPORT: EPILEPTIC ENCEPTHALOPATHY, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
MED13L encodes a subunit of the CDK8 kinase module of the Mediator complex, the paralog of MED13. It acts as a transcriptional coregulator that helps recruit RNA polymerase II and primes enhancer/gene activation during neurodevelopment (PMID: 40775066). Heterozygous loss of function causes MED13L haploinsufficiency syndrome (MRFACD), an intellectual disability syndrome in which epilepsy is a variable feature.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Number of reports: 7 clinical reports or series were retrieved: 5 single-patient reports, 1 two-patient report, and 1 larger phenotyping cohort.
- Total patients: about 74 (1 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 67). The 67-person cohort (PMID: 39257968) accounts for most of them, and its overlap with other reports is not established.
- Most common phenotypes: intellectual disability or global developmental delay, speech and language impairment, and hypotonia. The large cohort reported language impairment in 97%, hypotonia in 83%, vision problems in 72%, recurrent otitis media in 58%, gastrointestinal problems in 57%, and seizures in 31% (PMID: 39257968). Facial dysmorphism is frequent. Cardiac defects (VSD, PDA), renal anomalies, scoliosis, and autism were reported in individual patients.
- Epilepsy-specific evidence:
  - A de novo frameshift, NM_015335 c.4670dup (p.Ala1558SerfsTer6), was classified pathogenic in a patient with developmental and epileptic encephalopathy with early-onset drug-resistant epilepsy, identified by first-line WES (PMID: 38256219).
  - A de novo nonsense variant, p.(Lys1474*), was reported with seizures, an epileptiform EEG, and choreiform movements (ASHG 2014 - A Novel Mutation, p.(Lys1474*), in a Female adds Seizures and Ptosis to Clinical Findings in MED13L Haploinsufficiency Syndrome).
  - A 15-year-old with MED13L haploinsufficiency syndrome had catatonia and epilepsy diathesis (PMID: 38968441).
  - Seizures were also reported in a patient with p.Pro1526Thr (ASHG 2018 - Expanding the phenotype of MED13L-associated mental retardation and distinctive facial features with or without cardiac defects; MRFACD) and an abnormal EEG in a patient with p.Pro869Ser (PMID: 32646507).
- Variant types: LoF (nonsense, frameshift, intragenic deletions) is predominant, together with recurrent de novo missense variants. Examples are p.Pro869Ser (PMID: 32646507) and p.Asp860Gly (PMID: 28645799), which also appear recurrently. Most variants were de novo by WES.
- Cohorts: no UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL cohort membership was identified. Centers were in China, the USA, and Italy.
- Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG for MED13L as a dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene. For the epileptic encephalopathy phenotype specifically it is MODERATE, because seizures occur in about 31% and only a few reports describe a DEE presentation.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse and zebrafish.
- Mouse knockouts (PMID: 40775066):
  - Homozygous knockouts show neonatal lethality, reduced brain volume and cortical thickness, and severe neurogenesis deficits.
  - Heterozygotes are viable, with impaired learning and memory, reduced motor coordination, heightened anxiety, and microcephaly with simplified neuronal morphology.
  - Heterozygous exon 11 deletion mice show growth delay and craniofacial anomalies (pug or crooked snout, midface hypoplasia) in more than 60% (PMID: 40919805).
  - A cardiomyocyte Med13/Med13L double knockout caused lethal heart failure (PMID: 40989238).
  - Mendelian ratios were not reported. Spontaneous seizures were not reported in the retrieved findings.
- Zebrafish: med13b morpholino knockdown caused defective cranial neural crest migration, cartilage deformities, and abnormal neuronal distribution. The neuronal phenotype was rescued by human MED13L mRNA (PMID: 25137640).
- Consistency: the heterozygous phenotypes (cognitive and motor deficits, microcephaly, craniofacial anomalies, growth delay) match the human syndrome well. Epilepsy has not been modeled.
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE (consistent for neurodevelopmental and craniofacial features, but LIMITED for epilepsy).

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Function: MED13L is a CDK8-kinase module subunit of Mediator. It binds core Mediator and facilitates its association with RNA Pol II, priming activation of Neurod2, Sox5, Auts2, and Nfib (PMID: 40775066).
- Expression: it is enriched in the ventricular zone and in cortical plate neurons at E14 and becomes diffusely cytoplasmic by P30. It partially colocalizes with synaptophysin and PSD95 in cultured hippocampal neurons, suggesting a synaptic role (PMID: 33794529). Expression is also reported in the hippocampus and dentate gyrus (ASHG 2012 - De novo translocation disrupting Mediator complex subunit in a patient with Pierre-Robin sequence and developmental delay).
- Key experiments:
  - MED13L knockout hiPSC cerebral organoids produced only neuroretinal cells because of aberrant cis-regulatory element activation (ASHG 2022 - MED13L knockout in cerebral organoids leads to a shifted developmental program through abnormal cis-regulatory element activation).
  - A patient with a de novo translocation had more than 50% reduction of transcript and protein (ASHG 2012 - De novo translocation disrupting Mediator complex subunit in a patient with Pierre-Robin sequence and developmental delay).
  - CRISPR editing in fibroblasts reduced viability and suppressed RB1, E2F1, and CCNC (PMID: 37512036).
  - Missense variants cluster in exons 15-17 and 25-31 and were tested in ubiquitination, phosphorylation, and zebrafish rescue assays (ASHG 2018 - Functional analysis of MED13L missense variants).
  - Mosaic parental transmission of intragenic deletions supports a dosage-sensitive mechanism (PMID: 28371282).
- GWAS reports (colon cancer, obesity) are peripheral to this question.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE to STRONG for neurodevelopmental mechanism; LIMITED for a direct link to epilepsy.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: STRONG for MED13L as an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene. MODERATE for its classification as an epileptic encephalopathy gene.
- Key strengths: recurrent de novo LoF and missense variants, a sizeable phenotyped cohort (67 individuals), concordant heterozygous mouse phenotypes, and a defined role in transcriptional regulation of neurogenesis genes.
- Key gaps: seizures occur in only about 31% of patients, and DEE presentations are few (PMID: 38256219). Several reports lack variant detail, and patient overlap between reports is unclear. No established cohort membership was found. Epilepsy and EEG phenotypes are not modeled in animals. Functional validation of individual missense variants is limited.
- Conclusion: MED13L heterozygous pathogenic variants robustly cause a neurodevelopmental syndrome through haploinsufficiency. Epilepsy, including drug-resistant DEE, is a recognized but variable component. MED13L is best classified as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with epilepsy association, supported by moderate evidence for an epileptic encephalopathy phenotype.
