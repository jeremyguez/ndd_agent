C1QTNF6 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: EVALUATION AS A DOMINANT EPILEPTIC ENCEPHALOPATHY GENE

1. GENE SUMMARY
C1QTNF6 encodes CTRP6 (C1q/TNF-related protein 6), a secreted member of the C1q/TNF-related protein family. Its best-characterized function is as an endogenous suppressor of the alternative complement pathway: it competes with factor B for C3(H2O) binding (PMID: 26404464). It has also been implicated in inflammatory signaling, adipose and metabolic regulation, and cell proliferation. No neurological or epilepsy function has been established.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent case reports or case series: 0. The clinical evidence extraction returned no entries.
- Total patients: 0.
- Phenotypes: none reported. No epilepsy, developmental delay or encephalopathy phenotype has been linked to C1QTNF6 variants in the supplied evidence.
- Predominant variant type: not applicable, as no pathogenic variants (LoF, missense or other) were reported.
- Cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms studied: mouse only, with full knockouts in two studies.
- Key phenotypes:
  - C1qtnf6-/- mice are highly susceptible to induced arthritis because of enhanced complement activation. They also show an enhanced Arthus reaction and enhanced experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis (EAE). C1qtnf6-transgenic mice are refractory to arthritis (PMID: 26404464).
  - Knockout embryos are described as semi-lethal, with a sub-Mendelian ratio (PMID: 26404464). A second knockout study described mice as viable (PMID: 34632797), so the lethality findings are not fully consistent between reports.
  - CTRP6-KO mice gained more whole-body and adipose weight than wild-type littermates after 3 days of high-fat feeding. CTRP6-KO MEFs showed increased adipogenic gene expression and reduced inflammatory gene expression (PMID: 34632797).
  - No seizures, brain malformation, or neurodevelopmental or behavioral phenotypes were reported. The EAE finding reflects immune-mediated CNS inflammation rather than a primary neuronal phenotype.
  - The knockouts are homozygous. Heterozygous phenotypes were not described, so there is no support for a dominant mechanism.
- Consistency with human phenotype: none can be assessed, because no human phenotype exists.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED for any role in epileptic encephalopathy. The mouse data show immune, metabolic and partial embryonic viability phenotypes, but nothing neurological of relevance.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and molecular function:
  - Suppression of the alternative complement pathway (PMID: 26404464).
  - Pro-inflammatory modulation through AKT/NF-κB signaling in granulosa cells (PMID: 33959757).
  - AMPK signaling in bronchial epithelial inflammation, under regulation by miR-29b-3p (PMID: 31955152).
  - The miR-29b/C1QTNF6 axis in leukocyte inflammation after ischemic stroke, where C1QTNF6 overexpression worsened blood-brain barrier disruption and injury (PMID: 35322553).
- Expression pattern: C1QTNF6 is expressed in human synoviocytes and elevated in rheumatoid arthritis (PMID: 26404464). It is overexpressed in multiple cancers (PMID: 35401204; PMID: 32282241). It is expressed in chicken follicular granulosa cells (PMID: 39566174). Brain and neurodevelopmental expression was not addressed in the supplied evidence.
- Key experimental findings:
  - Oncogenic roles in non-small cell lung cancer (PMID: 33269376), oral squamous cell carcinoma (PMID: 34906149) and lung adenocarcinoma (PMID: 35578071).
  - Elevated levels in PCOS (PMID: 33959757).
  - None of these findings concern neuronal function, synaptic biology or seizure mechanisms.
- Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED. It supports roles in inflammation, complement regulation, metabolism and cancer, but offers no direct link to epileptic encephalopathy.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT.
- Key strengths: C1QTNF6 has a well-defined function in complement regulation, and knockout mouse models exist with reproducible immune and metabolic phenotypes (PMID: 26404464; PMID: 34632797).
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - There are no human patients or variants.
  - There are no neurological phenotypes in the models.
  - There are no data on heterozygous variants or a dominant-negative or haploinsufficiency mechanism.
  - Brain expression data are absent.
  - Most biological literature concerns cancer and inflammation.
- Conclusion: There is currently no evidence that heterozygous pathogenic variants in C1QTNF6 cause epileptic encephalopathy. The available literature describes complement-regulatory, inflammatory, metabolic and oncologic roles without a neurodevelopmental link. The gene should not be considered an epileptic encephalopathy candidate unless human genetic evidence emerges.
