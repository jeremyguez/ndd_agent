RETREG2 (FAM134A) EVIDENCE REPORT: Candidate gene for epileptic encephalopathy (heterozygous/autosomal dominant)

1. GENE SUMMARY
RETREG2 (also known as FAM134A) is a paralog of RETREG1 (FAM134B), a reticulophagy receptor family member involved in ER-phagy and ER remodeling. In this literature review, no study defined its function directly. The only retrieved evidence is a genetic-association study in glioma, so function is inferred from paralogy rather than shown experimentally.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent case reports/case series: 0 found.
- Total patients: 0.
- Phenotypes: None reported. No epileptic encephalopathy or neurodevelopmental phenotype has been linked to RETREG2 in the retrieved literature.
- Variant type: Not applicable. No heterozygous pathogenic variants (LoF, missense or otherwise) have been described.
- Known cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): None identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms studied: None identified in the retrieved evidence.
- Knockout phenotypes (neurological, lethality, Mendelian ratio deviations): No data.
- Consistency with human phenotype: Cannot be assessed.
- Strength of model organism evidence: NONE

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and molecular function: No direct functional experiments on RETREG2 were found. Its role in ER-phagy is inferred from its relationship to RETREG1, not from the retrieved evidence.
- Expression pattern: The only relevant data come from eQTL resources used in a glioma study, which included brain tissue (effective n=1,194) and 13 brain tissues (n=114-209). These show that RETREG2 expression is genetically regulated in brain. They do not establish neurodevelopmental relevance.
- Key findings: A transcriptome-wide Mendelian randomization study integrating multi-tissue eQTLs with glioma GWAS data (7,400 cases, 8,257 controls; GBM 3,112 cases; non-GBM 2,411 cases) found that genetically predicted increased RETREG2 expression was associated with glioma risk (PMID: 33504897). RETREG2 was one of three novel susceptibility genes, alongside FAM178B and MVB12B. The effect was relatively consistent across GBM and non-GBM subtypes, and colocalisation supported the finding. The abstract gave no specific p-values. This is common-variant, expression-based association evidence in a different disease (glioma). It involves no rare pathogenic variants and no functional experiments, and it is not relevant to epilepsy.
- Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED. The only evidence is a single eQTL/MR association for glioma, which does not support a role in epileptic encephalopathy.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT
- Key strengths: Essentially none for the epileptic encephalopathy hypothesis. The glioma MR study (PMID: 33504897) shows that RETREG2 expression is genetically regulated in brain tissue and supports a possible role in brain biology. It is methodologically reasonably robust, with large eQTL datasets and colocalisation.
- Key gaps: There are no patients, no variants, no segregation or de novo data, no cohort data, no animal models and no functional studies. The only association concerns glioma risk through expression changes, which is unrelated to a Mendelian epilepsy mechanism.
- Conclusion: There is currently no clinical, model organism or relevant biological evidence supporting RETREG2 as an autosomal dominant epileptic encephalopathy gene. The single glioma MR study does not address this disease. RETREG2 should not be classified as a disease gene for this phenotype unless patients with heterozygous variants and supporting functional data are reported.
