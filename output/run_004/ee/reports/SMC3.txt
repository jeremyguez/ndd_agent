SMC3 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: EPILEPTIC ENCEPHALOPATHY, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
SMC3 encodes a core subunit of the cohesin complex (with SMC1A, RAD21 and STAG proteins). Cohesin mediates sister chromatid cohesion and also organizes 3D chromatin architecture and enhancer-promoter interactions for transcriptional regulation. Heterozygous SMC3 variants are an established cause of Cornelia de Lange syndrome 3 (CdLS3), a neurodevelopmental disorder. Epileptic encephalopathy is not an established primary phenotype of SMC3.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Number of reports: Two single-patient reports were found, both of limited relevance to epileptic encephalopathy.
- Total patients: Approximately 2.
- Reports:
  - New-onset refractory status epilepticus (NORSE) (PMID: 27462243). One patient with an inherited heterozygous missense variant, c.1280A>G, p.Lys427Arg. The patient had cryptogenic NORSE with recurrent seizures and 47 days of status epilepticus refractory to third-line therapy. The authors judged the inherited variant insufficient to explain the seizures. It is likely a VUS or incidental finding and is not diagnostic.
  - Exome sequencing in 90 children with developmental delay (PMID: 39678379). One child had a heterozygous SMC3 variant, not specified in the available text, reported as pathogenic or likely pathogenic and associated with CdLS3 and developmental delay. The child also carried an MT-TL1 mitochondrial variant with MELAS features. Seizures or epileptic encephalopathy were not described as an SMC3-attributed feature.
- Most common phenotypes: Developmental delay and CdLS3 features. Seizures appear only in the NORSE case, where the variant was not considered causal.
- Variant type: Predominantly missense or unspecified. No LoF variants were described in the clinical reports.
- Cohorts: No patients were identified as belonging to UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL. Center and country were not reported.
- Supporting context from the biological evidence: 14 individuals with heterozygous SMC3 pLoF variants show a milder, variable, atypical CdLS-like phenotype with incomplete penetrance (PMID: 38297832). Four individuals with SMC3 or RAD21 variants had holoprosencephaly (PMID: 31334757). These reports do not describe epileptic encephalopathy.
- Strength of clinical evidence for SMC3 as an epileptic encephalopathy gene: LIMITED (bordering on NONE). Evidence for a neurodevelopmental disorder (CdLS3) exists, but evidence specifically for epileptic encephalopathy is absent.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: Mouse and zebrafish.
- Mouse:
  - Heart-specific conditional knockout causes outflow tract abnormalities, with downregulation of Ets2 and reduced super-enhancer–promoter interactions (PMID: 39085358).
  - B-cell conditional knockout: homozygous deletion abrogates germinal center formation. Haploinsufficiency causes GC hyperplasia, impaired plasma cell differentiation and accelerated lymphomagenesis (PMID: 33432228; PMID: 34621263).
  - A full knockout was listed among IMPC/MGP lines with brain structural abnormalities in a neuroanatomy screen (ASHG 2015 - Deciphering the genetic basis of intellectual disability using 1000 knockouts). Details were limited.
- Zebrafish:
  - Smc3 knockdown abolishes runx3 expression and hematopoietic runx1 expression (PMID: 17567667).
  - Knockdown reduces kinocilial length and hair cell number, with neomycin resistance. Mechanotransduction is preserved (PMID: 41263044). Abnormal otolith development was previously reported.
- Not reported: Mendelian ratios, lethality figures, sample sizes, seizure or epilepsy phenotypes.
- Consistency with human phenotype: The cardiac, sensory (ear) and brain structural findings fit CdLS-spectrum features, and the haploinsufficiency findings fit the human pLoF data. No model shows seizures or an epileptic encephalopathy phenotype.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED to MODERATE for a neurodevelopmental or CdLS-like disorder. LIMITED for epileptic encephalopathy specifically.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function:
  - SMC3 is a cohesin core ATPase subunit. Its acetylation by ESCO1/ESCO2 stabilizes cohesin on chromatin, and sororin acts through this acetylation by inhibiting WAPL (PMID: 26903600).
  - CDK phosphorylation of ESCO2 at serine 75 controls cohesion establishment (PMID: 41694619).
  - SMC3 acetylation status is linked to genomic stability (PMID: 38355793).
  - Cohesin has a transcriptional role separable from its mitotic role (PMID: 17567667), including enhancer connectivity and super-enhancer regulation (PMID: 39085358; PMID: 33432228).
- Expression: Brain expression data were not provided. Neurodevelopmental relevance is inferred from cohesin's role in midline forebrain development (PMID: 31334757) and from the human phenotypes.
- Key findings:
  - SMC3 is highly constrained against pLoF variants and depleted for missense variants, behaving as a haploinsufficient gene. pLoF variants reduce SMC3 expression but do not cluster with the CdLS epigenetic signature (PMID: 38297832).
  - Proteomic profiling of CdLS cell lines with SMC1A or SMC3 mutations showed dysregulated metabolism, cytoskeleton, antioxidant and RNA-processing pathways, with c-MYC down-regulated (ASHG 2012 - Dysregulated pathways in Cornelia de Lange syndrome cells mutated in core cohesin genes).
  - 16 rare potentially pathogenic SMC3 variants were found in isolated congenital heart disease (CHD) (PMID: 39085358).
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for SMC3 in neurodevelopmental disease and chromatin regulation. LIMITED for any epilepsy-specific mechanism.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level for SMC3 as an epileptic encephalopathy gene: LIMITED (close to INSUFFICIENT).
- Key strengths: SMC3 is a well-established CdLS3 and neurodevelopmental gene with clear haploinsufficiency and a defined molecular function. Mouse and zebrafish models show consistent developmental and transcriptional phenotypes.
- Key gaps:
  - Only two single-patient clinical reports were found. One variant was inherited and non-diagnostic, and the other was unspecified and co-occurred with a mitochondrial variant.
  - There are no cohort data, no de novo LoF cases with epilepsy, and no models with seizure phenotypes.
  - Phenotype penetrance is variable, and SMC3 pLoF carriers show a milder, atypical CdLS-like picture.
- Conclusion: SMC3 is a robust neurodevelopmental disease gene (CdLS3), but the available evidence does not support it as an epileptic encephalopathy gene. The single seizure-related case (PMID: 27462243) was judged non-causal, and no model or biological data link SMC3 to epilepsy. Seizures may be an occasional feature within the CdLS spectrum, but further patient data are needed before any gene-disease assertion for epileptic encephalopathy.
