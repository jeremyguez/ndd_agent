RAB5C LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: RAB5C AND EPILEPTIC ENCEPHALOPATHY (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
RAB5C encodes a small GTPase of the Rab5 family (with RAB5A and RAB5B). It regulates early endosome biogenesis, endocytic trafficking and receptor sorting. Isoform-specific roles have been described in cell motility, Notch and VEGFR2 signaling, and phagocytosis.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports found: 1 in the clinical evidence set, a targeted sequencing study of 351 candidate epileptic encephalopathy (EE) genes (PMID: 27652284).
- Patients: 1 (Kiel, Germany; sample KIEL92). That patient carried a heterozygous nonsense variant, p.W130X (chr17:40278712 C>T).
- Phenotype: EE only. The cohort was ascertained for EE and no individual-level phenotype was given.
- Variant type: LoF, but it affects only minor transcripts. The variant was not classified as likely pathogenic, and it was listed as a dominant candidate with a prioritization score of 0.83. No de novo status was established. The only de novo candidate-gene variant highlighted in the study was in HNRNPU.
- Cohorts: none of the known cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL).
- Supporting data from the biological evidence set: a separate series (PMID: 37552066) reported 12 individuals with 9 de novo heterozygous RAB5C variants. Missense variants gave macrocephaly with mild-to-moderate developmental delay, while LoF variants gave epilepsy and intellectual disability. This is the most relevant human data for an epilepsy/neurodevelopmental link. Only the summary is available, with no per-patient breakdown of how many had epilepsy. The LoF-associated epilepsy and ID is the point most relevant to the EE hypothesis.
- Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED. The single EE-cohort case is weak and uncertain. The de novo series adds support for a neurodevelopmental disorder, but its epilepsy component rests on LoF cases of unspecified number.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: zebrafish (the majority of studies) and Xenopus laevis. C. elegans work in PMID: 37552066 is invertebrate and is covered under biological evidence.
- Key phenotypes, all from knockdown, dominant-negative or overexpression approaches rather than germline knockout:
  - Rab5c deficiency impairs hematopoietic stem and progenitor cell (HSPC) production via Notch and AKT signaling (PMID: 32275659).
  - Blocking Rab5c phenocopies slb/wnt11 gastrulation defects, with impaired cell cohesion and altered E-cadherin endocytosis (PMID: 16198297).
  - RIN2/Rab5C-mediated VEGFR2 stabilization is required for angiogenesis and tip cell formation (PMID: 33983539).
  - Rab5c-mediated endocytosis affects endoderm polarity in gpc4-deficient embryos (PMID: 34131730).
  - Expressing human RAB5C missense variants in zebrafish embryos produced defective development (PMID: 37552066).
  - In Xenopus tadpoles, Rab5c-dependent endocytosis of AMPAR subunits GluA1 and GluA2 drives synaptic downscaling (PMID: 41507541). This is the only neuronal phenotype.
- Not reported: Mendelian ratios, lethality, sample sizes, seizure phenotypes, or a brain-specific knockout phenotype.
- Consistency with human phenotype: partial. The developmental disruption from variant expression in zebrafish fits a neurodevelopmental disorder, and the Xenopus AMPAR work gives a plausible synaptic mechanism. There is no direct model of seizures or macrocephaly.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED to MODERATE. Rab5c is clearly essential for vertebrate development, but there is no neurological or epilepsy-specific phenotype and no true knockout.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: Rab5c regulates endocytosis, endosomal sorting and recycling. It acts in:
  - Notch and AKT signaling (PMID: 32275659).
  - VEGFR2 signaling.
  - Rac1-mediated motility, as the isoform-specific Rab5 member (PMID: 24587345).
  - CD93/β1 integrin recycling (PMID: 31138217).
  - LC3-associated phagocytosis (PMID: 42102192).
  - VWF release (PMID: 41537263).
  - Autophagic degradation of ACC1 (PMID: 39779666).
  - Interaction with the GAP TBC1D16 (PMID: 34367131).
- Expression: oncology studies report upregulation in breast cancer (PMID: 36480789) and in rectal cancer after chemoradiation (PMID: 30968159). No brain-specific expression data were provided, so neurodevelopmental relevance is inferred from the human variant series and the Xenopus AMPAR findings.
- Key functional findings: in PMID: 37552066, 4 missense variants were damaging biochemically (faster nucleotide exchange, reduced GEF responsiveness). In C. elegans they caused endocytic defects, two acting by a dominant-negative mechanism. This supports a pathogenic mechanism for missense variants, but not specifically for LoF or epilepsy.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE. Gene function is well characterized and the variant functional work is informative, but most studies are non-neuronal.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: LIMITED for RAB5C as an epileptic encephalopathy gene with heterozygous variants. Evidence for a broader neurodevelopmental disorder (macrocephaly, developmental delay, ID) is stronger than for EE specifically.
- Strengths:
  - A series of 12 individuals with 9 de novo heterozygous variants (PMID: 37552066).
  - Functional validation in biochemical assays, C. elegans and zebrafish, including a dominant-negative mechanism.
  - Well-established roles in endocytic trafficking, including synaptic AMPAR trafficking (PMID: 41507541).
- Gaps and limitations:
  - The only EE-cohort case (PMID: 27652284) is a nonsense variant affecting minor transcripts, with no phenotype detail, no de novo confirmation and no pathogenic classification.
  - The epilepsy phenotype is linked only to LoF cases in the larger series, with unspecified numbers.
  - There is no true vertebrate knockout, no seizure model and no brain expression data in the evidence set.
  - There are no cohort-level or recurrent-variant data for EE.
- Conclusion: RAB5C has credible, functionally supported links to a dominant neurodevelopmental disorder, driven mainly by de novo missense variants with macrocephaly and developmental delay. Its link to epileptic encephalopathy rests on a single uncertain LoF case and on epilepsy reported in LoF carriers of the larger series. Additional EE cases with confirmed de novo status, and models showing seizure-relevant neuronal phenotypes, are needed before the gene can be considered an established EE gene.

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. Targeted sequencing of 351 candidate genes for epileptic encephalopathy in a large cohort of patients (PMID: 27652284)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. Macrocephaly and developmental delay caused by missense variants in RAB5C (PMID: 37552066)
2. Rab5c-mediated endocytic trafficking regulates hematopoietic stem and progenitor cell development via Notch and AKT signaling (PMID: 32275659)
3. Wnt11 functions in gastrulation by controlling cell cohesion through Rab5c and E-cadherin (PMID: 16198297)
4. The endosomal RIN2/Rab5C machinery prevents VEGFR2 degradation to control gene expression and tip cell identity during angiogenesis (PMID: 33983539)
5. Glypican 4 regulates planar cell polarity of endoderm cells by controlling the localization of Cadherin 2 (PMID: 34131730)
6. Prolonged visual experience accelerates developmental synaptic downscaling via epigenetic regulation and Rab5c mediated AMPA receptor trafficking (PMID: 41507541)

### Biological Evidence
1. Rab5 isoforms orchestrate a "division of labor" in the endocytic network; Rab5C modulates Rac-mediated cell motility (PMID: 24587345)
2. The small GTPase Rab5c is a key regulator of trafficking of the CD93/Multimerin-2/β1 integrin complex in endothelial cell adhesion and migration (PMID: 31138217)
3. RAB5c orchestrates LC3-associated phagocytosis to promote microbicidal function of macrophages (PMID: 42102192)
4. RAB5C Increases Endothelial Release of VWF by Regulating Vesicle Trafficking (PMID: 41537263)
5. S100P is a ferroptosis suppressor to facilitate hepatocellular carcinoma development by rewiring lipid metabolism (PMID: 39779666)
6. Novel Host Protein TBC1D16, a GTPase Activating Protein of Rab5C, Inhibits Prototype Foamy Virus Replication (PMID: 34367131)
7. RAB5C, a new mRNA binding target of HuR, regulates breast cancer cell proliferation (PMID: 36480789)
8. Rab5C enhances resistance to ionizing radiation in rectal cancer (PMID: 30968159)
