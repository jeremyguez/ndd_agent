KMT2B LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: Heterozygous (autosomal dominant) variants and epileptic encephalopathy

1. GENE SUMMARY
KMT2B (MLL2) encodes a 2715-aa lysine methyltransferase with a C-terminal SET domain. It deposits H3K4 methylation (H3K4me3, and also H3K4me1 at some enhancers) at promoters and cis-regulatory elements, notably at bivalent developmental genes (PMID: 34440566; PMID: 36028557). Heterozygous loss of function causes DYT-KMT2B, a childhood-onset generalized dystonia (PMID: 27839873). The evidence for KMT2B as an epileptic encephalopathy gene is very limited.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Number of reports: 6 independent reports/series were provided. This count is limited to the extracted clinical entries; the larger dystonia literature appears in the biological evidence (PMID: 27839873; PMID: 29653907; PMID: 36483457).
- Approximate patients: about 8 across the clinical entries (1 + 3 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1). The biological entries add more dystonia patients: 4 mutations in a German-Austrian cohort (PMID: 27839873), 4 Japanese patients (PMID: 29653907), and 12 adult-onset cases from 5 families (PMID: 36483457).
- Phenotypes:
  - Dystonia dominates. Examples include childhood-onset dystonia progressing to generalized dystonia with mild developmental delay and laryngeal dystonia in a family of 3 with variable expressivity (PMID: 35415007), a case of KMT2B-related dystonia in an 8-year-old boy (PMID: 40303543), and dystonia with anarthria/aphonia (PMID: 42817183).
  - Other presentations include complicated hereditary spastic paraparesis with cognitive impairment, associated with p.(Arg147*) (PMID: 41560358), and spastic ataxia (PMID: 41357347).
  - Epilepsy-related evidence is a single case: a de novo KMT2B variant in a female with Aicardi or suspected Aicardi syndrome (corpus callosum agenesis, chorioretinal lacunae, infantile spasms). The variant is unspecified, and it is one of five candidate genes, supported only by morpholino knockdown in zebrafish with no KMT2B-specific phenotype reported (PMID: 37628618).
  - No cases of classic epileptic encephalopathy were reported.
- Variant types: In the clinical entries, one is a defined nonsense variant (LoF) (PMID: 41560358), and the others are unspecified. The wider literature points to LoF and frameshift variants and haploinsufficiency (PMID: 27839873; PMID: 29653907), with missense variants in adult-onset disease (PMID: 36483457).
- Cohorts: No UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL cohort membership was reported.
- Strength of clinical evidence for epileptic encephalopathy: LIMITED (only 1 case, with unspecified variant and a confounded Aicardi phenotype). For dystonia, the evidence is strong but outside the scope of this question.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse (heterozygous KO, conditional KOs, full KO, knockdown) and zebrafish (morpholino).
- Key phenotypes:
  - Heterozygous KO mice are viable but smaller and show hyperlocomotion, enhanced startle and prepulse inhibition, impaired balance beam performance, a dystonic-like claw hindpaw posture, and reduced sociability. Striatal Kmt2b is reduced to about half (PMID: 42727616). No seizures were reported.
  - Full knockout is embryonic lethal (PMID: 31844322).
  - Forebrain excitatory neuron conditional KO impairs hippocampus-dependent memory (PMID: 23426673).
  - Retinal conditional KOs show a phenotype similar to Mll1 CKO, with a much more severe phenotype in the double CKO (PMID: 35223853).
  - The zebrafish morpholino study reported no KMT2B-specific phenotype (PMID: 37628618).
- Consistency with human disease: Good for the motor and dystonia phenotype and for neurodevelopmental and behavioral features. There is no epilepsy or seizure phenotype.
- Strength: MODERATE for DYT-KMT2B; LIMITED for epileptic encephalopathy.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: KMT2B forms a complex with WRAD, HCF1/2 and Menin, and deposits H3K4me3 at bivalent developmental promoters, working with PRC2 (PMID: 34440566; PMID: 36028557). It also contributes to enhancer H3K4me1 during neural differentiation of mouse ESCs (PMID: 38513661).
- Expression: It is widely expressed in adult human tissues (PMID: 34440566).
- Neuronal relevance: Kmt2b knockdown in hippocampal neurons decreases H3K4 methylation, deregulates plasticity genes and impairs memory. Kmt2b and Kmt2a regulate distinct genomic regions (PMID: 28723559).
- Human mechanism: Patient fibroblasts with LoF mutations show significantly reduced KMT2B mRNA (PMID: 27839873), and frameshift mutant transcripts show reduced expression (PMID: 29653907). Adult-onset missense cases show a distinct DNA methylation signature (PMID: 36483457).
- Other contexts: Cancer and atherosclerosis studies (e.g., PMID: 35597996; PMID: 41132030) are not relevant to epilepsy.
- Strength: MODERATE to STRONG for a chromatin and neurodevelopmental mechanism and haploinsufficiency. It is not specific to epilepsy.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level for KMT2B as an epileptic encephalopathy gene: LIMITED. For dystonia, evidence is STRONG.
- Strengths: Haploinsufficiency is well established, with consistent mouse and cellular support of a neurodevelopmental role.
- Gaps:
  - Only one de novo case with a seizure-related phenotype (Aicardi spectrum), with no variant details or functional validation (PMID: 37628618).
  - No seizure phenotype in the mouse models.
  - No cohort-level enrichment of KMT2B variants in epileptic encephalopathy cohorts.
  - Most clinical entries lack variant specifics.
- Conclusion: KMT2B is a well-validated autosomal dominant dystonia gene acting through haploinsufficiency, with neurodevelopmental features. Its association with epileptic encephalopathy rests on a single unresolved Aicardi-spectrum case and is not established. Additional cases with seizure phenotypes and defined variants are needed before it can be considered an epileptic encephalopathy gene.
