NEDD9 (epileptic encephalopathy, heterozygous pathogenic variants): literature evidence report

1. GENE SUMMARY
NEDD9 (also called HEF1 or CAS-L) encodes a non-catalytic scaffold protein of the Cas family. It sits at focal adhesions and the centrosome and coordinates integrin–FAK/SRC signaling, cell adhesion and migration, and Aurora-A kinase activation and cilia dynamics. The evidence below is drawn mainly from cancer, cell migration and renal biology. No evidence links it to epileptic encephalopathy.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent case reports or case series: 0. The clinical evidence set is empty.
- Total patients: 0.
- Phenotypes: none reported.
- Variant type: no human variants were reported, so no LoF versus missense pattern can be assessed. No de novo or heterozygous variant data are available.
- Cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organism: mouse only. Studies used full Nedd9 knockouts, a B-cell conditional knockout, knockout MEFs, and shRNA knockdown in xenografts.
- Viability: Nedd9 knockout mice are viable in all the studies that report lethality (PMID: 26683084; PMID: 35523865; PMID: 34006524; PMID: 23318423; PMID: 25139996; PMID: 27336669). No Mendelian ratio data were reported. Fertility was not reported.
- Neurological phenotype: the most relevant study found that Nedd9-null mice have extensive, age-dependent loss of dendritic spines in the dentate gyrus and CA1 (tested at 1.5, 6 and 10 months). They also have deficits in Morris water maze learning and memory. Gross hippocampal morphology and neurogenesis were unchanged (PMID: 26683084). This points to a role in synaptic or dendritic structure and cognition. Seizures, EEG abnormalities and developmental encephalopathy were not reported.
- Other phenotypes:
  - Knockout mice show no abnormal skeletal phenotype, though osteoclastogenesis in vitro is reduced (PMID: 27336669).
  - Loss of Nedd9 modifies tumor and cyst phenotypes: it enhances cystogenesis in Pkd1-/- mice (PMID: 25139996), accelerates early lung tumor growth (PMID: 34006524), and reduces mammary tumor incidence in MMTV-neu mice (PMID: 23318423).
  - Loss of Nedd9 also impairs CLL progression (PMID: 35523865).
- Consistency with human phenotype: there is no human phenotype to compare against. The cognitive and spine-loss findings are loosely compatible with a neurodevelopmental or cognitive disorder. The knockouts are homozygous or null, and heterozygous effects were not described. No epilepsy phenotype was documented.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED. One study is neurologically relevant, and the others are non-neurological.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: NEDD9 acts in FAK/SRC, PI3K-Akt and ERK signaling, Rac1 and integrin activation, and Aurora-A stabilization. Examples:
  - Aurora-A destabilization and ciliary defects (PMID: 23539442; PMID: 25139996).
  - FAK and PI3K-Akt signaling in muscle (PMID: 40641186).
  - Focal adhesion turnover in MEFs, with opposite effects on 2D and 3D migration (PMID: 22509381).
  - Hexokinase and glycolysis regulation (PMID: 35410460).
- Regulation: NEDD9 expression is controlled by EIF4A3-mediated mRNA decay (PMID: 40641186), SOX2 under hypoxia (PMID: 31462898), retinoic acid in Schwann cells (PMID: 27085739), glucocorticoid receptor signaling (PMID: 37224504), and SPAG9 (PMID: 30662804).
- Expression: the provided evidence contains no systematic brain or neurodevelopmental expression data. The only neural-lineage data are retinoic acid-induced NEDD9 in Schwann cells, where NEDD9 is needed for enhanced migration (PMID: 27085739).
- Disease association: the evidence is dominated by cancer. NEDD9 is overexpressed in 56.2% of 105 NSCLC cases and correlates with shorter survival (PMID: 23086683). It is also implicated in anaplastic thyroid cancer stemness (PMID: 35449243) and in pancreatic cancer (PMID: 28915595).
- Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED for epileptic encephalopathy. The molecular function is well characterized, but it is not linked to epilepsy, neuronal excitability or synaptic disease mechanisms beyond the spine phenotype.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT.
- Strengths:
  - NEDD9 is a well-characterized scaffold protein.
  - Knockout mice are viable and show hippocampal spine loss and cognitive impairment (PMID: 26683084).
  - Its signaling partners (FAK/SRC, Aurora-A, Rac1) are plausibly relevant to cytoskeletal and synaptic biology.
- Gaps and limitations:
  - There are no human patients or variants.
  - No seizure or epileptic phenotype has been described in any model.
  - Heterozygous (dominant) mechanisms have not been tested.
  - Brain expression data are lacking.
  - Nearly all studies concern oncology.
- Conclusion: there is currently no clinical or experimental support for NEDD9 as an autosomal dominant epileptic encephalopathy gene. The mouse hippocampal spine-loss and memory phenotype (PMID: 26683084) is the only neurologically relevant observation, and it is insufficient to support gene–disease validity. NEDD9 should be considered a candidate only, pending identification of human cases with heterozygous (likely de novo) variants and functional validation.

2. The scaffold protein NEDD9 is necessary for leukemia-cell migration and disease progression in a mouse model of chronic lymphocytic leukemia (PMID: 35523865)
3. Nedd9 Restrains Autophagy to Limit Growth of Early Stage Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (PMID: 34006524)
4. A requirement for Nedd9 in luminal progenitor cells prior to mammary tumorigenesis in MMTV-HER2/ErbB2 mice (PMID: 23318423)
5. Nedd9 restrains renal cystogenesis in Pkd1-/- mice (PMID: 25139996)
6. Identification of Nedd9 as a TGF-β-Smad2/3 Target Gene Involved in RANKL-Induced Osteoclastogenesis by Comprehensive Analysis (PMID: 27336669)
7. NEDD9 depletion destabilizes Aurora A kinase and heightens the efficacy of Aurora A inhibitors: implications for treatment of metastatic solid tumors (PMID: 23539442)
8. NEDD9 stabilizes focal adhesions, increases binding to the extra-cellular matrix and differentially effects 2D versus 3D cell migration (PMID: 22509381)
