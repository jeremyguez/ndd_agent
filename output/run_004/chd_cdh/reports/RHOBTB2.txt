RHOBTB2 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: CONGENITAL HEART DISEASE (CHD) AND CONGENITAL DIAPHRAGMATIC HERNIA (CDH)

1. GENE SUMMARY
RHOBTB2 encodes an atypical Rho GTPase with two BTB domains. It acts as a substrate adaptor for the CUL3 E3 ubiquitin ligase complex, and its own abundance is tightly controlled by CUL3-dependent proteasomal degradation (PMID: 15107402). Heterozygous de novo missense variants in the BTB domain cause a developmental and epileptic encephalopathy. This is not CHD or CDH.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
No clinical evidence entries were supplied for either condition. No case reports or case series, no patient counts, and no variant data relating RHOBTB2 to CHD or CDH were available for this review. No cohort membership (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL) can be assigned.

The only patient-level information appears indirectly in the biological studies. These describe de novo BTB-domain missense variants in individuals with epileptic encephalopathy: about 10 individuals (PMID: 29276004) and 3 patients (PMID: 29768694). The phenotype is neurological, and the summaries mention no cardiac or diaphragmatic findings. They therefore cannot be counted as CHD or CDH evidence. The variant type in these reports is predominantly missense, clustering in the BTB domain, with a gain-of-function or stabilization mechanism. This is not a loss-of-function mechanism.

Congenital heart disease: no patients reported.
Strength of clinical evidence for congenital heart disease (CHD): NONE

Congenital diaphragmatic hernia: no patients reported.
Strength of clinical evidence for congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH): NONE

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
No model organism entries were supplied. Drosophila work appears only under biological evidence. Elevated levels of the fly ortholog RhoBTB caused seizure susceptibility and severe locomotor defects, and knockdown in dendritic arborization neurons reduced dendrite number (PMID: 29276004). These findings are neurological and agree with the human encephalopathy phenotype. No cardiac or diaphragm phenotypes were described, and no mouse or zebrafish knockout data were provided (no Mendelian ratio or lethality data).
Strength of model organism evidence: NONE for CHD/CDH. The fly data are relevant only to the neurological phenotype.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathway and molecular function: RHOBTB2 binds CUL3 through its first BTB domain and is a CUL3 ligase substrate (PMID: 15107402). It is an Hsp90 client, and assembly of the DBC2-CUL3-COP9 complex depends on Hsp90 (PMID: 24608665). It targets Musashi-2 for ubiquitination (PMID: 27941885). It also modulates Hippo-YAP signaling by antagonizing LKB1 ubiquitination (PMID: 32111652) and acts through KLHL13 in AML cells (PMID: 41107424). It is a mitotic E2F1 target involved in apoptosis (PMID: 18039672).
- Expression: Breast tumors show promoter methylation and downregulation (PMID: 24356943). No data on cardiac, diaphragm, or developing-brain expression were provided.
- Key functional findings:
  - Patient BTB-domain missense mutants accumulate to higher levels than wild-type because of impaired CUL3-dependent degradation, in HEK293 cells (PMID: 29276004) and Neuro-2a cells (PMID: 29768694).
  - Hotspot mutants (R461H, R485C, R489Q) accumulate in the nucleus and mitochondria, reduce Na+/K+-ATPase through lysosomal degradation, and R489Q and W217C impair mitochondrial respiration (PMID: 41478322).
- These data support a mechanism for the neurodevelopmental disorder. None link RHOBTB2 to heart or diaphragm development.
Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for the neurological disease mechanism, but LIMITED/NONE for CHD or CDH specifically.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
Overall evidence level for congenital heart disease (CHD): INSUFFICIENT
Overall evidence level for congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH): INSUFFICIENT
Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT

The evidence does not point to a syndrome affecting CHD or CDH. The known RHOBTB2 disorder is a neurological syndrome (developmental and epileptic encephalopathy), and no multi-system association involving heart or diaphragm was identified.

Key strengths: the mechanism of the neurodevelopmental disease is well characterized. BTB-domain missense variants impair CUL3-mediated degradation, and this is supported by convergent cell and Drosophila data (PMIDs: 29276004, 29768694, 41478322).

Key gaps: there are no clinical reports of CHD or CDH, no animal model with cardiac or diaphragm phenotypes, and no expression data for those tissues. The assessed disease mechanism is gain-of-function or stabilization of the protein, so any CHD or CDH link would need independent demonstration.

Conclusion: RHOBTB2 is an established heterozygous (de novo, missense) cause of epileptic encephalopathy, but the supplied evidence provides no support for an association with CHD or CDH. Both conditions should be classified as insufficient evidence unless patient-level data emerge.
