DNM1L literature evidence report: congenital heart disease (CHD) and congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH)

1. GENE SUMMARY
DNM1L encodes dynamin-1-like protein (DRP1), a large GTPase that drives mitochondrial and peroxisomal fission. Its activity is regulated by post-translational modification (e.g., Ser616 and Ser637 phosphorylation) and by adaptors such as FIS1 and MFF. It also contributes to mitophagy and apoptosis.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
The clinical evidence array contains no entries, so no case reports or case series for CHD or CDH were retrieved. The questions about patient numbers, common phenotypes, variant type and cohort membership (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL) therefore cannot be answered for either condition.

Congenital heart disease: no patients with CHD were identified. Strength of clinical evidence for congenital heart disease: NONE

Congenital diaphragmatic hernia: no patients with CDH were identified. Strength of clinical evidence for congenital diaphragmatic hernia: NONE

The only human genetic observations are two ASHG abstracts of neurological DNM1L disease. These come from the biological evidence set, not from CHD/CDH searches. Neither reports CHD or CDH.
- Two patients carry middle-domain missense variants (p.G350R, p.A395D) and have infantile encephalopathy (ASHG 2015 - Missense mutations in the middle domain of DNM1L cause infantile encephalopathy in humans and peroxisomal and mitochondrial defects in Drosophila and humans).
- One patient carries a de novo heterozygous p.Thr59Ile GTPase-domain variant and has spastic paraparesis, cerebellar syndrome and optic atrophy (ASHG 2016 - DNM1L-related mitochondrial fission defect presenting as spastic paraparesis and optic atrophy).

These reports suggest that pathogenic DNM1L variants are predominantly missense and act in a dominant-negative or altered-function manner. The phenotype they describe is neurological, with no cardiac or diaphragmatic features.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
Mouse conditional knockouts and mouse embryonic fibroblasts (MEFs) have been studied, and Drosophila is covered under biological evidence. No data on lethality, Mendelian ratios or fertility were provided.
- Neuron-specific Drp1 loss causes oxidative damage, impaired respiration, neurodegeneration, and accumulation of LC3, p62 and ubiquitin on mitochondria (PMID: 22732519).
- Liver-specific Drp1 knockout causes megamitochondria, reduced mitophagy, liver injury and inflammation, and cGAS-STING-interferon activation (PMID: 35698731).
- Drp1 knockdown in neuronal ischemia-reperfusion models alters mitochondrial proteostasis (PMID: 40016670).
- In Pex3- or Pex5-knockout MEFs, mitochondrial fragmentation depends on Drp1 (PMID: 31076512).

None of these studies reports a cardiac or diaphragmatic phenotype. The neurodegenerative findings are consistent with the human neurological disease but are unrelated to CHD or CDH. Strength of model organism evidence for CHD/CDH: NONE (the evidence supports neurological and metabolic roles only).

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: DRP1 mediates mitochondrial fission, mitophagy and apoptosis.
  - It is regulated by ERK1/2 and RalA through Ser616 and Ser637 phosphorylation (PMID: 34767958; PMID: 38286821; PMID: 33300446).
  - Its function depends on FIS1 and MFF (PMID: 39350223).
  - Splice variants differ in localization and function (PMID: 39191946; PMID: 38241813).
- Expression: DNM1L is altered in obesity (adipose tissue), osteoarthritis (cartilage) and alcoholic hepatitis (liver; reduced via impaired TFEB induction) (PMID: 38286821; PMID: 34767958; PMID: 35698731).
- Disease variants: variants expressed in developing mouse brain cause severe neuronal loss. In a human stem-cell model, more than 75% of pathological transcriptional changes were reversible (PMID: 41786974). In flies, human wild-type DNM1L rescues drp1 mutants, whereas the G350R and A395D variants produce aggregated mitochondria with trafficking defects (ASHG 2015 - Missense mutations in the middle domain of DNM1L cause infantile encephalopathy in humans and peroxisomal and mitochondrial defects in Drosophila and humans).
- Relevance to CHD/CDH: none of the studies addresses heart or diaphragm development.

Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for DNM1L as a disease gene in general (neurological), but LIMITED to NONE for CHD or CDH specifically.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
Overall evidence level for congenital heart disease: INSUFFICIENT
Overall evidence level for congenital diaphragmatic hernia: INSUFFICIENT
Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT

Syndrome assessment: the evidence does not point to a syndrome affecting CHD and CDH. The only recognized human phenotype is neurological (encephalopathy, spastic paraparesis, optic atrophy), with no multi-system involvement including heart or diaphragm.

Strengths: DNM1L has a well-characterized role in mitochondrial fission. Its dominant, missense-driven neurological disorder is supported by fly and mouse data.

Gaps: there are no clinical reports of CHD or CDH, no cardiac or diaphragmatic phenotypes in animal models, no cohort or de novo enrichment data for these conditions, and no sample sizes or statistics.

Conclusion: DNM1L is an established gene for dominant neurodevelopmental mitochondrial fission disorders. There is currently no evidence linking heterozygous DNM1L variants to CHD or CDH. Association with either condition would require new patient-level genetic data.
