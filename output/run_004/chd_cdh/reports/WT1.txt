**WT1 REPORT: CONGENITAL HEART DISEASE (CHD) AND CONGENITAL DIAPHRAGMATIC HERNIA (CDH)**

---

**1. GENE SUMMARY**

WT1 (Wilms tumour 1, 11p13) encodes a zinc-finger transcription factor and RNA-binding protein. It is essential for development of the kidney, gonads, mesothelium and epicardium, and it regulates differentiation of podocytes and nephron progenitors. Heterozygous variants cause Denys-Drash syndrome, Frasier syndrome, isolated nephrotic syndrome and Wilms tumour predisposition. Deletions of 11p13 cause WAGR syndrome.

---

**2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY**

Overall, 8 reports were found, all case reports or small series. None came from the named cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL). The 8 reports cover roughly 41 patients, though some of these are not informative for the two conditions. Phenotypes were judged by what was actually reported, not by the disease label of the search.

**Congenital heart disease (CHD)**
- Only one report is relevant: (ASHG 2012 - Cardiac Malformations Associated With Germline WT1 Mutations in Children Presenting With Wilms' Tumour).
- It is a retrospective series of 14 children with Wilms tumour and germline WT1 mutations or 11p13 abnormalities. Five of 12 patients with adequate cardiac data had cardiac findings.
- The findings were complete transposition of the great arteries, VSD, ASD, atrial tachycardia, and a right-sided aortic arch with aberrant left subclavian artery.
- Two patients had WAGR (a contiguous deletion). One of these had normal cardiac findings and the other had insufficient cardiac data.
- Limitations: the specific variants were not given, the report is a conference abstract, and deletions are mixed with point mutations. Variant type is therefore unclear. The cardiac defects were heterogeneous, not a single recurrent lesion, and they occurred in a Wilms tumour ascertained cohort.
- No other patient in the CDH-labelled entries had a reported cardiac phenotype.
- Strength of clinical evidence for CHD: LIMITED

**Congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH)**
- Seven reports are relevant, covering about 27 patients, of whom about 9 had a diaphragmatic hernia or defect.
- The reports are:
  - A genotype-phenotype study of 21 children with WT1 glomerulopathy (PMID: 27300205). Three had a diaphragmatic defect or hernia, and the abstract does not say which genotype group they belonged to.
  - Three independent reports of the recurrent p.Arg366His (c.1097G>A) missense variant, each in a patient with Denys-Drash syndrome and a diaphragmatic defect (PMID: 7645607; PMID: 18203154; PMID: 16932893). One of these notes that Arg366His had been reported in two earlier CDH cases.
  - An IVS9 c.1228+4C>T donor splice variant in a patient with steroid-resistant FSGS, diaphragmatic hernia, proximal hypospadias and testicular ectopia, with a reduced +KTS/-KTS ratio (PMID: 10792605).
  - A Japanese infant with p.Arg439His, CDH, congenital nephrotic syndrome and death at 74 days (PMID: 41142893).
  - A fetal trio exome study that classified one WT1 variant as likely damaging in a fetus with CDH (PMID: 34315577). The variant was not specified.
- Common phenotypes: CDH together with nephropathy (nephrotic syndrome, FSGS or diffuse mesangial sclerosis), disorders of sexual development (DSD) or genital anomalies, and sometimes Wilms tumour.
- Variants are predominantly missense, notably the recurrent Arg366His, with some KTS-altering splice variants. These are consistent with the dominant-negative or altered-function mechanism of Denys-Drash and Frasier syndromes, not simple haploinsufficiency.
- Limitations: all reports are small, and several are older. Specific variants were not given in two reports (PMID: 27300205; PMID: 34315577), and segregation and de novo data are not described in the abstracts. CDH occurs in a minority of WT1 patients and almost always alongside renal or gonadal disease.
- Strength of clinical evidence for CDH: MODERATE

---

**3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY**

- Organisms studied: mouse (conditional knockouts and a +KTS-isoform-deficient line) and zebrafish (overexpression).
- Key phenotypes in mouse:
  - Forebrain-specific deletion causes impaired memory flexibility (PMID: 31434897).
  - Uterine deletion causes implantation failure and infertility (PMID: 40627402).
  - Adult inducible podocyte deletion causes podocyte apoptosis, glomerulosclerosis, albuminuria and Notch activation (PMID: 29398135).
  - Mice lacking the +KTS isoform have podocyte differentiation defects and altered MAGI2 expression (PMID: 25993318).
  - Six2-Cre deletion in nephron progenitors causes defective nephrogenesis (PMID: 41897393).
- In zebrafish, sustained wt1b expression drives cardiomyocytes toward an epicardial-like fate (PMID: 35312773). This is a gain-of-function experiment, not a loss-of-function model.
- Consistency with human disease: the renal and podocyte phenotypes match the human nephropathy, and the +KTS data match Frasier-type disease. No model in the supplied evidence directly shows a diaphragmatic or structural cardiac defect from Wt1 loss. Lethality and Mendelian ratios were not reported. One clinical report cites the mouse knockout diaphragmatic hernia as supporting causality (PMID: 7645607), but the primary mouse data are not in the supplied evidence.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED for CDH and CHD, though strong for the renal phenotype.

---

**4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY**

- Molecular function: WT1 is a transcription factor whose +KTS and -KTS splice isoforms regulate distinct target sets. ChIP-seq shows that almost half of podocyte-specific genes are direct WT1 targets (PMID: 25993318). It interacts with TET2 (PMID: 25601757), BASP1 (PMID: 21269271) and p53, and mutant WT1 can act as a gain-of-function factor in Wilms tumour cells (PMID: 24619359). It is stabilised in mitosis via USP9X (PMID: 32152317), degraded by autophagy via EPG5 in granulosa cells (PMID: 35786405), and sits downstream of Hippo/YAP in podocytes (PMID: 38423183).
- Expression: the clinical report notes expression in mesothelium (PMID: 7645607), which is relevant to diaphragm development. Mouse connective tissue expression is also noted in the GWAS report. The zebrafish work supports a role in epicardial versus myocardial identity (PMID: 35312773). Brain expression is supported only by the forebrain knockout work (PMID: 31434897).
- Genetic association: noncoding variants at the WT1 locus are associated with inguinal and other abdominal hernias (ASHG 2016 - Pleiotropic effects of noncoding variants near EFEMP1, WT1, ADAMTS6, and EBF2 on the risk of abdominal hernias). This is indirect support for a role in body-wall and connective-tissue integrity.
- Most mechanistic work concerns kidney, gonad, leukaemia and Wilms tumour, not diaphragm or heart.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE

---

**5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT**

Overall evidence level for CHD: LIMITED
Overall evidence level for CDH: MODERATE
Overall evidence level: MODERATE

**Key strengths**
- Multiple independent CDH cases, including a recurrent Arg366His variant in at least three reports.
- A plausible mechanism through WT1 expression in mesothelium.
- A well-defined WT1 syndromic context.
- Hernia GWAS signal at the locus.

**Key gaps**
- CHD rests on a single abstract with unspecified variants and mixed deletions.
- Several CDH reports lack variant details.
- There are no cohort-level data and no segregation or de novo data.
- The model organism data supplied do not show diaphragm or heart defects.
- The genotype-phenotype link for CDH is unresolved (missense and KTS-splice variants in a dominant-negative or altered-isoform context).

**Syndrome assessment**
The evidence points to a multisystem WT1-related syndrome (Denys-Drash, Frasier or WAGR spectrum) that affects the kidney, gonads, and tumour risk. CDH is an occasional but recurrent component, and CHD is a possible, less well-established one. Neither condition appears as an isolated phenotype.

**Conclusion**
WT1 is moderately supported as a gene for CDH, driven by recurrent heterozygous missense and splice variants in syndromic patients. For isolated CHD the evidence is limited. WT1 testing is most justified when CDH or a heart defect coexists with nephropathy, DSD or Wilms tumour. Larger cohort data with defined variants would be needed to upgrade either assessment.
