NALCN (NALCN) literature evidence report: congenital heart disease (CHD) and congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH), heterozygous (autosomal dominant) variants

1. GENE SUMMARY
NALCN encodes the pore-forming subunit of the sodium leak channel (Na+-leak channel, non-selective). It forms a complex with FAM155A, UNC79 and UNC80 and carries the background Na+ leak current that sets neuronal resting membrane potential and excitability (PMID: 17448995; PMID: 34929720). The established human disorders are autosomal dominant CLIFAHDD (congenital contractures of the limbs and face, hypotonia, developmental delay) and autosomal recessive IHPRF1 (infantile hypotonia with psychomotor retardation and characteristic facies). Neither involves CHD or CDH as a defining feature.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

Overall: 2 reports were found, with about 3 patients in total (2 fetuses in one report, 1 in another). All variants were heterozygous missense. None of these patients came from UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL. The prenatal exome study (PMID: 30712880) was the PAGE cohort in England and Scotland, which is related to the DDD/prenatal exome effort but is not one of the listed cohorts.

Congenital heart disease (CHD):
- Both entries were retrieved by the CHD search, but no patient has a reported cardiac phenotype.
- PMID: 36478354 describes 2 perinatally lethal fetuses with urorectal septum malformation sequence and multiple congenital anomalies. They carry p.Gln1589Leu and p.Gly19Cys. Both variants were classified as VUS (PM2, in silico predictions only). Genome sequencing was nondiagnostic and causality was not established.
- PMID: 30712880 (PAGE) describes 1 fetus (PP3387) with multisystem structural anomalies and a presumed de novo heterozygous NALCN missense variant. The variant was listed as diagnostic by a clinical review panel, but the specific change and the cardiac involvement are not given.
- No cardiac-specific phenotype is documented, so these cases do not support a NALCN-CHD association.

Strength of clinical evidence for congenital heart disease (CHD): NONE

Congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH):
- No reported patient has diaphragmatic hernia, and no entry was retrieved for CDH.
- The multisystem fetal anomalies are not specified as diaphragmatic.

Strength of clinical evidence for congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH): NONE

The only broader human context is the known CLIFAHDD (dominant, clustered in S5/S6 pore segments; 14 de novo missense variants in 14 families; PMID: 25683120) and recessive IHPRF1 phenotypes. These are neurodevelopmental and contracture disorders and do not include CHD or CDH.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Mouse is the only organism studied.
- Full Nalcn knockout causes severely disrupted respiratory rhythm and neonatal death within 24 hours (PMID: 17448995). This is a perinatal lethal phenotype and is consistent with severe human NALCN-related disease. Unc79/Unc80 knockouts are also neonatal lethal (PMID: 32620897).
- Heterozygous knockout mice are viable but hypernatremic (PMID: 21177381).
- Conditional knockouts show altered excitability in dopaminergic neurons (PMID: 30556810; PMID: 37640554), spinal projection neurons (PMID: 29746349), auditory cartwheel neurons (PMID: 38197879) and SCN neurons (PMID: 37339878). Smooth-muscle knockout causes abnormal labor (PMID: 30021195).
- No cardiac malformation or diaphragmatic defect is reported in any model. The respiratory rhythm failure is a neural phenotype and not a diaphragm structural defect.

Strength of model organism evidence (for CHD/CDH): LIMITED. The evidence is strong for neurological and respiratory-rhythm phenotypes but absent for heart or diaphragm development.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- The cryo-EM structure of the NALCN-FAM155A-UNC79-UNC80 channelosome, with calmodulin bound, has been solved (PMID: 34929720; PMID: 35387979). Disrupting the UNC79-UNC80 interface reduces currents.
- The channel is regulated by extracellular Ca2+ through CaSR/PKC and PRMT7 methylation (PMID: 31601786). Its expression is regulated by progesterone and estrogen in myometrium (PMID: 31935111).
- Functional work on CLIFAHDD and IHPRF variants shows gain-of-function (L509S, Y578S) and loss-of-function (W1287L) effects (PMID: 31409833). Dominant-negative effects have also been suggested (PMID: 25683120).
- Further data cover pacemaking in nigral dopaminergic neurons (PMID: 34409942), a C. elegans nca-2 link to unc-130 (PMID: 35115609), and a role in cancer metastasis (PMID: 36175792).
- The evidence is mainly about neuronal and smooth-muscle excitability. None of it addresses cardiac or diaphragm development, although the channel is broadly expressed.

Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for general channel function, but not specific to CHD or CDH.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
Overall evidence level for congenital heart disease (CHD): INSUFFICIENT
Overall evidence level for congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH): INSUFFICIENT
Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT

Key strengths:
- NALCN is a well-characterized channel with an established dominant neurodevelopmental disorder (CLIFAHDD).
- A perinatal lethal knockout mouse exists.
- There is detailed structural and functional characterization.

Key gaps:
- Only 3 fetal cases, all with missense variants. Two are VUS with nondiagnostic genome sequencing, and the third has an unspecified variant.
- No documented cardiac or diaphragmatic phenotype in any patient or model.
- No segregation or functional data for the fetal variants.

Conclusion: There is no meaningful evidence that heterozygous NALCN variants cause isolated CHD or CDH. The evidence does not point to a syndrome combining these conditions. The sparse fetal reports describe multisystem anomalies (urorectal septum malformation, nonspecific structural anomalies) that may reflect severe NALCN-related disease or unrelated causes. The established NALCN phenotypes are neurodevelopmental and contracture disorders (CLIFAHDD and IHPRF).
