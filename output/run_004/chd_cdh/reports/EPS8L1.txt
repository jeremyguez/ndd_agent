EPS8L1 literature evidence report: congenital heart disease (CHD) and congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH)

1. GENE SUMMARY
EPS8L1 (EPS8-like 1) encodes a member of the EPS8 family of actin-regulatory, SH3-domain-containing adaptor proteins. It links receptor tyrosine kinase signaling to actin remodeling through Abi1 and Sos-1 and the Ras/Rac pathway (PMID: 14565974). It is also reported as a component of the nexin-dynein regulatory complex (Drc3) in sperm flagella (PMID: 38650655).

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- No knockout or model organism studies of EPS8L1 were captured.
- Eps8l1 appears only incidentally in mouse work. It is differentially expressed in the hypothalamus of Asmt knockout mice (PMID: 35771226). In Tcte1-/- male mice, Eps8l1 (Drc3) and other N-DRC components fail to reach the sperm flagella (PMID: 38650655). Neither study is an Eps8l1 loss-of-function model.
- Eps8l1 protein accumulates in the regenerating lizard epidermis (PMID: 36854226), but this is an expression finding only.
- No cardiac or diaphragm phenotypes, lethality data, or Mendelian ratio data are available, so consistency with human CHD or CDH cannot be judged.
Strength of model organism evidence: NONE

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Molecular function and pathways:
  - Eps8L1 interacts with Abi1 and Sos-1, activates the Rac-GEF activity of Sos-1, binds actin, and localizes to PDGF-induced F-actin-rich ruffles. It can rescue RTK-mediated actin remodeling in eps8-/- fibroblasts, indicating redundancy with Eps8 (PMID: 14565974).
  - Its SH3 domain recognizes a PxxDY motif in CD3epsilon rather than the canonical PxxP (PMID: 18644376). In Jurkat T cells, this binding is induced by TCR ligation and lost on Y166 phosphorylation (PMID: 17617578).
- Expression:
  - EPS8L1 is co-expressed with filaggrin and loricrin in differentiated keratinocytes. The protein is found in the epidermal granular layer and hair follicle inner root sheath, suggesting a skin barrier role (PMID: 37889356).
  - It is expressed in progenitor cytotrophoblasts and syncytiotrophoblasts and is overexpressed in preeclampsia. An upstream MLT1 ERV element acts as its enhancer. Knockout is lethal in trophoblasts in vitro, and overexpression alters trophoblast behavior (PMID: 41188995).
  - It is a feature gene separating high-risk from non-high-risk neuroblastoma (PMID: 35911385).
  - It is a candidate marker of sperm motility in boar seminal extracellular vesicles (PMID: 39067049).
- None of the findings concern the heart, diaphragm, or developmental pathways relevant to CHD or CDH, and none concern neurodevelopment.
Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED. The protein has a plausible role in actin and Ras/Rac signaling, but there is no tissue-specific link to cardiac or diaphragm development.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
Overall evidence level for congenital heart disease (CHD): INSUFFICIENT
Overall evidence level for congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH): INSUFFICIENT
Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT

- Key strengths: the molecular function of EPS8L1 in actin remodeling and Ras/Rac signaling is well characterized, and the SH3 domain structure is resolved (PMID: 14565974, PMID: 18644376).
- Key gaps: there are no human cases with heterozygous variants, no segregation or de novo data, no animal model showing a cardiac or diaphragm phenotype, and no expression data in the developing heart or diaphragm. The evidence does not point to a syndrome affecting CHD, CDH, or both.
- Conclusion: EPS8L1 has no clinical or model organism support as a CHD or CDH gene with heterozygous pathogenic variants. The available literature is limited to general cell biology and incidental expression findings in skin, placenta, sperm, and other tissues. Any association with CHD or CDH is unsupported at present and would require case-level genetic data and functional validation.
