PCBP2 (poly(C)-binding protein 2, hnRNP E2) EVIDENCE REPORT: CHD and CDH

1. GENE SUMMARY
PCBP2 encodes a multifunctional RNA-binding protein that binds C-rich/CU-rich motifs. It regulates mRNA splicing, stability and translation, and it also acts as a cytosolic iron chaperone. No human disease association with heterozygous variants has been established in the evidence provided.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organism: mouse only. The studies used full knockout, conditional knockout and knockdown approaches. Mendelian ratios, lethality, infertility and sample sizes were not reported in the extracted data.
- Full knockout, hematopoiesis: Pcbp2-/- embryos show selective deficiency of the definitive erythroid lineage. Runx1 exon 6 is aberrantly excluded, and Runx1ΔE6 homozygous embryos phenocopy this loss (PMID: 29866654).
- Full knockout, oxidative stress: PCBP2-deficient mice and MEFs show decreased p73, increased ROS and accelerated senescence (PMID: 26907686).
- Conditional knockout, pancreatic β cells: loss impairs insulin secretion, calcium flux and granule exocytosis (PMID: 38950317). Pcbp1/2 co-deletion causes severe diabetes with reduced β cell viability and reduced translation (PMID: 40451383).
- Conditional knockout, liver: Alb-Cre Pcbp1/Pcbp2 double deletion causes chronic severe liver injury, and adult AAV8-iCre double deletion causes acute ferroptosis-like injury. Single PCBP2 deletion produced mild or no liver abnormality, which suggests redundancy with PCBP1 (PMID: 42379276).
- Knockdown models relate to heat stroke neuroferroptosis (PMID: 39731111), pancreatic cancer stroma (PMID: 33500719) and gastric cancer xenografts (PMID: 39586843).
- Consistency with human phenotype: no cardiac or diaphragmatic phenotype was reported in any model. The findings do not support or contradict a CHD or CDH role, and they are not tied to heterozygous loss-of-function effects.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED, for a general developmental role (hematopoietic and metabolic), and none specific to CHD or CDH.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and molecular function: PCBP2 binds CU-rich motifs on mRNAs and noncoding RNAs (PMID: 25411354). It promotes Dicer-mediated miRNA processing in an iron-dependent manner (PMID: 22633452). It also acts as an iron chaperone, for example delivering iron to GSDMD (PMID: 42361797), and it influences ferroptosis (PMID: 35089637).
- mRNA stabilization: PCBP2 stabilizes SLC7A11 mRNA (PMID: 36147463) and PARP1 mRNA (PMID: 40773674).
- Expression and disease context: PCBP2 is glucose-induced in islets and reduced in islets from T2D donors (PMID: 38950317). It is elevated in Alzheimer's disease brain (PMID: 41298370). Its levels are altered in cancers (PMID: 33037085). The CDKN2A/B atherosclerosis SNP rs1333046 recruits PCBP2 to regulate p16INK4a and senescence (PMID: 41216990).
- Relevance to CHD or CDH: none of this evidence is specific to cardiac or diaphragm development. The Runx1 splicing role in definitive hematopoiesis is the only developmental link.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for general function, and none for the two target conditions.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
Overall evidence level for congenital heart disease (CHD): INSUFFICIENT
Overall evidence level for congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH): INSUFFICIENT
Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT

Syndrome: with no patients, the evidence does not point to a syndrome affecting CHD, CDH or both.

Key strengths: PCBP2 is well characterized biochemically, and knockout mice show developmental (erythroid) and metabolic phenotypes.

Key gaps: there are no human cases, no variant data and no segregation or de novo evidence. No model shows a heart or diaphragm defect, and the models do not address heterozygous effects.

Conclusion: PCBP2 currently has no clinical or phenotype-matched experimental support as a heterozygous-variant gene for CHD or CDH. Its roles in RNA processing and iron homeostasis make it a plausible but unsupported candidate. Human genetic evidence, such as de novo variants in CHD or CDH cohorts, together with cardiac and diaphragm phenotyping of Pcbp2 mutant mice, would be needed to evaluate it.
