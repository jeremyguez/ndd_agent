CHAMP1 (chromosome alignment-maintaining phosphoprotein 1) is a C2H2 zinc-finger protein on 13q34. It is needed for kinetochore-microtubule attachment and chromosome alignment, and it works with POGZ and HP1α in heterochromatin assembly and homologous recombination (HR) DNA repair. Heterozygous loss-of-function variants cause the neurodevelopmental disorder MRD40 (MIM 616579).

1. GENE SUMMARY
See above. CHAMP1 is a chromatin- and kinetochore-associated zinc-finger protein. Its best-established disease link is neurodevelopmental (MRD40), not a structural birth defect.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

Overview: Four reports were found, each with one patient, so about 4 patients in total. None is from a UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL cohort. One is from the Pediatric Cardiac Genomics Consortium (PCGC) and one from a DECIPHER-based analysis.

Congenital heart disease (CHD):
- Three single-patient reports were found.
- One patient has non-syndromic tetralogy of Fallot with a de novo stop-gained variant, p.Gln230* (chr13:114324530 C>T), from trio exome sequencing in PCGC (PMID: 37840956). This is the only CHD case with a defined LoF variant and confirmed de novo status. It is listed among 120 likely deleterious variants, with no functional or segregation data.
- One NICU infant with CHD had an unspecified CHAMP1 single-nucleotide variant (PMID: 38499751). No variant detail or phenotype detail is available, so this report is of little use for interpretation.
- One patient has a terminal 13q34 deletion (~1.765 Mb, 33 genes including CHAMP1) with a 23.2 Mb 5q33.3-qter duplication from an unbalanced paternal t(5;13) (ASHG 2016 - Partial trisomy 5q and partial monosomy 13q result in developmental delay with characteristic facial features). The features were developmental delay, dysmorphism and a small patent foramen ovale. The phenotype is attributed mainly to the 5q trisomy, and the CHAMP1 contribution cannot be separated.
- Predominant variant type: LoF (one nonsense variant), with one deletion and one unspecified variant.
- The heart phenotypes are heterogeneous: tetralogy of Fallot, an unspecified CHD and a trivial PFO.
- Not a recurrent or consistent cardiac phenotype, and there is no segregation or functional evidence in the cases.

Strength of clinical evidence for congenital heart disease: LIMITED

Congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH):
- One patient (Subject 2) has CDH plus additional birth defects and a frameshift variant, c.1741delG, p.(Glu581Asnfs*4) (NM_001164144.3), classified as pathogenic (PMID: 35904974). The patient comes from DECIPHER.
- Inheritance is not stated, and the other birth defects and any neurodevelopmental features are not detailed in the excerpt. The patient is labelled with MRD40, so CDH may be a coincidental or rare associated feature in a known neurodevelopmental syndrome.
- This is a single case with no replication.

Strength of clinical evidence for congenital diaphragmatic hernia: LIMITED

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Only mouse models were reported.
- The Champ1 knockout shows perinatal lethality on a pure C57BL/6J background, with homozygous knockouts slightly smaller than wild type. There were no gross brain anatomical defects, but cortical mitoses were increased and neuronal differentiation was delayed. Heterozygotes show mild memory defects, altered social behaviour and depression-like behaviour (PMID: 36106092).
- A 35 bp exon 2 deletion model shows delayed motor development and impaired learning and memory in heterozygotes (ASHG 2023 - Characterization of a novel mouse model with 35 bp deletion in Champ1 gene and exploration of AAV gene therapy to correct phenotypic consequences arising from CHAMP1 mutations). Females showed dystocia (ASHG 2022 - Characterization of novel mouse model with 35 bp deletion in Champ1 gene and exploration of AAV gene therapy to correct syndromes arising from mutations in the CHAMP1).
- The neurological findings are consistent with the human neurodevelopmental syndrome. No cardiac or diaphragmatic phenotype was reported in any model, though the perinatal lethality was not explained and heart and diaphragm were not described as examined. Mendelian ratios were not reported.

Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED (for CHD and CDH specifically; the neurodevelopmental evidence is moderate)

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- CHAMP1 binds REV7/MAD2L2 through its WK motif (PMID: 28887307; PMID: 36044844). It acts with POGZ and HP1α to promote HR, DNA end resection and heterochromatin assembly (PMID: 35393543; PMID: 39962076). It also protects stalled replication forks (PMID: 41481470).
- Patient cells carrying CHAMP1 premature-termination codon variants express truncated proteins and have an HR defect. Heterozygous depletion also causes an HR defect, supporting haploinsufficiency (PMID: 39738383).
- CHAMP1 acts as a MyoD cofactor that activates Myomaker and is needed for human myoblast fusion; patient cells show fusion defects (PMID: 41540007). This is relevant to muscle development, and potentially to the diaphragm, but this was not tested directly.
- CHAMP1 maintains Mcl-1 and cell survival (PMID: 34107118).
- Patient iPSC-derived neurons show electrophysiological and transcriptomic abnormalities (PMID: 42395457).
- Expression data in the heart or diaphragm were not provided; the neuronal and muscle data support a neurodevelopmental mechanism. The biology supports pathogenic LoF/haploinsufficiency in general but gives no specific link to cardiac or diaphragm development.

Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE (general function), LIMITED for CHD/CDH relevance

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
Overall evidence level for congenital heart disease: LIMITED
Overall evidence level for congenital diaphragmatic hernia: LIMITED
Overall evidence level: LIMITED

Strengths:
- A de novo nonsense variant in tetralogy of Fallot.
- A pathogenic frameshift in a CDH+ patient.
- Well-established haploinsufficiency mechanism for CHAMP1 LoF variants.
- Mouse and cell data support a neurodevelopmental role.

Gaps:
- Only about 4 single-patient reports.
- Phenotyping is incomplete, and neurodevelopmental status is unreported in the CHD cases.
- The deletion case is confounded by 5q trisomy.
- One report has no variant detail.
- No cohort-level enrichment or statistical evidence.
- No cardiac or diaphragm phenotype in mouse models.
- No functional studies in heart or diaphragm tissue.

Syndrome question: The evidence does not point to a distinct syndrome joining CHD and CDH. CHAMP1 is a neurodevelopmental (MRD40) gene, and the single CHD and CDH cases are better read as occasional or coincidental extra-neurological features, though a rare expansion of the phenotype cannot be excluded.

Conclusion: CHAMP1 has limited evidence as a CHD gene and as a CDH gene with heterozygous LoF variants. The isolated, poorly characterized cases are compatible with, but do not establish, a causal role, and the LoF mechanism is credible. More cases with detailed phenotyping, cohort enrichment analyses and cardiac or diaphragm assessment in models are needed.
