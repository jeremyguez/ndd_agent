SEPTIN2 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: CONGENITAL HEART DISEASE (CHD) AND CONGENITAL DIAPHRAGMATIC HERNIA (CDH)

1. GENE SUMMARY
SEPTIN2 (SEPT2) encodes a GTP-binding cytoskeletal septin. It forms hetero-oligomeric filaments (e.g., the septin2-6-7 complex) that associate with actin and membranes. It contributes to cytoskeletal organization, cell migration, cell division and ciliogenesis.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
No clinical case reports or case series were identified for either condition.
- Independent case reports/series: 0
- Approximate number of patients: 0
- Phenotypes: none reported
- Variant type: no variants reported, so no LoF versus missense pattern can be assessed
- Known cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none identified

Congenital heart disease: no patients with heterozygous SEPTIN2 variants and CHD were reported.
Strength of clinical evidence for congenital heart disease (CHD): NONE

Congenital diaphragmatic hernia: no patients with heterozygous SEPTIN2 variants and CDH were reported.
Strength of clinical evidence for congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH): NONE

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organism: mouse only.
- Endocardial lineage-specific Septin2 conditional knockout mice developed normally and were viable, with no detectable cardiac or valve abnormalities (PMID: 42657464). In the same study, Septin7 conditional knockout caused juvenile lethality from aortic stenosis and myxomatous valve degeneration. The septin family is therefore relevant to the heart, but Septin2 loss in the endocardial lineage was tolerated. This does not support a Septin2 CHD phenotype, although the knockout was lineage-restricted and may be compensated by other septins.
- Sept2 knockdown in mice impaired spermatogonial proliferation (G1-S arrest) and phenocopied hypoxia-induced spermatogenesis defects and reduced fertility (PMID: 40487848). The sample size was small (n = 3-5 per group).
- No diaphragm phenotype has been reported, and no germline heterozygous knockout data were provided.
- Consistency with the human phenotype: none for CHD or CDH. The only observed phenotype in the model organism evidence is male reproductive.
Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED (and negative for the cardiac phenotype)

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Ciliogenesis and neural tube closure: folate-dependent methylation of septin2 affects GTP binding and septin2-6-7 complex formation, and neural tube defect embryos showed inadequate septin2 methylation (PMID: 28432198). Cilia are relevant to heart development, but no direct link to CHD was shown.
- Vascular and angiogenic function: Septin2, Septin6 and Septin7 are required for endothelial podosome maturation and invasion in angiogenesis (PMID: 31865373).
- Vascular disease: S-nitrosylation of Septin2 at Cys111 impairs its interaction with TIAM1. This activates the TIAM1-RAC1 axis in macrophages and exacerbates aortic aneurysm and dissection in mice and patient tissue (PMID: 38357802). This is an acquired, not congenital, mechanism.
- Inflammation and ER stress: SEPT2 regulates acetylation and ubiquitination of HSPA5 and limits proinflammatory macrophage polarization (PMID: 37978190).
- Cytoskeleton and signaling: Septin2 stabilizes actin bundles (PMID: 15541171) and regulates Rac signaling through ARHGAP25 (PMID: 40205813). It is required for chromosome congression in mouse oocytes (PMID: 20372094), and its stabilization by LH1 promotes cancer cell migration (PMID: 36721170).
- Expression: the provided evidence includes no specific data on cardiac or diaphragm expression.
- Overall, septin2 has well-characterized cytoskeletal, ciliary and endothelial roles that are plausibly relevant to cardiovascular development. None of these studies tests a link to CHD or CDH.
Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
Overall evidence level for congenital heart disease (CHD): INSUFFICIENT
Overall evidence level for congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH): INSUFFICIENT
Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT

Syndrome: the evidence does not point to a syndrome affecting several of these conditions. No patients were reported.

Key strengths:
- Septin2 has well-defined roles in cytoskeletal, endothelial and ciliary biology.
- A related septin (Septin7) is clearly implicated in murine valve disease (PMID: 42657464).

Key gaps and limitations:
- There are no human genetic data (no patients, variants, de novo or segregation data) for either condition.
- The only cardiac-relevant model organism result for Septin2 was negative (PMID: 42657464).
- There are no diaphragm data.
- The available biological studies are mostly mechanistic cell-culture or disease-context studies rather than developmental ones.

Conclusion: there is currently no clinical evidence supporting SEPTIN2 as a gene for heterozygous CHD or CDH. The endocardial Septin2 conditional knockout showed no cardiac phenotype. Any association remains speculative until patient-level variant data and relevant functional studies are available.
