FGF12 EVIDENCE REPORT: CONGENITAL HEART DISEASE (CHD) AND CONGENITAL DIAPHRAGMATIC HERNIA (CDH)

1. GENE SUMMARY
FGF12 encodes fibroblast growth factor homologous factor 1 (FHF1). It is an intracellular, non-secreted FGF-family protein that binds the C-terminal tail of voltage-gated sodium channels (NaV1.5 in heart, NaV1.6 in brain) and modulates their inactivation. FGF12 is the major FHF expressed in human ventricle (PMID: 24096171). The best-established disease links are heterozygous gain-of-function missense variants and duplications in developmental and epileptic encephalopathy (DEE), not structural heart disease or CDH.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

Overview: Four entries were found, all tagged to the CHD search. About 8 patients are counted in total (1 + 5 + 1 + 1). None involves CDH. No patients come from UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL. One entry is a CHOP exome cohort (ASHG 2017).

Congenital heart disease:
- None of the reports describes a structural congenital heart defect with a convincing FGF12 variant. The one structural phenotype is Ebstein anomaly.
  - A population-based Ebstein anomaly CNV study of 60 cases noted one CNV overlapping or near FGF12. The gene was prioritized on gene content alone, and no variant details were given (PMID: 29216221).
- The other reports concern primary arrhythmia, not structural CHD.
  - Q7R-FGF12 was found in 1 of 102 genotype-negative Brugada syndrome individuals. It reduced NaV1.5 C-terminus binding, Na+ current density and availability (PMID: 24096171).
  - p.P211Q, a de novo missense variant, was reported in idiopathic (right ventricular outflow tract) ventricular tachycardia. It reduced FGF12 expression on western blot. It was one of 5 rare variants in 320 VT patients, and only P211Q was shown to be functional (PMID: 28775062).
- A likely pathogenic FGF12 duplication was reported in a CHOP exome-sequencing CNV validation cohort of 160 probands. The phenotype was unspecified and probably neurodevelopmental (ASHG 2017 - Copy number reanalysis: The hidden contribution of MED13L to intellectual disability). It has no cardiac relevance.
- Variants are mainly missense (2) and CNVs (2). Loss of function is only weakly represented.
- Cohort and inheritance data are sparse. There is no segregation evidence apart from one de novo variant.

Strength of clinical evidence for congenital heart disease: LIMITED. This reflects arrhythmia reports and one unresolved Ebstein anomaly CNV, not structural CHD with a robust variant.

Congenital diaphragmatic hernia:
- No patients with CDH or any diaphragm phenotype were reported.

Strength of clinical evidence for congenital diaphragmatic hernia: NONE

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
Only mouse models are described.
- Full Fgf12 knockout mice have an increased auditory brainstem response threshold and impaired rotarod and balance beam performance, with normal inner ear morphology (PMID: 30065296). The phenotype is neurosensory and vestibular, not cardiac or diaphragmatic.
- Myeloid-specific conditional knockouts are viable and are protected from liver fibrosis (PMID: 35753047) and from pressure-overload cardiac fibrosis (PMID: 40188526).
- The FGF12 p.V52H knock-in mouse shows seizure susceptibility, hippocampal hyperexcitability, and cognitive and social deficits (PMID: 39142611). The Fhf1 R52H/+ knock-in has seizures and SUDEP with bradycardia and shifted cardiac sodium channel inactivation (PMID: 33982289). These are consistent with the human DEE gain-of-function phenotype.
- A colorectal cancer xenograft knockdown is unrelated (PMID: 35860801).
- No model shows structural heart defects, embryonic lethality or diaphragm defects. Mendelian ratios are not reported.

Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED for CHD and NONE for CDH. The models are informative for neurological disease and cardiac electrophysiology but not for congenital structural defects.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Molecular function: FGF12 modulates NaV channel inactivation. The Q7R mutant reduced binding to NaV1.5 and lowered Na+ current density and action potential amplitude without affecting Ca2+ channels (PMID: 24096171).
- Other interactions and pathways:
  - Nucleolar NOLC1/TCOF1 ribosome biogenesis complex (PMID: 36411431).
  - MDM2 stabilization and p53 suppression in keratinocytes (PMID: 39234815).
  - FGFR1/AMPK/NRF2 signaling in doxorubicin cardiotoxicity (PMID: 38349269).
  - CaM/CaMKII/CREB1 signaling in cardiac hypertrophy, with reduced FGF12 in HCM myocardium (PMID: 41979475).
- Disease associations in other conditions:
  - Biallelic loss-of-function structural variants and a homozygous SNV in DEE, in contrast to the heterozygous gain-of-function variants (PMID: 37286232).
  - A Kashin-Beck disease association (PMID: 26290467).
- Expression is notable in ventricular myocardium, brain, spiral and vestibular ganglia, and hair follicle outer root sheath.
- Evidence directly bearing on cardiac morphogenesis or diaphragm development is absent. The links are to cardiac electrophysiology, hypertrophy and fibrosis.

Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for general cardiac relevance (electrophysiology and remodeling). It is NONE for CDH.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
Overall evidence level for congenital heart disease: LIMITED
Overall evidence level for congenital diaphragmatic hernia: INSUFFICIENT
Overall evidence level: LIMITED

Syndrome across conditions: The evidence does not point to a syndrome affecting both CHD and CDH. FGF12 disease is mainly a neurological (DEE) and cardiac-arrhythmia phenotype, and no patient has both heart and diaphragm anomalies.

Key strengths:
- Functional data link FGF12 to NaV1.5 and cardiac electrophysiology (PMID: 24096171).
- A de novo functional variant is reported in idiopathic VT (PMID: 28775062).
- Mouse knock-ins support pathogenicity of gain-of-function variants (PMID: 33982289).

Key gaps:
- No convincing structural CHD cases and no CDH cases.
- The Ebstein anomaly CNV is poorly characterized (PMID: 29216221).
- The arrhythmia cases are single variants in large cohorts with limited segregation data.
- Models show no structural cardiac or diaphragm phenotypes.

Conclusion: FGF12 is a validated gene for DEE and plausibly modulates cardiac electrical function. Evidence for heterozygous pathogenic variants causing structural CHD is limited to an uncharacterized Ebstein anomaly CNV, and there is no evidence for CDH. Further cases with structural defects and functional validation are needed before FGF12 can be considered a CHD or CDH gene.
