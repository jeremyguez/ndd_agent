SOX10 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: CONGENITAL HEART DISEASE (CHD) AND CONGENITAL DIAPHRAGMATIC HERNIA (CDH)

1. GENE SUMMARY
SOX10 encodes a SOX-family transcription factor that is essential for the development and maintenance of neural crest derivatives (melanocytes, enteric nervous system, Schwann cells, olfactory ensheathing cells), the inner ear, and oligodendrocytes (PMID: 34667088). Heterozygous variants cause Waardenburg syndrome types 2 and 4, PCWH, intestinal pseudo-obstruction, Kallmann syndrome and isolated hearing loss (PMID: 34667088). CHD and CDH are not established features of these disorders.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

Overview: Three clinical reports were retrieved, all under the CHD search. Together they cover about 2 definite patients plus an unspecified number in a cohort study. No reports were retrieved for CDH. No patients were identified as belonging to UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL. Variant types were mixed or unspecified: one whole-gene contiguous deletion and two variants with no stated notation.

Congenital heart disease:
- A 1.99 Mb de novo-type heterozygous deletion at 22q12.3-q13.1 (chr22:36656004-38643920, hg19) removed SOX10 and 13 other RefSeq genes. The patient had atypical Waardenburg syndrome, severe congenital heart disease, strabismus and iris pigment disturbance/heterochromia (PMID: 32703023). This is the only report with a cardiac phenotype. The deletion is multigenic, so the CHD cannot be attributed to SOX10 haploinsufficiency specifically.
- A trio exome study reported a pathogenic/likely pathogenic SOX10 variant in a CHD-labelled cohort. Grandparental genotyping showed it was de novo in the unaffected parent, consistent with parental mosaicism or a de novo event (PMID: 32027463). The abstract gives no phenotype, variant notation or evidence that the proband had CHD. It therefore cannot be counted as clear CHD evidence for SOX10.
- A Kallmann syndrome / normosmic idiopathic hypogonadotropic hypogonadism cohort from South Korea found a SOX10 defect in at least one patient who had deafness or CHARGE-like features (PMID: 25883924). The phenotype was hypogonadism, hearing loss and olfactory bulb abnormalities. No cardiac phenotype was reported, so this does not support CHD.

Strength of clinical evidence for congenital heart disease: LIMITED (a single multigenic deletion with CHD, and no confirmed SOX10-specific intragenic variant with CHD).

Congenital diaphragmatic hernia:
- No patients with CDH or any diaphragmatic defect were reported in the retrieved literature.

Strength of clinical evidence for congenital diaphragmatic hernia: NONE

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
Organisms studied are mouse and zebrafish.
- Sox10Dom/+ mice develop enteric aganglionosis with variable penetrance, and the severity is modified by EdnrB alleles (PMID: 15294878).
- Mice carrying a BAC transgene with the PCWH mutation (c.1400del12) show hypomyelination in the PNS and CNS, deafness, hypopigmentation, loss of the enteric nervous system and circling behavior. Severity increases with transgene copy number (ASHG 2014 - Additive toxicity of SOX10 mutation underlies a complex neurological phenotype of PCWH; ASHG 2010 - Sox10 BAC transgenic mouse modeling a complex neurocristopathy, PCWH).
- In zebrafish, sox10 perdurance antagonizes the ectomesenchyme transition in nr2f mutants (PMID: 36367707).
- A subset of adult sox10+ zebrafish cardiomyocytes contributes to regeneration after cryoinjury, and their ablation impairs it (PMID: 31644901). This shows sox10 expression in the adult heart but not a developmental CHD phenotype.
- Lethality, Mendelian ratios and sample sizes were not reported. No model showed congenital structural heart defects or diaphragmatic hernia.
- The models are consistent with the human neurocristopathy spectrum (Waardenburg-Hirschsprung, PCWH) but not with CHD or CDH.

Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED for CHD (only an indirect cardiomyocyte regeneration finding) and NONE for CDH.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- SOX10 is a neural crest transcription factor. It sits in a gene regulatory network with RET, ELP1 and EDNRB in the enteric nervous system. SOX10 expression fell by about 80% in RET heterozygous knockout SK-N-SH cells and by about 15% in ELP1 knockout cells (ASHG 2023 - Genetic epistasis between RET and ELP1 explains gastrointestinal motility dysfunction in Familial Dysautonomia patients).
- SOX10 drives alternative promoters, including those of MTMR2 and SOX6, in Schwann cells (ASHG 2016 - Investigating the role of SOX10-regulated alternative promoter use in Schwann cells; ASHG 2016 - Investigating the role of SOX6 during the development of the peripheral nervous system; ASHG 2015 - SOX10 Regulates an Alternative Promoter at the Charcot-Marie-Tooth Disease Locus MTMR2).
- SOX10 contributes to neural crest induction when combined with other factors, and to oligodendrocyte generation from iPSCs (PMID: 33040687; PMID: 28246330).
- SOX10 regulates CMTM7 and PD-L1 in cancer contexts (PMID: 30392914; PMID: 40514069), which is not relevant to CHD or CDH.
- Cardiac neural crest contributes to the outflow tract, which gives a plausible but unproven link to CHD. No functional data directly connect SOX10 to cardiac or diaphragm development.

Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for neural crest biology in general, and LIMITED for relevance to CHD and CDH.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

Overall evidence level for congenital heart disease: LIMITED
Overall evidence level for congenital diaphragmatic hernia: INSUFFICIENT
Overall evidence level: LIMITED

Key strengths: SOX10 is a well-established neurocristopathy gene with consistent human, mouse and zebrafish data for Waardenburg syndrome, Hirschsprung disease and PCWH. One patient with a SOX10-containing deletion had severe CHD together with Waardenburg features.

Key gaps and limitations:
- The only CHD case involves a 14-gene deletion.
- The trio exome report lacks variant detail and phenotype.
- No intragenic LoF or missense SOX10 variants with CHD have been described.
- No CDH cases have been reported.
- No animal model shows a cardiac or diaphragmatic malformation.
- No cohort-level data (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL) or statistical enrichment were presented.

Syndromic assessment: The evidence points to a neurocristopathy syndrome (Waardenburg syndrome, Hirschsprung disease, PCWH, Kallmann syndrome) rather than a syndrome combining CHD and CDH. CHD appears only as a possible rare feature in an atypical Waardenburg deletion case, and CDH is not associated at all.

Conclusion: Evidence that heterozygous SOX10 variants cause isolated or syndromic CHD is limited. It rests on one multigenic deletion and an uninformative exome report, and it does not implicate SOX10 haploinsufficiency specifically. There is no evidence for CDH. SOX10 should be considered a neurocristopathy gene, and any association with CHD would need further intragenic variant cases to be confirmed.
