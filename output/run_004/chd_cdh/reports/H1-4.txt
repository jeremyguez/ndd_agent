H1-4 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: CONGENITAL HEART DISEASE (CHD) AND CONGENITAL DIAPHRAGMATIC HERNIA (CDH)

1. GENE SUMMARY
H1-4 (formerly HIST1H1E, encoding linker histone H1.4/H1E) is a replication-dependent linker histone. It binds nucleosomes and linker DNA to compact chromatin and regulate gene expression. Heterozygous C-terminal frameshift variants in H1-4 cause Rahman syndrome, a neurodevelopmental disorder with intellectual disability.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Number of reports: One case report was found (PMID: 29704315), a single patient with autism spectrum disorder and intellectual disability.
- Total patients: about 1.
- Variant: de novo frameshift c.435dupC, p.Thr146Hisfs*50, in the C-terminal domain. It is a loss-of-function variant that truncates the positively charged C-terminus, and reduced protein expression was shown.
- Phenotypes: autism spectrum disorder and intellectual disability. No congenital heart disease or diaphragmatic hernia was reported. The entry was labelled "CHD" only because of the literature search that retrieved it, not because of the patient's phenotype.
- Cohorts: none identified (no UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL attribution).
- Other reports of Rahman syndrome are mentioned only in passing, as "consistent with other reported HIST1H1E cases". They are not extracted here and contain no CHD or CDH data in this dataset.

Congenital heart disease: no patient with a cardiac phenotype was reported.
Strength of clinical evidence for congenital heart disease (CHD): NONE

Congenital diaphragmatic hernia: no patient with a diaphragmatic phenotype was reported.
Strength of clinical evidence for congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH): NONE

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organism: mouse only.
- In an H1f2/H1f4 double knockout, chromatin compaction fails to mature in cerebellar granule neurons (PMID: 42619895). H1.4 is highly expressed in mature postmitotic neurons and accumulates in heterochromatin. No cardiac or diaphragm phenotypes were reported.
- H1t knockout mice (PMID: 10933220) are not H1-4 knockouts. They are viable, with normal development and spermatogenesis. H1.4 expression increased as a compensatory change.
- The findings are neurological, which is consistent with the human neurodevelopmental syndrome. They are not informative for CHD or CDH.
Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED (for neurodevelopment); NONE for CHD/CDH specifically

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Function: H1.4 compacts chromatin and undergoes phase separation. Rahman syndrome frameshifts truncate the C-terminal domain and reduce its charge. This gives extended nucleosome arrays, more accessible linker DNA, reduced phase separation and greater nuclear mobility (PMID: 42173878). Together these point to loss of chromatin binding.
- Gene regulation: H1.4 ablation alters enhancer accessibility, particularly at AP-1 motifs, and changes transcription (PMID: 41054521). H1.4 depletion in T47D cells caused cell death and changed a distinct subset of genes (PMID: 18927631). H1.4 loss also promotes an eosinophil-like program and affects lineage determination (PMID: 32391789).
- Cancer: H1-4 is recurrently mutated in B-cell lymphoma, and combined H1c/H1e loss in mice drives lymphoma through chromatin decompaction and altered H3K36me2/H3K27me3 (PMID: 33299181). Other studies show H1.4 acting as a tumour suppressor through ERK1/2 and Ras-ERK/PKA-dependent S35/S36 phosphorylation in lung cancer and osteosarcoma cells (PMIDs: 38472566, 31184227, 31126199). Further studies describe H1.4 interactions with CYP1B1 and with the pseudogene CMAHP (PMIDs: 39395328, 34716430).
- Expression: H1.4 is widely expressed, and is notably abundant in mature neurons (PMID: 42619895). No cardiac or diaphragm-specific expression or function data were provided.
Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for general chromatin and neurodevelopmental function; LIMITED for relevance to CHD/CDH

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
Overall evidence level for congenital heart disease (CHD): INSUFFICIENT
Overall evidence level for congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH): INSUFFICIENT
Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT

Syndrome: The evidence points to a neurodevelopmental syndrome (Rahman syndrome: intellectual disability, autism) rather than a syndrome that includes CHD or CDH. No multi-system syndrome involving these conditions is supported by the data provided.

Strengths: The mechanism of C-terminal frameshift loss of function is well characterized (PMIDs: 29704315, 42173878), and the mouse data support a role for H1.4 in neuronal chromatin.

Gaps: No patients with CHD or CDH were reported, and there are no cohort or de novo enrichment data for either condition. No animal model shows a cardiac or diaphragmatic phenotype, and the single case is not specific to either condition.

Conclusion: H1-4 is an established neurodevelopmental disease gene, but there is no evidence in this dataset linking heterozygous H1-4 variants to isolated or syndromic CHD or CDH. The evidence for both conditions is insufficient. Support would require patients with cardiac or diaphragmatic anomalies carrying H1-4 variants, or relevant model phenotypes.
