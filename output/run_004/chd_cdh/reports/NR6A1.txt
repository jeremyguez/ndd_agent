NR6A1 (nuclear receptor subfamily 6 group A member 1, also called GCNF or germ cell nuclear factor) is an orphan nuclear receptor that acts mainly as a transcriptional repressor. It represses pluripotency genes such as Oct4 and regulates Hox expression and axial patterning during embryonic development.

1. GENE SUMMARY
See the summary above. In brief, NR6A1 is a transcriptional repressor needed for early embryogenesis, trunk and axial patterning, and neural crest development.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
No clinical case-level entries were supplied for either condition, so there are no patient-level data to evaluate for NR6A1 in CHD or CDH.

- Independent case reports or series: none were extracted for CHD or CDH.
- Total patients: none from the clinical evidence provided.
- Phenotypes and variant type: not assessable from the clinical entries. The biological evidence does mention a separate syndrome, in which six rare NR6A1 variants were found in six families with autosomal dominant oculo-vertebral-renal syndrome (colobomatous microphthalmia, missing vertebrae, kidney anomalies) (PMID: 40610405). The summary reports neither cardiac nor diaphragmatic phenotypes, and the variant types are not specified. A further study titled "Variants in NR6A1 as a cause for congenital renal, vertebral and uterine anomalies" (PMID: 41733738) is known only from its zebrafish data here. None of these reports describes CHD or CDH.
- Known cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): not documented.

Strength of clinical evidence for congenital heart disease (CHD): NONE
Strength of clinical evidence for congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH): NONE

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: zebrafish, mouse and Xenopus.
- Zebrafish: knockdown of nr6a1a and/or nr6a1b caused eye, kidney and somite defects. Wild-type but not variant mRNA rescued these defects (PMID: 40610405; PMID: 39606449). CRISPR mutants of both paralogs showed pronephros segmentation defects, adult kidney hypoplasia, axial skeletal abnormalities, cloacal anomalies and disrupted posterior hox expression (PMID: 41733738).
- Mouse: full knockout is embryonic lethal. Embryos die around E10.5 and fail to restrict Oct4 expression (PMID: 24029702). Loss of Nr6a1 causes agenesis of migratory neural crest caudal to the first pharyngeal arch and persistent pluripotency factors (PMID: 41611698). A conditional knockout has also been used to bypass the embryonic lethality (PMID: 17485308).
- Xenopus: morpholino or dominant-negative inhibition caused neural tube defects, a short curved axis, altered retinoic acid signaling, and anterior defects of the eye and cement gland (PMID: 12729562; PMID: 12676320; PMID: 10349627).
- Consistency with human phenotype: the models fit the eye, vertebral and renal features of the OVR syndrome. No cardiac or diaphragmatic phenotypes were reported in any model. Neural crest loss and retinoic acid and Hox perturbation are plausible mechanisms for cardiac outflow or diaphragm defects, but this is inferential only.
- Mendelian ratios and sample sizes were not reported.

Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE for a developmental and axial syndrome, but LIMITED for CHD or CDH specifically.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Function: NR6A1 is a nuclear receptor that represses target genes. In mouse it controls Hox expression dynamics, vertebral number and trunk segmentation, and the neural versus mesodermal fate choice of axial progenitors (PMID: 36522318). It acts as a switch in neural crest specification, repressing pluripotency factors and activating specifiers (PMID: 41611698).
- Expression: it is found in gametes and in somatic tissues including brain (hippocampus, cortex, cerebellum, thalamus), lung bronchioles, pituitary and pancreatic islets (PMID: 19164181). Cardiac and diaphragm expression was not described.
- Other experimental findings: it regulates lipid metabolism via mTORC1 in HepG2 cells (PMID: 31315616). It interacts with CREB and represses SPP1 in vascular smooth muscle cells (PMID: 26546462). It is regulated by LUBAC and promotes RIPK3-mediated apoptosis (PMID: 35543488). It represses MFN2 in stroke models (PMID: 39259438) and impairs CREB-BDNF signaling in depression-like behavior (PMID: 35183538). It is also expressed in prostate cancer (PMID: 28969082).

Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE. The role in development is well supported, but no data link NR6A1 to cardiac or diaphragm development.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
Overall evidence level for congenital heart disease (CHD): INSUFFICIENT
Overall evidence level for congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH): INSUFFICIENT
Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT

- Key strengths: heterozygous NR6A1 variants have been reported in an oculo-vertebral-renal syndrome (PMID: 40610405). Zebrafish rescue experiments support variant pathogenicity, and mouse, Xenopus and zebrafish models support a conserved role in axial, neural crest and renal development.
- Key gaps: there are no CHD or CDH patients in the evidence provided. The reported syndrome does not include cardiac or diaphragmatic features, and the variant types and counts are not detailed. The models report no heart or diaphragm phenotypes, and no cohort information is available.
- Syndrome assessment: the evidence points to a multisystem syndrome affecting eye, vertebrae and kidney (with uterine anomalies in one report), but not to a syndrome that includes CHD or CDH.

Conclusion: NR6A1 is a plausible developmental gene with emerging human disease associations, but the supplied evidence does not support it as a CHD or CDH gene. Any link would require new patients with heterozygous variants and cardiac or diaphragmatic phenotypes, together with model data examining those organs.
