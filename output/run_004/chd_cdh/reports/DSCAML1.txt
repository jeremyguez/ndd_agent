DSCAML1 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: CHD and CDH (heterozygous / autosomal dominant)

1. GENE SUMMARY
DSCAML1 (11q23) encodes Down syndrome cell adhesion molecule-like 1, a neuronal cell-surface immunoglobulin superfamily protein with 10 Ig and 6 FNIII domains and homophilic adhesion activity (PMID: 11453658). Its best-characterized roles are in neurodevelopment: dendrite self-avoidance, neuronal migration and retinal circuit patterning. No literature in this dataset links it to cardiac or diaphragm development.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
Organisms studied: mouse and zebrafish.
- Zebrafish full knockouts are viable. They show retinal patterning and light-adaptation deficits and oculomotor abnormalities resembling human ocular motor apraxia, including impaired saccade-command signals in abducens neurons (PMID: 31685652). They also show hypothalamic CRH neuron overproduction and a dysregulated HPA stress axis (PMID: 36875755). Retinofugal connectivity differs from wild type (PMID: 32038180).
- Mouse full knockouts are viable. They show fasciculated rod bipolar dendrites and clumped AII amacrine cell bodies, a self-avoidance defect, with intact laminar stratification and synapse formation (PMID: 19945391). Aging mutants show rod synapse and bipolar cell alterations (PMID: 37910517). Cortical development, interneuron migration and cortical-subcortical connectivity were not significantly affected (PMID: 33585465).
- A patient-specific A2105T knock-in mouse shows intracellular retention of the mutant protein, dendritic retraction in layer V pyramidal neurons, abnormal clustering of somatostatin interneurons and spike-and-wave discharges. These were ameliorated by 4-phenylbutyrate (PMID: 42003794).
- No cardiac or diaphragm phenotypes were reported in any model. Mendelian ratios were not reported, and the knockouts are not lethal.
- Consistency with the human CHD/CDH phenotypes is therefore absent. The phenotypes are neural, retinal, oculomotor, neuroendocrine and epileptic.

Strength of model organism evidence (for CHD/CDH): NONE. Models support a neurodevelopmental role only.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Function: DSCAML1 mediates homophilic adhesion (PMID: 11453658) and dendrite self-avoidance. Knockdown in cortical neurons causes dendritic fasciculation, and Y1808 phosphorylation is required for its function (PMID: 24084194). In vivo knockdown impairs radial migration and callosal projection (PMID: 25451118).
- Signaling: its intracellular domain binds importin IPO5 and enters the nucleus, where it alters expression of genes for differentiation, apoptosis and synapse function (PMID: 30745319).
- Conservation: human DSCAML1 partially rescues Drosophila Dscam mutants (PMID: 21645617).
- Expression: in mouse brain it is found in cerebellar Purkinje cells, dentate granule cells, cortical neurons and olfactory bulb (PMID: 11453658). In zebrafish retina it is found in the inner nuclear and ganglion cell layers (PMID: 30078982).
- Other: a goat GWAS associated DSCAML1 indels with litter size and semen quality, which is not relevant to CHD or CDH (PMID: 32036056).
- No expression or functional data in heart or diaphragm were provided.

Strength of biological evidence (for CHD/CDH): LIMITED. The data are well supported for neurodevelopment, but nothing links the gene to cardiac or diaphragm biology.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
Overall evidence level for congenital heart disease: INSUFFICIENT
Overall evidence level for congenital diaphragmatic hernia: INSUFFICIENT
Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT

Strengths: DSCAML1 is well characterized as a neurodevelopmental gene, with consistent mouse and zebrafish data on dendrite self-avoidance, retinal patterning and neuroendocrine function. An A2105T knock-in mouse also exists.

Gaps and limitations: there are no human patients, no heterozygous variants and no segregation or de novo data for either CHD or CDH. No model shows cardiac or diaphragmatic defects, and no heart or diaphragm expression data were provided. The knockout phenotypes are viable and neural.

Syndrome assessment: the evidence does not point to a syndrome affecting CHD and CDH together, because neither condition has any supporting data.

Conclusion: there is currently no evidence that heterozygous DSCAML1 variants cause CHD or CDH. DSCAML1 should be regarded as unsupported for these conditions unless clinical cohorts with relevant variants emerge. Its established role is in neurodevelopment, including the epilepsy phenotype in the A2105T knock-in mouse.
