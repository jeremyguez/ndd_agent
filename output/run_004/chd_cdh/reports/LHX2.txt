LHX2 EVIDENCE REPORT: CONGENITAL HEART DISEASE (CHD) AND CONGENITAL DIAPHRAGMATIC HERNIA (CDH), HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
LHX2 encodes a LIM-homeodomain transcription factor that works with the cofactor LDB1 and regulates progenitor fate, proliferation and patterning in the developing forebrain, eye, hair follicle, olfactory system and other tissues. It is constrained against loss of function (pLI = 0.99, as cited in PMID: 40127276).

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

Number of reports: One study contributed clinical evidence, a large exome-based CHD cohort analysis from the Pediatric Cardiac Genomics Consortium (PMID: 40127276). No individual case reports were found.

Patients: Approximately 2 probands carried damaging de novo variants (DNMs) in LHX2.

Phenotypes: Both probands had CHD with aortic atresia and mitral atresia, a left-sided obstructive phenotype. No extracardiac phenotypes were described in the data provided.

Variant type: The text describes "damaging" de novo variants, meaning LoF or predicted-damaging missense. The specific variants and their classes were not given, so the predominant type cannot be determined.

Cohorts: The patients come from the PCGC, a multicenter, likely US cohort of 11,555 probands. None are from UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL.

Congenital heart disease: The gene-level evidence rests on a significant burden of damaging DNMs in CHD probands, plus supporting constraint and mouse data. Only 2 cases carry the variants, no variant-level detail is available, and there are no independent replication cohorts, segregation data or functional validation of the human variants. The recurrent aortic and mitral atresia phenotype is notable.
Strength of clinical evidence for CHD: LIMITED

Congenital diaphragmatic hernia: No patient with CDH or any diaphragmatic phenotype was reported. The clinical entry was assigned to CHD only.
Strength of clinical evidence for CDH: NONE

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

Organisms: Mouse is the main model, with additional zebrafish and chick work described under biological evidence.

Key phenotypes:
- Full Lhx2 knockout mice die in utero. They have anophthalmia, neocortical hypoplasia, absent hippocampal anlagen and severe anemia from a defect in definitive erythropoiesis (PMID: 9247336).
- Loss of Lhx2 expands the cortical hem across the dorsal telencephalon (PMID: 11165475).
- Conditional knockouts show cortical size reduction through altered β-catenin-dependent neurogenesis (PMID: 26371318), loss of retinal Müller glia with impaired Notch signaling (PMID: 26911688), thalamocortical and barrel cortex defects (PMID: 28122236; PMID: 22457488), and osteoporosis in viable mice (PMID: 24902903).
- Hair follicle stem cell maintenance is also affected (PMID: 16809539).

Consistency with human phenotype: The provided data do not report a cardiac phenotype in Lhx2 mutants. The cited CHD paper states that mouse knockouts produce CHD (PMID: 40127276), but the primary mouse papers listed here do not show it. No diaphragm phenotype is reported. The embryonic lethality, with erythropoietic failure, is compatible with an essential developmental role but does not directly model human CHD. Mendelian ratios and heterozygous phenotypes are not reported.
Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE for general developmental essentiality; LIMITED for CHD specifically; NONE for CDH.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

Pathways and function: LHX2 acts with LDB1 (PMID: 30626972) and interacts with MRG1 through its LIM domain (PMID: 10593900). It is required for Wnt/β-catenin function in cortical progenitors (PMID: 26371318), supports Notch signaling in retinal gliogenesis (PMID: 26911688), mediates the FGF-to-SHH loop in limb development (PMID: 29914077), and restrains RANKL/c-Fos/NFATc1 signaling in osteoclasts (PMID: 24902903). It also regulates TGF-β/SMAD6 signaling in hepatic stellate cells (PMID: 39693275). In neuroendocrine prostate cancer, it forms a lactate/LDHA/DNMT1 feedback loop (PMID: 40899980).

Expression: Expression is prominent in the developing forebrain, where LHX2 limits the cortical hem, septum and thalamic eminence (PMID: 23307637), and also in the hair follicle outer root sheath (PMID: 25808706). In zebrafish, lhx2 acts downstream of or parallel to six3 in forebrain growth (PMID: 16226737). No cardiac or diaphragm expression data were provided.

Assessment: The molecular function is well characterized. The Wnt, Notch, FGF/SHH and TGF-β pathways it regulates are plausible links to cardiac development, but this connection is indirect and inferred. No link to diaphragm development was shown.
Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for LHX2 as a developmental regulator; LIMITED for relevance to CHD; NONE for CDH.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

Overall evidence level for CHD: LIMITED
Overall evidence level for CDH: INSUFFICIENT
Overall evidence level: LIMITED

Strengths:
- A statistically significant burden of de novo variants in a large cohort of 11,555 probands (PMID: 40127276).
- High constraint (pLI = 0.99).
- A consistent, specific cardiac phenotype (aortic and mitral atresia) in both carriers.
- A well-established essential developmental role in mouse.

Gaps:
- Only 2 probands, with unspecified variants.
- No segregation, functional or independent replication data.
- No cardiac phenotype in the primary mouse studies provided.
- No evidence of LHX2 involvement in CDH.
- No evidence of a multi-system syndrome.
- Neurological and ocular phenotypes seen in mice have not been described in the human carriers.

Syndrome assessment: The evidence does not point to a syndrome affecting both CHD and CDH. The human data support only isolated, left-sided obstructive CHD, with no diaphragmatic or extracardiac features reported.

Conclusion: LHX2 is a candidate dominant CHD gene, supported by a de novo variant burden in one large cohort and by its developmental biology, but the clinical evidence is limited to two probands and needs replication with variant-level detail. There is no support for LHX2 as a CDH gene.
