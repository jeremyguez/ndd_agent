H2BC8 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: CONGENITAL HEART DISEASE (CHD) AND CONGENITAL DIAPHRAGMATIC HERNIA (CDH)

1. GENE SUMMARY
H2BC8 (formerly HIST1H2BG) encodes a replication-dependent core histone H2B type 1 variant on chromosome 6p22 in the histone cluster 1. Its primary function is as a structural nucleosome component that packages DNA and contributes to chromatin organization and transcriptional regulation. No curated gene-disease association with CHD or CDH was identified in the evidence provided.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
The biological evidence is indirect, derived from bioinformatic and expression studies unrelated to developmental disease:
- Expression/transcriptomics: In a WGCNA of PBMC transcriptomes (GSE133601) from obstructive sleep apnea patients before and after CPAP, H2BC8 fell in a CPAP-associated module (the black module). It was one of eleven hub genes in a random forest model predicting CPAP efficacy (AUC 0.92), and the hub genes partly correlated with monocyte infiltration (PMID: 34529670). The exact sample size was not stated, and no module-trait value was given.
- Pathway/prognostic: H2BC8 was one of nine pyroptosis-related genes in the PyroScore prognostic model for papillary thyroid cancer (TCGA), with validation of the prognostic genes in six external datasets, 16 cell lines, and 20 paired clinical samples (PMID: 38287330). No H2BC8-specific statistics were reported.
- These studies concern immune/inflammatory and oncologic contexts. They give no information on cardiac or diaphragm development, brain expression, or the effect of heterozygous pathogenic variants.
Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED (and not relevant to CHD or CDH)

Key strengths: None specific to CHD or CDH. H2BC8 is a bona fide chromatin component, and histone and chromatin-regulation genes are broadly implicated in developmental disorders, which gives only generic biological plausibility.

Key gaps and limitations: There are no human genetic data (no patients, variants, de novo or segregation evidence, or cohort enrichment) and no functional or animal model data. The available papers are transcriptomic or prognostic bioinformatic analyses in sleep apnea and thyroid cancer, with no statistics specific to H2BC8. Redundancy within the histone cluster and the difficulty of variant calling in multicopy histone genes further limit interpretation.

Conclusion: The evidence does not support H2BC8 as a CHD or CDH gene with heterozygous pathogenic variants. The evidence also does not point to a syndrome spanning these conditions, because no patients with either phenotype were reported. H2BC8 should be considered a gene of uncertain significance for these phenotypes unless patient-level genetic and functional data emerge.
