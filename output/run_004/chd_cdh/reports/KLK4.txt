KLK4 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: CONGENITAL HEART DISEASE (CHD) AND CONGENITAL DIAPHRAGMATIC HERNIA (CDH)

1. GENE SUMMARY
KLK4 encodes kallikrein-related peptidase 4, a trypsin-like serine protease. It is expressed in androgen-regulated prostate epithelium and in maturation-stage ameloblasts, where it degrades retained enamel matrix proteins during final enamel mineralization. The established human disease association is recessive hypomaturation amelogenesis imperfecta, not CHD or CDH.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
The clinical evidence list was empty. No case reports or case series were found for either condition, so there are no patient counts, phenotype patterns, variant types or cohort memberships (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL) to report.

- Congenital heart disease: no patients with heterozygous or any other KLK4 variants were identified.
Strength of clinical evidence for congenital heart disease: NONE

- Congenital diaphragmatic hernia: no patients with heterozygous or any other KLK4 variants were identified.
Strength of clinical evidence for congenital diaphragmatic hernia: NONE

The only human genetic finding in the supplied evidence is outside both conditions. A homozygous frameshift (c.620_621delCT, p.Ser207Trpfs*38) was found in consanguineous families with recessive hypomaturation amelogenesis imperfecta (2 families). The mutant protein was degraded intracellularly and was inactive (PMID: 26124219). This is a recessive loss-of-function mechanism with an enamel-restricted phenotype, and it does not support autosomal dominant disease.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
Only mouse models were reported. Klk4 null mice (a full knockout and a lacZ knock-in null allele) are viable and fertile, with no abnormalities outside the enamel (PMID: 26620968).
- Enamel has normal thickness and rod decussation but is retarded in maturation. Retained matrix proteins and a failure to mature beyond about 85% mineral by weight were described (PMID: 21454549; PMID: 22243248; PMID: 22243238).
- Mineral density is reduced (2.5 vs 3.1 gHA/cm3 in wild type), hardness is reduced, and incisors are chalky and chipped (PMID: 26620968).
- The lacZ reporter is expressed in maturation-stage ameloblasts only (PMID: 19578120).
- Amtn/Klk4 and Mmp20/Klk4 double nulls show additive enamel defects (PMID: 26620968; PMID: 22243248).

No cardiac or diaphragmatic phenotypes, lethality, or Mendelian ratio deviations were reported. The mouse phenotype matches the human hypomaturation amelogenesis imperfecta and gives no support for CHD or CDH.
Strength of model organism evidence: NONE for CHD/CDH (strong for the enamel phenotype only)

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Function: KLK4 is a trypsin-like protease with a P1-Arg preference. It activates pro-PSA/KLK3 and degrades IGFBPs (PMID: 15389820). Structural work shows allosteric inhibition between a metal-binding exosite and the active site (PMID: 27767076).
- Expression: KLK4 is prostate-specific, with low-level mRNA in adrenal gland, salivary gland and thyroid (PMID: 12370833). In ameloblasts it is a maturation-stage marker regulated by Dlx3, Runx2 and Foxo1/Runx2 (PMID: 22243224; PMID: 34781073). No heart or diaphragm expression or developmental role was reported.
- Cancer studies: KLK4 promotes CAF-like features in prostate stromal cells (PMID: 28510269). Its silencing inhibits growth in oral squamous cell carcinoma through Wnt/β-catenin signaling (PMID: 28150891) and in chromophobe renal cell carcinoma (PMID: 36260980). These are not relevant to cardiac or diaphragm development.
Strength of biological evidence: NONE for CHD/CDH (relevant to enamel and prostate/cancer biology only)

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
Overall evidence level for congenital heart disease: INSUFFICIENT
Overall evidence level for congenital diaphragmatic hernia: INSUFFICIENT
Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT

Key strengths: KLK4 function is well characterized, and the enamel phenotype is consistent across humans and mice. The mouse data are extensive and consistent.

Key gaps: There are no patients with CHD or CDH, no heterozygous variants of any kind, and no cardiac or diaphragm phenotypes in the knockout mice. There is no evidence of cardiac or diaphragmatic expression or function. The only human disease mechanism is recessive and restricted to enamel. The evidence does not point to a syndrome affecting CHD, CDH or both.

Conclusion: KLK4 has no supporting evidence as a heterozygous CHD or CDH gene. Its established disease role is recessive, enamel-limited hypomaturation amelogenesis imperfecta. It should not be considered a candidate for either condition without new clinical and functional data.
