ANK2 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER

1. GENE SUMMARY
ANK2 encodes ankyrin-B, a membrane adaptor/scaffold protein that links membrane proteins (e.g., L1CAM, NaV1.2, ion channels) to the spectrin-actin and microtubule cytoskeleton. Its neuron-specific giant isoform (AnkB440/giant ankB) regulates axon branching, connectivity and microtubule organization. ANK2 is also expressed in heart, where it has an established role in cardiac ion channel targeting.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: eight entries were found. Seven are case reports, family reports, or small series, and one is a cohort-level report (PMID: 37195288). One is a conference abstract (ASHG 2015 - Role of ANK2 in autism).
- Approximate patient total: about 25 or more. This comprises 12 patients with de novo LoF variants (PMID: 37195288), 3 patients in the ASHG 2015 abstract, 2 in a missense family (PMID: 39978592), 2 in the Polish families (PMID: 41096937), 1 each in PMID: 40035441 and PMID: 37088467, plus a segregating family (PMID: 39962910) and two families with a duplication rearrangement (PMID: 39472019). The latter two did not report patient numbers. PMID: 40035441 also reviewed 16 previously published patients. Some overlap with other reports is possible.
- Common phenotypes: ASD, intellectual disability, language delay, and epilepsy. Epilepsy was usually early-onset in the RUMC cohort, with later-onset seizures in one nonsense case. Other features were hyperactivity, behavioral problems, and self-injury (PMID: 40035441). Agenesis of the corpus callosum was seen with a translocation (ASHG 2015 - Role of ANK2 in autism). A milder end of the spectrum includes self-limited familial focal epilepsy with no cardiac phenotype (PMID: 39962910) and subthreshold autistic traits in a carrier father (PMID: 39978592).
- Variant types: predominantly LoF, including de novo LoF variants (PMID: 37195288), the nonsense variant p.R1003* (PMID: 40035441), the frameshift p.T2312Lfs*2 affecting giant ankB (PMID: 39962910), nonsense variants and a t(4;8) translocation (ASHG 2015 - Role of ANK2 in autism). Missense variants are less well supported: p.Arg987Trp was inherited from a mildly affected parent (PMID: 39978592), and the Polish variants p.Ile3285Thr and p.Val3634Asp were not classified as definitively pathogenic (PMID: 41096937). A complex duplication rearrangement was reported in two families (PMID: 39472019).
- Cohorts: RUMC (Radboud) is the only named cohort (PMID: 37195288). No UDN, GREGoR, DDD, or GEL cohort was identified in the extracted data.
- Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE to STRONG. Multiple de novo LoF cases with a consistent NDD/ASD/epilepsy phenotype support the association, but many reports are single cases, and there is variable expressivity and incomplete penetrance. Missense evidence is weak.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse (conditional, heterozygous and knock-in models), cynomolgus and rhesus monkey, and zebrafish.
- Neurological phenotypes:
  - Early forebrain deletion of Ank2 caused severe spontaneous seizures, increased mortality, hyperactivity, social deficits and network hyperexcitability. Adolescent deletion did not (PMID: 37428632).
  - Conditional mutants showed ASD-related behaviors, juvenile seizure-related death, hyperactivity, and reduced Kv7.2/7.3 with a lengthened axon initial segment. Retigabine rescued these phenotypes (PMID: 37321992).
  - An ASD-mutation knock-in mouse showed increased axon branching, ectopic connectivity, transient excess excitatory synapses, and dominantly inherited social/communication deficits (PMID: 31285321).
  - Giant AnkB-depleted monkeys (n=4) showed infant brain volume loss, hyperactivity, cognitive deficits, and impaired social and emotional behavior (PMID: 42707610).
  - Zebrafish ank2 mutants showed network hyperexcitability that estradiol dampened (PMID: 41757108).
- Other phenotypes: heterozygous and cardiac-specific mice showed arrhythmia, atrial fibrillation susceptibility, bradycardia, and cardiomyopathy (PMID: 31264976; PMID: 21859974; PMID: 42580048). Lens-specific loss caused cataract (PMID: 39558792).
- Lethality and Mendelian ratios: models were reported as viable, and no Mendelian ratio data were extracted. Mortality was increased in early forebrain knockouts.
- Consistency with human phenotype: strong for the neurological features (ASD-like behavior, epilepsy, hyperexcitability, NDD-like features in primates). The cardiac phenotypes in mice are not clearly mirrored in the neurodevelopmental patients; one patient family had no ECG abnormality (PMID: 39962910).
- Strength: STRONG.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: giant ankB localizes to periodic axonal membrane domains via L1CAM, couples microtubules to the membrane, and restrains axon branching (PMID: 31285321). It stabilizes the L1CAM/neuropilin-1 complex in Sema3A signaling, and ASD-linked variants failed to rescue growth cone collapse (PMID: 34812142). Ankyrin-B scaffolds NaV1.2 in dendrites, and Ank2 haploinsufficiency phenocopies Scn2a+/- deficits (PMID: 38290518).
- Expression: Ank2 is highly expressed in the embryonic mouse cortex, and knockdown altered neural stem cell differentiation, neuronal migration, and neurodevelopmental gene expression (PMID: 35313230).
- Human cell and protein data:
  - Heterozygous LoF hiPSC-derived neurons showed hyperactive, desynchronized networks, increased dendritic complexity, and impaired AIS plasticity (PMID: 37195288).
  - Patient PBMCs showed reduced ANK2 and L1CAM, and L1CAM was rescued by ANK2 transfection (ASHG 2015 - Role of ANK2 in autism).
  - ANK2 co-complexes with other ASD proteins (CHD8, CUL3, DYRK1A, POGZ) in a network enriched in early fetal brain (ASHG 2015 - Integrative Analysis of Human Protein Complexes Reveals Biochemical Activities and Convergent Mechanisms of Action in Autism Spectrum Disorders).
- Cross-species support: C. elegans unc-44 loss altered GABAergic neuron morphology and behavior (PMID: 40795403).
- Non-neural data: a common ANK2 SNP modulates QT interval (PMID: 20031550), and Ank2.3 supports oocyte cytokinesis (PMID: 31511568).
- Strength: STRONG.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: STRONG (for heterozygous LoF variants causing a neurodevelopmental phenotype with ASD, ID, and epilepsy).
- Key strengths:
  - Multiple de novo LoF variants, including a 12-patient series (PMID: 37195288).
  - Concordant mouse, primate, and hiPSC functional data showing hyperexcitability and altered connectivity, including pharmacological rescue (PMID: 37321992; PMID: 37428632).
  - Mechanistic links to L1CAM, NaV1.2, and Sema3A signaling (PMID: 31285321; PMID: 34812142; PMID: 38290518).
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - Many reports are single cases or small families, and some are abstracts or lack variant detail (PMID: 37088467; ASHG 2015 - Role of ANK2 in autism).
  - Missense pathogenicity is unresolved, and carrier parents show variable expressivity (PMID: 39978592; PMID: 41096937).
  - Isoform-specific effects (giant vs. canonical) and the role of variant location are not fully defined.
  - No quantitative genotype-phenotype data, Mendelian ratios, or large statistical burden analyses were extracted. Cardiac risk in neurodevelopmental carriers is unclear.
- Conclusion: ANK2 is a well-supported autosomal dominant ASD/neurodevelopmental disorder gene, with a LoF (haploinsufficiency) mechanism supported by clinical, model organism, and functional data. Epilepsy and ID commonly accompany ASD, and expressivity ranges from severe NDD to mild or self-limited phenotypes. Missense variants and non-coding or structural variants need further curation.
