PDE4C AND AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT MODEL): LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT

1. GENE SUMMARY
PDE4C encodes a cAMP-specific phosphodiesterase of the PDE4 family. It hydrolyzes cAMP and so regulates cAMP/PKA signaling. Several splice isoforms exist, and some are enzymatically active and sensitive to PDE4 inhibitors (PMID: 9349724).

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent case reports or case series: 0 identified.
- Total patients: 0.
- Phenotypes: none reported. No autism, developmental delay, or intellectual disability cases were found.
- Variant type: not applicable. No heterozygous pathogenic variants in PDE4C have been described in patients with a neurodevelopmental phenotype.
- Known cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none identified.
- The only human genetic signal is tangential. A whole-genome sequencing association and rare-variant study reported rare missense and splice-site PDE4C variants associated with alcohol-related life events, in 742 American Indians and 1711 European Americans (PMID: 30718457). This is a substance-use/affective trait, not ASD or a neurodevelopmental disorder.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- No knockout or knock-in model organism data were identified.
- Mouse studies are only indirect. In a mouse spinal cord injury model, exosomal miR-615-3p downregulated PDE4C and improved recovery (PMID: 41526970). In plateau hypoxia mouse models, diosgenin was reported to protect cognition by regulating PDE4C expression (PMID: 40430556). Neither study involves genetic disruption of Pde4c or an ASD-relevant phenotype.
- Consistency with human phenotype: cannot be assessed.
- Strength of model organism evidence: NONE.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Molecular function and pathways: PDE4C is a cAMP-hydrolyzing enzyme. Recombinant human PDE4C (61.4 kDa) is highly asymmetric and can form dimers (PMID: 9515166). Splice variants differ in activity. PDE4C-delta54 and the 426-aa form are active, and delta109 is inactive (PMID: 9349724). The gene modulates cAMP/PKA signaling, including in injured spinal neurons, where PDE4C suppression reduced mitochondrial calcium overload and ER stress (PMID: 41526970).
- Expression: PDE4C is tissue-restricted. Isoforms are found in lung, testis and melanoma cells (PMID: 9349724). It is absent from rat bladder (PMID: 28687079). It is upregulated during decidualization of endometrial stromal cells (PMID: 14715868) and is differentially expressed in Th1 versus Th2 cells (PMID: 9223593). No brain developmental expression data were presented in the evidence set.
- Key experimental findings are mostly oncology and non-neural:
  - Oncogenic role in papillary thyroid carcinoma through the HNF4α/LINC02560/miR-505-5p/PDE4C axis, with AKT and EMT activation (PMID: 40427523).
  - Tumor-suppressor-like behavior in glioma. The promoter is hypermethylated, expression is reduced in high-grade tumors (124 samples), and PDE4C influences p53 (PMID: 24842301).
- None of these findings links PDE4C to synaptic function, neurodevelopment, or ASD mechanisms.
- Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED. The enzymatic function is plausibly relevant to cAMP signaling in neurons, but there is no direct neurodevelopmental data.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT.
- Key strengths: PDE4C has well-characterized enzymology and a clear role in cAMP signaling, which is a pathway relevant to neuronal function. Other PDE4 family members are better known in neurobehavioral contexts.
- Key gaps: no clinical cases, no de novo or inherited heterozygous variants, no segregation data, no functional testing of patient variants, no animal knockout data, and no evidence of brain-specific developmental expression. The only human genetic signal (PMID: 30718457) concerns alcohol-related phenotypes.
- Conclusion: There is currently no evidence supporting PDE4C as an autosomal dominant autism spectrum disorder gene. Its biological role in cAMP signaling is plausible but nonspecific, and available studies are mostly cancer, tissue-expression, or pharmacology-related. PDE4C should not be considered an ASD candidate unless new patient-level genetic and functional evidence emerges.
