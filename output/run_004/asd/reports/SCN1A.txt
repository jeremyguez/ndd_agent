SCN1A AS AN AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER GENE (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
SCN1A encodes the alpha subunit of the voltage-gated sodium channel Nav1.1. The channel is expressed mainly in inhibitory (especially parvalbumin-positive) interneurons, where it supports action potential firing and GABAergic inhibition. Heterozygous variants cause a spectrum of epilepsies (Dravet syndrome, GEFS+) and familial hemiplegic migraine. Autism is a recognized but secondary comorbidity.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: 4 (2 case reports/series, 1 clinical laboratory abstract, 1 cohort screening study).
- Total patients: about 11 (5 + 1 + 4 + 1). Only 3 had ASD documented.
- ASD-relevant cases:
  - Case 4 in the ASHG abstract (ASHG 2016 - The many faces of SCN1A): an adult with a missense variant, unspecified seizures and ASD, found on a 196-gene neurodevelopment panel. The variant is not specified and inheritance is unknown.
  - One patient with c.3521C>G (p.T1174S), a missense variant with intractable myoclonic epilepsy and ASD (PMID: 22550089). It was inherited from a mother with migraine, and the variant had previously been reported in familial hemiplegic migraine. This makes it hard to attribute the ASD to the variant, because the carrier mother and maternal grandmother had only migraine with aura.
  - One de novo protein-altering SCN1A mutation among 1,703 ASD probands screened (PMID: 22495309). It is the only de novo finding in a primary ASD cohort, but the variant is unspecified in the abstract and the patient's seizure status is unknown.
- Other phenotypes: febrile seizures, intractable epilepsy, suspected Dravet syndrome, intellectual disability, dysmorphic features and childhood-onset epilepsy. Four SCN1A diagnoses came from a childhood epilepsy panel, some de novo (PMID: 30977854). These are largely epilepsy-driven and do not document ASD.
- Variant types: 3 nonsense/LoF (all in the ASHG abstract, unspecified), 3 missense, and 2 unspecified protein-altering. No clear predominance.
- Cohorts: none of UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL are named. Children's Hospital of Philadelphia is the only center identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence for ASD: LIMITED. Only about 3 patients have ASD, with sparse variant detail, confounding epilepsy, and no segregation or case-control data.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse (Scn1a+/- and Scn1a-/-) and zebrafish (scn1lab mutant).
- Mouse phenotypes:
  - Heterozygous knockouts are viable but show spontaneous and hyperthermia-induced seizures, SUDEP and premature mortality (PMID: 32848094; PMID: 35435735).
  - Respiratory dysfunction precedes seizures and correlates with mortality (PMID: 40924494).
  - Behavioral comorbidities relevant to ASD include impaired social behavior and social anxiety, anxiety- and depressive-like behavior, motor deficits and gait abnormality, and memory impairment. Cannabidiol improved these (PMID: 32321192).
  - Homozygous Scn1a-/- neonates show poor welfare and reduced survival, consistent with perinatal lethality (PMID: 32321192).
  - Interneuron dysfunction: parvalbumin interneurons are impaired (PMID: 34843701; PMID: 37812817), as are VIP interneurons (PMID: 31282864) and medial septum PV neurons, a possible contributor to cognitive comorbidity (PMID: 39554146).
  - Mendelian ratios and sample sizes were not reported.
- Zebrafish: scn1lab mutants show ictal-like discharges and electrographic biomarkers resembling human DEE (PMID: 41118267).
- Consistency with human disease: high for Dravet syndrome (haploinsufficiency, seizures, SUDEP, cognitive and social deficits). ASD-like social deficits are modeled but are not specific to ASD.
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE to STRONG for epilepsy/DEE, MODERATE for the ASD-related behavioral phenotype.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Function: Nav1.1 mediates sodium currents that drive fast-spiking GABAergic interneuron firing. Haploinsufficiency reduces inhibitory tone.
- Expression: brain-enriched, with expression in PV interneurons, cortex and medial septum (PMID: 40622667). Expression timing relates to age at seizure onset, and SCN1A shows significant constraint for truncating and missense variation (PMID: 32090326; 504 SCN1A patients).
- Key experimental findings:
  - PV interneurons have reduced sodium current density and reduced GABAergic output, while pyramidal neuron excitability is unchanged. ASO-84 rescued these deficits (PMID: 37812817).
  - ASOs targeting poison exon 20N increased Nav1.1 and reduced seizures and SUDEP (PMID: 32848094).
  - In patient iPSC excitatory networks, phenotypes depended on mutation type, with LoF distinguishable from missense and pore-domain from voltage-sensing-domain mutations. Networks did not segregate by clinical severity (PMID: 37467479).
  - Variants near poison exon 20N increase its inclusion and reduce Nav1.1 protein (ASHG 2022 - Long-read sequencing and profiling of RNA-binding proteins reveals the pathogenic mechanism of aberrant splicing of an SCN1A poison exon in individuals with epilepsy).
  - Some variants cause gain of function (PMID: 35696452; PMID: 30779207). These cluster in inactivation regions, and sodium channel blockers reduced seizures in 81% of 35 gain-of-function patients.
  - Partial loss of function was found for G682V and L1296M/E1308D in SIDS cases (ASHG 2018 - SCN1A mutations in a SIDS cohort).
- None of these studies tested ASD-associated variants directly.
- Strength of biological evidence: STRONG for the role of SCN1A in neuronal excitability and epilepsy, MODERATE for a link to neurodevelopmental and ASD-relevant circuitry.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level for SCN1A as an ASD gene: LIMITED. The evidence for SCN1A as an epilepsy gene is robust but outside the scope of this question.
- Strengths:
  - Strong mechanistic and model organism data for haploinsufficiency.
  - Mouse models show social, anxiety and cognitive deficits.
  - A de novo protein-altering variant was found in an ASD proband screen (PMID: 22495309).
  - Gene constraint is high.
- Gaps:
  - Few ASD patients (about 3) with unspecified variants.
  - ASD cases are confounded by epilepsy or migraine, and one is inherited from a migraine-affected parent (PMID: 22550089).
  - No cohort-level or segregation data, and no case-control enrichment for isolated ASD.
  - No functional testing of ASD-associated variants.
  - Phenotypic and mechanistic heterogeneity (LoF vs gain of function).
- Conclusion: SCN1A is a well-established autosomal dominant neurological disease gene, and ASD occurs as a comorbidity in a minority of patients, particularly in Dravet-type disease. Direct evidence for SCN1A as a gene for ASD without epilepsy is limited to a few cases, and strong support will require larger, well-phenotyped cohorts with variant-level detail.
