WDFY3 EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
WDFY3 (WD repeat and FYVE domain containing 3; also called ALFY) encodes a large scaffold protein that acts in selective autophagy. It targets aggregated proteins, mitochondria and glycogen for degradation. It also has roles in neurodevelopment, with links to Wnt/β-catenin and Notch signaling, and in efferocytosis.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: Seven clinical sources were identified. These are one multicenter case series, one patient cohort with a functional study, two Chinese ASD cohort de novo studies, two ASHG abstracts (a multiplex-family study and an iPSYCH burden study), and one Slovak ASD cohort case.
- Patient numbers: The two largest series contribute about 13 patients (PMID: 31327001) and 32 patients (PMID: 42800802). Overlap between these two is not clear. The Slovak report adds 1 patient (PMID: 40869941). The other sources did not report counts. The total is roughly 45 or more patients, with a possible overlap that cannot be excluded.
- Phenotypes: The most common are autism spectrum disorder, ADHD, neurodevelopmental delay and intellectual disability, and macrocephaly. In the 13-patient series (PMID: 31327001), 9 individuals carried truncating variants and 4 carried missense variants. Variants were mostly de novo, with significant de novo enrichment (P = 0.003). The PH-domain missense proband had microcephaly. The 32-patient LoF cohort (PMID: 42800802) reported autism, ADHD, mild to moderate delay and macrocephaly, with reduced head circumference in one case.
- Variant type: Predominantly LoF (nonsense, frameshift, splice), consistent with haploinsufficiency. Missense variants are a minority and are less well characterized.
- ASD cohort support: Recurrent de novo likely gene-disrupting mutations were found in Chinese ASD cohorts (PMID: 27824329; PMID: 30564305), with macrocephaly noted in the latter. Burden of rare PTVs was seen in cases but not controls in iPSYCH (ASHG 2017 - Rare variants conferring risk for autism and ADHD identified by whole exome sequencing of dried bloodspots). Rare LoF and damaging missense variants were found in female-enriched multiplex families (ASHG 2015 - Autism gene discovery in rare female-enriched multiplex families (FEMFs)). A heterozygous frameshift, c.1080del, was reported in a Slovak ASD child (PMID: 40869941).
- Known cohorts: None of UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL is identified. Cohorts include iPSYCH (Denmark), ACGC (China) and the multicenter German-led series. Individual variant notations are mostly unavailable from the abstracts.
- Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG (for an ASD/neurodevelopmental association). Several independent cohorts show recurrent de novo and LoF variants with statistical enrichment. Detailed variant-level data are limited here.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: Mouse is the main model, with full, heterozygous and conditional knockouts. Drosophila (Bchs overexpression) appears under biological evidence. Human SH-SY5Y knockdown cells were also used.
- Neurological phenotypes: Full knockout causes failure of axonal tract formation and disrupted forebrain commissures (PMID: 27648578). It also causes perinatal lethality with congenital heart defects (PMID: 30428088). Haploinsufficient mice are viable. They show macrocephaly with Wnt dysregulation and deficits in motor coordination and associative learning (PMID: 31327001). Cortical enlargement and neuronal migration defects have also been described (PMID: 25198012), and neurons show impaired neurite branching (PMID: 42800802). Heterozygous mice show presymptomatic neurodegenerative proteomic signatures (PMID: 41935068) and accelerated Huntington's disease pathology on a disease background (PMID: 31899071).
- Non-neuronal phenotypes: Myeloid conditional knockouts show altered osteoclastogenesis, efferocytosis, autoimmunity and arthritis (PMID: 27330028; PMID: 41027899; PMID: 28449847).
- Mendelian ratios and sample sizes: Not reported.
- Consistency with human phenotype: Good. Heterozygous macrocephaly, neurodevelopmental and behavioral deficits parallel the human macrocephaly, delay and ASD phenotype. The microcephaly seen with the PH-domain missense variant suggests a different mechanism for that variant.
- Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: WDFY3 acts in selective macroautophagy of aggregates, mitophagy and glycophagy. Haploinsufficiency reduces mitophagy and synaptic density and causes brain glycogen deposits and cerebellar hypoplasia (PMID: 34187232). It also interacts with Notch1 (PMID: 30428088) and TRAF6 (PMID: 27330028), and it modulates Wnt/β-catenin and GSK3B signaling (PMID: 31327001; PMID: 42800802).
- Expression: It is highly expressed in proliferating cortical neural progenitors in the human embryonic brain (PMID: 31327001). It is expressed in the developing mouse heart, peaking at E12.5 (PMID: 30428088).
- Key findings: WDFY3 regulates neural progenitor divisions and migration (PMID: 25198012). It is cell-autonomously required for laminar positioning of cortical neurons, and its loss alters dendritic arborization and synaptic density (PMID: 35733184). Lithium chloride rescued neurite branching, supporting Wnt/GSK3 involvement (PMID: 42800802). Drosophila Bchs overexpression impaired autophagy and locomotion (PMID: 40000652). A rare WDFY3 SNP delays Huntington's disease onset (PMID: 40945514).
- Strength of biological evidence: STRONG.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: STRONG
- Key strengths: Multiple independent cohorts show recurrent de novo LoF variants, with significant de novo enrichment (P = 0.003). Heterozygous mouse models recapitulate macrocephaly and behavioral deficits, and cell-autonomous cortical development defects have been shown. The molecular mechanism is coherent, involving autophagy and Wnt signaling, with a lithium rescue.
- Key gaps and limitations: Variant-level details, segregation data and detailed phenotypes are largely unavailable from the abstracts. Overlap between cohorts cannot be ruled out. The missense variants are poorly characterized, and the opposing brain-size effect (microcephaly) is based on a single proband. Mendelian ratios and sample sizes are not reported in the mouse studies. Some ASD evidence comes from conference abstracts. No UDN, DDD or similar cohorts are identified.
- Conclusion: WDFY3 is a well-supported autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental and autism gene, with haploinsufficiency as the principal mechanism. Heterozygous LoF variants are associated with ASD, ADHD, developmental delay and typically macrocephaly. The clinical, mouse and mechanistic data are concordant. Missense variant interpretation remains less certain.

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. Le Duc et al. 2019 - Pathogenic WDFY3 variants cause neurodevelopmental disorders and opposing effects on brain size (PMID: 31327001)
2. Lithium chloride in vitro treatment shows potential to rescue the neuronal phenotype caused by WDFY3 haploinsufficiency (PMID: 42800802)
3. Rare Variant Burden and Behavioral Phenotypes in Children with Autism in Slovakia (PMID: 40869941)
4. Wang et al. 2016 - De novo genic mutations among a Chinese autism spectrum disorder cohort (PMID: 27824329)
5. Wang et al. 2018 - Inherited and multiple de novo mutations in autism/developmental delay risk genes suggest a multifactorial model (PMID: 30564305)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. Autophagy linked FYVE (Alfy/WDFY3) is required for establishing neuronal connectivity in the mammalian brain (PMID: 27648578)
2. WD40 repeat and FYVE domain containing 3 is essential for cardiac development (PMID: 30428088)
3. Wdfy3-dependent autophagy impairment recapitulates presymptomatic neurodegenerative signatures in mice (PMID: 41935068)
4. Huntington's Disease Pathogenesis Is Modified In Vivo by Alfy/Wdfy3 and Selective Macroautophagy (PMID: 31899071)
5. Autophagy-linked FYVE containing protein WDFY3 interacts with TRAF6 and modulates RANKL-induced osteoclastogenesis (PMID: 27330028)
6. Macrophage WDFY3 mitigates autoimmunity by enhancing efferocytosis and suppressing T cell activation in mice (PMID: 41027899)
7. Loss of WDFY3 ameliorates severity of serum transfer-induced arthritis independently of autophagy (PMID: 28449847)

### Biological Evidence
1. Orosco et al. 2014 - Loss of Wdfy3 in mice alters cerebral cortical neurogenesis reflecting aspects of the autism pathology (PMID: 25198012)
2. Wdfy3 regulates glycophagy, mitophagy, and synaptic plasticity (PMID: 34187232)
3. WDFY3 mutation alters laminar position and morphology of cortical neurons (PMID: 35733184)
4. Drosophila WDFY3/Bchs overexpression impairs neural function (PMID: 40000652)
5. A rare genetic variant confers resistance to neurodegeneration across multiple neurological disorders by augmenting selective autophagy (PMID: 40945514)
