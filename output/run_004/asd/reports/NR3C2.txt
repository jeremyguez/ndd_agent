NR3C2 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) LOSS-OF-FUNCTION

1. GENE SUMMARY
NR3C2 encodes the mineralocorticoid receptor (MR), a ligand-activated nuclear receptor that binds aldosterone and glucocorticoids. It regulates sodium and water homeostasis in the kidney and mediates glucocorticoid and stress signaling in the brain as part of the HPA axis. Heterozygous loss-of-function variants are the established cause of autosomal dominant renal pseudohypoaldosteronism type 1 (PHA1). A role in neurodevelopment and ASD is only suggested.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: 3 were identified. Only 1 concerns autism. The other 2 describe PHA1 and are not relevant to an ASD phenotype.
- Total patients: about 5 (3 brothers with ASD, 1 infant with PHA1, 1 boy with PHA1).
- Phenotypes:
  - ASD: three affected brothers carry a maternally inherited stop-gain variant, p.Q919X, found by WES with CNV analysis (PMID: 32064789).
  - PHA1: a heterozygous nonsense variant, c.1768C>T p.(R590*), in an infant with neonatal salt-wasting (PMID: 34243750).
  - PHA1: a novel NR3C2 variant, with details not given, in a boy with infant salt-wasting (PMID: 40091330).
- Variant type: predominantly LoF (two nonsense variants, one unspecified).
- Cohorts: none of the patients are from UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL. All came from single-family or single-center reports.
- ASD-specific limitations:
  - The ASD evidence rests on one family.
  - The variant is maternally inherited, and the abstract gives no information on the mother's phenotype, so penetrance is unclear.
  - There are no de novo cases.
  - There is no segregation analysis beyond the three brothers.
  - The PHA1 patients show no neurodevelopmental phenotype, and none is reported.
- Strength of clinical evidence for ASD: LIMITED.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: zebrafish (full nr3c2 knockouts) and mouse (conditional knockouts).
- Zebrafish:
  - Loss of nr3c2 disrupts sleep and social function, overlapping with ASD-related phenotypes (PMID: 31398340). This is the only directly ASD-relevant model finding.
  - Mutants are viable.
  - Mutants have altered skeletal muscle glucose handling (PMID: 36041019) and altered GC-dependent transcription (PMID: 35269817).
  - Mutants show normal rapid stress-induced locomotor responses (PMID: 30588759) and normal light adaptation (PMID: 38565594; PMID: 37720015), unlike nr3c1 and mc2r mutants. This is a negative finding for some behavioral domains.
- Mouse:
  - Conditional knockout converts hippocampal CA2 neurons to a CA1-like molecular identity (PMID: 40640339; PMID: 39651204). This is potentially relevant to social cognition circuitry, but no behavior is reported.
  - Other conditional knockouts are in kidney parietal epithelial cells (PMID: 41005568) and astrocytes in EAE (PMID: 36599367). These are not neurodevelopmental.
- No data are reported on Mendelian ratios, fertility, or heterozygous models. Germline mouse knockout lethality is not covered in these findings.
- Consistency with human phenotype: partial. The zebrafish sleep and social phenotypes and the CA2 identity change are compatible with ASD, but several behavioral assays were normal.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED to MODERATE (LIMITED to MODERATE leaning limited, as the ASD-relevant findings are from one zebrafish study and lack quantitative detail).

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: MR is a nuclear receptor and transcription factor that acts in HPA-axis and glucocorticoid signaling and interacts with GR (PMID: 35269817). It also participates in:
  - metabolic regulation, including insulin-stimulated glucose uptake (PMID: 36041019);
  - innate antiviral responses (PMID: 30367157);
  - astrocyte regulation via NCOR2 and XBP1 (PMID: 36599367).
- Expression: Nr3c2 is expressed in aldosterone-sensitive HSD2 neurons of the brainstem NTS (PMID: 30343334). MR is also expressed in the hippocampus, where it determines CA2 identity (PMID: 40640339).
- Genetic and genomic findings: WGS of multiplex ASD families (2,308 individuals) identified recurrent structural deletions in the NR3C2 promoter (PMID: 31398340). This is non-coding, supports a dosage-sensitivity mechanism, and was not tested directly in other cohorts.
- Other findings (cancer, T2DM, and pharmacogenetic associations such as rs5522 with spironolactone response, PMID: 34569641) are not relevant to ASD.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for plausibility in neurodevelopment, but LIMITED for direct ASD causality.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: LIMITED
- Key strengths:
  - A LoF variant segregates in a family with three affected ASD brothers (PMID: 32064789).
  - Recurrent promoter deletions were found in multiplex ASD families (PMID: 31398340).
  - Zebrafish knockouts show sleep and social phenotypes (PMID: 31398340).
  - MR has a biologically plausible role in brain stress and hippocampal circuitry (PMID: 40640339).
  - Heterozygous LoF is an established disease mechanism for PHA1 (PMID: 34243750).
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - There is only one ASD family, with an inherited variant and no de novo cases.
  - There is no large-cohort statistical support.
  - PHA1 patients with LoF variants lack a reported neurodevelopmental phenotype, which argues against high penetrance for ASD.
  - Several zebrafish behavioral assays were normal.
  - No mouse neurobehavioral or heterozygous phenotypes are reported.
  - No functional validation of Q919X is described.
- Conclusion: NR3C2 is a plausible but unproven candidate gene for ASD with heterozygous LoF variants. Evidence is limited to one multiplex family, a promoter deletion signal, and supportive but incomplete zebrafish data. Additional de novo or well-phenotyped LoF cases, together with enrichment in large cohorts and neurobehavioral studies of heterozygous models, are needed before it can be considered an established ASD gene.
