VEZF1 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: autism spectrum disorder, heterozygous (autosomal dominant) pathogenic variants

1. GENE SUMMARY
VEZF1 (vascular endothelial zinc finger 1, also called DB1) encodes an endothelial-enriched C2H2 zinc finger transcription factor. It regulates vascular and lymphatic development, cardiac gene expression, and chromatin and transcriptional processes such as DNA methylation and RNA polymerase II pausing. The evidence does not show a known neurodevelopmental function.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent case reports or case series: none were found in the clinical evidence, which is empty. No patients with autism spectrum disorder (ASD), intellectual disability, or other neurodevelopmental disorder carrying VEZF1 variants were identified.
- Total patients: 0 in the clinical evidence. The only human genetic data come from the biological evidence. One family with dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) carried a heterozygous nonsense variant, p.(Lys164*), which segregated with autosomal dominant DCM with complete penetrance. The variant was absent from 200 unrelated DCM patients and 400 controls (PMID: 36657711). This is a cardiac phenotype, not ASD.
- Common phenotypes: no neurodevelopmental phenotypes were reported. The only human phenotype is DCM, in a single family.
- Predominant variant type: not applicable for ASD. The single DCM variant is a loss-of-function (LoF) nonsense variant.
- Known cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence for ASD: NONE.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms studied: mouse, including knockout animals and Vezf1-/- embryonic stem cells (ESCs), and zebrafish (knockdown).
- Key phenotypes:
  - Homozygous mouse knockouts show vascular remodeling defects, loss of vascular integrity with localized hemorrhage, and defective endothelial adhesion and tight junctions. Heterozygotes show haploinsufficiency with lymphatic hypervascularization, jugular hemorrhage and edema resembling cystic hygroma (PMID: 15882861).
  - Zebrafish knockdown causes reduced cardiac growth and impaired ventricular contractile response to β-adrenergic stimulation, with Myh7/β-MHC as a key target (PMID: 31911272).
  - Vezf1-/- ESCs show reduced hematoendothelial marker expression (PMID: 36923254). They also show increased Cited2 expression and defective endothelial differentiation and tube formation (PMID: 29794136).
- Lethality, Mendelian ratios, and neurological phenotypes were not reported in the extracted data. No behavioral, social, or brain phenotypes were described.
- Consistency with human phenotype: the cardiac findings in zebrafish are broadly consistent with the DCM family (PMID: 36657711). There is no model evidence relevant to ASD.
- Strength of model organism evidence for ASD: NONE. Evidence for vascular and cardiac roles is MODERATE, but it is not relevant to ASD.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and molecular function: VEZF1 is a DNA-binding transcription factor active in endothelial cells.
  - It activates the endothelin-1 promoter (PMID: 11504723).
  - It co-activates Flt1 with ETV2 (PMID: 36923254).
  - It represses Cited2 (PMID: 29794136).
  - It binds G-quadruplex DNA and thereby regulates VASH1 alternative polyadenylation (PMID: 33231681).
  - It influences genomic DNA methylation through Dnmt3b (PMID: 18676812).
  - It is associated with Pol II pausing and alternative splicing, and interacts with Mrg15 (PMID: 22308494).
  - It acts downstream of RhoB in angiogenesis and lymphangiogenesis (PMID: 24280686).
  - It regulates Myh7 and interacts with TEAD-1 in the heart (PMID: 31911272).
  - O-GlcNAcylation stabilizes it in hepatocellular carcinoma (PMID: 40858565).
- Expression: endothelium-specific expression is emphasized (PMID: 36923254), and expression is decreased in diseased myocardium (PMID: 31911272). No brain or neurodevelopmental expression data were provided.
- Key experimental findings: in the DCM family, the mutant protein failed to transactivate the MYH7 and ET1 promoters, indicating loss of function (PMID: 36657711).
- Strength of biological evidence for ASD: LIMITED. The biology is well characterized for vascular, cardiac, and chromatin functions, but nothing links it to neuronal function or ASD.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT for VEZF1 as an ASD gene with heterozygous pathogenic variants.
- Key strengths: VEZF1 has well-documented transcriptional and chromatin roles. Mouse heterozygous haploinsufficiency phenotypes (PMID: 15882861) and a LoF variant in a DCM family (PMID: 36657711) suggest dosage sensitivity, mainly in cardiovascular and lymphatic systems.
- Key gaps: there are no ASD or neurodevelopmental patients, no de novo variant data, no cohort data, no neurological phenotyping of models, and no brain expression or neuronal functional studies. The only human disease association is a single DCM pedigree.
- Conclusion: the available evidence does not support VEZF1 as an autosomal dominant ASD gene. Its established disease relevance is cardiovascular (DCM, vascular and lymphatic development). Any ASD association would require new clinical data, such as de novo LoF variants in neurodevelopmental cohorts, and neurological functional studies.
