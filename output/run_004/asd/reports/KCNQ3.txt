KCNQ3 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
KCNQ3 encodes the Kv7.3 voltage-gated potassium channel subunit. It co-assembles with KCNQ2 (and KCNQ5) to form the neuronal M-current, which limits repetitive firing and stabilizes membrane potential. Variants are classically linked to self-limited (benign) familial neonatal epilepsy, and more recently to neurodevelopmental disorder (NDD) and autism.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: 10 clinical reports or series were extracted. Of these, 8 provide a specific or characterizable KCNQ3 variant. Two give no variant details in the abstract (PMID: 31171384; PMID: 34653234).
- Total patients: about 28 (11 + 1 + 1 + 4 + 1 + 4 + 3 + 1 + 1 + 1). The 3 patients in the donepezil report (PMID: 39175503) carry R227Q or R230C, recurrent variants that overlap with those in the Bosch series, so some counting overlap is possible.
- Key study: Bosch et al. 2019 (PMID: 31177578) described 11 patients with de novo heterozygous missense variants at R227 and R230 in the S4 voltage sensor. Patch-clamp showed gain-of-function effects. Features were global developmental delay, absent or limited speech, autistic features/ASD, and sleep-activated multifocal epileptiform discharges, sometimes near-continuous. Seizures occurred in only 2/11, so autism and DD can occur without epilepsy.
- Other ASD-relevant reports:
  - Donepezil case series: R227Q and R230C carriers with autism and developmental encephalopathy, with improvement on donepezil (PMID: 39175503).
  - De novo frameshift FS534 (loss of function) with autism traits, ADHD-like features, and ataxia (PMID: 32580997).
  - De novo t(3;8) translocation truncating KCNQ3 in a child with autism (PMID: 23596459). The same paper reported P574S in 3 unrelated autistic individuals. This variant is also a dbSNP polymorphism (rs74582884) reported in epilepsy, which weakens its evidence.
  - Family with R364H: 3 siblings and their mother, with neonatal seizures, DD, ASD, and anxiety (PMID: 35384780).
  - G553R in an ASD patient (PMID: 42589554).
  - De novo variant with ASD, moderate ID, and neonatal-onset then drug-resistant seizures (PMID: 42255468).
  - G244Ser (S4) with self-limited neonatal epilepsy, GDD, and a high-threshold sensory profile (PMID: 42024975).
- Most common phenotypes: developmental delay/ID, autism/autistic features, speech impairment, and epilepsy or epileptiform EEG, which is variable and often absent or mild in the S4 gain-of-function group.
- Variant type: predominantly missense, many de novo and clustered in S4 (R227, R230, G244). One frameshift and one translocation (both loss of function) were also reported.
- Cohorts: no UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL cohorts were identified. Small contributions came from a Japanese undiagnosed NDD panel cohort (PMID: 31171384) and a Spanish Angelman-like cohort (PMID: 34653234).
- Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE. Recurrent de novo S4 variants with a consistent ASD/DD phenotype are supportive. However, the main series has only 11 patients, many of the other reports are single cases, and ASD ascertainment is not uniform. The loss-of-function evidence is sparse and confounded.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse (full knockouts) and zebrafish (morpholino knockdown).
- Mouse:
  - Kcnq3 loss caused sprawling, multifocal hyperactivity across the neonatal neocortex and hypersynchronous hippocampal activity (PMID: 33863780).
  - Kcnq3 knockouts showed less theta-coordinated pyramidal bursting and smaller place fields, which may contribute to cognitive deficits (PMID: 34376649).
  - ImAHP was reduced by about 50% in dentate granule cells but not in CA1 (PMID: 19060215).
  - Retigabine efficacy in CA1 and subiculum depends on KCNQ3 (PMID: 33729829).
  - No data on lethality, Mendelian ratios, or autism-relevant behavioral phenotypes (social, repetitive) were extracted. Heterozygous or knock-in gain-of-function models were not reported.
- Zebrafish: kcnq3 knockdown altered burst discharges and models neonatal seizures (PMID: 21692188). Details are limited.
- Consistency with humans: network hyperexcitability and cognitive/hippocampal changes fit the epilepsy and NDD spectrum. Autism-like behaviors have not been demonstrated, and knockout models reflect loss of function whereas the main ASD/NDD mechanism is gain of function.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Function: KCNQ3 forms M-current heteromers with KCNQ2 and KCNQ5, which controls neuronal excitability. A Kv7.3/Kv7.5 link to ASD was proposed (PMID: 23596459).
- Expression: Widespread in mouse brain, including hippocampus, cortex, thalamus, and cerebellum, with expression increasing through development (PMID: 16513263). KCNQ2, 3, and 5 mRNA is present in zebrafish brain from 2 to 7 dpf (PMID: 21692188).
- Key findings:
  - A patch-clamp study of 24 missense variants found pore variants cause near-complete loss of function and are associated with self-limited neonatal epilepsy. Leftward-shifted V1/2 gain-of-function variants, including G553R, are associated with NDD. Loss-of-function variants were rescued by pynegabine and the gain-of-function variant was corrected by amitriptyline (PMID: 40095209).
  - NDD-associated variants alter S4 movement or S4-to-pore coupling. PUFAs partially restored R227Q function and fully restored R236C function (PMID: 39300259).
  - Other epilepsy-linked variants show a severity gradient: W309R is dominant-negative (PMID: 18425618), and R330L is more severe than R330C (PMID: 25524373).
  - Population genetics: KCNQ3 showed increased burden of gene-disruptive and severe missense variants among 18,836 NDD cases (ASD/DD) versus 45,376 controls (ASHG 2018 - Large-scale targeted sequencing identifies risk genes for autism and developmental delay).
  - Pharmacological modulators such as triclosan (PMID: 35459955) support a tractable VSD target.
  - A proteomic finding of reduced KCNQ3 in suicide cases with mood disorder (PMID: 32120974) is not relevant to ASD.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE to STRONG. The functional genotype-phenotype dichotomy (gain of function with NDD/ASD, loss of function with neonatal epilepsy) and the burden data are well supported.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: MODERATE
- Key strengths: recurrent de novo S4 variants (R227, R230) with concordant gain-of-function electrophysiology and a consistent ASD/DD phenotype (PMID: 31177578; PMID: 40095209; PMID: 39300259); supportive NDD burden data (ASHG 2018); multiple independent ASD reports; pharmacological rescue data.
- Key gaps: small numbers and few large cohorts; the loss-of-function evidence for ASD is weak or confounded (P574S is a common polymorphism, and the translocation and frameshift cases are single reports); variable and incomplete phenotyping; no ASD-relevant animal behavioral or gain-of-function model; the mechanism (gain versus loss of function) is variant-dependent; some reports lack variant details.
- Conclusion: KCNQ3 has moderate evidence as an autosomal dominant ASD/NDD gene, driven mainly by recurrent de novo S4 gain-of-function missense variants that cause developmental delay, autism, and sleep-activated EEG abnormalities, often without seizures. Replication in larger, systematically phenotyped cohorts and development of gain-of-function animal models would be needed to upgrade the evidence to strong. Evidence is weaker for loss-of-function variants as a cause of isolated autism.
