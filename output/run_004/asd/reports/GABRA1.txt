GABRA1 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: GABRA1 AND AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
GABRA1 encodes the α1 subunit of the pentameric GABA-A receptor, the main fast inhibitory ligand-gated chloride channel in the brain. The α1 subunit is a core component of the most abundant synaptic receptor subtype (α1β2γ2). Heterozygous variants are an established cause of epilepsy, ranging from genetic generalized epilepsy to developmental and epileptic encephalopathy. An autism link is far less well documented.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: 5 were extracted, each reporting a single patient. They comprise one case report (PMID: 37434477) and four cohort or diagnostic studies that each mention a GABRA1 finding.
- Total patients: about 5 in the extracted set. Phenotypic spectrum cohorts add more cases, such as 16 probands plus 3 relatives (PMID: 27521439), but these are epilepsy-focused and not counted in the clinical set.
- Phenotypes: epilepsy dominates, including therapy-resistant focal epilepsy, infantile spasms/Lennox-Gastaut syndrome (Epi4K, PMID: 23934111), refractory pediatric epilepsy (PMID: 30185235), and unclassified childhood-onset epilepsy (PMID: 30977854). Only one patient has autism spectrum disorder, with psychiatric comorbidity. That patient carries p.(Arg112Gln), a functionally characterized loss-of-function missense variant, and became seizure-free with improved quality of life on vinpocetine (PMID: 37434477). One cryptogenic cerebral palsy case came from trio exome sequencing (ASHG 2017 - Discovery of cerebral palsy genes through trio-based whole exome sequencing in cryptogenic individuals). It was linked to autism and intellectual disability genes, but the patient's own phenotype was not autism.
- Variant type: mostly missense, or unspecified. De novo status is documented for the Epi4K case.
- Cohorts: none of the patients is identified as coming from UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL. The sources are Epi4K, the Children's Hospital of Philadelphia, a Chinese cohort, and a USA/Australia cerebral palsy cohort.
- Strength of clinical evidence for ASD specifically: LIMITED. It rests on one patient with autism, and epilepsy is the primary phenotype. Clinical evidence for epilepsy is much stronger.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse and zebrafish.
- Mouse:
  - Gabra1+/- Gabrg2+/- double haploinsufficient mice had spontaneous seizures, reduced GABA-A receptor subunit expression, and optic nerve changes (PMID: 37703949). This model cannot isolate the Gabra1 contribution.
  - The Gabra1+/A322D knock-in model of juvenile myoclonic epilepsy showed absence and myoclonic seizures (PMID: 27573707).
  - Full Gabra1 knockout altered the formation of hippocampal GABAergic synapses (PMID: 28643105).
  - Conditional knockout in the dorsal motor nucleus of the vagus affected gut fat absorption, which is unrelated to neurodevelopment (PMID: 39261733).
- Zebrafish:
  - The gabra1 sa43718 nonsense mutant shows light-induced hypermotility (a seizure-like behavior), decreased expression of other GABA-A subunit genes, and abnormal synapse and mitochondrial proteomic changes (PMID: 36747751; PMID: 38908344).
  - The same mutant responds to cannabinoids (PMID: 30949046).
  - Morpholino knockdown caused hypomotility, which human GABRA1 partially rescued but the c.875C>T variant did not (PMID: 32205311).
  - F0 crispants show photosensitivity (PMID: 38474238).
- Lethality, Mendelian ratios and fertility: not reported in the extracted data.
- Consistency: the models are consistent with human epilepsy, showing seizure-like phenotypes and altered GABAergic synapses. No autism-relevant behavioral phenotypes, such as social behavior, were reported.
- Strength: MODERATE for epilepsy, LIMITED for autism.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Function and pathways: GABRA1 is a core subunit of synaptic GABA-A receptors that mediate inhibitory neurotransmission. A network module seeded on GABRA1 was linked to long-term potentiation, the synaptic vesicle cycle and calcium signaling, and was enriched for de novo mutations in neurodevelopmental cohorts (ASHG 2018 - Dissecting heterogeneity of neurodevelopmental disorders using seed centric genetic modules).
- Expression: zebrafish gabra1 is restricted to the nervous system (PMID: 32205311). Human brain and developmental expression data were not provided. GABRA1 expression is regulated by miR-129-2-3p in rat temporal lobe epilepsy models (PMID: 34029007).
- Variant functional studies:
  - Variants have both loss-of-function and gain-of-function effects. Four mutations tested in Xenopus oocytes showed loss of function (PMID: 27521439).
  - P260L/S increased GABA sensitivity 3- to 23-fold (PMID: 39642202).
  - T292S was gain of function and T292I was loss of function, with pharmacological rescue of each (PMID: 35269865).
  - D219N, G251D and P260L are trafficking-deficient, and IRE1/XBP1s activation improved their surface levels (PMID: 41178115).
  - D219N and the P351fsintX insertion reduced cell-surface expression (ASHG 2009 - Novel mutations found by resequencing multiple subunits of GABA A receptor in families with Idiopathic Generalized Epilepsy).
- Non-neurological findings: GABRA1 acts as a target in glioma (PMID: 34001135) and as the binding site for puerarin in gut fat absorption (PMID: 39261733).
- Strength: MODERATE to STRONG for pathogenic mechanisms in epilepsy, but LIMITED for autism specifically.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level for GABRA1 as an autism spectrum disorder gene: LIMITED. For epilepsy, the evidence is strong.
- Strengths:
  - Robust functional characterization of variants, including loss-of-function, gain-of-function and trafficking defects.
  - Concordant seizure phenotypes in mouse and zebrafish.
  - Documented de novo variants in epileptic encephalopathy.
  - One functionally validated loss-of-function patient with epilepsy and ASD (PMID: 37434477).
- Gaps:
  - Only one patient has documented ASD, and the autism phenotype is likely confounded by epilepsy.
  - Many variants were unspecified in the abstracts.
  - No known-cohort membership (UDN, DDD and others) was identified.
  - There were no autism-relevant behavioral assays in models, and no segregation or case-control statistics.
  - The mechanism is mixed (loss and gain of function).
- Conclusion: GABRA1 is a well-established dominant epilepsy gene with strong functional support. Its association with autism spectrum disorder rests on a single case and on plausible neurodevelopmental biology. The association is likely secondary to a primary epilepsy and developmental phenotype, and ASD-specific cohort and model data are needed to support it.
