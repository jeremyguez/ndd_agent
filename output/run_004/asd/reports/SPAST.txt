SPAST AS AN AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER GENE (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT): LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT

1. GENE SUMMARY
SPAST encodes spastin, an AAA ATPase that severs microtubules. It also has roles in ER shaping, endosomal tubule fission, lipid droplet metabolism and axonal transport. Heterozygous variants cause SPG4 hereditary spastic paraplegia (HSP, OMIM #604277), the most common autosomal dominant HSP. Its association with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is a proposed extension of the phenotype, not a well-established feature.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Number of reports: 7 independent reports or series were identified. Five are single-patient reports and one is a 3-patient series.
- Total patients: about 9. The Spanish and Japanese entries are single patients drawn from larger neurodevelopmental cohorts.
- Patients with an explicit ASD phenotype: only 2-3 carry an ASD diagnosis attributable to the patient.
  - A de novo case with two missense variants in cis, with early-onset complex HSP, global developmental delay and severe ASD (PMID: 40870017).
  - A boy with a de novo mosaic missense pair at one SPAST position, with severe spastic paraplegia, ASD and dysmorphism. He also carried a paternally inherited 1q21.1 microduplication, which confounds attribution (PMID: 28778789).
  - A Spanish neurodevelopmental cohort patient with a canonical splice-donor variant, c.1321+1G>A, and ID/GDD and/or ASD. The phenotype was not resolved between ID/GDD and ASD, and parental inheritance was unavailable (PMID: 39457434).
- Other reports are supportive or indirect:
  - ASHG 2011 - A novel nucleotide change in the intron 12 of the SPAST gene might produce an aberrant protein transcript. This was an adult-onset HSP case with c.1493+18G>T, a variant of uncertain significance with mRNA studies pending. Relatives had autism-like behavior, but segregation was not shown.
  - A childhood-onset HSP series of 3 SPAST patients. Autism was a comorbidity in the cohort but was not attributed to individual patients (PMID: 35303589).
  - A Finnish case with a novel SPAST variant and progressive childhood neurological disease, without ASD (PMID: 37563452).
  - A Japanese trio WGS case with p.Arg499His and neurodevelopmental disorder/ID, without ASD specified (ASHG 2017 - Whole genome sequencing of neurodevelopmental disorders in Japanese).
- Common phenotypes: spastic paraparesis or pyramidal signs, developmental delay or ID, and ASD in a minority.
- Variant types: mixed. Missense predominates (including cis, mosaic and recurrent p.Arg499His), plus one canonical splice-site LoF variant and one intronic VUS. The ASD-associated variants are mostly unusual, non-classical genotypes (de novo cis or mosaic missense).
- Known cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none identified. Sources were a Spanish NDD cohort, a Japanese WGS study, a US children's hospital series and a Finnish clinic.
- Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED. Case numbers are very small, the ASD attribution is confounded or non-specific, and there is no case-control or statistical evidence.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse (full knockout, hSPAST-C448Y transgenic, shRNA knockdown, AAV9 therapy), zebrafish (morpholino/gRNA knockdown), and C. elegans and Drosophila (in PMID: 26744324).
- Mouse Spast knockout:
  - Mice are viable. They show axonal swellings but no corticospinal dieback or gait deficit (PMID: 34935948).
  - Males are infertile, with meiotic failure and abnormal manchette and acrosome (PMID: 36971361).
  - Knockdown in neural stem cells reduces proliferation and neuronal differentiation, and alters ciliary microtubule dynamics (PMID: 31150727). This hints at neurodevelopmental relevance.
- hSPAST-C448Y transgenic mice show adult-onset gait defects and corticospinal dying-back, consistent with a gain-of-function component (PMID: 30520996). Gene therapy that silences endogenous Spast and replaces it with human M1/M87 prevents these phenotypes (PMID: 41311060).
- Zebrafish: spast knockdown causes motor axon outgrowth and pathfinding defects (PMID: 20829563; PMID: 39334479) and locomotor defects (PMID: 26744324).
- Consistency with humans: good for the motor axonopathy of HSP. No model has been assessed for ASD-relevant behavior such as social or repetitive behavior. Mendelian ratios and lethality data are largely unreported, with the viable knockout the main exception.
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE for HSP, LIMITED for ASD or neurodevelopment specifically.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways: microtubule severing and dynamics, ER morphology, endosomal/lysosomal trafficking, lipid droplets, axonal transport, and calcium signaling.
- Expression: SPAST is predominantly CNS-expressed and is poorly expressed in skin fibroblasts (PMID: 38272032).
- Key findings:
  - Patient iPSC-derived neurons show reduced store-operated calcium entry and axonal swellings, both rescued by CRISPR correction (PMID: 36103408).
  - The I344K missense variant abolishes ATPase and severing activity but prolongs protein half-life, and impairs neurite growth (PMID: 30006150).
  - A truncating frameshift variant causes toxic accumulation of spastin in a five-generation family (49 members, 9 affected), supporting mechanisms beyond haploinsufficiency (PMID: 38066582).
  - MIT-domain mutants disrupt lysosomal function (PMID: 31787869).
  - Spastin-M1 regulates lipid droplets (PMID: 25875445).
  - Loss of function exacerbates mutant toxicity (PMID: 34935948).
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE to STRONG for SPAST function and HSP pathogenesis. It is LIMITED for a specific link to ASD, as no study connects spastin dysfunction to autism-relevant circuits or behavior.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level for SPAST as an ASD gene: LIMITED.
- Key strengths:
  - SPAST is a well-established HSP gene with robust mechanistic and animal evidence.
  - Two de novo cases with severe ASD plus complex HSP (PMID: 40870017; PMID: 28778789).
  - Neurodevelopmental phenotypes are seen in several sequenced NDD cohorts.
  - Spastin regulates neural stem cell proliferation and differentiation (PMID: 31150727).
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - There are only about 9 patients in total, and ASD is explicitly documented in only 2-3.
  - ASD is confounded by a CNV, mosaicism, cis variant pairs and developmental delay.
  - Most variants lack specific notation in the abstracts.
  - No recurrent ASD-associated variant, no statistical burden evidence and no large-cohort enrichment have been shown.
  - Model organisms lack behavioral or social assays.
  - Most pathogenic SPAST variant carriers have pure or complicated HSP without ASD.
- Conclusion: SPAST is a definitive gene for autosomal dominant HSP, but evidence for it as an ASD gene is limited. It rests on a few atypical de novo cases and an NDD cohort observation. ASD is best regarded as a rare, possibly early-onset or complex expansion of the SPG4 phenotype. Further cohort-level and functional neurodevelopmental studies are required before a gene-level ASD association can be asserted.
