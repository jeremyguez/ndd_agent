IRF2BPL AS AN AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER GENE (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT): EVIDENCE REPORT

1. GENE SUMMARY
IRF2BPL (interferon regulatory factor 2 binding protein-like) is a single-exon gene encoding a nuclear transcriptional regulator with a C-terminal RING-finger domain that has E3 ubiquitin ligase activity. It also carries an N-terminal polyglutamine tract. It represses Wnt signaling and targets proteins such as β-catenin for degradation. Heterozygous, mostly truncating, de novo variants cause NEDAMSS (neurodevelopmental disorder with regression, abnormal movements, loss of speech, and seizures).

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: six entries were found. They comprise a systematic review (PMID: 39031186), three ASHG abstracts, an autism-focused Drosophila screen (PMID: 35294868), and a Chinese trio-WES cohort (PMID: 38764027).
- Patient numbers: the systematic review covers about 33 NEDAMSS cases (1 new plus 32 published) (PMID: 39031186). The 2019 Duke/Baylor abstract describes 7 patients (5 nonsense, 2 missense) (ASHG 2019 - PgmNr 3148). Other entries add about 1 to 2 patients each. Because of overlap, the total unique patient count is roughly 35 or more. Only 2 probands are explicitly described as having autism spectrum disorder (ASD) from the Simons Simplex Collection (PMID: 35294868).
- Common phenotypes: the core phenotype is neurodevelopmental regression, loss of speech, abnormal movements and seizures. Psychiatric features are also reported, including catatonia, affective and psychotic symptoms (PMID: 39031186). Missense carriers show a milder picture of developmental delay, ASD and seizures without regression (ASHG 2019 - PgmNr 3148; ASHG 2019 - PgmNr 1279). A developmental and epileptic encephalopathy (DEE) case has also been reported (ASHG 2018 - The genetic landscape of the developmental and epileptic encephalopathies).
- ASD-specific data: de novo p.Arg86Pro (missense) and an N701Tfs*66 frameshift were found in ASD probands. One further variant, possibly F30L, is ambiguously reported. Both variants behaved as loss-of-function alleles in Drosophila (PMID: 35294868). The Chinese cohort flags IRF2BPL as a SFARI high-confidence/syndromic gene, but the specific variant and phenotype details are lacking (PMID: 38764027).
- Variant type: loss-of-function (nonsense/truncating) predominates, with a minority of missense variants. All are de novo and heterozygous.
- Cohorts: no UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL cohorts are identified. The ASD cases come from the Simons Simplex Collection.
- Strength of clinical evidence for IRF2BPL as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene: STRONG. For ASD specifically it is MODERATE-to-LIMITED. Few ASD cases are individually documented, and ASD is a secondary feature, mainly in missense carriers.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: Drosophila (ortholog pits), zebrafish, and mouse (non-neurological context).
- Drosophila: complete loss of pits is lethal early in development. Neuronal RNAi knockdown causes neurodegeneration, and patient nonsense variants act as severe loss-of-function alleles while missense variants give milder or variable effects (PMID: 30057031; ASHG 2019 - PgmNr 3148). Heterozygous Pits truncations cause progressive motor defects (PMID: 36476864). The ASD-associated variants also behaved as loss-of-function alleles (PMID: 35294868).
- Zebrafish: irf2bpl loss causes neurological defects with increased wnt1 signaling, and Wnt inhibition rescues them (PMID: 35044823). Crispants show reduced body length and electrographic seizure-like discharges (PMID: 38481258).
- Mouse: only an Irf2bpl knockdown in an osteoarthritis model is available (PMID: 35733354). No neurological mouse knockout has been reported. Mendelian ratios, lethality and fertility data are not reported for vertebrates.
- Consistency: the findings fit the human phenotype of neurodegeneration, motor defects and seizures, but ASD-relevant behavioral phenotypes have not been assessed.
- Strength: MODERATE.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Function: IRF2BPL is an E3 ubiquitin ligase and transcriptional regulator. It promotes ubiquitination and degradation of β-catenin, which blunts Wnt signaling (PMID: 29374064; PMID: 35733354). It also degrades FOSL2 (PMID: 40834975), enhances AR activity (PMID: 34585037), and activates IGFBP2 in cancer (PMID: 41413713).
- Expression: pits is broadly expressed, including in the nervous system (PMID: 30057031). Postmortem brain from a p.Arg188Ter patient showed DRPLA-like pathology with polyQ inclusions, and decreased brain mRNA (PMID: 39224955).
- Key findings:
  - Loss of IRF2BPL or Pits raises Wnt/wingless signaling, causes axonal loss, and Wnt inhibition is neuroprotective. Patient astrocytes show increased WNT1, and IRF2BPL interacts with CKIα (PMID: 35044823).
  - Truncated variants mislocalize and sequester wild-type protein to the cytoplasm, suggesting a dominant-negative mechanism. Patient astrocytes show mitochondrial dysfunction and impaired neuronal support, which CuATSM rescues (PMID: 36476864).
  - A truncating variant altered localization and reduced ubiquitinated proteins (PMID: 33278788).
- Strength: MODERATE to STRONG for neurological disease mechanism. The link to ASD-specific pathways is indirect.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: MODERATE for ASD specifically. The evidence is STRONG for a heterozygous neurodevelopmental disorder (NEDAMSS), of which ASD is a recognized but secondary feature.
- Strengths:
  - About 33 or more patients with recurrent de novo variants.
  - Concordant functional data across flies, zebrafish and patient cells.
  - A defined mechanism involving Wnt dysregulation and dominant-negative truncations.
  - ASD-ascertained de novo variants shown to be damaging in functional assays (PMID: 35294868).
- Gaps and limitations:
  - Few ASD cases are individually documented, and some variant descriptions are ambiguous.
  - Many sources are conference abstracts with no variant-level detail.
  - There is no mammalian neurological model.
  - ASD behavioral phenotyping is absent in models.
  - Cohort overlap is possible, and no major consortium cohorts are identified.
- Conclusion: IRF2BPL is a well-established cause of a severe, regressive neurodevelopmental disorder through de novo heterozygous loss-of-function and dominant-negative variants. ASD occurs, particularly with missense variants or in de novo variants from ASD cohorts, and is supported by functional data. As an ASD-associated gene it is supported at a moderate level, and further ASD-specific cases and mammalian models would strengthen the association.
