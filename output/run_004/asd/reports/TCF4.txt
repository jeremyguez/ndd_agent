TCF4 EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER / NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) PATHOGENIC VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
TCF4 (transcription factor 4, also called E2-2 or ITF2) encodes a class I basic helix-loop-helix (E-protein) transcription factor. It is highly expressed in the developing nervous system and regulates neurogenesis, neuronal differentiation and connectivity. It also functions in immune cell lineage specification and, through its HMG-box interactions, in Wnt/β-catenin signaling. Heterozygous loss of function causes Pitt-Hopkins syndrome (PTHS), a syndromic neurodevelopmental disorder with intellectual disability and frequent autistic features.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: 10 case reports or case series were found (8 ASHG abstracts and 2 recent publications, plus one Spanish panel study).
- Total patients: about 63 (42 + 9 + 4 + 2 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1). Most come from two series: the Italian PTHS cohort (42 of 61 subjects with a molecular diagnosis) (ASHG 2018 - Diagnostic flow-chart to improve the detection rate and to uncover the genetic heterogeneity in Pitt-Hopkins syndrome: Experience on 61 subjects) and the Spanish Rett-like panel study of 9 patients (PMID: 31105003).
- Most common phenotypes:
  - Intellectual disability and developmental delay, often severe, with severe speech impairment.
  - Hypotonia and motor incoordination.
  - Breathing abnormalities. These were reported in the Italian series and in a Japanese patient with p.R580W (ASHG 2017 - Whole genome sequencing of neurodevelopmental disorders in Japanese), but were absent in the Colombian patient.
  - Typical facial gestalt, postnatal microcephaly, seizures and movement disorder.
  - Autism spectrum disorder in two reports: a de novo nonsense variant, c.955C>T p.Gln319*, in a patient with ASD and severe ID (PMID: 42427955), and a multiexonic deletion of exons 9-15 (PMID: 42293335).
  - A Rett-like presentation in MECP2-negative patients, including a de novo splice variant found by trio exome sequencing (ASHG 2014 - Identifying novel genes that cause Rett syndrome by trio-based exome sequencing of MECP2-negative patients) and the 9-patient panel cohort (PMID: 31105003).
- Variant types: predominantly loss of function, namely frameshift (c.1191_1194del, ASHG 2018 - An 8-year-old girl with Pitt-Hopkins syndrome), nonsense, splice (c.1486+3A>C, shown de novo with aberrant transcripts on RNA studies, ASHG 2016 - Splice site mutation of TCF4 gene), and whole-gene or partial deletions (18q21 deletions, partial TCF4 deletions, balanced translocations). Missense is rare (p.R580W, a recurrent PTHS variant). Deletions of only the 5' exons (2 patients) gave milder, non-classical phenotypes: high-functioning ASD in one and mild ID in the other (ASHG 2014 - Deletion of the 5' exons of the TCF4 gene in patients without classical Pitt-Hopkins syndrome). This is consistent with isoform-specific effects.
- Cohorts: none of the patients are reported from UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL. Most reports are single-center, from Italy, Spain, Germany, Colombia, Japan, China and the USA.
- Caveats: several variants are not specified in the abstracts. The four half-siblings with 18q21.1 deletions had a confounding fetal alcohol syndrome diagnosis (ASHG 2013 - Fetal Alcohol Syndrome and Pitt-Hopkins Syndrome in four maternal half siblings).
- Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG for a neurodevelopmental disorder (PTHS) with heterozygous LoF, with ASD as a recurrent feature. The evidence is MODERATE for isolated or primary ASD specifically, because ASD is documented in only a few individuals here and mostly within the syndromic context.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse (5 studies) and Xenopus (1 study, in which tcf-4 is only a downstream readout of CD44 knockdown and so is not informative for TCF4 loss).
- Key mouse phenotypes:
  - A heterozygous Tcf4 knockout altered cortical progenitor dynamics, neuronal layering and interneuron generation (an excitation/inhibition imbalance), with sexual dimorphism and stronger effects in males (PMID: 38636614).
  - A conditional PTHS model showed anxiety-like behavior, altered activity, impaired innate behaviors, memory deficits, EEG abnormalities and altered TCF4-target gene expression. Postnatal neuronal restoration of Tcf4 rescued many of these phenotypes (PMID: 35535852).
  - Conditional Tcf4 loss in Satb2+ projection neurons impaired commissure formation, acting with SOX11 (PMID: 34184026).
  - Thalamocortical Tcf4 knockout impaired late memory stabilization (PMID: 41299170).
  - Conditional deletion affected the DC2A dendritic cell lineage (PMID: 40702338).
- Lethality, Mendelian ratios and sample sizes were not reported in the extracted data.
- Consistency with human disease: good. The phenotypes include cognitive and memory deficits, anxiety, EEG abnormalities, altered cortical development and haploinsufficiency as the mechanism. Reversibility after postnatal restoration is a notable finding.
- Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG (mouse only, no other vertebrate or invertebrate LoF model in the data provided).

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function:
  - TCF4 is an E-protein bHLH transcription factor.
  - Class I E-proteins also function in dendritic cell and erythroid lineage specification.
  - Its HMG-box domain mediates the interactions listed in the next bullet; the "TCF4" described in these papers is the HMG-box β-catenin partner. Much of the literature (cancer, Wnt) concerns this β-catenin/TCF function rather than neurodevelopment (see limitations below).
- Interactions and isoforms:
  - ZIC2 binds the TCF4 HMG box and inhibits β-catenin·TCF4 activity (PMID: 21908606).
  - UHRF2 SUMOylates and stabilizes TCF4 (PMID: 32372448).
  - Isoform-specific function is documented: long and short isoforms (PMID: 27986456), and C-clamp-containing Tcf4E variants that determine target selection (PMID: 22859735). This is relevant to the milder phenotype of 5' exon deletions.
- Expression: isoform-specific expression is documented in dendritic cells (PMID: 27986456). Brain expression is supported mainly indirectly by the mouse neurodevelopmental studies (PMID: 38636614, PMID: 34184026).
- Other experimental findings: many studies address TCF4 in cancer (hepatocellular carcinoma, PMID: 15040893 and PMID: 32669971; melanoma, PMID: 21599871; breast and intestinal tumors) and hematopoiesis (PMID: 30315825). These show broad transcriptional regulatory roles but are largely not relevant to ASD.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE. Molecular function is well established, but neuronal-specific mechanistic data are limited in this set, and the transcriptional targets relevant to ASD are only partly defined (PMID: 35535852 shows altered TCF4-regulated gene expression).

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: STRONG for TCF4 haploinsufficiency as a cause of a neurodevelopmental disorder (Pitt-Hopkins syndrome) with autistic features. For ASD as a primary phenotype it is MODERATE.
- Key strengths:
  - About 63 patients with predominantly LoF variants, including confirmed de novo variants (c.955C>T, c.1486+3A>C).
  - Deletions and translocations support haploinsufficiency.
  - Splice variants have RNA-level functional confirmation.
  - Mouse models replicate neurodevelopmental, behavioral and EEG phenotypes, and postnatal rescue supports the mechanism.
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - Many clinical entries are conference abstracts with unspecified variants.
  - ASD is described in only a few individuals, mostly in a syndromic context.
  - No data are available from large cohorts (DDD, UDN and others).
  - Mouse studies lack reported lethality or Mendelian ratio data.
  - Many biological citations are non-neuronal (cancer, immune).
  - 5' exon deletions show a milder, non-classical phenotype, including high-functioning ASD without dysmorphism, so genotype-phenotype correlations remain incompletely defined.
- Conclusion: TCF4 is a well-established autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disease gene, with heterozygous LoF variants and deletions causing Pitt-Hopkins syndrome, in which ASD and severe ID are common features. Consistent mouse haploinsufficiency models support this. TCF4 is a strongly supported ASD-associated gene, although ASD usually occurs as part of a syndromic presentation.
