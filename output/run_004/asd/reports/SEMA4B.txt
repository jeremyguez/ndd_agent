SEMA4B LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT MODEL)

1. GENE SUMMARY
SEMA4B encodes semaphorin 4B, a class 4 transmembrane semaphorin that signals through Plexin-B2 and acts as both ligand and receptor. In the literature it is mainly implicated in astrocyte and microglia responses to brain injury, immune regulation (basophils, T cells) and cancer cell growth and invasion. No role in human neurodevelopmental disease has been established.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent case reports or case series: 0. The clinical evidence search returned nothing.
- Total patients: 0.
- Phenotypes: none reported.
- Variant type: no patient variants are described, so no LoF, missense or other pattern can be assessed.
- Cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE. There is no human genetic evidence linking heterozygous SEMA4B variants to autism spectrum disorder or any other neurodevelopmental condition.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organism: mouse only. Models include full Sema4B knockouts, Sema4B/Plexin-B2 double heterozygotes, a microglia-specific Plexin-B2 conditional knockout, and hippocampal Sema4B knockdown.
- Viability: Sema4B knockout mice are viable (PMID: 36531136; PMID: 38781210; PMID: 21270411). No Mendelian ratio deviations, infertility data or sample sizes were reported.
- Key phenotypes:
  - After cortical injury, knockouts show reduced neuronal death, more surviving neurons, altered astrocyte activation and attenuated cytokine expression (PMID: 36531136; PMID: 38781210).
  - Microglia/macrophage reactivity is reduced after injury, and microglia-specific Plexin-B2 knockout and double heterozygotes phenocopy this (PMID: 38781210).
  - Knockouts have markedly increased serum IgE and enhanced basophil-mediated memory IgE production, with normal lymphocyte and dendritic cell function (PMID: 21270411).
  - Reducing Sema4B in the hippocampus reversed the enhanced hippocampus-dependent memory of forebrain YTHDF2 conditional knockout mice (PMID: 40959883). This suggests Sema4B influences memory and protein synthesis, but it was tested only as a downstream modifier of YTHDF2 loss.
- Consistency with human phenotype: not assessable, because no human phenotype exists. No baseline autism-relevant behavioral phenotypes (social behavior, repetitive behavior, communication) were reported in the knockouts, and no behavioral or neurodevelopmental phenotype was described in heterozygous animals.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED. The mice are viable with injury-response, immune and memory-modifier phenotypes, but there is no evidence of an autism-like or developmental neurological phenotype.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and molecular function:
  - Sema4B is a ligand for Plexin-B2, and astrocytic Sema4B enhances microglia/macrophage reactivity after injury (PMID: 38781210).
  - Sema4B also acts as an astrocyte receptor. Its function depends on phosphorylation of the intracellular domain at Ser825, and the ectodomain behaves as an antagonist (PMID: 26464987).
  - It is a target of YTHDF2-mediated m6A mRNA decay in hippocampal neurons (PMID: 40959883).
  - It is an ADAM17-shed adipokine that represses adipogenesis and thermogenesis (PMID: 37121509).
  - It interacts with CLCP1 and promotes its degradation (PMID: 17213806).
  - It suppresses PI3K/Akt signaling in lung cancer (PMID: 25746385).
  - It is a direct HIF-1 target repressed by hypoxia (PMID: 24474252).
- Expression:
  - It is mostly expressed by astrocytes in cortex (PMID: 36531136) and is expressed in hippocampal neurons as an m6A target (PMID: 40959883).
  - It is also expressed in T and B cells (PMID: 21270411).
  - It is elevated in rheumatoid arthritis synovium (PMID: 40598553).
- Key findings:
  - Neuronal protection in knockouts is glia-dependent and mediated by a secreted factor. Sema4B applied directly to neurons has no effect (PMID: 36531136).
  - Sema4B/Plexin-B2 signaling shapes neuroinflammatory responses (PMID: 38781210).
  - Sema4B reduction reverses YTHDF2-loss-driven memory enhancement (PMID: 40959883).
  - Recombinant Sema4B inhibits basophil IL-4 and IL-6 production (PMID: 21270411).
- Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED to MODERATE for general function, but LIMITED for relevance to autism. The brain data concern injury, glial and neuroinflammatory biology and memory modulation, not neurodevelopment or synaptic mechanisms linked to autism. The semaphorin–plexin family is generally relevant to neural development, but that is indirect.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT for SEMA4B as an autism spectrum disorder gene with heterozygous pathogenic variants.
- Key strengths: the gene is well characterized biochemically, with a defined Sema4B–Plexin-B2 axis. It is expressed in brain, mainly in astrocytes. Knockout mice are viable and show CNS-relevant phenotypes in glial and microglial responses and memory modulation.
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - There are no human patients, variants, cohort data or de novo/segregation evidence.
  - Mouse studies do not examine autism-relevant behavior or neurodevelopment, and heterozygous knockout phenotypes at baseline are not described.
  - The existing brain phenotypes concern injury and neuroinflammation, not developmental disease.
  - The YTHDF2 study shows Sema4B knockdown reducing memory, which is not a clear neurodevelopmental disease model.
  - Other literature concerns cancer, immunity, adipose biology and arthritis.
- Conclusion: There is currently no clinical or genetic support for SEMA4B as an autosomal dominant autism gene. Mouse and biological data show roles in astrocyte–microglia signaling and hippocampal memory regulation but do not implicate it in autism pathogenesis. SEMA4B should be regarded as a candidate lacking disease-gene evidence, and would need human genetic data, such as de novo or LoF variants in neurodevelopmental cohorts, plus behavioral phenotyping of mutant mice, before it could be considered.
