CTCF LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) PATHOGENIC VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
CTCF encodes CCCTC-binding factor, a zinc-finger DNA-binding protein. It is the main architectural protein of vertebrate genomes. Together with cohesin, it anchors chromatin loops, establishes TAD boundaries, and acts as an enhancer blocker/insulator that regulates gene expression (PMID: 28525758).

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: Two entries were found, both from the same Eichler-lab targeted-sequencing effort. One is a published paper (PMID: 33004838) and the other is an earlier conference abstract of the same project (ASHG 2018 - Large-scale targeted sequencing identifies risk genes for autism and developmental delay). They are not truly independent. No individual case reports or detailed case series were extracted.
- Patient numbers: Not specified. CTCF-specific counts, individual variants and inheritance details are not given in the available abstracts.
- Phenotypes: Neurodevelopmental disorder, autism spectrum disorder and developmental delay. The 2020 paper reports that genotype-phenotype correlations were presented for CTCF, but the details are not in the abstract (PMID: 33004838).
- Variant type: Predominantly gene-disruptive/LoF. The ASHG 2018 abstract also mentions severe missense variants (CADD>30) absent from ExAC controls, with no CTCF-specific detail.
- Cohorts: Neither study is from UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL. Both come from a multi-center international collaboration (University of Washington, 17 centers).
- Statistical support: CTCF was among genes with a significant burden of ultra-rare (MAF<0.01%) gene-disruptive variants in NDD cases versus ExAC controls (FDR 5%), with de novo excess (PMID: 33004838).
- Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE. The statistical burden evidence is genuine and supports CTCF as an NDD/ASD risk gene. However, no case-level data were captured, and the evidence is not specific to ASD without ID or developmental delay.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: Mouse and zebrafish.
- Zebrafish: Full CTCF knockout causes loss of TADs, altered expression of thousands of genes including developmental regulators, and disrupted enhancer-promoter interactions (PMID: 34518536). Complete CTCF abrogation is lethal, while CTCF depletion had little effect on zygotic genome activation (PMID: 29158440).
- Mouse, neurological: Purkinje cell-specific conditional knockout produced progressive motor dysfunction, dendritic self-avoidance defects, giant lamellar bodies, mitochondrial abnormalities and near-complete Purkinje cell loss. The mice were viable (PMID: 36447271). In the MGP/IMPC screen, Ctcf knockouts were among known ID genes with brain morphology abnormalities (ASHG 2015 - Deciphering the genetic basis of intellectual disability using 1000 knockouts).
- Mouse, other tissues: Endothelial conditional knockout is embryonically lethal, with defective yolk sac and placental vasculature (PMID: 29610276). Other conditional knockouts cause kidney failure with hypotension (PMID: 32619009) and hair cell degeneration with profound hearing loss (PMID: 30107916).
- Limitations: Mendelian ratios, sample sizes, fertility data and statistics were not reported. The studies use homozygous or conditional complete loss, not heterozygous models, and no behavioral or social phenotypes relevant to autism were reported.
- Consistency with human phenotype: Partial. The data show an essential role in neural tissue and brain morphology abnormalities, but no ASD-like behavioral phenotypes.
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE. The data show essentiality and neurological involvement, but they are not specific to ASD or to haploinsufficiency.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: CTCF mediates chromatin looping and TAD insulation in a dose-dependent manner. Compartments remain segregated after CTCF loss (PMID: 28525758). Its binding is promoted by R-loops and G-quadruplexes (PMID: 37552993; PMID: 42201978) and is sensitive to DNA methylation (PMID: 26700815).
- Chromatin and transcription: Acute CTCF degradation rewires chromatin accessibility and alters transcription (PMID: 34429148).
- Neurodevelopmental relevance: CTCF-RBP interactions increase on differentiation of embryonic stem cells to neural stem cells, and CTCF degradation shows it silences neuronal genes and prevents premature activation (PMID: 40921733). CTCF is highly expressed in mature cerebellar Purkinje cells (PMID: 36447271).
- Dosage sensitivity: Ctcf+/- MEFs support haploinsufficiency effects on proliferation, and CTCF is essential for somatic cell viability (PMID: 30513694).
- Other contexts: CTCF acts in corneal epithelial differentiation (PMID: 27583466) and in Treg function via cGAS (PMID: 40966077). These show broad roles but are not neurodevelopment-specific.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE to STRONG for a plausible mechanism in neurodevelopment through chromatin architecture and dosage sensitivity. It is not ASD-specific.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: MODERATE
- Strengths: A significant ultra-rare LoF burden with de novo excess in a large NDD/ASD cohort (PMID: 33004838). Mechanistic evidence is consistent with haploinsufficiency (PMID: 30513694). Mouse models show essentiality and neural phenotypes (PMID: 36447271).
- Gaps: No case-level phenotype, variant or inheritance data were captured, and the two clinical entries represent a single dataset. There are no extracted reports of the well-described CTCF-related neurodevelopmental syndrome with ID. ASD-specific enrichment, behavioral models, and heterozygous animal models are lacking. Statistical measures and sample sizes are mostly missing.
- Conclusion: CTCF is supported as a dominant, likely haploinsufficient neurodevelopmental disorder gene, with ASD as one of the observed phenotypes alongside developmental delay. The evidence rests on statistical burden data and strong functional biology, without detailed case data. Further curation of clinical case series is needed to establish a firm ASD-specific gene-disease relationship.
