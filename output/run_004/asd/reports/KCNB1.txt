KCNB1 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) PATHOGENIC VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
KCNB1 encodes Kv2.1, the pore-forming alpha subunit of a voltage-gated delayed-rectifier potassium channel. It is abundant in cortical and hippocampal neurons and regulates neuronal excitability and repetitive firing (PMID: 26477325). Kv2.1 also has a non-conducting structural role, forming ER-plasma membrane junctions (PMID: 25908859).

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Number of reports: 10 clinical reports or series were extracted. Two are large cohorts and one is a functional survey. The two French reports (PMID: 32954514, PMID: 34915430) probably overlap in patients.
- Approximate patients: about 93 summed across entries (36 + 25 + 3 + 1 + 2 + 3 + 1 + 4 + 17 + 1). Overlap, especially between the French cohorts and the functional survey, means the true number of unique individuals is lower, perhaps 60-80.
- Common phenotypes: developmental and epileptic encephalopathy with infantile-onset seizures, developmental delay, and moderate to profound intellectual disability. Autism or autistic features were reported in several studies:
  - The 36-patient international cohort reported autistic features (PMID: 32954514).
  - The 25-patient French series reported pathological adaptive behavior, attention problems and autism spectrum disorder risk (PMID: 34915430).
  - A case with centrotemporal spikes had autism, intellectual disability and epilepsy (c.595A>T, p.Ile199Phe) (PMID: 29264390).
  - A family with c.342C>A (p.Ser114Arg) had autism spectrum disorder, developmental delay, absence seizures and hypotonia (PMID: 39135902). The variant was inherited from a father with autistic features and was classified as a VUS.
  - Other reports described autism spectrum disorder and ADHD (PMID: 34877642), autism features with CSWS (PMID: 38769754), and Rett-like presentations (PMID: 29322350, PMID: 37408271).
- Variant types: predominantly missense, with de novo origin typical. Examples are p.Arg306Cys (PMID: 37408271) and the de novo missense variants in PMID: 25164438. Truncating variants appear in the 36-patient cohort and are associated with a milder epilepsy course but still with intellectual disability and autistic features (PMID: 32954514). Germline mosaicism was described in one family (PMID: 34877642).
- Known cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none identified. One case was found by clinical WES after negative MECP2 testing (PMID: 29322350).
- Limitations: autism is usually a secondary feature of a DEE/ID syndrome, not an isolated phenotype. Most abstracts did not list individual variants, and the autism-specific data are limited.
- Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG for KCNB1 as a neurodevelopmental disorder/DEE gene, with autism as a recurrent comorbid feature. For autism specifically, it is MODERATE.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse (knockout, R312H knock-in, conditional knockout) and zebrafish (knockout and mutants). A G379R knock-in mouse is described under biological evidence (PMID: 33132203).
- Key phenotypes:
  - Zebrafish kcnb1+/- and kcnb1-/- showed impaired swimming, spontaneous and provoked epileptiform activity, PTZ hypersensitivity and GABA dysregulation. Human Kv2.1 WT DNA rescued the phenotypes (PMID: 40332468). An earlier report found PTZ sensitivity in heterozygous and homozygous fish (ASHG 2018 - Using zebrafish as a tool to model patient mutations of KCNB1 and its role in epilepsy).
  - Mouse null and R312H knock-in animals showed impaired neuronal migration, disrupted morphology and connectivity, seizures, anxiety and compulsive behavior (PMID: 36207442). The compulsive behavior is relevant to autism-like features.
  - G379R knock-in mice showed hyperactivity, repetitive behaviors, spontaneous seizures and abnormal EEG (PMID: 33132203).
  - Other phenotypes were non-neurological or developmental: hypothalamic and leptin signaling defects in null mice (PMID: 39436109), retinal ERG abnormalities (PMID: 30820446), and inner ear and brain ventricle defects in zebrafish (PMID: 33316259, PMID: 27729411).
- Lethality and Mendelian ratios: null mice and fish are viable. No Mendelian ratio deviations were reported.
- Consistency with human phenotype: good. Seizures, hyperactivity, repetitive and compulsive behavior and anxiety parallel the human epilepsy and autistic features. Heterozygous zebrafish and the dominant-negative knock-in show phenotypes in line with autosomal dominant disease.
- Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG for epilepsy and behavioral phenotypes, MODERATE for autism-specific modeling.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Function and pathways: Kv2.1 is the major delayed rectifier in pyramidal neurons. Its conducting and non-conducting roles include ER-PM junction formation, which supports Ca2+ signaling and membrane trafficking (PMID: 25908859). Oxidation-induced oligomerization at Cys73 promotes apoptosis (PMID: 23275378, PMID: 22442077). Integrin and leptin receptor complexes have also been described (PMID: 36207442, PMID: 39436109).
- Expression: abundant in cortex and hippocampus (PMID: 22442077). In zebrafish it is expressed in neurons, oligodendrocytes and microglia (PMID: 40332468). Stress increases its translation in lateral habenula neurons (PMID: 38193032).
- Key functional findings:
  - R306C and G401R inhibited repetitive firing. R306C disrupted voltage sensing, and G401R was dominant-negative (PMID: 26477325).
  - Variants caused loss of ion selectivity with a gain of depolarizing cation conductance (PMID: 25164438; ASHG 2014 - De Novo KCNB1 Mutations in Epileptic Encephalopathy).
  - p.Ile199Phe caused partial loss of function (PMID: 29264390).
  - p.Ser114Arg slowed activation, deactivation and inactivation (PMID: 39135902).
  - The P17T gain-of-function variant caused developmental delay and speech apraxia without seizures (PMID: 36618935).
  - A survey of 17 variants showed diverse functional defects (PMID: 31600826).
  - G379R showed a dominant-negative effect in cells and in mice (PMID: 33132203).
- Mechanisms are heterogeneous: dominant-negative, loss-of-function and gain-of-function effects all occur. The variable mechanism may explain some of the phenotypic variability.
- Strength of biological evidence: STRONG.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: STRONG for KCNB1 as a dominant neurodevelopmental disorder/DEE gene. For autism specifically, the evidence is MODERATE, with autism as a frequent associated feature.
- Key strengths:
  - Many de novo heterozygous cases, supported by recurrent variants such as p.Arg306Cys.
  - Extensive functional electrophysiology showing dominant-negative, loss-of-function and gain-of-function effects.
  - Concordant mouse and zebrafish models, including heterozygous phenotypes with seizures and repetitive or compulsive behaviors.
  - Genotype-phenotype observations (missense variants cause more severe epilepsy than truncating variants) (PMID: 32954514).
- Key gaps:
  - Autism is rarely the isolated phenotype and is confounded by intellectual disability and epilepsy.
  - Individual variants are often not specified, and cohort overlap is likely.
  - No UDN, DDD or similar cohort data were identified.
  - Some autism-associated variants are VUS or inherited from affected or mosaic parents (PMID: 39135902, PMID: 34877642).
  - Formal autism-specific model phenotyping (for example social behavior) is limited.
- Conclusion: KCNB1 is a well-established autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental gene in which heterozygous variants cause DEE and intellectual disability, with autism or autistic features frequently reported. Clinical, functional and animal data are concordant. Support for KCNB1 as an autism gene is therefore moderate to strong, but the autism is best described as part of a broader neurodevelopmental syndrome.
