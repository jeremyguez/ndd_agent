SARM1 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) PATHOGENIC VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
SARM1 (sterile alpha and TIR motif-containing 1) is an NAD+-hydrolase (NADase) that acts as the central executioner of programmed axon degeneration (Wallerian-like degeneration). When activated by injury or by metabolic stress, it depletes NAD+ and ATP and generates cADPR, which leads to axon self-destruction. It also has roles in neuroinflammation and in cell death pathways.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent case reports or case series: 0. No clinical evidence was extracted.
- Total patients: none reported.
- Phenotypes: no patient phenotypes, including autism spectrum disorder or neurodevelopmental disorder, are documented.
- Variant type: not applicable. The only human-variant data are somatic SARM1 variants in focal cortical dysplasia (PMID: 41160700), which are not germline, not heterozygous constitutional, and not linked to autism.
- Cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse (the majority of studies, mostly full knockout, plus one conditional knockout study) and zebrafish (a sarm1-null line, in the biological evidence section).
- Viability and development: Sarm1 knockout mice are viable in every study reviewed. No Mendelian ratio deviations, infertility or developmental lethality were reported, though ratios were not quantified. Conditional deletion in Nestin+ or GFAP+ cells gave normal spinal cord development and motor function (PMID: 33754056). Zebrafish lacking Sarm1 had normal sensorimotor transformations (PMID: 33688624).
- Key phenotypes: the studies mainly test a protective role of Sarm1 loss in neurodegeneration and injury models.
  - Loss was protective in TDP-43 Q331K ALS-FTD mice (PMID: 31661035), in rotenone-induced retinal ganglion cell degeneration (PMID: 35163535), in paclitaxel neuropathy (PMID: 33964142), and in Nmnat2-dependent congenital neuropathy (PMID: 36287209).
  - Loss was not protective in the mSOD1 ALS model (PMID: 35863521) or in a CMT1A model (PMID: 35137510).
  - Loss delayed peripheral nerve regeneration, with a hostile nerve microenvironment (PMID: 41061042).
  - Passenger-mutation confounding, such as 129-derived Xaf1 variation, may explain some phenotypes of older knockout lines (PMID: 32268088).
- Consistency with human phenotype: no autism-relevant or social/behavioural phenotypes were assessed, and no neurodevelopmental phenotype was described. Normal development of knockouts argues against a loss-of-function neurodevelopmental mechanism. Heterozygous loss-of-function models were not studied for neurodevelopment, apart from gene-dosage effects in paclitaxel neuropathy.
- Strength of model organism evidence for ASD: NONE to LIMITED. The mouse data are extensive but not relevant to autism.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: the SARM1 TIR domain has NADase activity that triggers an ordered cascade of ATP loss, mitochondrial depolarization, calcium influx, phosphatidylserine exposure and axon self-destruction (PMID: 34779400). SARM1 also acts in neuronal parthanatos downstream of PARP1 (PMID: 41997149). It contributes to NF-κB-mediated neuroinflammation (PMID: 33754056) and to macrophage inflammatory responses, where METTL1-mediated m7G modification stabilizes Sarm1 mRNA (PMID: 40911698). It also functions in cilia disassembly (F2R, SARM1, ryanodine receptors, calcium, RhoA), and somatic SARM1 variants from focal cortical dysplasia potentiate cilia loss (PMID: 41160700).
- Expression: SARM1 is expressed in neurons and astrocytes, and is upregulated after spinal cord injury (PMID: 33754056). It is reduced in hippocampal neurons of APP/PS1 mice, and CNS conditional knockout delayed cognitive decline and reduced Aβ deposition in those mice (PMID: 37307837). No developmental or autism-related expression data were provided.
- Key experiments: SARM1 inhibition, using small molecules or AAV-delivered dominant-negative constructs, protects axons (PMID: 33964142; PMID: 30642945). TRPV1 binds the SARM1 TIR domain (PMID: 36669703), a finding relevant to liver rather than brain.
- Relevance to ASD: the biology concerns axon degeneration, neuroinflammation and cell death. The cilia and focal cortical dysplasia link is the only possible neurodevelopmental connection, but it involves somatic variants and no autism data.
- Strength of biological evidence for SARM1 as an ASD gene: LIMITED. The molecular function is well characterized, but its relevance to autism is indirect and speculative.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT.
- Key strengths: SARM1 is very well characterized mechanistically, and knockout animals are viable and develop normally. This means SARM1 is not obviously essential for development, and it is a validated therapeutic target in neurodegeneration.
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - There are no patients, de novo or inherited variants, or cohort data linking heterozygous SARM1 variants to autism or neurodevelopmental disorders.
  - No behavioural assessments relevant to autism (social, repetitive or communication) were reported in the model organism studies.
  - The knockout phenotypes concern neurodegeneration and injury, not neurodevelopment, and the unassessed phenotypes in the older knockout lines are confounded by passenger mutations (PMID: 32268088).
  - The mechanism by which heterozygous variants would act (gain versus loss of function) is undefined.
- Conclusion: There is currently no clinical, model organism or biological evidence supporting SARM1 as an autosomal dominant autism spectrum disorder gene. Existing data support SARM1 as a mediator of axon degeneration, not of neurodevelopment, so the gene-disease relationship remains unsupported. Reassessment would require de novo or rare heterozygous variants identified in ASD cohorts, with functional validation.

## REFERENCES


### Model Organism Evidence
1. SARM1 promotes neuroinflammation and inhibits neural regeneration after spinal cord injury through NF-κB signaling (PMID: 33754056)
2. Sarm1 deletion suppresses TDP-43-linked motor neuron degeneration and cortical spine loss (PMID: 31661035)
3. SARM1 Ablation Is Protective and Preserves Spatial Vision in an In Vivo Mouse Model of Retinal Ganglion Cell Degeneration (PMID: 35163535)
4. Pharmacological SARM1 inhibition protects axon structure and function in paclitaxel-induced peripheral neuropathy (PMID: 33964142)
5. Macrophage depletion blocks congenital SARM1-dependent neuropathy (PMID: 36287209)
6. Sarm1 knockout modifies biomarkers of neurodegeneration and spinal cord circuitry but not disease progression in the mSOD1(G93A) mouse model of ALS (PMID: 35863521)
7. SARM1 knockout does not rescue neuromuscular phenotypes in a Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease Type 1A mouse model (PMID: 35137510)
8. Deletion of murine Sarm1 results in a microenvironment that delays peripheral nerve regeneration after injury (PMID: 41061042)
9. Passenger Mutations Confound Phenotypes of SARM1-Deficient Mice (PMID: 32268088)
