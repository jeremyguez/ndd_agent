SOX11 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER / NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
SOX11 encodes a SoxC-family transcription factor (HMG-box) that is highly expressed in the developing nervous system. It regulates neuronal differentiation, survival and axonal growth, as well as development of other organs such as the eye, inner ear and skeleton. Heterozygous pathogenic variants and deletions cause Coffin-Siris syndrome 9 / "SOX11 syndrome", a neurodevelopmental disorder.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: 4 clinical reports or series were found. These were a 7-patient series (PMID: 39333428), a single case with a missense variant (PMID: 33430815), a single case with a 2p25.2 deletion (PMID: 18992374), and a 13-individual communication study (PMID: 37916390).
- Total patients: about 22 across studies. Overlap between the 7-patient and 13-patient series cannot be excluded, because the cohort sources are not stated.
- Common phenotypes:
  - Neurodevelopmental delay and intellectual disability, present in most patients.
  - Autism/ADHD and autistic features. The autism-relevant data include autism in the 2p25.2 deletion patient (PMID: 18992374), autism/ADHD in the 7-patient series (PMID: 39333428), and impaired pragmatic language and autistic social features in 13 individuals (PMID: 37916390), with significant communication difficulties on CCC-2.
  - Microcephaly, short stature, hypotonia and 5th-finger clinodactyly.
  - Hypogonadotropic hypogonadism features.
  - Ocular anomalies, including Peters anomaly, aniridia, glaucoma and optic nerve agenesis (PMID: 33430815; PMID: 18992374).
- Variant types:
  - In the 7-patient series (PMID: 39333428), five were de novo missense, one was de novo nonsense and one was a whole-gene deletion.
  - The single case report describes de novo c.251G>T, p.(Gly84Val) (PMID: 33430815).
  - Another case has a de novo 2p25.2 deletion encompassing SOX11 (PMID: 18992374).
  - Missense variants predominate, but LoF and deletions (haploinsufficiency) are also documented.
  - All variants with stated inheritance are de novo.
- Cohorts: none of the studies identify membership in UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL. The one stated center is Mainz, Germany (PMID: 33430815).
- Autism is a feature but is not the sole or primary phenotype. It occurs within a broader syndromic neurodevelopmental disorder.
- Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE. Evidence is consistent, with de novo variants and deletions, but the number of patients with explicit autism phenotyping is limited, and variant details are largely missing from the abstracts.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse and zebrafish.
- Key findings:
  - Sox11-/- mice show lateral semicircular canal dysplasia. Sox11+/- mice show canal abnormalities (10 of 26 ears abnormal) and mild high-frequency hearing loss (ASHG 2016 - SOX11 is necessary for development of the bony labyrinth). Heterozygous knockouts also have hearing impairment (PMID: 35642566).
  - Homozygous ablation reduces sensory neuron survival and axonal growth (cited, PMID: 35642566).
  - Epithelial conditional knockout causes failure of eyelid closure (PMID: 33662665), and systemic knockouts are born with open eyelids.
  - Conditional Sox11/Sox4 knockout in the dentate gyrus reduces granule cell excitability and upregulates potassium channel subunits (PMID: 32080705).
  - TCF4 and SOX11 cooperatively control commissure formation (PMID: 34184026).
  - Zebrafish sox11a/b knockdown causes brain abnormalities (ASHG 2014 - De novo SOX11 mutations cause Coffin-Siris syndrome). Sox11 depletion also reduces enteric neuron differentiation (PMID: 40789027).
  - Tubule-specific deletion worsens kidney injury (PMID: 41279940).
- No data on Mendelian ratios, lethality or infertility were provided. Homozygous null lethality was not detailed in the supplied data.
- Consistency with human phenotype: reasonable. Dosage sensitivity (heterozygous phenotypes), neural and sensory (inner ear) involvement, ocular defects and brain abnormalities are all concordant with the human syndrome. No direct autism-like behavioral assays were reported.
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Function and pathways: SOX11 is a transcription factor. Targets and regulators include:
  - CATSPER1/PI3K-AKT in colorectal cancer (PMID: 35305240).
  - PAX5 in mantle cell lymphoma (PMID: 23321250).
  - Wnt/β-catenin in HCC (PMID: 30517979).
  - MYC super-enhancer looping in group 3 medulloblastoma (PMID: 36450980).
  - Dependence on CHD7 for its expression, linking it to Coffin-Siris and CHARGE pathogenesis (ASHG 2016 - SOX11 is necessary for development of the bony labyrinth).
- Expression: it is enriched in the fetal cerebellar progenitor populations (PMID: 36450980). It is activity-induced in adult dentate gyrus granule neurons, where about 75% of SOX11+ neurons are c-FOS+, and it modulates excitability and potassium channel expression (PMID: 32080705). It is also highly expressed in SCLC (PMID: 35402616).
- Most of the biological literature concerns cancer (mantle cell lymphoma, HCC, breast, neuroblastoma, SCLC), with conflicting directions in HCC (PMID: 30517979 vs PMID: 39246708). It is of limited direct relevance to autism or neurodevelopmental mechanisms, although it supports a role in progenitor and neuronal differentiation.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE. Neurodevelopmental and activity-dependent roles are supported, but disease-specific mechanistic data (e.g., functional testing of patient variants) are lacking.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: MODERATE for SOX11 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with autism features. Evidence for autism as an isolated phenotype is LIMITED.
- Key strengths:
  - Multiple de novo missense, nonsense and whole-gene deletion variants, which indicate haploinsufficiency.
  - A recognizable syndromic phenotype across studies.
  - Concordant heterozygous mouse and zebrafish phenotypes.
  - Known neurodevelopmental transcription factor function.
- Key gaps:
  - Variant notations are missing from the abstracts.
  - Possible patient overlap between series.
  - No cohort provenance.
  - No functional assays of patient variants.
  - Only small numbers with formal autism diagnoses.
  - No behavioral or autism-relevant phenotyping in models.
  - Much of the biological evidence comes from cancer contexts.
- Conclusion: SOX11 is an established cause of a syndromic neurodevelopmental disorder (Coffin-Siris syndrome 9 / SOX11 syndrome) via heterozygous, mostly de novo variants and deletions, with autism/ADHD and communication and social impairments as recurrent features. The evidence supports SOX11 as an autism-associated gene in a syndromic context, but not as a gene specific to autism. Further large cohort studies with systematic autism assessment and functional variant validation would strengthen the association.
