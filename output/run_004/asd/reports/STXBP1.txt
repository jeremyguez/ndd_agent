STXBP1 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) PATHOGENIC VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
STXBP1 encodes Munc18-1, a presynaptic SM-family protein that binds syntaxin-1 and is essential for SNARE-mediated synaptic vesicle docking, priming and fusion, and therefore for neurotransmitter release. Heterozygous de novo variants cause STXBP1 encephalopathy, a developmental and epileptic encephalopathy in which autistic features are a recurrent comorbidity.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Number of reports: 10 clinical reports or case series were extracted. These include large series, small series, single case reports, and conference abstracts.
- Approximate total patients: about 224 (147 + 30 + 15 + 7 + 7 + 1 + 3 + 12 + 1 + 1). Some overlap between series is possible. The 12 Rett-like patients (PMID: 31105003) are not itemized.
- Most common phenotypes:
  - Intellectual disability or developmental delay, severe to profound in 87-88% (PMID: 26865513; PMID: 35851549).
  - Early-onset epilepsy, in 95-100%. This includes Ohtahara syndrome (21%), West syndrome (9.5%) and nonsyndromic early-onset epileptic encephalopathy (53%) (PMID: 26865513). Median onset was 3.5 months, and 80% had treatment-resistant seizures (PMID: 35851549).
  - Movement disorders, including stereotypies (63%) and tremor (56%) (PMID: 35851549). Head stereotypies were also reported (PMID: 23763664).
  - Autistic features were explicitly reported in 42% of the adult natural history cohort (PMID: 35851549). They were also reported in the series of PMID: 26865513, in children (PMID: 32521962), and in de novo cases with ASD (PMID: 29264391). Autism was reported with 9q34.11 deletions (Yatsenko et al. 2010 - Genomic and intragenic deletions of the STXBP1 gene within the 9q34.11 chromosome region, ASHG 2010).
  - Rett-like or Rett syndrome phenotypes, including a typical Rett case with a frameshift, c.60delG (PMID: 29544889). Behavioral problems occurred in 65%, and 71% were nonverbal (PMID: 35851549).
- Variant types: Variants are heterozygous and mostly de novo. They include LoF (nonsense, frameshift, splice), missense (8 of 15 in the Chinese series, PMID: 32521962), and whole-gene or intragenic deletions (haploinsufficiency). Somatic mosaicism has also been described (PMID: 29264391). LoF and deletions appear to predominate overall, with a substantial missense fraction.
- Known cohorts: No UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL involvement was identified. The sources are international clinical networks and single centers (Canada, China, Japan, Spain, USA, Europe).
- Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG for STXBP1 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene. For autism specifically, the evidence is moderate: autism is a common comorbidity within the encephalopathy rather than an isolated phenotype, and it is not quantified in most series.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: Zebrafish, mouse, and (in the biological evidence) C. elegans and Drosophila.
- Key phenotypes:
  - Mouse heterozygous knockouts are viable and show cognitive, psychiatric and motor dysfunction, cortical hyperexcitability and seizures, and reduced cortical inhibitory transmission (PMID: 32073399). They also show absence seizures with spike-and-wave discharges due to impaired cortico-striatal excitatory transmission (PMID: 31015467). Cortical microcircuit feedforward inhibition is defective (PMID: 38086378). Serotonergic deficits are present (PMID: 41263692).
  - Full mouse knockout is perinatally lethal, with neuronal death before synapse formation (PMID: 36257704).
  - Zebrafish stxbp1a homozygous mutants show profound lack of movement, low brain activity and early larval death. stxbp1b homozygous mutants are viable with spontaneous electrographic seizures (PMID: 26963117; PMID: 34083748; PMID: 35451230). Clemizole and trazodone reduced seizure events by about 80% (PMID: 35451230), while 4-phenylbutyrate did not rescue the phenotypes (PMID: 42268630).
- Consistency with human phenotype: Haploinsufficiency models reproduce epilepsy, motor and cognitive deficits, and psychiatric or behavioral features. This is consistent with the human disorder. No Mendelian ratio data were reported. Autism-specific behavioral assays (social behavior) were not described in the extracted evidence, although "psychiatric" dysfunction was reported in mice.
- Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: Munc18-1 acts in the SNARE complex and synaptic vesicle exocytosis. Loss of Munc18-1 reduces syntaxin-1 by about 70% and also reduces dynamin-1 and other endocytic proteins (PMID: 36245272). Munc18-1 also regulates the submembrane F-actin network (PMID: 31719162) and interacts with CASK and APBA1 (PMID: 33159991).
- Expression: Zebrafish stxbp1a and stxbp1b are highly conserved and prominently expressed in the larval brain (PMID: 26963117). Human data show that STXBP1 knockdown in foetal cortical slice cultures (15-18 PCW) reduced subplate network activity and dysregulated synaptogenesis genes (PMID: 42214744).
- Key findings:
  - Missense variants cause protein destabilization and aggregation, including co-aggregation of wild-type protein, which lowers functional protein below hemizygous levels. Pharmacological chaperones reversed this (PMID: 33332765).
  - A homozygous L446F variant causes a gain of function in release probability, which is a distinct mechanism (PMID: 31855252).
  - In humanized C. elegans, the R388X truncation acted dominant-negatively, about 3-fold more severe than a heterozygous full knockout (ASHG 2019 - PgmNr 3136: Humanized STXBP1 animal models as a precision medicine approach to detecting pathogenicity in clinical variants).
  - Drosophila Rop haploinsufficiency (about 20% lower protein) exacerbated α-synuclein pathology (ASHG 2023 - α-Synuclein pathology in Drosophila melanogaster is exacerbated by haploinsufficiency of Rop).
- Strength of biological evidence: STRONG for synaptic function and haploinsufficiency mechanisms. It is indirect for autism-specific pathways.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: STRONG for STXBP1 as a heterozygous neurodevelopmental disease gene, with autism as a recurrent associated feature. Evidence for autism as an isolated or primary phenotype is MODERATE.
- Key strengths:
  - Large clinical series (n=147 and n=30) with consistent phenotypes.
  - De novo heterozygous LoF variants and deletions, supporting haploinsufficiency.
  - Concordant heterozygous mouse and zebrafish models.
  - Well-characterized synaptic mechanisms.
- Key gaps:
  - Autism is reported as a feature rather than systematically assessed (for example with ADOS or ADI-R), and often alongside severe ID and epilepsy.
  - Variant-level data are not itemized in several reports.
  - Some evidence comes from abstracts only.
  - Cohorts may overlap.
  - There are no social-behavior phenotypes in the model organisms.
  - No standard research cohorts were identified.
- Conclusion: STXBP1 is a well-established autosomal dominant, haploinsufficiency-driven neurodevelopmental gene. Autistic features are a frequent, though not universal, part of the phenotype (about 42% in one adult cohort). The evidence supports STXBP1 as an ASD-associated gene in the context of developmental and epileptic encephalopathy. It is less well supported as a cause of non-syndromic autism.
