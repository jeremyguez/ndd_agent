LRRC4 EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
LRRC4 (also called NGL-2) encodes a leucine-rich repeat–containing synaptic cell adhesion molecule. It is enriched in the brain and is localized to the postsynaptic side of excitatory synapses, where it associates with PSD-95 and NMDA receptors. Outside the nervous system it has been studied mainly as a tumor suppressor (glioma and other cancers).

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: Four reports were found, but only one describes an individual patient. None provides detailed, variant-level case data.
- Patient numbers: About 1 patient is explicitly counted. The remaining studies are cohort-level or functional and give no counts.
- Phenotypes: Autism or autism spectrum disorder and neurodevelopmental disorder are the common features. The single deletion case also had severe intellectual disability.
- Variant types:
  - The single patient carries a de novo 1.9-Mb heterozygous 7q31.33q32.1 microdeletion encompassing LRRC4, GRM8 and 11 other genes (PMID: 28224041). Because this is a multigene deletion, LRRC4 is not proven to be causal.
  - A meta-analysis of ASD trios reported recurrent de novo coding variants (LoF or damaging missense) in LRRC4 as a new ASD candidate (PMID: 31332282). Specific variants and counts are not given in the abstract.
  - Two massively parallel reporter assay studies identified 5'UTR variants from autism/NDD probands that altered translation (PMID: 37961498; PMID: 41344325). These provide functional support only, and the variants were not described in detail.
  - Overall, the variants are of mixed or "other" type: a deletion, de novo coding variants, and 5'UTR variants. No single class predominates.
- Cohorts: No UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL involvement is reported. The ASD trio meta-analysis likely draws on large public ASD cohorts, but this is not specified.
- Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED. The evidence consists of one multigene deletion, statistical de novo enrichment without variant-level detail, and 5'UTR reporter data.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: Mouse only.
- Neurological phenotypes: Full Lrrc4/NGL-2 knockout mice are viable and show suppressed NMDAR-dependent hippocampal synaptic plasticity, mild social interaction deficits and repetitive behaviors (PMID: 29949768). The autistic-like behaviors improved rapidly with pharmacological NMDAR activation. A second knockout study reported impaired synaptic plasticity and cognitive impairment, and these were worsened with a high-fat diet (PMID: 41494607). Reduced PSD-95 and increased apoptosis markers were seen, and LRRC4 overexpression rescued the abnormalities.
- Other phenotypes: Knockouts show exacerbated experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis (PMID: 33932995). A knock-in of a recurrent human Lrrc4 variant causes rosacea-like inflammation (PMID: 37402769).
- Lethality and Mendelian ratios: Knockouts are viable. No Mendelian ratio data or sample sizes were reported. Heterozygous phenotypes were not described.
- Consistency with human phenotype: The social deficits, repetitive behaviors and NMDAR hypofunction are a good match for ASD. The models are full homozygous knockouts, so they do not directly test the heterozygous dominant mechanism.
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE. The phenotype is relevant and replicated across two knockout studies, though effects are mild and heterozygotes are untested.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Expression: LRRC4 expression is restricted to the adult brain in human and mouse and is developmentally regulated (PMID: 15967442).
- Synaptic function: The synaptic role is shown mainly through the mouse studies above, where NGL-2 links to NMDARs and PSD-95.
- Other pathways: Much of the biochemical literature is cancer-focused.
  - LRRC4 binds PIK3R1 and inhibits AKT/GSK3β/β-catenin signaling (PMID: 32117780).
  - It blocks SAM68 and promotes circCD44 formation (PMID: 34631278).
  - It acts through miR-185 to suppress glioma invasion (PMID: 22834685).
  - It engages AP2A1 clathrin-coated vesicles to disrupt mitochondrial cristae (PMID: 42439431).
  - It promotes YAP degradation in ovarian granulosa cells, and its deficiency is linked to premature ovarian insufficiency (PMID: 40317712).
- Neurodevelopmental relevance: These non-neuronal findings are largely tangential to ASD. The brain-restricted expression and the synaptic and NMDAR roles are the most relevant points.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE. Brain-specific expression and a synaptic role are plausible for ASD, but most mechanistic work concerns cancer.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: LIMITED
- Key strengths:
  - Recurrent de novo variants in ASD trios (PMID: 31332282).
  - A de novo deletion in a patient with severe ID and autistic features (PMID: 28224041).
  - Reporter-assay evidence that 5'UTR variants alter translation (PMID: 37961498).
  - Knockout mice with autistic-like behaviors and NMDAR dysfunction (PMID: 29949768).
  - Brain-enriched expression and a synaptic role.
- Key gaps:
  - No well-described individuals with intragenic LoF or missense variants.
  - The only individual case has a multigene deletion.
  - Variant details, segregation data and inheritance information are lacking.
  - No heterozygous mouse data are available.
  - No cohort provenance is stated.
  - Most mechanistic literature is oncology-related.
- Conclusion: LRRC4 is a plausible ASD candidate gene. Statistical de novo enrichment, a synaptic function and a knockout mouse with autistic-like behavior all support the link. Detailed clinical data on heterozygous pathogenic variants are lacking, so the evidence for an autosomal dominant ASD disorder remains limited. Additional well-phenotyped patients with intragenic variants are needed to confirm the association.
