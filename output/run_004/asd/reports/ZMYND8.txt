ZMYND8 EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER / NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
ZMYND8 (zinc finger MYND-type containing 8, also called RACK7) is a chromatin reader. It carries PHD, BROMO, PWWP and MYND domains, and it takes part in transcriptional regulation as a component of both activating (P-TEFb) and repressive (NuRD, LSD1) complexes. It also regulates enhancers and super-enhancers and takes part in the DNA damage response.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: Only one clinical report was extracted for this gene, Yamada et al. 2020 (PMID: 32530565). It was a Japanese NDD exome cohort that identified a single de novo truncating ZMYND8 variant. The abstract gives no exact variant notation, and it proposed ZMYND8 as a novel candidate gene.
- Patients: About 1 patient in the extracted clinical data. A separate paper in the biological evidence set (PMID: 35916866, "De Novo ZMYND8 variants result in an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder with cardiac malformations") describes 11 unrelated individuals with de novo variants and intellectual disability. It was not extracted as clinical evidence, so those patients are not counted in the clinical total. If they are counted, the total is about 12.
- Phenotypes: Non-specific NDD in the single case. Intellectual disability is the main feature in the 11-person series, and its title also points to cardiac malformations. The extracted data contain no autism-specific phenotype data.
- Variant types: The single clinical case is LoF (truncating). The 11-person series reportedly includes missense variants in the PWWP and MYND domains, so the likely spectrum is missense plus LoF.
- Cohorts: No UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL cohorts were identified in the extracted data. The Yamada cohort is from Japan.
- Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED. The extracted data show a single de novo case with no phenotype detail. The 11-person series, if its data are verified, would support a rating closer to MODERATE. Evidence for autism specifically is lacking.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: Mouse (conditional knockout, xenograft and knockdown models) and zebrafish (knockdown in xenografts). Drosophila knockdown is covered under biological evidence.
- Key phenotypes: Astrocyte-specific Rack7/Zmynd8 deletion in mice caused overt developmental defects and aberrant astrocyte development. It reduced genome-wide H3K27me3, altered SUZ12 localization, and overactivated Wnt signaling and astrocyte differentiation genes (PMID: 40125808). The other mouse and zebrafish studies are cancer models and are of limited relevance to neurodevelopment. They show reduced tumor growth or angiogenesis with ZMYND8 loss (PMIDs: 33148660, 34462784, 25117453). No data on lethality, Mendelian ratios, behavior or cognition were reported for germline heterozygous models. Neuronal knockdown of the Drosophila ortholog decreased habituation learning (PMID: 35916866).
- Consistency with human phenotype: Partial. Abnormal neural/glial development and the fly learning deficit are broadly compatible with an NDD. No heterozygous mammalian model with behavioral or autism-like phenotypes exists.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: ZMYND8 binds CyclinT1 as a homodimer to activate transcription through P-TEFb. As a monomer it associates with CHD4 of NuRD for repression (PMID: 30134174). It is required for ATRA-induced neuronal differentiation. It promotes the coding MAPT isoform while silencing the non-coding MAPT213 (PMID: 36064715), and it supports SHH signaling (GLI1, PTCH1) during neuroblastoma differentiation (PMID: 38804064). Outside the nervous system, it regulates super-enhancers in macrophages (PMID: 34764296), Igh enhancers in B cells (PMID: 30293785), and IL-2 signaling in T cells (PMID: 42778612). It also acts in several cancer contexts (PMIDs: 40102673, 38488001, 39128723).
- Expression: ZMYND8 is broadly expressed in brain, with the highest expression in early brain development (PMID: 35916866).
- Variant mechanism: PWWP missense variants abolish the Drebrin interaction, and MYND-domain variants disrupt the GATAD2A interaction (PMID: 35916866). This gives a functional basis for pathogenicity of the domain-specific variants.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE. Chromatin regulation and neuronal differentiation roles are plausible for an NDD gene, and variant-level functional data exist. Most of the literature is in cancer and immunology.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: LIMITED to MODERATE for a neurodevelopmental disorder, and LIMITED for autism specifically.
- Key strengths: Recurrent de novo variants have been reported (1 case in PMID: 32530565 and 11 individuals in PMID: 35916866). Domain-specific functional consequences have been shown for the missense variants. There is a fly learning phenotype. The gene has well-established roles in chromatin regulation and neuronal differentiation, and it is highly expressed in early brain development.
- Key gaps: Autism-specific phenotyping is lacking. The extracted clinical data are sparse, with no variant notation or phenotype detail. There is no germline heterozygous mouse model with neurobehavioral assessment. Many studies are cancer-focused. No large cohort replication was identified.
- Conclusion: ZMYND8 is a credible candidate gene for an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder with intellectual disability, and possibly cardiac malformations, supported by de novo variants and functional data. Evidence for a specific autism association is currently insufficient. Additional cases with detailed phenotyping, including autism diagnoses, and a neurobehavioral heterozygous animal model are needed.
