UBR5 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: autism spectrum disorder, heterozygous (autosomal dominant) variants

1. GENE SUMMARY
UBR5 encodes a large HECT-type E3 ubiquitin ligase, also called EDD or hyd in Drosophila. It is essential for mammalian embryonic development and regulates protein degradation in transcription, DNA damage and replication, and signaling pathways (PMID: 37040767; PMID: 37478862).

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Number of reports: six publications. They comprise one large case series (PMID: 39721588), one small case series (PMID: 41318701), two single-patient observations in ASD or neurodevelopmental cohorts (PMID: 38519481; PMID: 41168819), and two single cases with confounding features (PMID: 31413120; PMID: 29069077).
- Total patients: about 36 (29 + 3 + 4 single cases). Some overlap between the case series and the single reports cannot be excluded because the abstracts lack detail.
- Common phenotypes:
  - The 29-patient series reports developmental delay, autism, intellectual disability, epilepsy, movement disorder and genital anomalies (PMID: 39721588).
  - The 3-patient series reports ASD and intellectual disability (PMID: 41318701).
  - The Italian cohort patient had autism (ADOS-2) with atypical language and borderline IQ (PMID: 38519481).
  - The French cohort patient had a severe learning/language disorder (PMID: 41168819).
- Variant types:
  - Loss of function predominates. The reports include a de novo frameshift, c.7441del p.His2481Metfs*7, classified likely pathogenic (PMID: 38519481); a de novo nonsense variant in a Williams-Beuren syndrome patient with ASD (PMID: 31413120); and three de novo LoF variants (PMID: 41318701).
  - The 29-patient series includes both LoF and missense variants, and several missense variants showed reduced autoubiquitination and altered localization in vitro (PMID: 39721588).
  - De novo missense variants are also reported: p.Leu1205Pro, a VUS (PMID: 41168819), and p.Arg1907His, which co-occurred with a mosaic KMT2C LoF variant and is likely not the main driver (PMID: 29069077).
- Cohorts: one patient came from the RUMC cohort (PMID: 29069077). No UDN, GREGoR, DDD or GEL membership is stated. The other studies come from Italy and France or have an unspecified origin.
- Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE to STRONG. De novo LoF variants recur across independent reports and a 29-person series defines a syndrome. However, the abstracts lack individual variant details, and the single cases are confounded by other genetic findings (Williams-Beuren deletion, KMT2C).

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse (full and conditional knockouts, plus knockdown and tumor-cell models), zebrafish, and C. elegans (reduced movement in ubr-5 knockout, reported in PMID: 39721588). Drosophila hyd is covered under biological evidence below.
- Key phenotypes:
  - Zebrafish ubr5 stable LoF mutants showed seizure-like swim behavior, one of 5 of 48 epilepsy-candidate lines (PMID: 38370728; PMID: 39021799). This is consistent with the epilepsy seen in patients.
  - Ubr5 full knockout mice are viable enough to be studied and show susceptibility to RNA virus infection (PMID: 38278841). Limb-mesenchyme conditional knockout caused cartilage degradation and heterotopic ossification (PMID: 33819267). Conditional liver knockout delayed hepatocyte maturation (PMID: 37907970).
  - The remaining mouse studies concern cancer models and are not relevant to neurodevelopment.
  - No brain or behavioral phenotype has been reported in mouse models, and no Mendelian ratio or lethality data are given. Embryonic requirement is noted only in a structural paper (PMID: 37040767).
- Consistency with human phenotype: partial. The zebrafish seizure phenotype and the C. elegans movement defect align with the human neurological features, but there is no mammalian neurodevelopmental or autism-relevant characterization.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function:
  - UBR5 is a signaling hub E3 ligase in the c-Myc network that degrades orphan subunits of transcriptional regulators (PMID: 37478862).
  - It regulates innate immunity by K63-ubiquitinating TRIM28 (PMID: 38278841).
  - It protects replication forks and regulates H2A ubiquitylation (PMID: 31586398).
  - It degrades VGLL4 to permit Hippo-YAP signaling (PMID: 40379800).
  - Drosophila Hyd forms a tumor-suppressor complex with Lin that degrades Bowl, and the pathway appears conserved in human cells (PMID: 39137945).
  - Several cancer-related substrates are described, including Smad3 (PMID: 39260061) and CDC73 (PMID: 35551175).
- Structure: cryo-EM shows head-to-tail dimers and cage-like tetramers. Jaw-lining residues are functionally important (PMID: 37040767).
- Expression: the provided data contain no brain expression or neurodevelopment-specific data. UBR5 is described as ubiquitously functioning, with nuclear and cytoplasmic localization (PMID: 35980206).
- Functional testing of patient variants: reduced autoubiquitination and mislocalization of several missense variants (PMID: 39721588).
- Other: a Huntington's disease age-at-onset GWAS locus study found that Ubr5 inactivation did not alter CAG repeat instability, implicating RRM2B instead (ASHG 2017 - Functional prioritization of Huntington's disease onset modifier genes in the Hdh Q111/+ mouse). This is not relevant to ASD.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for a broadly important E3 ligase with plausible haploinsufficiency mechanisms and some variant functional data, but LIMITED specifically for neurodevelopmental or ASD mechanisms.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: MODERATE (approaching STRONG for a neurodevelopmental disorder with ASD, but weaker for ASD as an isolated phenotype).
- Key strengths:
  - Recurrent de novo LoF variants across independent cohorts (PMID: 38519481; PMID: 41318701; PMID: 31413120).
  - A 29-patient series defining a consistent syndrome with developmental delay, autism and intellectual disability (PMID: 39721588).
  - In vitro functional deficits for missense variants.
  - Zebrafish seizure and C. elegans movement phenotypes.
- Key gaps:
  - Individual variant notations and segregation data are lacking in the abstracts.
  - The phenotype is broad, with ASD one of several features.
  - Some cases are confounded by other genetic findings (PMID: 31413120; PMID: 29069077).
  - There are no mammalian neurodevelopmental or behavioral models, and no brain-specific expression data.
  - Most model organism data come from cancer or immunity studies.
  - Missense pathogenicity is only partly established, and the p.Leu1205Pro case remains a VUS (PMID: 41168819).
- Conclusion: UBR5 haploinsufficiency is supported as a cause of an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental syndrome in which autism and intellectual disability are core features, mainly through de novo LoF variants. Evidence for ASD specifically is moderate, and mechanistic neurodevelopmental data and detailed variant-level reporting would strengthen the gene–disease relationship.
