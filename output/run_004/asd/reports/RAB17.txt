RAB17 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT MODEL)

1. GENE SUMMARY
RAB17 encodes a small GTPase of the Rab family that localizes to recycling and early or late endosomes. It regulates endosomal sorting and receptor-mediated transcytosis in polarized epithelial cells (PMID: 9624171). Its best-characterized roles are in endosomal trafficking and in tumor biology. No neurodevelopmental role has been established in the evidence reviewed.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and molecular function:
  - Rab17 localizes to recycling endosomes in a Rab11-dependent manner. It interacts with ALS2 and RABGEF1. RABGEF1 acts as its GEF, and ALS2 acts downstream to mature Rab17-positive endosomes into EEA1-positive early endosomes (PMID: 31959474).
  - Rab17 is monosumoylated at K68. In polarized hepatic WIF-B cells it regulates apical delivery of transcytotic vesicles (PMID: 30256711).
  - In melanocytes it controls melanosome trafficking and filopodia formation, downstream of Rab27a and under MITF regulation (PMID: 21291502).
  - It associates with Vamp8 at late endosomes and restrains NRP2 surface expression (PMID: 28062852).
- Expression: Rab17 is restricted to epithelial cells and is induced upon polarization (PMID: 9624171). The evidence provided contains no data on brain or neurodevelopmental expression. The ALS2 interaction is notable because ALS2 is a neuronal disease gene, but the cited study does not address neuronal function.
- Key experimental findings are almost entirely cancer- or vascular-focused:
  - Rab17 acts as a tumor suppressor in hepatocellular carcinoma. It was present in 75% of paraneoplastic tissues versus 35% of HCC samples, and knockdown acts through Erk signaling (PMID: 26191189).
  - Low Rab17 expression is associated with poor survival in NSCLC through STAT3/HIF-1α/VEGF signaling (PMID: 31841274).
  - Rab17 is a tumor suppressor in oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC), where LMO4 drives its degradation (PMID: 41213908).
  - In endometrial cancer, RAB17 promotes progression by inhibiting TFRC-dependent ferroptosis (PMID: 39242574).
  - RAB17 expression is reduced in diabetic foot ulcer endothelial cells, and overexpression enhances angiogenesis (PMID: 37605780).
- Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED for general cell biology (endosomal trafficking is well described). It is NONE with respect to ASD or neurodevelopment.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT.
- Key strengths: Rab17's role in endosomal trafficking is well characterized mechanistically. It has plausible links to ALS2 and to vesicle and receptor trafficking pathways, which are broadly relevant to neuronal biology.
- Key gaps:
  - No patients or variants have been reported.
  - There are no animal models.
  - There are no brain expression or neuronal functional data.
  - Existing literature is dominated by oncology and angiogenesis, and by epithelial cell biology.
  - Nothing supports a heterozygous or dominant mechanism such as haploinsufficiency.
- Conclusion: The available literature provides no clinical, model organism or neurodevelopment-specific evidence linking RAB17 to autism spectrum disorder. Its known biology (endosomal trafficking and cancer signaling) is not specific to the nervous system. RAB17 should be regarded as having insufficient evidence as an autosomal dominant ASD gene unless de novo or case-control variant data and neuronal functional studies emerge.
