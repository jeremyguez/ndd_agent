DYRK1A LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) PATHOGENIC VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
DYRK1A encodes dual-specificity tyrosine-phosphorylation-regulated kinase 1A, a serine/threonine kinase on chromosome 21q22.13. It regulates neurodevelopmental processes including neuronal proliferation, survival, and differentiation, and it phosphorylates substrates in the Wnt, mTORC1, and mitochondrial import pathways. Heterozygous loss of function causes DYRK1A-related intellectual disability syndrome (MRD7), in which autism is a frequent feature.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: 8 reports or series were extracted. Five are single-patient or small case reports (PMID: 42256340; PMID: 35598272; ASHG 2022 - Expansion of the histidine-rich domain of DYRK1A; PMID: 25156961; PMID: 42721435). One is a 7-patient series (ASHG 2014 - Ji et al.). Two are cohort or targeted-sequencing studies that report recurrent de novo DYRK1A variants without a stated patient number (PMID: 23160955, ASD, SSC cohort; PMID: 25167861, ID, 217-gene panel).
- Total patients: roughly 12 individually enumerated (7 + 5 single cases). The additional recurrent cases in the two cohort studies are uncounted.
- Common phenotypes: intellectual disability and developmental delay, microcephaly, speech impairment, seizures or epilepsy, autism spectrum disorder, and distinctive facial features. Less frequent features include ataxia or abnormal gait, feeding difficulties, brain abnormalities, and short stature. Autism was explicitly reported in the epilepsy and developmental delay case (PMID: 42256340), the Chinese frameshift case (PMID: 35598272), the Brazilian ASD cohort frameshift (PMID: 42721435), and the SSC ASD cohort (PMID: 23160955), where microcephaly was also reported. Autism was described as "less common" in Ji et al. One truncating-variant patient lacked microcephaly (PMID: 25156961).
- Variant types: predominantly LoF. These include nonsense variants, the frameshifts p.Ser641Argfs*31 (PMID: 35598272) and c.281_282insT (PMID: 42721435), and recurrent truncating or splice variants (PMID: 23160955). Other variants are 2 missense variants and 3 microdeletions of 1.7–4.2 Mb (Ji et al.), an exon 5 deletion (PMID: 42256340), and an in-frame histidine-repeat duplication (p.His601_His610dup; ASHG 2022). Most variants are de novo, supporting haploinsufficiency.
- Known cohorts: no UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL cohorts are identified. The SSC (PMID: 23160955) and a Brazilian ASD cohort (PMID: 42721435) are the only named cohorts.
- Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG for DYRK1A-related neurodevelopmental disorder with frequent autism. MODERATE for autism as an isolated or primary phenotype, since autism is often co-occurring with ID and the extracted ASD-specific cases are few.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse, zebrafish, and Xenopus tropicalis.
- Key phenotypes:
  - Dyrk1a+/- mice are viable (PMID: 24922073; PMID: 25188425). They show reduced midbrain dopaminergic neuron number from excess Casp9-dependent apoptosis (PMID: 24922073). Heterozygous mice also show impaired social recognition (PMID: 37797581) and abnormal macroglial and oligodendrocyte development, with thinner myelinated callosal axons and slower conduction (PMID: 36402907).
  - Dyrk1a dosage normalization in Down syndrome models improved cognition and LTP (PMID: 25188425) and rescued heart defects (PMID: 38266108). This is dosage evidence but does not directly model heterozygous loss.
  - dyrk1aa zebrafish mutants show cerebral hemorrhage and defective hindbrain angiogenesis that require kinase activity (PMID: 31043432). Adult DYRK1A KO zebrafish show reduced social approach (PMID: 41527712).
  - Xenopus dyrk1a loss of function altered brain structure (PMID: 34739029).
- Mendelian ratios and lethality: heterozygous mice are viable. No Mendelian ratio data were extracted.
- Consistency with human phenotype: the neuronal, social behavior, and glial or myelin findings are consistent with the human phenotype of ID, microcephaly, and autistic features. The vascular and immune phenotypes are less directly relevant.
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE to STRONG. Multiple species show concordant neurodevelopmental and social phenotypes, but formal Mendelian ratio and sample-size data are lacking.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: DYRK1A phosphorylates TOM70 at Ser91 to activate mitochondrial import (PMID: 34257281). It also phosphorylates TSC2 at Ser540 to activate mTORC1 (PMID: 42024470) and phosphorylates SIRT1 and FOXO1 (PMID: 31132406). It regulates Wnt/β-catenin signalling in both directions (PMID: 31086297), and its dosage affects Wnt and TGF-β balance (PMID: 36535930).
- Expression and network: DYRK1A co-complexes with other ASD risk proteins (ANK2, CHD8, CUL3, POGZ). This network is preferentially expressed in early fetal brain and regulated by FMRP and MeCP2 (ASHG 2015 - Li et al.).
- Key findings: DYRK1A supports PV interneuron feedforward inhibition and the synaptic substrate ABLIM3 for social recognition, and that deficit is rescuable (PMID: 37797581). Loss impairs glial development and axonal conductivity (PMID: 36402907).
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE to STRONG. Its protein interactions with ASD genes and its role in synaptic and circuit function are directly relevant to autism.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: STRONG for DYRK1A as a gene underlying a syndromic neurodevelopmental disorder in which autism is a core feature. Evidence for isolated ASD is MODERATE.
- Key strengths:
  - Recurrent de novo LoF variants across independent cohorts, plus microdeletions of the same locus.
  - Concordant mouse, zebrafish, and Xenopus phenotypes, including social behavior deficits.
  - Mechanistic and network links to other ASD genes.
  - Episignature support for pathogenicity (PMID: 42256340).
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - Many reports lack specific variant notation or patient counts.
  - Autism is usually co-occurring with ID and developmental delay rather than isolated, and some reports describe it as less common.
  - No UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL cohort data were identified.
  - Model organism sample sizes and Mendelian ratios were not reported.
  - Some biological papers concern Down syndrome overdosage or non-neuronal contexts and are only indirectly relevant.
- Conclusion: DYRK1A haploinsufficiency, usually from de novo LoF variants, causes a well-established syndrome with ID, microcephaly, seizures, and frequent autism. It is also a recurrently mutated gene in ASD cohorts. Model organism and biological data support a role in neurodevelopment and social behavior, so DYRK1A is a strongly supported autosomal dominant ASD-associated gene.
