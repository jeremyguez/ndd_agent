EBF3 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: ASD / HADDS (heterozygous, autosomal dominant)

1. GENE SUMMARY
EBF3 (Early B-cell Factor 3) encodes a member of the COE/EBF family of DNA-binding transcription factors. These factors have a DNA-binding domain (DBD), an IPT/TIG domain and a zinc finger (ZNF) motif, and they act as obligate dimers. EBF3 regulates neuronal differentiation, cell-cycle arrest and apoptosis. Heterozygous variants cause hypotonia, ataxia and developmental delay syndrome (HADDS; MIM 617330), which frequently includes autism.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: The extracted data contain 7 clinical reports or series. Two foundational series (Harms 2017, PMID: 28017373; Chao 2017, PMID: 28017370) appear only in the biological evidence, so they are not counted among the clinical reports. They describe about 10 affected individuals in Harms et al. and further cases in Chao et al.
- Patient numbers: Roughly 95 or more individuals are reported in the clinical list. These include 83 in the integrated cohort (PMID: 35340043; 41 newly phenotyped plus literature cases), 7 in the early WES series (PMID: 29162653), 2 neuroimaging cases (PMID: 34177436), and single cases of an in-frame deletion (PMID: 41530369) and a nonsense variant (PMID: 40050609). Some patients likely overlap with earlier literature. Other reports gave no case numbers: de novo noncoding enhancer variants (PMID: 34256850) and a progressive myoclonus ataxia cohort (PMID: 38844245).
- Common phenotypes: Global developmental delay, hypotonia, intellectual disability, ataxia, dysmorphic facial features, short stature, and autism or ASD (PMID: 29162653; PMID: 35340043). Milder presentations also occur: normal-range IQ with abnormal longitudinal fasciculus on DTI (PMID: 34177436), isolated dysarthria with average cognition (PMID: 41530369), and autism with intact cognition and motor delay or hypotonia in the noncoding enhancer cases (PMID: 34256850). One report describes early-onset progressive myoclonus ataxia in a Dutch cohort (PMID: 38844245).
- Variant types: These include de novo LoF variants (frameshift, nonsense, splice; e.g., p.Arg206Ter, PMID: 40050609; p.Tyr321*, PMID: 34177436), missense variants clustering in the DBD and ZNF (e.g., recurrent p.Arg209Trp), an in-frame deletion (p.Asn237del), and noncoding enhancer (hs737) variants. Missense and LoF are both well represented. ZNF missense variants were associated with greater severity (PMID: 35340043).
- Cohorts: No UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL membership is stated. Reports include a Dutch PMA cohort and a tertiary-hospital speech clinic (probably Australia).
- Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG. Many de novo variants occur across multiple independent studies, and the associated phenotype is consistent. Autism is a recurrent feature, though ASD-specific enrichment is better supported by the large cohort and the enhancer data than by individual cases.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: Mouse, zebrafish and Xenopus.
- Key phenotypes:
  - Zebrafish ebf3a full knockout: smaller brain (hypothalamus, cerebellum and hindbrain), increased cerebellar neural activity, abnormal baseline and sensory-evoked behavior, reduced Purkinje-neuron markers and Parvalbumin, and fewer lateral line neuromasts (PMID: 40406967).
  - Mouse full knockout: perinatal lethality from respiratory failure with a hypercontractile diaphragm and reduced Serca1, rescued by Atp2a1 transgene (PMID: 24786561). Sternum ossification defects were also reported (PMID: 32398354).
  - Mouse enhancer deletion (a partial ~10-20% reduction of Ebf3 expression): viable, but with sub-Mendelian transmission (P=0.02), sex-specific reduced mobility in males, altered brain-development gene expression, and body composition differences (PMID: 41081394). This is relevant to the noncoding ASD findings.
  - Xenopus knockdown: disrupted muscle development (PMID: 21839736). Ebf3 also drives ectopic neurogenesis via Maturin (PMID: 24095902).
- Consistency with humans: The zebrafish cerebellar and behavioral phenotypes fit the human ataxia and neurodevelopmental features. The mouse enhancer-deletion behavioral changes fit the autism data. The hypotonia seen in humans may relate to the muscle phenotypes, though this is speculative. Heterozygous knockout mouse behavior is not described in the extracted data, and the perinatal lethality of the full knockout does not directly mirror the human heterozygous condition.
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: EBF3 is a transcriptional regulator that binds the p21 (CDKN1A) and p27 promoters and induces cell-cycle arrest and apoptosis (PMID: 17018599). It also binds Atp2a1 (PMID: 24786561), Vimentin (PMID: 34906623) and CNTNAP4 (PMID: 38479556), and acts downstream of Notch signaling (PMID: 37752132). It is a miR218 target in dopaminergic neuron development (PMID: 25192643).
- Expression: Ebf3 shows dynamic expression during midbrain dopaminergic neuron development, and overexpression increases TH+ neurons (PMID: 25192643). It is also expressed in bone marrow stromal cells (PMID: 29563184). Fetal brain expression is supported by the enhancer-target data (PMID: 34256850).
- Key experiments: In HEK293T cells, patient missense mutants were mislocalized to the cytoplasm, showed reduced CDKN1A transactivation, and had reduced chromatin and genome-wide DNA binding (PMID: 28017373). Mutants also showed reduced transcriptional activity and impaired heterodimerization with wild-type EBF3 (PMID: 28017370). Drosophila rescue and luciferase assays showed ZNF variants failed to rescue viability, whereas p.Arg209Trp partially rescued (PMID: 35340043). These results support loss of function and haploinsufficiency (PMID: 29162653).
- Strength of biological evidence: STRONG for loss-of-function mechanism. Several extracted biological studies concern cancer, bone and Parkinson's disease and are less relevant to ASD.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: STRONG
- Key strengths: Many de novo LoF and missense variants occur across independent studies (83-patient integrated cohort, PMID: 35340043). Functional assays show loss of function. There is a genotype-phenotype correlation (ZNF variants more severe). Noncoding enhancer variants are enriched in simplex autism (PMID: 34256850). Zebrafish and mouse models show relevant neurological and behavioral phenotypes.
- Key gaps: Autism is part of a broader syndrome, so ASD-specific effects are difficult to separate from ID and ataxia. Variant details are often missing from abstracts. Some cases likely overlap across publications. The stated cohorts (UDN, DDD and others) are not identified. There is no heterozygous mouse behavioral model of coding variants. Phenotypic variability is wide, from severe to nearly isolated speech or autism phenotypes.
- Conclusion: EBF3 is a well-established cause of an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder (HADDS) in which autism is a frequent feature. The mechanism appears to be haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative loss of function, supported by functional and animal data. Autism-predominant presentations with intact cognition, including noncoding enhancer variants, extend the spectrum, and EBF3 merits inclusion as an ASD gene.
