PHF12 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) PATHOGENIC VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
PHF12 (also called PF1) encodes a PHD finger chromatin-associated protein. It is a component of the SIN3/HDAC corubrinar co-repressor complex and associates with MRG15, SIN3A/B, HDAC1 and KDM5A/B. Its known functions are transcriptional modulation, maintenance of nucleolar integrity and ribosome biogenesis, and prevention of premature cellular senescence (PMID: 27956701).

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent case reports/case series: 0. No clinical evidence was provided.
- Total patients: 0 with autism spectrum disorder or a neurodevelopmental disorder.
- Common phenotypes: none reported.
- Predominant variant type: not applicable. The only human genetic observation is indirect. Rare protein-altering PHF12 variants were reported in three or more multiplex age-related macular degeneration (AMD) families (264 individuals from 63 families), and PHF12 lies at an AMD GWAS locus (PMID: 32246154). This is unrelated to autism or neurodevelopment.
- Known cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse (full Pf1/Phf12 knockout). Chick work on neural crest is described under biological evidence.
- Key phenotypes: Homozygous knockout is embryonic lethal, with Pf1 required for mid- to late gestation viability (PMID: 27956701). Pf1-deficient MEFs show impaired proliferation, reduced BrdU incorporation, increased SA-β-gal (premature senescence), elevated γ-H2AX (DNA double-strand breaks), abnormal nucleolar structure and dysregulated ribosome biogenesis transcripts. No Mendelian ratio data, heterozygous phenotypes, neurological or behavioral phenotypes were reported.
- Consistency with human phenotype: cannot be assessed. There is no human phenotype, and heterozygous mice were not described as having any neurodevelopmental or behavioral features. Embryonic lethality of the null suggests the gene is essential, which does not directly support or refute a heterozygous haploinsufficiency mechanism for autism.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED (for relevance to autism; the gene is clearly essential in mouse development).

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and molecular function: PHF12 binds the SIN3A PAH2 domain through its SID and forms a complex with MRG15 and KDM5A/B (PMID: 34968869). It also associates with an MRG15/Sin3B/HDAC1 chromatin complex, and its interactors are enriched for ribosome biogenesis factors (PMID: 27956701). Disrupting PF1/SIN3A in triple-negative breast cancer cells reduced invasion and induced senescence (PMID: 34968869).
- Neurodevelopmental relevance: Phf12 is a regulator of neural crest delamination together with Snail2, with a reciprocal expression pattern to miR-203, which targets its 3'UTR. In chick, ectopic miR-203 inhibits neural crest migration and miR-203 inhibition causes premature delamination (PMID: 30910825). This points to a developmental role but not specifically a brain or synaptic one. No brain expression data were provided.
- Other experimental findings are mostly oncology-related. PHF12 is a MYC-repressed gene whose translation is regulated by m6A, and it acts as a growth suppressor in B-cell lymphoma (PMID: 33426808). In NSCLC it regulates HDAC1 and EGFR/AKT signaling and promotes tumorigenesis (PMID: 39075515). It also acts as an oncogenic effector in lung squamous cell carcinoma (PMID: 34500373). Expression studies in PDAC (PMID: 38989421) and a urine prostate cancer biomarker (PMID: 26856686) are not relevant to autism.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for a role in chromatin regulation and development, but LIMITED for relevance to autism specifically.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT
- Key strengths: PHF12 has a well-characterized role in the SIN3/HDAC chromatin-modifying complex, a pathway class commonly implicated in neurodevelopmental disorders. Mouse knockout shows it is essential for embryonic development. It also has a role in neural crest development.
- Key gaps: There are no reported patients with heterozygous PHF12 variants and autism or any neurodevelopmental phenotype, and no de novo variant, cohort or segregation data. There are no heterozygous mouse phenotypes and no neurobehavioral data. Brain expression and neuronal function have not been studied. Most functional literature concerns cancer. The only human genetic association is with AMD.
- Conclusion: There is currently no direct evidence supporting PHF12 as an autism spectrum disorder gene with heterozygous pathogenic variants. Its biological plausibility rests on its role in chromatin regulation and developmental essentiality. Clinical case reports with de novo or loss-of-function variants, along with neurodevelopmental models, would be needed before any gene-disease association can be assessed.
