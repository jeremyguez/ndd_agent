ATP2B4 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT MODEL)

1. GENE SUMMARY
ATP2B4 encodes plasma membrane calcium ATPase 4 (PMCA4), a calcium efflux pump that extrudes cytosolic Ca2+ and helps maintain intracellular calcium homeostasis. It is best characterized in red blood cells (where common regulatory variation affects hydration and malaria susceptibility), sperm, smooth muscle and other tissues. It has no established role in neurodevelopmental disorders.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: 1 (PMID: 34368859), a family-based WES study from China.
- Patients: 2, both affected siblings (IV2 and IV4) from a single pedigree.
- Phenotype: autism spectrum disorder only. No additional phenotypic detail was extracted.
- Variant type: a single rare heterozygous missense variant, NM_001001396 c.G1996A, p.A666T (rs138521935), shared by both siblings. It passed the study's harmfulness and quality filters. No loss-of-function variants were reported.
- Cohorts: none of the known cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL).
- Limitations: ATP2B4 was not among the credible autism genes (AutismKB score >16) highlighted by the authors. Segregation is limited to two siblings. No de novo status, functional testing or case-control burden data are available. Sharing a rare variant between siblings is weak evidence by itself, and the variant has an rsID, so it is present in population databases.
- Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED (bordering on NONE for establishing pathogenicity).

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organism: mouse only, with full Pmca4 (Atp2b4) knockouts in three studies.
- Viability and Mendelian ratio: knockouts are viable, with no embryonic lethality and Mendelian inheritance. They are outwardly normal (PMID: 15178683).
- Key phenotypes:
  - Male infertility. Spermatogenesis and mating are normal, but sperm fail to achieve hyperactivated motility and show mitochondrial condensation consistent with Ca2+ overload (PMID: 15178683).
  - Strain-dependent impairment of portal vein smooth muscle contraction and apoptosis in vitro (PMID: 15178683).
  - Reduced red blood cell size and haemoglobin content, with no change in parasite burden and slight protection against experimental cerebral malaria (PMID: 34215257).
  - Normal B-cell development and calcium handling (PMID: 33098669).
- Neurological findings: none reported. No behavioral, social, cognitive or neuroanatomical phenotyping is described.
- Consistency with human phenotype: the red cell phenotype is consistent with human erythroid data, but nothing in the mouse data resembles autism or neurodevelopmental disease. The knockouts are homozygous, so they do not model heterozygous dominant variants.
- Strength of model organism evidence for ASD: NONE to LIMITED.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Function and pathways: PMCA4 is a Ca2+ efflux pump. It is required for EGF-induced Ca2+ oscillations and macropinosome formation in A431 cells, and this depends on pump activity rather than the PDZ-binding motif (PMID: 38597132).
- Expression: PMCA4 is found in red blood cells, distal nephron, intestinal smooth muscle (PMID: 26180241), myometrium (PMID: 33945511), adrenal tissue (PMID: 32002807) and chicken uterus (PMID: 34702171). The extracted evidence includes no brain or neurodevelopmental expression data.
- Key findings, mostly in the red blood cell and malaria context:
  - Erythroid-specific enhancer variants act as eQTLs for ATP2B4. Deleting the enhancer lowers expression and raises intracellular Ca2+ (PMID: 28714864; PMID: 35563239).
  - Reduced RBC PMCA4b is linked to a minor haplotype (PMID: 28216081).
  - Reduced PMCA4b expression and slower calcium clearance in rs1541252 C/C red cells lowered P. falciparum growth (96 donors; p<0.01) (PMID: 36604655).
  - Rare ATP2B4 variants from bilateral adrenal hyperaldosteronism patients had no functional effect on CYP11B2 expression, so no pathogenic role was confirmed (PMID: 32002807).
- Relevance to ASD: calcium signaling is broadly implicated in neurodevelopmental disease, but this is a general argument. No functional data address the p.A666T variant or neuronal function.
- Strength of biological evidence for ASD: LIMITED. The evidence is strong for the gene's role in erythroid and malaria biology but not for ASD.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT

Key strengths:
- ATP2B4 has well-characterized calcium pump function.
- The mouse knockouts are well studied and provide a clear in vivo baseline.
- A rare heterozygous missense variant co-occurs with ASD in two siblings (PMID: 34368859).

Key gaps and limitations:
- There is only one family, with no de novo variants, no additional unrelated cases and no gene-level burden evidence.
- The original authors did not prioritize ATP2B4 as an autism gene.
- The variant has no functional validation.
- Mouse knockouts show no neurological or behavioral phenotype, and neurological assessment is not documented.
- The biological literature concerns erythroid, reproductive, smooth muscle and epithelial functions, with no data on brain expression.
- Heterozygous loss-of-function has not been linked to disease.

Conclusion: There is currently insufficient evidence to support ATP2B4 as an autosomal dominant autism spectrum disorder gene. The single missense variant in one sibling pair is not sufficient for gene-disease association, and neither model organism nor biological data provide neurodevelopmental support. Further unrelated cases, de novo or LoF variants, and neuronal functional studies would be needed to reconsider the association.
