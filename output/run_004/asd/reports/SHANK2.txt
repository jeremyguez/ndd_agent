SHANK2 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
SHANK2 (ProSAP1) encodes a multidomain scaffolding protein of the postsynaptic density at excitatory glutamatergic synapses. It links NMDA/AMPA receptors and other synaptic proteins to the actin cytoskeleton and regulates spine and synapse formation and maturation. Heterozygous loss of function (haploinsufficiency) is the proposed disease mechanism.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: Seven clinical sources were extracted. Two of them (the Berkel 2010 paper and its ASHG 2010 abstract) describe the same cohort, so there are about six independent studies. They are:
  - Berkel et al. 2010 (PMID: 20473310)
  - the multi-center multiple-hit study (ASHG 2011 - Genetic and Functional Analyses of SHANK2 Mutations Support a Multiple Hit Model of Autism Spectrum Disorders)
  - an Italian 11q13.4 deletion case (ASHG 2015 - 11q13.4 deletion disrupting SHANK2 gene in a male with ASD and moderate ID)
  - a French phenotypic-spectrum paper (PMID: 32987185)
  - a US protein-truncating variant/deletion cohort (PMID: 40307697)
  - a Brazilian screening study (ASHG 2018 - Mutation screening in SHANK2 and SHANK3 genes in Brazilian individuals with autism spectrum disorder)
- Patient numbers: Reported counts sum to about 50-60, with overlap between the 2010 and 2011 cohorts. Examples are 10 in Berkel 2010, 15 in the 2011 study, 11 in the French literature-based cohort, 10 in the US cohort, 6 in the Brazilian study and 1 in the Italian case.
- Key observations:
  - De novo deletions and a de novo C-terminal nonsense variant were reported in ASD/ID patients (PMID: 20473310).
  - Three de novo deletions were reported, including a novel 421 kb deletion (ASHG 2011).
  - Two additional de novo variants were reported in the French paper (PMID: 32987185).
  - The Italian deletion was paternally inherited, and the patient's sister had the same phenotype (ASHG 2015).
  - Many missense variants were inherited or also present in controls (ASHG 2011; ASHG 2018), and their significance is uncertain.
- Common phenotypes: ASD (90% in the US cohort, PMID: 40307697), mild to moderate intellectual disability or developmental delay, speech and language delay, and ADHD (50%). Less frequent features were hypotonia, sensory hyperreactivity, recurrent ear infections and GI abnormalities.
- Variant types: The strongest evidence comes from LoF variants (deletions/CNVs, nonsense and protein-truncating variants). Missense variants are numerous but of uncertain significance. In the 2011 study, variants affecting conserved residues were enriched in patients (3.4%) versus controls (1.5%; P=0.004, OR=2.37) (PMID: 22346768). A splice-site variant was found in an unaffected control, and de novo deletion carriers also had inherited 15q11-q13 CNVs. This suggests incomplete penetrance and a multiple-hit model.
- Known cohorts: No UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL cohorts are identified. The cohorts are ASD/ID research collections from Germany/Canada, France/Europe, Italy, Brazil and the USA (remote-assessment cohort).
- Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE. Recurrent de novo LoF events and deletions in multiple cohorts support the association. However, phenotypes are variable, penetrance is incomplete, and inherited and missense variants are poorly resolved.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: Mouse (multiple lines: full KOs with exon 6-7 or exon 7 deletions, conditional and Purkinje-cell-specific KOs, and Shank2/3 double KO) and zebrafish (shank2b-/-).
- Key phenotypes: Mouse KOs are viable, with no lethality reported and no Mendelian ratio deviations reported.
  - Autism-like behaviors: reduced ultrasonic vocalizations with abnormal structure (PMID: 23994547), social deficits (PMID: 33126897), repetitive grooming and hyperactivity (PMID: 22699619).
  - Mania-like behavior responsive to lithium and valproate (PMID: 29046483), and hyperactivity insensitive to methylphenidate (PMID: 30337855).
  - Spatial memory deficits (PMID: 27544825).
  - Synaptic phenotypes: excess silent synapses and enhanced LTP in Δex7 mice (PMID: 30023428), and NMDAR hyperfunction followed by hypofunction (PMID: 30466882).
  - Cerebellar-specific deletion reduces Purkinje cell excitatory synapses and impairs motor coordination (PMID: 27903723).
  - Reduced nociception (PMID: 27145803).
  - The Shank2/3 double KO shows severe autism-related core symptoms (PMID: 36100669).
  - Zebrafish shank2b-/- show reduced social preference, stereotypy, hyperactivity and auditory hypersensitivity, with GABAergic dysfunction (PMID: 36768529).
- Consistency with humans: Social, communication and repetitive-behavior deficits, hyperactivity (ADHD-like) and sensory hyper-reactivity parallel the human phenotypes. Most studies used homozygous KOs, though human disease is heterozygous. Differences between the e6-7 and e7 lines suggest modifier or compensation effects.
- Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG. Multiple independent lines and labs show consistent ASD-relevant behavior, with mechanistic rescue.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: SHANK2 is a postsynaptic scaffold regulating glutamate receptors (NMDAR/AMPAR), spine density and synaptic maturation, and it modulates GABAergic signaling (PMID: 27544825). Outside the brain it forms a CFTR-Shank2-PDE4D complex in epithelia (PMID: 24445315). It also organizes auditory hair bundles downstream of RAP1 (PMID: 40627398), which is potentially relevant to hearing-related features.
- Expression: It is enriched in brain, with extensive isoform complexity. Long-read sequencing found about 350 Shank1/2 isoforms in mouse cortex and about 500 SHANK1-3 isoforms in human (ASHG 2022 - Extensive Transcriptional Complexity of Autism Associated SHANK Family Genes by Capture Based and Long Read sequencing Method). A novel exon 4' transcript in e6-7 KO mice may act as a genetic modifier (PMID: 32252796).
- Key findings:
  - Patient-derived variants reduced synaptic density in neuronal cultures (P=0.0013) (PMID: 22346768).
  - Truncating and several missense variants impaired dendritic spine enlargement (PMID: 20473310).
  - KO mice show reduced spines, altered mEPSCs and upregulated glutamate receptors (PMID: 22699619).
  - SHANK2 is disrupted by somatic structural variants in 10-14% of MYCN-nonamplified high-risk neuroblastomas and acts as a tumor suppressor (PMID: 32796005). This is a non-neurodevelopmental role.
- Strength of biological evidence: STRONG.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: MODERATE to STRONG (assessed as MODERATE-STRONG, with the clinical evidence being the limiting factor).
- Key strengths:
  - Recurrent de novo deletions and truncating variants in ASD/ID patients across independent cohorts.
  - Statistical enrichment of conserved-residue variants in patients (PMID: 22346768).
  - Consistent mouse and zebrafish models with ASD-like behaviors.
  - Functional data showing synaptic deficits, plus therapeutic rescue in mice.
  - A clear role as a synaptic scaffold within the SHANK family.
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - Many patient reports are conference abstracts or have small cohorts, and the cohorts overlap.
  - Missense variants are often inherited or found in controls, so their pathogenicity is uncertain.
  - Penetrance is incomplete, with modifier CNVs and a multiple-hit model.
  - A truncating variant was found in an unaffected control.
  - Individual variants are not specified in several recent papers (PMID: 32987185; PMID: 40307697).
  - Phenotypes are variable.
  - There are no mouse Mendelian ratio or sample-size data, and most mouse work used homozygous rather than heterozygous KOs.
  - None of the cohorts are large rare-disease cohorts such as DDD or GEL.
- Conclusion: SHANK2 haploinsufficiency is reasonably established as a cause of autism spectrum disorder, often with intellectual disability, language delay and ADHD features. The support is recurrent de novo LoF and deletion variants, together with strong concordant animal-model and synaptic biology. Variable expressivity, incomplete penetrance and uncertain missense variants mean that LoF variants and deletions carry the clearest evidence, and missense variants need case-by-case interpretation.
