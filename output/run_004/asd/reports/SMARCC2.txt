SMARCC2 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) PATHOGENIC VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
SMARCC2 (BAF170) is an invariable core subunit of the SWI/SNF (BAF) ATP-dependent chromatin-remodeling complex. It is important in embryogenesis and corticogenesis (PMID: 30580808). Heterozygous variants cause Coffin-Siris syndrome 8 / a SMARCC2-related neurodevelopmental disorder.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: five studies were extracted for the ASD question. Three were ASD cohort or genome/exome studies, one was a single-patient case report, and one was a learning/language disorder cohort. The large SMARCC2 NDD series (65 individuals, PMID: 37551667; 15 individuals, PMID: 30580808) appear only as biological or functional citations, and their clinical details were not extracted here.
- Patients in the extracted ASD-relevant studies: about 7 (1 + 2 + 1 + 2 + 1), with 2 further patients from the learning/language cohort.
- Individual studies:
  - A de novo 233 kb inversion disrupting SMARCC2 (between exons 16 and 17) in a child of Arab descent with CSS8. Features were ASD, ADHD, OCD, speech difficulties and behavioral problems (PMID: 41291750).
  - A de novo LoF variant returned as a genetic diagnosis in an autistic individual (PMID: 40169255).
  - Brazilian ASD cohort: a stop-gain variant, c.415C>T, classified VUS and apparently inherited, which suggests incomplete penetrance or a mild phenotype. A second proband carried a P/LP SMARCC2 variant alongside SCN1A (dual diagnosis) (PMID: 42721435).
  - A Japanese case-control study found one frameshift variant in an ASD or schizophrenia case. It was not formally classified (PMID: 35699097).
  - A French cohort with severe learning and language disorders, without ID or ASD, had 2 patients with P/LP SMARCC2 variants (PMID: 41168819).
- Common phenotypes: ASD, speech and language difficulties, ADHD, OCD, behavioral problems. Broader NDD (ID/DD) is described in the larger series but was not extracted here.
- Variant types: predominantly LoF (de novo inversion, frameshift, stop-gain, de novo LoF). Several variants were unspecified. Larger series report that missense variants in functional domains cause more severe DD than truncating variants (PMID: 37551667).
- Known cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none are identified in the extracted data.
- Strength of clinical evidence for ASD: MODERATE. Several independent de novo or LoF observations support the gene, but variant details are sparse. There is a VUS with inheritance, a dual diagnosis, and an unclassified case. ASD is often accompanied by broader NDD.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- No model organism data were provided. No organisms, knockout phenotypes, lethality, Mendelian ratio data or consistency with human phenotype can be assessed.
- Strength: NONE (from the provided data).

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: SMARCC2 is a core BAF subunit that regulates chromatin accessibility at enhancers and promoters. Reported interactions and roles include:
  - Phosphorylation by RIPK1 in the nucleus (PMID: 35661830).
  - Direct interaction with HSP110, which facilitates SWI/SNF assembly (PMID: 40498025).
  - Binding to c-Myc (PMID: 34080022).
  - Regulation of DKK1 and PI3K-AKT signaling in glioblastoma (PMID: 36418306).
  - Regulation of Ras-PI3K signaling in breast cancer (PMID: 38852505).
- Neurodevelopmental relevance: validated SMARCC2 phosphosites have a role in neuronal differentiation (PMID: 31819260). Fibroblast transcriptomics from affected individuals showed differential expression of H19, SCRG1, RELN and CACNB4 (PMID: 30580808).
- Variant mechanism: N-terminal missense variants decrease protein expression, similar to LoF variants. Non-truncating variants cluster in functional domains (PMID: 37551667). This supports haploinsufficiency as a mechanism and is consistent with the LoF variants seen in ASD.
- Limitations: most functional work is in cancer or virology contexts (glioma, breast cancer, HBV) and is only indirectly relevant to ASD. Brain expression data were not extracted.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: MODERATE for SMARCC2 as a gene associated with ASD through heterozygous variants. Evidence for a broader NDD (CSS8) is stronger than for ASD alone.
- Key strengths:
  - Multiple independent ASD and NDD cohorts report de novo or LoF SMARCC2 variants.
  - Large NDD series (n = 65 and n = 15) establish the disorder.
  - Functional data support a loss-of-function / haploinsufficiency mechanism (PMID: 37551667).
  - SMARCC2 has a clear role as a core BAF chromatin-remodeling subunit.
- Key gaps:
  - No model organism data.
  - Variant-level details are missing for many ASD cases.
  - There is a possibly inherited VUS and a dual diagnosis, which complicate attribution.
  - There is no cohort provenance (UDN, DDD, etc.).
  - ASD-specific functional and brain expression data are lacking.
  - ASD may be one feature of a broader NDD rather than an isolated phenotype.
- Conclusion: Heterozygous, mainly LoF, SMARCC2 variants are credibly linked to a neurodevelopmental disorder in which ASD, language and behavioral features are common. This is supported by de novo cases, a haploinsufficiency mechanism and its role in the BAF complex. Model organism data and fuller phenotyping of ASD cases would strengthen the ASD-specific association.
