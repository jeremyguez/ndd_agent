SLC4A9 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
SLC4A9 (AE4) encodes an electroneutral, monovalent cation-dependent Cl-/HCO3- exchanger. It is a 990-aa, 12-transmembrane-domain protein at 5q31 (PMID: 11305939; PMID: 27114614). It is best characterized in salivary acinar cells and renal intercalated cells, where it supports Cl- uptake and fluid secretion. No neurological function has been established.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent case reports or case series: none were found for autism spectrum disorder (ASD) or neurodevelopmental disorders.
- Total patients: 0 with ASD or neurodevelopmental phenotypes.
- Phenotypes: not applicable. The only human data are non-neurological and come from a 2025 study (PMID: 42855468). That study reports SLC4A9 missense variants in 3 amelogenesis imperfecta cases and copy number variations with reduced SLC4A9 expression in 1 Sjögren's syndrome patient. These are not ASD cases, and the inheritance and pathogenicity of the variants are not established in the summary available.
- Predominant variant type: not assessable for ASD. The amelogenesis imperfecta variants are missense.
- Known cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organism: mouse only. Three full knockout studies were found, all in viable animals (PMID: 25745107; PMID: 42855468; PMID: 17170027).
- Key phenotypes:
  - Saliva secretion is reduced by about 35% in Ae4-/- mice, with reduced HCO3--dependent Cl- uptake (PMID: 25745107).
  - Cl-/HCO3- exchange is nearly abolished in Ae4/Ae2 double knockouts (PMID: 25745107).
  - A 2025 knockout study reports reduced salivation, female-predominant Sjögren's-like salivary inflammation and autoantibodies, enamel malformation, RPS27 upregulation, ribosomal stress, and p53-dependent arrest and apoptosis. Nutlin-3a reduced the inflammation (PMID: 42855468).
  - Duodenal apical Cl-/HCO3- exchange is reduced by less than 5% in the knockout, a negligible contribution (PMID: 17170027).
- Neurological phenotypes, lethality and Mendelian ratio deviations: no neurological or behavioral phenotypes were reported, and the knockouts are viable. No Mendelian ratio data were reported. Heterozygous phenotypes were not described.
- Consistency with human ASD phenotype: none demonstrated. The mouse phenotypes (salivary, enamel, immune) overlap only with the non-neurological human findings.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED for a SLC4A9-related disorder, and NONE for ASD or neurodevelopment.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function:
  - AE4 mediates Na+ (or other monovalent cation)-dependent, electroneutral Cl-/HCO3- exchange (PMID: 27114614).
  - It is activated by PKA, likely through S173 phosphorylation (PMID: 34585968).
  - It is a direct target of the transcription factor Foxi1 (PMID: 16159312).
  - It is induced in a compensatory NaCl reabsorption network in SPAK-deficient mice (PMID: 25893600).
  - It is HIF-dependent and hypoxia-induced in cancer cells, and its knockdown acidifies intracellular pH and reduces tumor growth (PMID: 27197160).
- Expression: expression is largely restricted to kidney, with 14 splice isoforms (PMID: 11305939). Expression in salivary glands, type B intercalated cells and chicken uterus is also reported (PMID: 16159312; PMID: 29115887). Brain or neurodevelopmental expression is not documented in the supplied evidence.
- Other findings: a methylation association with all-cause mortality (PMID: 31642367) is not relevant to ASD.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for molecular function, but NONE to LIMITED for relevance to ASD or the brain.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT for SLC4A9 as an autosomal dominant ASD gene.
- Key strengths: the transport function is well characterized, and knockout mice have a consistent epithelial secretory phenotype (PMID: 27114614; PMID: 25745107).
- Key gaps:
  - There are no ASD or neurodevelopmental patients and no heterozygous variant data.
  - There is no brain expression data and no neurobehavioral phenotyping of knockout mice.
  - The limited human data concern amelogenesis imperfecta and Sjögren's syndrome, not ASD.
  - There is no evidence for a dominant or haploinsufficiency mechanism.
- Conclusion: there is currently no clinical, model organism or biological evidence linking heterozygous SLC4A9 variants to autism spectrum disorder. The gene's known roles are in epithelial ion transport, with emerging links to salivary gland and enamel disease. Supporting an ASD association would require de novo or segregating variants in affected individuals, brain expression data, and neurobehavioral phenotyping of mutant mice.
