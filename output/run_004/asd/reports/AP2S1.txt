AP2S1 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
AP2S1 encodes the sigma-2 subunit (AP2σ) of the adaptor protein 2 (AP2) complex, which mediates clathrin-mediated endocytosis and regulates calcium-sensing receptor (CaSR) trafficking and signaling (PMID: 27647839). Its best-established human disease association is familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia type 3 (FHH3), caused by recurrent heterozygous missense variants at codon Arg15. This is a calcium-homeostasis disorder, not a neurodevelopmental one.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent case reports/case series for AP2S1 and ASD: 0. The clinical evidence extracted for this report is empty.
- Total patients with ASD-related phenotypes: none identified in the supplied data.
- Phenotypes: no ASD, neurodevelopmental or behavioral phenotypes were documented in the clinical findings. Outside the clinical section, FHH3 cohorts are described. These include 19 FHH3 families in one summary (PMID: 26963950), 5 of 39 CASR-negative FHH patients (13%) with R15C/R15L/R15H (PMID: 24731014), and 2 of 33 Danish CASR-negative patients (~6%) with Arg15Leu/Arg15His (PMID: 27913609). A review notes that FHH3 can include cognitive dysfunction (PMID: 27647839). A zebrafish review states that human AP2S1 variants are associated with ASD, ADHD, learning disability and cognitive deficits (PMID: 41323234), but no primary human case data were provided.
- Predominant variant type: the FHH3 variants are recurrent Arg15 missense changes. The mechanism is described as loss of function, though the Arg15 variants impair the interaction with CaSR and with other AP2 subunits (PMID: 33729479). No variants specific to ASD were reported.
- Known cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence for ASD: NONE

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse and zebrafish.
- Mouse:
  - Heterozygous Ap2s1 p.Arg15Leu knock-in mice were viable and showed hypercalcaemia, hypermagnesaemia, hypophosphataemia, raised ALP and FGF23. Homozygotes died perinatally. This model recapitulates FHH3 and responds to cinacalcet (PMID: 33729479).
  - ENU-induced del17 heterozygotes were viable with normal calcium and electrolyte homeostasis. Homozygotes died between E3.5 and E9.5 (PMID: 29479578).
  - No behavioral or neurological phenotyping of the mice was reported in the supplied data.
- Zebrafish:
  - ap2s1 mutant larvae showed impaired habituation learning and altered acoustically evoked behavioral selection (PMID: 38550987).
  - Ap2s1 promotes habituation by antagonizing dopamine signaling (PMID: 36972301).
  - A review reports severe acoustic startle habituation deficits (PMID: 41323234).
  - Habituation deficits are relevant to ASD, and the zebrafish work is framed as a model of "autism-linked ap2s1" (PMID: 38550987).
- Consistency with human phenotype: the mouse data fit FHH3 and show that complete loss is embryonic or perinatal lethal. The heterozygous del17 haploinsufficiency mice show no calcium phenotype, which is relevant to a dominant LoF mechanism for any neurodevelopmental disorder. The zebrafish behavioral data are suggestive of a neurobehavioral role but are not direct evidence of ASD. Sample sizes and statistics were not reported.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED (the zebrafish habituation findings are relevant but indirect, and the mouse evidence concerns calcium homeostasis and lethality)

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: AP2σ is a component of the AP2 clathrin-adaptor complex, which mediates clathrin-mediated endocytosis and regulates CaSR trafficking (PMID: 27647839). AP2S1 interacts with the E3 ligase RNF41, which stabilizes and relocalizes it, and AP2S1 has a role in leptin and LIF receptor signaling (PMID: 29560723). It cooperates with HAVCR1 in Zika virus internalization (PMID: 40156834). In zebrafish, it acts in a dopamine-antagonizing habituation module (PMID: 36972301).
- Expression: no brain or neurodevelopmental expression data were provided. Expression data concern intervertebral disc degeneration mesenchymal stromal cells (PMID: 42253998) and leukocyte methylation in obesity with hypercholesterolemia (PMID: 33028190), neither of which bears on ASD.
- Key findings: AP2σ is essential for early embryonic development (PMID: 29479578). Clathrin-mediated endocytosis is broadly relevant to synaptic function, but this was not directly tested in the supplied data.
- Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT for AP2S1 as an autosomal dominant ASD gene.
- Strengths: the gene has a clear and well-characterized function in clathrin-mediated endocytosis. Heterozygous Arg15 variants cause a well-validated dominant disorder (FHH3) with supporting mouse models (PMID: 33729479). Zebrafish mutants show habituation deficits and altered behavioral selection, which are ASD-relevant endophenotypes (PMID: 38550987, PMID: 36972301).
- Gaps and limitations:
  - No clinical case reports or cohorts of ASD with AP2S1 variants were provided, and there are no de novo variant data or statistical burden evidence.
  - Human ASD association is asserted only in a review (PMID: 41323234) without primary data.
  - Mouse heterozygotes show no neurobehavioral assessment, and the heterozygous del17 mice show no overt phenotype.
  - Brain expression data are absent.
  - The recurrent Arg15 variants act through a calcium-sensing mechanism, and the relationship between that mechanism and ASD is unresolved.
- Conclusion: the supplied evidence does not support a definitive link between heterozygous AP2S1 variants and ASD. The only established dominant phenotype is FHH3. Zebrafish habituation data give a plausible neurobehavioral rationale, but robust clinical evidence, such as de novo variants in ASD cohorts, is needed before the gene can be classified as an ASD gene.
