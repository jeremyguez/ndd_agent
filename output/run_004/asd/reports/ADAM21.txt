ADAM21 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT MODEL)

1. GENE SUMMARY
ADAM21 encodes a member of the ADAM (a disintegrin and metalloproteinase) family. It is an intronless gene in humans with testis-enriched expression (PMID: 10524237). Its function is poorly defined. It is a presumed membrane-anchored proteolytic and adhesion protein, with reported expression in the rodent neurogenic niche and growing axons (PMID: 16052496).

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent case reports or case series: 0. No clinical reports were identified.
- Total patients: 0.
- Phenotypes: none reported. There are no ASD, neurodevelopmental, or other human phenotype data linked to ADAM21 variants.
- Variant type: not applicable. No LoF, missense, or other pathogenic variants have been described in patients.
- Cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms studied: mouse only. This was a CRISPR/Cas9 full knockout (PMID: 34631320).
- Key phenotypes: Adam21-null mice were viable. Males had normal fertility, spermatogenesis, sperm motility, and meiotic progression. Seminiferous epithelium histology was normal, and Sertoli and Leydig cells appeared normal. The study concluded that Adam21 is dispensable for male fertility and reproductive processes (PMID: 34631320). No lethality was reported. Mendelian ratios and sample size were not reported in the extracted data. No neurological or behavioral phenotyping (for example social behavior, cognition, or repetitive behaviors) was described, and heterozygous animals were not specifically assessed.
- Consistency with human phenotype: there is no human phenotype for comparison. The absence of an overt phenotype in the knockout does not support an essential role for the gene, and it does not inform on ASD. Possible redundancy among ADAM family members is plausible but not demonstrated.
- Strength of model organism evidence: NONE for ASD. The only knockout is reproductive-focused and reports no overt phenotype, so it provides no supporting evidence.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and molecular function: ADAM21 is a putative metalloproteinase-disintegrin. A review notes a possible role in regenerative processes after neuronal injury, together with ADAM19 (PMID: 31236626). It was identified as a membrane protein associated with α-dystrobrevin in etoposide-treated HL-60 cells (PMID: 22507200). This was a proteomic finding without functional follow-up.
- Expression: in humans it is intronless and testis-specific (PMID: 10524237). In mice, germ cell transcripts contain exons 1 and 2, and exon 2-only transcripts occur in testicular somatic cells (PMID: 20117247). Of neurodevelopmental relevance, ADAM21 is expressed in adult rodent ependyma and subventricular zone cells, in early postnatal radial glia, in the rostral migratory stream, and in growing axonal tracts and olfactory axons. This suggests roles in neuroblast migration, neurogenesis, and axonal outgrowth (PMID: 16052496). A review highlights it as uniquely associated with neurogenesis and growing axons in the adult brain (PMID: 16824663).
- Key experimental findings outside the nervous system: in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), ADAM21 knockdown in KYN-2 cells reduced invasion, migration, proliferation, and metastasis. ADAM21 positivity in 119 patient tumors predicted poorer survival (overall survival P=0.003; recurrence-free survival P=0.001) (PMID: 29138461). ADAM21 was also among genes targeted by HBV integration in HCC (PMID: 23236287). An ADAM21-like gene (ADAM21-L) at 14q24 was reported near an inversion breakpoint in one adult T-cell leukemia patient (PMID: 16520872). These findings are not relevant to ASD.
- Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED. Rodent CNS expression is of some neurodevelopmental interest, but there is no functional, pathway, or human genetic link to ASD.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT
- Key strengths: rodent expression data place Adam21 in neurogenic niches and developing axons (PMID: 16052496), and a viable knockout mouse model exists (PMID: 34631320).
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - There are no human cases or variants and no cohort or statistical genetic evidence for ASD.
  - The only knockout shows no overt phenotype and was not assessed for neurobehavioral traits.
  - Human expression is largely testis-restricted, and the gene is intronless, with pseudogene or paralog complexity (PMID: 10524237, PMID: 16520872).
  - The functional literature is dominated by cancer and reproductive biology, and neural function is inferred only from expression data and reviews.
  - No evidence addresses a heterozygous or dominant mechanism.
- Conclusion: There is currently no clinical, model organism, or mechanistic evidence supporting ADAM21 as an autosomal dominant ASD gene. The limited neural expression data are hypothesis-generating only, and the null mouse shows no overt phenotype. ADAM21 should not be considered an ASD candidate unless human variant data, such as de novo LoF or missense variants in neurodevelopmental cohorts, emerge.
