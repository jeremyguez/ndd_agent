TMEM184C literature evidence report: autism spectrum disorder, heterozygous (autosomal dominant) variants

1. GENE SUMMARY
TMEM184C encodes a multi-pass transmembrane protein of poorly defined function. It belongs to the TMEM184 family and has no well-established role in neurodevelopment. The input data contain no functional characterization of it.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent case reports or series: 1 study mentions TMEM184C (PMID: 41260215). It is a report on bi-allelic PRMT9 loss-of-function variants, not a TMEM184C study.
- Total patients: 1 (individual Z-II.1, family Z). This person carried a single-copy gain (duplication) spanning the last exons of PRMT9 and TMEM184C. Exact coordinates were not given in the excerpt.
- Phenotypes: Individual-level phenotype was not given. The overall PRMT9 cohort is described as having global developmental delay, learning disabilities, intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, epilepsy and hypotonia. These features cannot be attributed to this individual or to TMEM184C.
- Variant type: The only variant is a copy-number gain (duplication) involving two genes. There are no LoF, missense or other intragenic TMEM184C variants.
- Cohorts: No known cohort (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL) is identified. Center and country were not reported.
- Attribution: The authors considered the duplication relevant to PRMT9, as a possible bi-allelic PRMT9 loss-of-function mechanism. No second PRMT9 variant was found in trans by Sanger sequencing or WES. Whole-genome sequencing was performed. The authors claim no pathogenic role for TMEM184C. TMEM184C was only co-involved in the CNV.
- Heterozygous inheritance: The data contain no evidence of a de novo or inherited heterozygous TMEM184C variant segregating with autism spectrum disorder.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms studied: None reported.
- Knockout phenotypes: No data on neurological phenotypes, lethality or Mendelian ratio deviations.
- Consistency with human phenotype: Cannot be assessed.
- Strength of model organism evidence: NONE

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and molecular function: No findings were provided.
- Expression pattern: No brain or neurodevelopmental expression data were provided.
- Key experimental findings: None.
- Strength of biological evidence: NONE

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT
- Key strengths: None specific to TMEM184C. The one study (PMID: 41260215) is transparent that the CNV was interpreted in the context of PRMT9, which limits the risk of overinterpretation.
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - There are no patients with intragenic TMEM184C variants.
  - The single contributing observation is a multigene duplication with no individual phenotype given.
  - The duplication is attributed to PRMT9, not TMEM184C.
  - There are no segregation, de novo or functional data.
  - There is no model organism or biological evidence.

Conclusion: There is currently no evidence supporting TMEM184C as an autism spectrum disorder gene with heterozygous pathogenic variants. Its only appearance is as a bystander gene in a PRMT9-region duplication (PMID: 41260215), where the authors do not claim a role for it. Any gene-disease association would require new cases with intragenic, preferably de novo, variants together with functional or model organism support.
