CAMK2A LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
CAMK2A encodes the alpha subunit of calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II. It is a highly abundant neuronal serine/threonine kinase in the postsynaptic density. It is central to NMDA receptor-dependent long-term potentiation, synaptic plasticity, and learning, and it is regulated by Thr286 autophosphorylation. Heterozygous variants are associated with intellectual developmental disorder, autosomal dominant 53 (MRD53).

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: Five clinical entries were extracted. Three describe individual patients with specific variants. Two are cohort studies that lack per-patient detail.
  - Stephenson et al. 2017 (PMID: 28130356): one proband with de novo p.Glu183Val and ASD.
  - Italian case (PMID: 37510258): de novo p.Lys292del with severe ID and epileptic encephalopathy.
  - Türkiye ASD WES study (PMID: 41751633): de novo p.Arg104Trp, classified likely pathogenic (PM2, PS2, PP2, PP3, PP5), with ASD, hearing loss and behavioral abnormalities.
  - Saudi Arabian ASD WES study (PMID: 42606785): CAMK2A listed among recurrently affected genes; no variant or patient number given.
  - Indian trio study (ASHG 2023 - Deciphering the genetic architecture of autism spectrum disorder using chromosomal microarray and whole exome sequencing in patient-parent trios in India): one de novo SNV in CAMK2A among 101 trios; variant unspecified.
- Approximate patients: about 3 patients with specific variants, plus roughly 2 or more unspecified ASD cases from cohorts, so about 5 or more in the ASD-focused literature. In addition, Kury et al. 2017 (PMID: 29100089) reported de novo CAMK2A/CAMK2B variants in 24 unrelated individuals with ID (19 variants). The CAMK2A-specific share was not given, and the ASD phenotype was not detailed. An ASHG 2018 abstract (De novo variants affecting autoinhibition of Calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II, cause neurodevelopmental disorders) described 3 CAMK2A individuals.
- Phenotypes: ASD (the main phenotype in 4 of 5 entries), ID, epileptic encephalopathy, behavioral abnormalities, and hearing loss in one patient.
- Variant type: Predominantly de novo missense (E183V, R104W) plus one in-frame deletion (K292del). No clear loss-of-function (truncating) variants were reported in this dataset. The mechanism may involve altered autophosphorylation, either gain or loss of function, or dominant-negative effects.
- Cohorts: No UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL cohorts were identified. Patients came from Italy, Türkiye, Saudi Arabia and India.
- Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE. Multiple de novo variants and a recognized NDD syndrome support it. However, the ASD-specific cases are few, with limited phenotyping, and several are unspecified in the abstracts.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse (knockout, conditional, and knock-in) and zebrafish (morpholino knockdown).
- Mouse:
  - Full Camk2a knockout mice show deficits in spatial learning, conditional learning and synaptic plasticity. Adult re-expression rescued these deficits (PMID: 36304100).
  - Knockouts also show decreased sleep duration (PMID: 26996081).
  - Conditional forebrain deletion impairs water maze and contextual fear learning. Adult-onset deletion was as damaging as germline deletion (PMID: 25143599).
  - Knock-in mice carrying the human E183V variant show ASD-related behaviors, dendritic and synaptic abnormalities, and reduced autophosphorylation (PMID: 28130356).
  - Heterozygous P212L knock-in mice show gain-of-function autophosphorylation, spine abnormalities, exaggerated LTP and learning deficits resembling human ID/NDD (PMID: 40140673).
- Zebrafish: Morpholino knockdown decreased the slow NMDA mEPSC component, increased Mauthner cell dendritic arborization, and altered touch-response behavior (PMID: 25047640).
- Lethality, Mendelian ratios and sample sizes were not reported.
- Consistency: Cognitive, synaptic and behavioral phenotypes are consistent with human ID/ASD. The knock-in models of patient variants are especially relevant.
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE to STRONG. Overall rated MODERATE-STRONG; the evidence is concordant, but knockout data alone are not direct ASD models, and the variant knock-ins provide the most direct support.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: CAMK2A acts in glutamatergic (AMPAR/NMDAR) signaling and regulates synaptic plasticity through Thr286 autophosphorylation (PMID: 29100089).
- Expression: Camk2a is strongly expressed in forebrain excitatory neurons and also in striatal cholinergic interneurons (PMID: 34349780). This complicates interpretation of Camk2a-Cre conditional knockouts.
- Key findings:
  - Patient de novo CAMK2A/CAMK2B variants either increase or decrease Thr286/287 autophosphorylation, and all of those variants impaired neuronal migration (PMID: 29100089).
  - E183V reduces substrate phosphorylation and autophosphorylation, with a dominant-negative effect (PMID: 28130356).
  - Variants affecting autoinhibition, a splice variant causing exon 11 skipping, and P212Q increasing A-type K+ currents (ASHG 2018 - De novo variants affecting autoinhibition of Calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase II, cause neurodevelopmental disorders).
  - Non-neurological roles of CAMK2A in macrophages, glioma and muscle (e.g., PMID: 38936066, PMID: 35287547, PMID: 38015546) are not relevant to ASD.
- Strength of biological evidence: STRONG. Functional data on patient variants, a well-defined synaptic role, and concordant knock-in data support this.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: MODERATE (leaning toward STRONG for neurodevelopmental disorder in general; moderate for ASD specifically).
- Key strengths: Recurrent de novo variants; functional validation showing altered kinase activity; knock-in mouse models reproducing ASD-like and ID-like phenotypes; a well-characterized role in synaptic plasticity.
- Key gaps: Few ASD-specific patients with detailed phenotyping; cohort studies without variant-level data (Saudi and Indian reports); no truncating variants reported, so haploinsufficiency is not established; mixed gain- and loss-of-function mechanisms; no data from large consortia (DDD, UDN, etc.) in this dataset; ASD is often accompanied by ID, so ASD specificity is uncertain.
- Conclusion: CAMK2A is a well-supported neurodevelopmental disorder gene, with heterozygous de novo missense and in-frame variants causing ID with frequent ASD features. Functional and mouse evidence support pathogenicity through altered CaMKII autophosphorylation and synaptic function. Evidence for ASD as a distinct phenotype is moderate and would benefit from larger, well-phenotyped cohorts.
