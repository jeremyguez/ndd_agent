LMTK3 (lemur tyrosine kinase 3) is a member of the LMTK family of serine/threonine/tyrosine-type kinases. It is highly expressed in the brain and is implicated in endosomal/receptor trafficking, including NMDA and AMPA receptors. It also has a well-studied oncogenic role in breast and other cancers.

CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: 1, a large exome-sequencing study of simplex autism families (Iossifov et al. 2012, PMID: 22542183).
- Total patients: about 1, a male proband from family 13092.
- Phenotype: autism spectrum disorder only. No further clinical detail is given.
- Variant type: a single de novo frameshift insertion, ins(AGGTCAG), predicted loss of function (LoF), at about residue 307 of 1,490. The exact HGVS notation is not given, and the row assignment was inferred from a garbled table.
- Cohort: Simons Simplex Collection families. This is not UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL.
- Supporting context: the study found a cohort-level excess of de novo gene-disrupting mutations in affected children over unaffected siblings. There is no gene-specific segregation, recurrence or functional evidence for LMTK3.
- Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED (close to minimal). It rests on a single de novo LoF case and no recurrence.

MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organism: mouse only, with full Lmtk3 knockouts in two independent studies.
- Behavioral phenotypes (PMID: 31310731):
  - Knockouts are viable.
  - They show hyper-sociability, prepulse inhibition (PPI) deficits and cognitive dysfunction.
  - Behavioral abnormalities linked to psychiatric disease are suppressed by clozapine.
  - Long-term potentiation (LTP) induction is severely impaired.
  - GluA1 trafficking after AMPA stimulation is abnormal, and GluA1 in the postsynaptic density is reduced.
- Locomotor and neurochemical phenotypes (PMID: 24760852):
  - Knockouts are viable.
  - They show pronounced locomotor hyperactivity, reduced anxiety-like behavior and decreased depression-like behavior.
  - Striatal dopamine metabolites and dopamine turnover are increased.
  - NMDA receptor endocytic trafficking is impaired in cultured primary neurons.
  - A citing review reports no gross brain histological abnormalities.
- Mendelian ratios, fertility and sample sizes were not reported. Heterozygous phenotypes are not described.
- Consistency with human phenotype: partial. The neurobehavioral and cognitive abnormalities and the synaptic dysfunction are relevant to neurodevelopmental and psychiatric disorders. However, the social phenotype is hyper-sociability rather than the social deficits typical of autism, and the effects are seen in homozygous knockouts rather than in a heterozygous model.
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE. Two independent knockout studies show consistent neurobehavioral and synaptic phenotypes, but none models autism-like social deficits or the heterozygous state.

BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Molecular function: LMTK3 is a kinase, and its 2.1 Å kinase-domain crystal structure has been solved. It is an HSP90 client (PMID: 33188023). It phosphorylates Rab7 and affects extracellular vesicle biogenesis and cargo sorting (PMID: 40405280). It interacts with KAP1 and affects chromatin remodeling (PMID: 26212333). It interacts with DDX5 and affects miRNA processing (PMID: 26739063). It regulates KIT translation (PMID: 30242244).
- Pathways: ERK/MAPK signaling (PMID: 32865871) and ERα/PKC/AKT signaling (PMID: 30944027).
- Expression: the available data come largely from tumors, such as elevated levels in thyroid carcinoma (PMID: 28260052). Brain expression is not described in the supplied findings, apart from the neuronal trafficking data in the mouse studies.
- Neuronal relevance: it comes from the mouse studies, which implicate LMTK3 in glutamate receptor trafficking (GluA1, NMDA receptors) and LTP (PMID: 31310731, PMID: 24760852).
- The bulk of the biological literature concerns oncogenesis, including chemoresistance (PMID: 29540829), and does not address neurodevelopmental mechanisms or the effect of human variants.
- Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED to MODERATE for autism specifically. LMTK3 function is well characterized in general, but neurodevelopmental relevance rests mainly on the mouse trafficking data.

OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: LIMITED.
- Key strengths:
  - A de novo LoF variant was found in an autism proband.
  - Two independent knockout mouse studies show neurobehavioral, cognitive and synaptic deficits.
  - There is a plausible mechanism through glutamate receptor trafficking.
  - The kinase structure and biochemistry are well characterized.
- Key gaps:
  - Only one human case, with no recurrence, no segregation evidence and no functional testing of the variant.
  - Imprecise variant annotation.
  - No heterozygous mouse data.
  - Hyper-sociability in knockouts does not match autism-type social deficits.
  - Biological studies are mostly oncologic.
  - No gene-level constraint data or burden analyses were provided.
- Conclusion: LMTK3 is a candidate autism gene with a single de novo frameshift in a simplex case and supportive but indirect mouse and synaptic-trafficking evidence. Without additional unrelated patients carrying LoF variants and evidence of a heterozygous mechanism, its association with autosomal dominant autism spectrum disorder cannot be considered established, and it remains limited.

### Clinical Evidence

### Model Organism Evidence
1. Lmtk3-KO Mice Display a Range of Behavioral Abnormalities and Have an Impairment in GluA1 Trafficking (PMID: 31310731)
2. LMTK3 deficiency causes pronounced locomotor hyperactivity and impairs endocytic trafficking (PMID: 24760852)

### Biological Evidence
1. The structure-function relationship of oncogenic LMTK3 (PMID: 33188023)
2. LMTK3 regulation of EV biogenesis and cargo sorting promotes tumour growth by reducing monocyte infiltration and driving pro-tumourigenic macrophage polarisation in breast cancer (PMID: 40405280)
3. LMTK3 Represses Tumor Suppressor-like Genes through Chromatin Remodeling in Breast Cancer (PMID: 26212333)
4. LMTK3 escapes tumour suppressor miRNAs via sequestration of DDX5 (PMID: 26739063)
5. LMTK3 is essential for oncogenic KIT expression in KIT-mutant GIST and melanoma (PMID: 30242244)
6. LMTK3 promotes tumorigenesis in bladder cancer via the ERK/MAPK pathway (PMID: 32865871)
7. The lncRNA MIR2052HG regulates ERα levels and aromatase inhibitor resistance through LMTK3 by recruiting EGR1 (PMID: 30944027)
8. LMTK3 knockdown retards cell growth and invasion and promotes apoptosis in thyroid cancer (PMID: 28260052)
9. LMTK3 confers chemo-resistance in breast cancer (PMID: 29540829)
