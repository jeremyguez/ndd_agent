NCKAP1 EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER / NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
NCKAP1 (NCK-associated protein 1, also called NAP1/Hem2) is a core subunit of the WAVE regulatory complex (WRC). The WRC activates Arp2/3-dependent actin polymerization downstream of RAC1, and this drives lamellipodia formation, cell migration and morphogenesis. The gene is highly expressed in the developing brain.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: 7 publications were found. Two are larger series or cohort analyses (PMID: 33157009; PMID: 40836247), and one is a mosaic-variant study (PMID: 27632392). Four are individual or family case reports (PMID: 41462819; PMID: 40551827; PMID: 41531171) plus the cohort-level studies above. The ID family and the ASHG 2013 resequencing study (listed under biological evidence) add supporting data.
- Total patients: about 28 from the clinical extraction. This includes 21 in Guo/Chen et al. 2020 (PMID: 33157009), 1 in each of PMID: 41462819, PMID: 40551827 and PMID: 27632392, 2 in PMID: 41531171, and 2 in PMID: 40836247. Some overlap with other cohorts is possible, and this was not assessed.
- Key cohort study: Guo et al. 2020 (PMID: 33157009) described 21 individuals from 20 families. Of these, 16 carried truncating variants (8 de novo, 6 transmitted, 2 unknown inheritance), 2 had structural variants, and 3 had de novo missense variants. Transmitting parents often had neuropsychiatric disorders, so penetrance and expressivity are variable.
- Other variants: Specific de novo truncating variants include p.Ser986Hisfs*34 (PMID: 41462819), p.Gly169ValfsTer14 and p.Glu1082Ter (PMID: 40836247), and a canonical splice variant, c.2021+1G>A, segregating in a father and daughter (PMID: 41531171). A truncating NCKAP1 variant also segregated in a large family with mild autosomal dominant ID (PMID: 28940097).
- Common phenotypes: autism or autistic features (nearly universal), developmental, language and motor delay, and variable intellectual or learning disability. Less frequent findings include epilepsy, craniofacial dysmorphism, nail dystrophy and inflammatory skin lesions (one family, PMID: 41531171), and congenital heart disease (total anomalous pulmonary venous return, one case, PMID: 41462819). Expressivity varies considerably between families (PMID: 40836247).
- Predominant variant type: loss of function (nonsense, frameshift, canonical splice), plus a few structural variants and de novo missense variants of uncertain significance.
- Known cohorts: none of UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL were identified in the extracted data. Case ascertainment came from ASD cohorts and NDD collaborations. The Guo et al. cohort includes de novo and ultra-rare variant burden analyses in NDD cohorts.
- Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG. This rests on multiple independent de novo and segregating LoF variants, a case series of over 20 patients, and recurrent ASD ascertainment. Caveats are variable expressivity and incomplete penetrance.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse, zebrafish and Xenopus.
- Mouse: In utero electroporation of Nckap1 knockdown altered cortical neuronal migration, with promotion of migration in early cortical development (PMID: 33157009). This is a plausible neurodevelopmental mechanism. A melanocyte-specific conditional knockout delayed tumor progression in a Braf;Pten model (PMID: 32777214), but this is not neurologically relevant.
- Zebrafish: CRISPR knockdown of nckap1 disrupted cardiac development (PMID: 32368696). This is relevant to the congenital heart defect reported in one patient (PMID: 41462819).
- Xenopus: nckap1 is enriched in the neural tube, and knockdown impaired neural tube closure (PMID: 26434722).
- Gaps: no germline heterozygous knockout mouse with behavioral or social phenotypes, and no lethality or Mendelian ratio data were reported. Sample sizes were not provided.
- Consistency: neuronal migration and neural tube defects fit a neurodevelopmental disorder, and the cardiac phenotype fits the single congenital heart disease case. Autism-relevant behavior has not been modeled.
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE to LIMITED. I rate it LIMITED-to-MODERATE because the data are knockdown-based and lack organism-level behavioral phenotyping.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: NCKAP1 is a WRC component and stabilizes the complex (with CYFIP1, WASF proteins and others). It links RAC1 signaling to Arp2/3 actin nucleation. NCKAP1 silencing destabilizes the WASF3-NCKAP1-CYFIP1 complex and blocks RAC1 association (PMID: 27432794). In B16-F1 cells, disrupting Nap1 together with Hem1 shows that lamellipodia-like structures can form without the WRC but lack Ena/VASP recruitment (PMID: 35971979). NCKAP1 depletion partially rescued disulfidptosis, which places NCKAP1 in actin-dynamics-driven cell death (PMID: 39715824).
- Expression: NCKAP1 is broadly and highly expressed in the prenatal and postnatal human brain, with enrichment in excitatory neurons and radial glia and depletion in inhibitory neurons (PMID: 33157009). Expression in human brain also supported candidacy in the ID family (PMID: 28940097).
- Genetic and population support: targeted resequencing of 64 candidate genes in more than 4,000 ASD probands and 2,200 siblings implicated NCKAP1 as a novel risk gene with severe mutations in sporadic ASD (ASHG 2013 - Recurrently mutated genes contribute to the risk for developing sporadic autism spectrum disorder). A multi-tissue mosaic de novo variant was found in an ASD proband (PMID: 27632392). De novo variants in WAVE2 complex genes were enriched in 2,881 CHD probands (PMID: 32368696).
- Other: Cancer studies show NCKAP1 acts as a tumor suppressor in HCC via Rb1/p53 (PMID: 31068575) and in ccRCC (PMID: 35957696). These are not relevant to neurodevelopment.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE. The WRC and actin pathway is well established and brain expression is consistent. Direct functional testing of patient variants is absent, and much of the mechanistic literature comes from cancer biology.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: STRONG (for an autosomal dominant NDD with prominent autism, via haploinsufficiency).
- Key strengths:
  - Over 20 reported patients with predominantly LoF variants, many de novo.
  - Segregation in several families.
  - Replication across independent ASD and NDD cohorts and recent case reports.
  - A consistent phenotype of autism with developmental and language delay.
  - Convergent support from mouse migration data and from WRC biology.
- Key gaps:
  - Variable expressivity and incomplete penetrance, with transmitting parents showing neuropsychiatric phenotypes.
  - Uncertain significance of the missense and structural variants.
  - No functional assays of patient variants.
  - No germline animal model with autism-relevant behavior.
  - Phenotypic expansion (cardiac, skin) based on single reports.
  - Cohort overlap and lack of explicit penetrance estimates.
- Conclusion: Heterozygous loss-of-function variants in NCKAP1 cause a neurodevelopmental disorder with core features of autism. Evidence comes from multiple de novo and inherited truncating variants in independent cohorts, supported by a brain-expressed WRC role and altered neuronal migration in mouse. NCKAP1 can be considered an established ASD/NDD gene. The main work still needed is penetrance and genotype-phenotype refinement, variant-level functional validation, and germline animal models.
