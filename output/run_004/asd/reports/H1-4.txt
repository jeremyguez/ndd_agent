H1-4 (HIST1H1E; histone H1.4) LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) PATHOGENIC VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
H1-4 encodes the replication-dependent linker histone H1.4. It binds nucleosomes and linker DNA and promotes higher-order chromatin compaction. Its positively charged C-terminal domain neutralizes linker DNA and supports chromatin condensation and phase separation. Heterozygous C-terminal frameshift variants cause Rahman syndrome, a neurodevelopmental disorder.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: Two clinical reports were extracted: a case report (PMID: 29704315) and a series (PMID: 34788807). A third study (PMID: 28475857), an overgrowth/intellectual disability cohort, is catalogued as biological evidence but also contains human mutation data.
- Patient numbers: The case report describes 1 patient. The series (PMID: 34788807) does not give a patient count in the abstract. The overgrowth cohort (PMID: 28475857) included 710 individuals with overgrowth and intellectual disability, 353 with a causal mutation. HIST1H1E was one of six epigenetic regulator genes that together accounted for 44% of cases, but the number of HIST1H1E carriers is not stated. The exact total number of patients cannot be determined from the extracted data. It is a small number from these sources, although the broader Rahman syndrome literature is larger.
- Phenotypes: Intellectual disability is the core feature. Others include autism spectrum disorder, hypotonia, anxiety, vision problems, bone abnormalities, and dysmorphic facies. Overgrowth was reported in the cohort study (PMID: 28475857). ASD was explicitly reported in the single-patient case (PMID: 29704315) and in the series (PMID: 34788807).
- Variant type: Predominantly C-terminal frameshift variants, which are functionally LoF/truncating but which likely act through an altered, charge-reduced C-terminal tail rather than simple haploinsufficiency. An example is c.435dupC, p.Thr146Hisfs*50, which arose de novo (PMID: 29704315). Novel N-terminal variants were also noted (PMID: 34788807), with details unavailable. Variants cluster at the 3' end, consistent with a shared mechanism.
- Cohorts: No UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL involvement is identified in the extracted data. The case was found by clinical exome sequencing.
- Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE. The gene-disease relationship with a neurodevelopmental disorder (Rahman syndrome) is well supported by recurrent de novo C-terminal frameshifts. ASD is a recurrent but non-exclusive feature, and the ASD-specific evidence is limited to few described patients.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: Mouse only.
- Key findings: In H1f2/H1f4 double-knockout mice, maturation of chromatin compaction in cerebellar granule neurons is disrupted. H1.4 accumulates in heterochromatin during neuronal maturation, and epitope-tagged H1f4 knock-in mice show robust H1.4 expression throughout the brain, including mature postmitotic neurons (PMID: 42619895). No lethality, Mendelian ratio, or behavioral/ASD-relevant data are reported. In H1t-null mice, H1.4 expression was compensatorily increased, and the mice were viable and fertile (PMID: 10933220). That study did not knock out H1-4 itself, so it is only indirectly informative.
- Consistency with human phenotype: Partially consistent. The neuronal chromatin phenotype supports a role in neuronal maturation, but there are no neurobehavioral, ASD-like, or single-gene heterozygous or frameshift-allele models. Redundancy among H1 variants complicates interpretation.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Function and pathways: H1.4 regulates chromatin compaction and gene expression. The Rahman syndrome frameshift truncates the C-terminal domain and reduces positive charge. This yields more extended nucleosome arrays, greater linker DNA accessibility, reduced phase separation, and increased nuclear mobility, which indicates weaker chromatin binding (PMID: 42173878). Neuronal expression of the C-terminal frameshift tail (H1.4 CFT) altered about 400 genes and reduced neuronal activity (PMID: 34788807). The C-terminal truncation is predicted to impair neutralization of linker DNA (PMID: 28475857). Expression studies in the ASD case showed reduced protein expression (PMID: 29704315).
- Expression: H1.4 is broadly expressed in the brain, including mature neurons, and accumulates in heterochromatin during neuronal maturation (PMID: 42619895).
- Other findings: H1 variants have roles in cancer and differentiation. H1-4 is recurrently mutated in B-cell lymphoma, where combined H1c/H1e loss decompacts chromatin and acts as a tumour suppressor (PMID: 33299181). H1.4 knockdown causes cell death in T47D cells (PMID: 18927631). H1.2/H1.4 loss shifts neutrophil lineage determination (PMID: 32391789). Phosphorylation of H1.4 (S35/S36) is regulated by Ras signalling (PMIDs: 31126199, 31184227). These are mostly cancer-focused and have limited relevance to ASD.
- Statistical support: In the OGID cohort, overlap with height GWAS genes had p=6.84e-8 and overlap with cancer driver genes had p=1.75e-14 (PMID: 28475857).
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE. The mechanism for the C-terminal frameshift is well characterized, but the link to autism-specific pathways is indirect.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: MODERATE (strong for a neurodevelopmental disorder, Rahman syndrome; moderate to limited for ASD specifically).
- Key strengths: Recurrent de novo C-terminal frameshift variants with a consistent phenotype. Mechanistic functional data showing impaired chromatin compaction and altered neuronal gene expression and activity. Neuronal chromatin phenotype in mouse.
- Key gaps: Few reported ASD cases, with patient numbers not given in the extracted abstracts. No cohort-level data (UDN, DDD, etc.) and no systematic ASD prevalence among carriers. The mechanism appears to be a dominant-negative or gain-of-function effect of the truncated tail rather than simple haploinsufficiency. There is no mouse model of the patient variants and no behavioral phenotyping. Most biological work is cancer-focused.
- Conclusion: Heterozygous C-terminal frameshift variants in H1-4 are an established cause of a syndromic neurodevelopmental disorder in which ASD and intellectual disability occur. Functional work supports pathogenicity through altered chromatin compaction. As an ASD gene specifically, the evidence is moderate, and it would be strengthened by larger genotype-phenotype series and in vivo models carrying the frameshift alleles.
