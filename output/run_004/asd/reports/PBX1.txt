PBX1 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: PBX1 AND AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
PBX1 encodes a TALE-class homeodomain transcription factor (pre-B-cell leukemia homeobox 1). It acts as a HOX/MEIS cofactor and regulates transcription during organogenesis and development. Its functions include patterning of the heart, kidney, skeleton, and diaphragm, and cell-fate specification in several tissues.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- The structured clinical extraction returned no entries. No independent case reports or case series were captured, so the number of patients from this source is zero.
- Cohort membership (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL) cannot be determined from the extracted data.
- Phenotypes, variant type, and ASD association cannot be assessed from the clinical extraction.
- One conference abstract appears in the biological evidence set (ASHG 2017 - De novo, deleterious sequence variants that alter the transcriptional activity of the homeoprotein PBX1 are associated with intellectual disability and pleiotropic developmental defects). It describes 7 patients with de novo heterozygous PBX1 missense or truncating variants.
  - Reported features were intellectual disability, ear anomalies, heart defects, diaphragmatic hernia with lung hypoplasia, renal hypoplasia, and cryptorchidism.
  - Functional testing of five variants showed impaired transactivation, altered nuclear translocation, and abnormal interactions with TALE/HOX cofactors.
  - This supports a de novo, dominant, likely loss-of-function or dominant-negative mechanism for a multisystem neurodevelopmental syndrome.
  - Autism was not mentioned in the summary.
- Association studies are not relevant to ASD. They concern cleft lip/palate (ASHG 2013 - Interrogating the PBX-WNT-TP63 pathway in human nonsyndromic cleft lip/palate; rs1044263, p=0.002) and cystinuria (PMID: 30450686; 6 patients and 6 controls).
- Strength of clinical evidence for ASD: NONE from the extracted clinical data. Abstract-level evidence for a PBX1-related neurodevelopmental syndrome exists, but it does not specifically implicate ASD.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Only the mouse has been studied.
- Pbx1-null mice are embryonic lethal with cardiac outflow tract defects. These include persistent truncus arteriosus, and with compound Pbx/Meis1 mutants, Tetralogy of Fallot-like malformations and bicuspid aortic valves (PMID: 18723445). Reduced Pbx2 or Pbx3 dosage on a Pbx1-deficient background shows Pbx1 haploinsufficiency effects.
- Conditional knockouts are viable and show tissue-specific phenotypes:
  - Corneal dystrophy and disorganized stroma with K14-Cre (PMID: 19797217).
  - Testicular dysgenesis and complete male sterility with Leydig cell deletion (PMID: 38724675).
  - Hypoplastic spleen and impaired stress hematopoiesis with splenic mesenchyme deletion (PMID: 27075259).
  - Reduced NK progenitors and NK cells with hematopoietic deletion (PMID: 32190943).
- The most neurologically relevant finding is that Pbx1 acts as a terminal selector of olfactory bulb dopaminergic neurons. Its deletion causes loss of dopaminergic identity, impaired morphological maturation, derepression of alternative interneuron fates, and dysregulated alternative splicing (PMID: 32156753).
- Mendelian ratios, sample sizes, and behavioral phenotypes (social, repetitive, or cognitive) were not reported.
- Consistency with human phenotype is partial. Heart, renal, and gonadal defects parallel the human syndrome described in the ASHG 2017 abstract. No autism-like behavioral assessment is described.
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE for a developmental disorder, LIMITED for ASD specifically.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Molecular function: PBX1 is a DNA-binding transcription factor that partners with MEIS and HOX proteins.
  - It directly regulates Nfil3 (PMID: 32190943), Rtkn2 in Tregs (PMID: 38536923), and SOX3 with MEIS1 in NT2/D1 cells (PMID: 19799567). SOX3 is itself a neurodevelopmental gene.
  - It represses DCDC2 in colorectal cancer (PMID: 36739270).
  - It enables IL7R signaling in hepatic stellate cells, downstream of Notch3 (PMID: 40080840).
  - The E2A-PBX1 oncoprotein self-associates through the PBC-B domain (PMID: 30894657).
- Expression: Pbx1b is broadly expressed during mouse organogenesis. It is found first in condensing mesoderm and later at sites of mesenchymal-epithelial interaction, with overlap in proliferative zones (PMID: 11118899). Specific brain expression data were not captured, apart from its role in olfactory bulb neurogenesis (PMID: 32156753).
- Key findings: PBX1 regulates cell fate and differentiation in many lineages, and its regulation of neuronal identity and splicing fits a neurodevelopmental mechanism. Most biological studies concern cancer, fibrosis, and immunity rather than the brain.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for a role in development, LIMITED for ASD or brain-specific mechanisms.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level for PBX1 as an ASD gene with heterozygous pathogenic variants: LIMITED, bordering on INSUFFICIENT for ASD specifically.
- Strengths:
  - Mouse knockouts show consistent developmental defects.
  - There is a neuronal fate-specification role (PMID: 32156753).
  - A conference abstract reports 7 de novo heterozygous variants with functional impairment (ASHG 2017 - De novo, deleterious sequence variants that alter the transcriptional activity of the homeoprotein PBX1 are associated with intellectual disability and pleiotropic developmental defects).
- Gaps:
  - No peer-reviewed clinical case data were captured.
  - ASD is not documented in any patient.
  - Cohort origin and variant details are unavailable.
  - No behavioral phenotyping has been done in mice.
  - Brain expression data are sparse.
  - Heterozygous Pbx1 effects are only indirectly addressed.
- Conclusion: PBX1 is a well-supported developmental transcription factor linked to a syndromic neurodevelopmental disorder with congenital anomalies and intellectual disability. The data provided do not establish ASD as a distinct or frequent phenotype. Any ASD claim would need systematic clinical cohort data with autism phenotyping and mouse behavioral studies.

## REFERENCES


### Model Organism Evidence
1. Pbx/Meis deficiencies demonstrate multigenetic origins of congenital heart disease (PMID: 18723445)
2. Essential role for Pbx1 in corneal morphogenesis (PMID: 19797217)
3. Loss of PBX1 function in Leydig cells causes testicular dysgenesis and male sterility (PMID: 38724675)
4. Spleen hypoplasia leads to abnormal stress hematopoiesis in mice with loss of Pbx homeoproteins in splenic mesenchyme (PMID: 27075259)
5. PBX1 promotes development of natural killer cells by binding directly to the Nfil3 promoter (PMID: 32190943)
6. PBX1 acts as terminal selector for olfactory bulb dopaminergic neurons (PMID: 32156753)

### Biological Evidence
1. Polymorphism in the PBX1 gene is related to cystinuria in Brazilian families (PMID: 30450686)
2. Lupus susceptibility gene Pbx1 controls the development, stability, and function of regulatory T cells via Rtkn2 expression (PMID: 38536923)
3. PBX1 and MEIS1 up-regulate SOX3 gene expression by direct interaction with a consensus binding site within the basal promoter region (PMID: 19799567)
4. Spindle function and Wnt pathway inhibition by PBX1 to suppress tumor progression via downregulating DCDC2 in colorectal cancer (PMID: 36739270)
5. Pre-B-cell leukemia transcription factor 1 contributes to liver fibrosis by enabling IL-7 signaling in HSC (PMID: 40080840)
6. Oligomeric self-association contributes to E2A-PBX1-mediated oncogenesis (PMID: 30894657)
7. Expression of Pbx1b during mammalian organogenesis (PMID: 11118899)
