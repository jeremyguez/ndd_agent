ADCY5 AND AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER: LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT (heterozygous / autosomal dominant model)

1. GENE SUMMARY
ADCY5 encodes adenylyl cyclase type 5, a membrane-bound enzyme that converts ATP to cAMP downstream of G-protein-coupled receptors. It is highly expressed in the striatum, where it regulates cAMP/PKA signaling. Heterozygous gain-of-function/missense variants are the established cause of ADCY5-related dyskinesia (familial dyskinesia with facial myokymia), a movement disorder rather than primarily an autism disorder.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: Five were retrieved, but none is a dedicated ADCY5 case series for autism. Only four of the five are relevant to autism or neurodevelopment. The fifth, a childhood hyperkinetic movement disorder cohort (PMID: 36054588), concerns movement disorders and is not an autism report.
- Approximate patient numbers: About 2-3 autism-related individuals can be identified with certainty. These are one ASD patient with an ADCY5 VUS (ASHG 2014 - Diagnostic exome sequencing of patients with Autism Spectrum Disorder...), one individual with a brain-somatic mosaic missense variant (PMID: 39085355), and an unspecified number of de novo likely gene-disrupting cases in the SSC/MSSNG cohorts (ASHG 2018 - Insights into the genetic architecture of autism by whole genome sequencing analyses of >3000 families). The hyperkinetic cohort (PMID: 36054588) and the motor speech cohort (PMID: 39931922) report unspecified numbers of ADCY5 patients.
- Phenotypes: Autism, with seizures, intellectual disability, and dysmorphism noted only at cohort level. Elsewhere, ADCY5 patients show chorea, dystonia, hyperkinetic movements (PMID: 36054588) and motor speech disorder with neurodevelopmental comorbidity (PMID: 39931922).
- Variant types: Mixed and poorly specified. The only defined variant is a mosaic brain-somatic missense, p.His701Tyr, VAF 1.0%, which is not a germline heterozygous variant (PMID: 39085355). The ASHG 2014 variant is a VUS and was reported as inherited. The ASHG 2018 variants are de novo likely gene-disrupting, but details are not given. This is not the same mechanism as the known missense gain-of-function dyskinesia variants.
- Cohorts: No UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL patients identified. The autism data derive from Ambry Genetics, SSC/MSSNG, and a brain somatic-mutation study.
- Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED. There are no detailed phenotypes, no segregation data, and a mosaic variant, a VUS and unspecified LoF calls rather than well-characterized germline heterozygous cases.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: Mouse, zebrafish and Nile tilapia.
- Key findings: Full Adcy5 knockout mice are viable and show autistic-like behaviors, namely increased repetitive behaviors, sociability deficits and impaired social/object novelty preference. Dorsal striatal siRNA knockdown reproduces these, and they are rescued by mGluR5 blockade or by optogenetic inhibition of prelimbic cortex-to-dorsal striatum projections (PMID: 27878759). An Adcy5 R419W knock-in mouse shows dystonia/dyskinesia-like motor symptoms with increased striatal cAMP signaling, improved by caffeine via A2A antagonism (PMID: 41660259). Whole-body knockout mice show only adipose changes and no metabolic benefit (PMID: 33919448). Zebrafish adcy3a/adcy5 double mutants and tilapia adcy5 mutants are viable and show pigment/melanosome dispersion defects (PMID: 36430661; PMID: 42587757), which are not relevant to autism. No Mendelian ratio deviation or lethality was reported.
- Consistency with human phenotype: Partial. The mouse knockout autism-like phenotype is consistent with an autism association, but it reflects loss of function in homozygous knockouts, with no heterozygous phenotype described. The known human disease mechanism is gain of function (the R419W knock-in models the movement disorder).
- Strength: MODERATE for a neurobehavioral role, but LIMITED specifically for heterozygous autism.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways: Gs/Golf-coupled cAMP–PKA signaling. ADCY5 couples glucose to insulin secretion in islets (PMID: 24740569) and also acts in melanosome transport, osteoblast differentiation and lipolysis (PMID: 36430661; PMID: 33684620; ASHG 2017 - An epigenetic switch confers pleiotropic risk for bone mineral density and hyperglycaemia).
- Expression: High in striatum, as noted in the ASHG 2011 Raskind et al. report (Linkage and single exome analyses identify ADCY5 as the gene for Familial Dyskinesia with Facial Myokymia). That study found the A726T variant co-segregating in a 5-generation family (9 affected, 3 unaffected) and absent from 1200 control exomes. Adcy5 also changes in rat orbitofrontal cortex after heroin exposure (PMID: 35163373).
- Key experiments: Most biological data concern T2D/glycemic regulation, for example the rs11708067 regulatory variant (ASHG 2015 - A functional regulatory variant associated with type 2 diabetes and fasting glucose levels at the ADCY5 locus; eQTL P=6.5x10-13), and bone/adipose biology. None of these address autism. Striatal mGluR5 upregulation after AC5 loss offers a mechanistic link to autistic-like behavior (PMID: 27878759).
- Strength: LIMITED for autism specifically (strong for general ADCY5 function and for movement disorders).

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: LIMITED
- Strengths: ADCY5 is a well-validated neurological disease gene with strong striatal expression. Knockout mice show autism-like behavior with a defined circuit mechanism (PMID: 27878759). Autism sequencing cohorts report ADCY5 variants, including de novo likely gene-disrupting calls (ASHG 2018 - Insights into the genetic architecture of autism...).
- Gaps: No well-characterized heterozygous germline autism cases with detailed phenotypes. The ASD variants are a VUS, a mosaic somatic variant, or unspecified. There is no segregation or functional validation of the ASD variants. The established human mechanism (gain-of-function missense causing dyskinesia) differs from the loss-of-function mouse model. Most biological data are on metabolism and other non-neurological functions. No heterozygous knockout phenotype has been reported.
- Conclusion: ADCY5 has limited support as an autosomal dominant autism gene. Its strong neurological credentials and the autistic-like behaviors in knockout mice are suggestive, but the human data are sparse and poorly defined. Autism is better regarded as a possible, unconfirmed feature of ADCY5 disorders than as an established stand-alone ADCY5 phenotype, and further case-level data are needed.
