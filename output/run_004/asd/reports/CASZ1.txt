CASZ1 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: CASZ1 AND AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
CASZ1 encodes a zinc finger transcription factor (castor homolog) that acts largely through chromatin regulation, including recruitment of the NuRD and polycomb complexes. It is implicated in cardiac, vascular, neural progenitor, cochlear hair cell and muscle development.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: One study was identified that bears directly on neurodevelopmental disease. It is a large-scale targeted sequencing study of 17,426 NDD trios (PMID: 33004838). No individual case reports or case series with detailed clinical descriptions were found.
- Patients: The number of CASZ1 carriers is not given in the extracted data. The cohort is about 17,426 trios, but the number of affected individuals with CASZ1 de novo mutations is unspecified.
- Phenotypes: The study reports neurodevelopmental disorder and autism spectrum disorder in general terms. No gene-specific phenotype data (ASD vs. ID/DD split, comorbidities) were extracted.
- Variant type: Not specified. The abstract describes only "de novo mutations," so LoF vs. missense cannot be determined.
- Cohorts: No UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL involvement was identified. The study is a large NDD trio meta-cohort.
- Other human genetic data: A heterozygous nonsense variant, p.K351X, segregated with familial dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) and showed loss of transcriptional activity (PMID: 28099117). This is a cardiac, not neurodevelopmental, phenotype, but it suggests that heterozygous LoF can be pathogenic.
- Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED. The statistical signal is strong (FWER significance, p < 3.64E-07 for de novo mutation excess), but there is no variant-level or phenotype-level detail and ASD is not specifically resolved.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: Mouse (full and conditional knockouts, knockdown) and Xenopus (knockdown).
- Key phenotypes:
  - The full Casz1 knockout is embryonic lethal, with myocardial hypoplasia, ventricular septal defect, noncompaction-like morphology and reduced cardiomyocyte proliferation (PMID: 25190801).
  - Conditional deletion in the cochlea causes inner hair cell transdifferentiation into outer hair cells, progressive outer hair cell loss and hearing impairment (PMID: 39883789).
  - Endothelial conditional knockout impairs vascular growth and pulmonary endothelial differentiation (PMID: 41702400).
  - Xenopus knockdown disrupts vascular assembly and lumenization (PMID: 23639441), and produces milder cardiac defects (PMID: 24993940).
  - Casz1 knockdown in mouse retina alters the neurogenesis-to-gliogenesis transition, reducing rod fate and increasing gliogenesis (PMID: 33594190).
- Neurological relevance: The retinal progenitor temporal-identity phenotype is the closest neurodevelopmental finding. No brain, behavioral, social or cognitive phenotypes were reported. Mendelian ratios, heterozygous phenotypes and sample sizes were not reported.
- Consistency with human phenotype: The cardiac phenotype fits the DCM association. No model data support an autism-like phenotype, and heterozygous animals have not been characterized for neurobehavioral traits.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED for ASD (robust for cardiovascular and sensory development, but not neurobehavioral).

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Molecular function and pathways:
  - Casz1 interacts with the NuRD complex in retinal cells and controls temporal identity transitions in neural progenitors, with polycomb also required (PMID: 33594190).
  - Casz1 interacts with polycomb repressor complex in a splice variant-specific manner and controls higher-order chromatin organization in rods (PMID: 30072429).
  - CASZ1b recruits Mi-2/NuRD to repress ENaCα and SGK1 as a mineralocorticoid receptor corepressor (PMID: 33077905).
  - CASZ1 drives muscle differentiation through a feed-forward loop with MYOD and MYOG, altering chromatin accessibility (PMID: 32060262).
  - It is a tumor suppressor in neuroblastoma, repressed by HAND2 (PMID: 36243768). CASZ1b is oncogenic in T-ALL via PI3K-AKT-mTOR (PMID: 38058200) and in lung cancer via ITGAV (PMID: 36777515).
  - Casz1 is an ATOH1 target and regulates outer hair cell maturation (PMID: 42707066).
- Expression: Expression is documented in developing chromaffin cells, pulmonary endothelium, cochlear hair cells, retinal progenitors and rods. The extracted data contain no cortical or brain expression information.
- Key findings: CASZ1 is a chromatin-associated transcriptional regulator that governs cell fate and differentiation timing in neural lineages (retina) and elsewhere. This is mechanistically plausible for neurodevelopmental disease, given NuRD/chromatin regulators' known role in ASD/NDD genes.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for a general role in neural progenitor fate and chromatin regulation. It is not specific to ASD or the cortex.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: LIMITED
- Key strengths:
  - Genome-wide significant de novo mutation excess in a large NDD trio cohort (PMID: 33004838).
  - A plausible mechanism, namely chromatin regulation through NuRD/polycomb with a role in neural progenitor fate (PMID: 33594190).
  - Evidence that heterozygous LoF is disease-causing in humans (DCM; PMID: 28099117).
- Key gaps:
  - No detailed case-level clinical or variant data (variant types, inheritance, phenotypes, patient count).
  - ASD is not specifically distinguished from broader NDD.
  - No brain-specific animal model or behavioral phenotyping, and heterozygous mice are not characterized.
  - No functional validation of NDD-associated variants.
  - No replication in independent cohorts or case reports.
- Conclusion: CASZ1 has a statistically significant excess of de novo variants in a large NDD cohort and a biologically plausible role as a chromatin-associated regulator of neural progenitor fate. However, the neurodevelopmental/ASD link rests on a single statistical association without phenotypic or variant-level characterization, and the model organism and functional data mainly concern cardiovascular, sensory and oncologic contexts. CASZ1 is best considered a candidate ASD/NDD risk gene with limited evidence for autosomal dominant disease until additional well-phenotyped cases and variant-level functional data are reported.

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. Wang et al. 2020 - Large-scale targeted sequencing identifies risk genes for neurodevelopmental disorders (PMID: 33004838)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. Essential role of the zinc finger transcription factor Casz1 for mammalian cardiac morphogenesis and development (PMID: 25190801)
2. Casz1 is required for both inner hair cell fate stabilization and outer hair cell survival (PMID: 39883789)
3. A single-cell time-series atlas of endothelial cell embryonic development (PMID: 41702400)
4. CASZ1 promotes vascular assembly and morphogenesis through the direct regulation of an EGFL7/RhoA-mediated pathway (PMID: 23639441)
5. Congenital heart disease protein 5 associates with CASZ1 to maintain myocardial tissue integrity (PMID: 24993940)
6. A Casz1-NuRD complex regulates temporal identity transitions in neural progenitors (PMID: 33594190)

### Biological Evidence
1. CASZ1 loss-of-function mutation contributes to familial dilated cardiomyopathy (PMID: 28099117)
2. Casz1 controls higher-order nuclear organization in rod photoreceptors (PMID: 30072429)
3. CASZ1b is a novel transcriptional corepressor of mineralocorticoid receptor (PMID: 33077905)
4. CASZ1 induces skeletal muscle and rhabdomyosarcoma differentiation through a feed-forward loop with MYOD and MYOG (PMID: 32060262)
5. Loss of CASZ1 tumor suppressor linked to oncogenic subversion of neuroblastoma core regulatory circuitry (PMID: 36243768)
6. CASZ1 upregulates PI3K-AKT-mTOR signaling and promotes T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (PMID: 38058200)
7. CASZ1 promotes migration, invasion, and metastasis of lung cancer cells by controlling expression of ITGAV (PMID: 36777515)
8. CASZ1 regulates the maturation of outer hair cells and is required for hearing in mice (PMID: 42707066)
