RFX3 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
RFX3 encodes a winged-helix regulatory factor X transcription factor that controls ciliogenesis and the expression of ciliary genes (e.g., FOXJ1 targets, axonemal dyneins, intraflagellar transport genes). It also has roles in neurodevelopment and endocrine pancreas differentiation. Heterozygous loss of function (haploinsufficiency) is proposed as the disease mechanism for a neurobehavioral syndrome with autism.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: Five sources were identified. Three are large or multicenter series: Harris et al. 2021 (PMID: 33658631), and two ASHG abstracts, Nakayama et al. (ASHG 2019 - PgmNr 557: Mutations in RFX family members are a common cause of autism and ADHD) and Hodges et al. (ASHG 2017 - Delineation of a new neurobehavioral syndrome associated with mutations in RFX3). Two are single-patient reports: an infantile epileptic spasms syndrome case with a de novo ~147 kb 9p24.2 deletion involving the initial exons of RFX3 (PMID: 42653242), and a de novo RFX3 deletion in an ASD patient with a balanced chromosomal rearrangement, classified as of unknown significance (PMID: 25844147).
- Patient numbers: Harris et al. report 38 individuals from 33 families, but this figure combines RFX3, RFX4 and RFX7, so the RFX3-only count is not separable from the abstract. The ASHG 2019 abstract reports 17 RFX3 patients and the ASHG 2017 abstract reports 8 de novo cases. These abstracts likely overlap with each other and with Harris et al. (same RUMC-linked collaboration, GeneMatcher-assembled), so the true number of unique patients is probably around 20-40 and cannot be summed reliably. Two further single cases bring the total to a handful more.
- Common phenotypes: autism spectrum disorder, intellectual disability (mostly mild/moderate; 5 of 8 in the 2017 series), ADHD (4 of 8), sensory hypersensitivity, sleep problems, hypotonia, joint laxity, subtle dysmorphisms and behavioral dysregulation. The single deletion case had epileptic spasms and global delay.
- Variant types: Predominantly loss of function (frameshift, splice-donor, deletions), with some missense variants reported. Both de novo and inherited variants occur, so penetrance and expressivity may be variable. Individual variant notations were not available in the abstracts.
- Cohorts: Cases are tied to the RUMC (Radboud) cohort and GeneMatcher collaborations. No UDN, GREGoR, DDD or GEL membership was reported.
- Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE. A multi-family series with consistent phenotypes and a LoF pattern supports the association. Limitations are the lack of per-variant detail in the available abstracts, the presence of inherited variants, the pooling with RFX4/RFX7, and the conference-abstract status of two of the three series.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: Mouse (multiple full and conditional knockouts) and Xenopus (knockdown/rescue).
- Key mouse phenotypes: Full Rfx3 knockout causes sub-Mendelian ratios and a high rate of embryonic lethality, with left-right asymmetry defects, situs inversus in survivors and stunted nodal cilia (PMID: 15121860). Knockouts also develop hydrocephalus with choroid plexus and subcommissural organ defects (PMID: 16930429) and impaired motile cilia growth and beating (PMID: 19671664). They show agenesis of the corpus callosum, with disorganized midline guidepost neurons, ectopic Fgf8 and a reduced GLI3 repressor/activator ratio (PMID: 22479201). This agenesis is rescued by a Gli3 repressor allele (PMID: 26071364). Pancreatic phenotypes include reduced and disorganized islets, impaired beta-cell differentiation and glucose intolerance (PMID: 17229940; PMID: 20413507).
- Xenopus: rfx3 acts downstream of rnf20 in cilia motility and heart looping/left-right patterning (PMID: 31235600).
- Consistency with human phenotype: Partial. The mouse neurodevelopmental findings (callosal agenesis, hydrocephalus) show that RFX3 is required for brain development. However, the reported models are largely homozygous nulls, and no behavioral phenotyping of heterozygous animals relevant to autism, ADHD or ID is reported. Situs inversus, hydrocephalus and diabetes are not features of the human heterozygous syndrome as described, though pancreatic or midline phenotypes have not been systematically assessed in patients.
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE. The evidence is robust for developmental function, but it lacks heterozygous behavioral or ASD-relevant models.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: RFX3 is a ciliogenic transcription factor. It acts as a co-activator with FOXJ1 on cilia gene promoters (PMID: 23822649), regulates axonemal dyneins and intraflagellar transport genes, and modulates Hedgehog/GLI3 signaling. Dimerization and ciliogenic function depend on auto-fatty acylation at a conserved cysteine (PMID: 30127002).
- Expression: Strongly expressed in developing and adult human brain (PMID: 33658631), and in ciliated ependymal cells, choroid plexus and node cells in mouse (PMID: 16930429; PMID: 15121860). X-box motifs and RFX ChIP-seq peaks are enriched in the cis-regulatory regions of known ASD risk genes (PMID: 33658631).
- Key experimental findings: In human iPSC-derived neurons and forebrain organoids, biallelic RFX3 loss disrupted ciliary genes and delayed differentiation. Monoallelic loss disrupted synaptic gene expression and activity-dependent transcription, with reduced CREB binding at co-localized RFX3/CREB sites (PMID: 40060598). This directly supports a haploinsufficiency mechanism relevant to ASD. Further functional work in human islet-lineage cells shows broad developmental roles (PMID: 40263183).
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE to STRONG. Heterozygous human neuronal data are directly relevant, but this rests on a limited number of studies.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: MODERATE (approaching STRONG if the Harris et al. variant-level data confirm multiple de novo LoF variants).
- Key strengths: A multi-center cohort with consistent ASD/ID/ADHD phenotypes. A predominantly LoF mutational spectrum with de novo occurrence. High brain expression. Human neuron models showing that monoallelic loss alters synaptic and activity-dependent transcription. Extensive mouse work showing essential roles in brain midline and ciliary development.
- Key gaps and limitations: Per-patient variants, segregation data and ACMG classifications are not available in the extracted evidence. The RFX3-specific numbers are not separable from the RFX4/RFX7 totals, and the ASHG abstracts likely overlap with the published cohort. Inherited variants, reduced-penetrance questions and missense pathogenicity are unresolved. The two single-patient reports are of limited or uncertain significance. No heterozygous mouse behavioral model is available. Core clinical data derive from a single collaborative network (RUMC).
- Conclusion: RFX3 is a credible autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental gene, in which heterozygous loss of function causes autism spectrum disorder, often with ADHD, mild-to-moderate intellectual disability and hypotonia. The case series, human neuronal data and mouse developmental biology converge on a haploinsufficiency mechanism involving ciliary and activity-dependent transcriptional regulation. Independent replication and detailed variant-level and heterozygous-animal data would strengthen the classification.
