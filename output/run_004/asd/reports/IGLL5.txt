IGLL5 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) PATHOGENIC VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
IGLL5 (immunoglobulin lambda-like polypeptide 5) is a gene at 22q11.22-q11.23 that encodes an immunoglobulin lambda light chain-related sequence, including a constant Cλ segment. It is expressed in B-lineage cells and is a recurrent somatic hypermutation target in B-cell malignancies. No neurodevelopmental function has been established.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: Two sources were found, and neither is a genuine case report or case series implicating IGLL5 in a germline disorder.
- Total patients: About 1 patient with a phenotype (autism spectrum disorder, ASD).
- The ASD case is a single maternally inherited ~656 kb gain at 22q11.23 in an ASD cohort CNV study (PMID: 23275889). The interval spans RAB36, FBXW4P1, RTDR1, GNAZ, MIR650, IGLL5 and BCR. IGLL5 was not singled out as causal, and the inheritance from a parent (phenotype not stated) argues against a specific effect.
- The second source reports an excess of protein-coding de novo variants in IGLL5 in 1000 Genomes lymphoblastoid cell line (LCL) WGS data (PMID: 36054329). The authors interpreted this as likely a cell-line somatic hypermutation artifact, not pathogenic germline variation. No phenotypes were associated.
- Phenotypes: ASD in 1 patient only.
- Variant type: Copy-number gain (multi-gene) and non-phenotypic cell-line de novo variants. There are no LoF or missense germline variants in affected individuals.
- Cohorts: No UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL patients were identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE to very limited. I rate it NONE for a monoallelic pathogenic-variant mechanism.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- No model organism data were identified.
- No knockout phenotypes (neurological, lethality, Mendelian ratio) are available, so consistency with human phenotype cannot be assessed.
- Strength: NONE

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Function and pathways: IGLL5 is linked to B-cell receptor/immunoglobulin biology. It is a frequent somatic mutation target in B-cell neoplasms. It is the most frequently mutated gene in DLBCL in one driver analysis (PMID: 32565964). It is recurrently mutated in 18% of 95 multiple myeloma tumors, with mutual exclusivity with RAS mutations and association with progression risk (p=0.03) (PMID: 29563506). IGLL5/IGH translocations occurred in two myeloma samples (PMID: 29563506). A super-enhancer-controlled IGLL5 supports survival and MYC expression in mature B-cell lymphoma lines, since knockdown induced cell death and reduced MYC (PMID: 38444524). Somatic IGLL5 mutations also occur in autoreactive B-cell clones in MAG neuropathy (PMID: 40915157).
- Expression: Expression is immune/B-cell/plasma cell-related. It is upregulated in rheumatoid arthritis synovium and blood (PMID: 33604823) and in inflamed periodontitis gingival tissue (PMID: 29921943). It correlates with tumor-infiltrating immune cells in ccRCC (PMID: 33449444). There are no data on brain or neurodevelopmental expression in the provided evidence.
- Other findings (fusions, m6A modification) are cancer-related and not relevant to ASD (e.g., PMID: 39380003, PMID: 26311227).
- Strength of biological evidence for ASD: NONE. The biological evidence is substantial for B-cell malignancy and immunity but has no neurodevelopmental relevance.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT
- Strengths: The gene's role in B-cell biology and lymphoid malignancy is well documented across several studies. This is not relevant to ASD, however.
- Gaps and limitations: There are no germline LoF or missense variants in affected individuals, no de novo or segregation data, no model organisms, and no brain expression or functional neuronal data. The single ASD observation is an inherited multi-gene CNV in which IGLL5 is one of seven genes. The IGLL5 de novo excess in 1000 Genomes data is probably a somatic hypermutation artifact of LCLs. The region's high mutability and its immune-specific function further reduce the likelihood of a neurodevelopmental role.
- Conclusion: There is no credible evidence that heterozygous germline IGLL5 variants cause autism spectrum disorder. The sole link is an incidental, inherited 22q11.23 copy gain involving multiple genes. IGLL5 should be classified as having insufficient evidence for ASD (no gene-disease relationship supported at present).
