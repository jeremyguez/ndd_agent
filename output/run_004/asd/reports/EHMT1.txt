EHMT1 EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) PATHOGENIC VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
EHMT1 encodes euchromatic histone lysine methyltransferase 1 (also called GLP). It forms a heteromeric complex with EHMT2 (G9a) that deposits H3K9me1/me2 to repress transcription. Its ankyrin-repeat domain acts as a "reader" of histone marks and its SET domain as the "writer". Heterozygous loss of EHMT1 causes Kleefstra syndrome 1 (KLEFS1), a neurodevelopmental disorder in which autism is a core feature.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Number of reports: 10 studies or reports were extracted. Of these, 8 are case reports or series with variants directly in EHMT1 or a 9q34 deletion including it. The other two are an ASHG 2011 abstract on an intragenic deletion and a Türkiye/China ASD exome study (see below).
- Patient numbers: roughly 80 individuals are counted in the extracted studies. The largest is the Boston Kleefstra Clinic cohort of 65 patients (PMID: 39746677), in which about 54% (~35) had sequence variants and 46% had 9q34 deletions (17% ≥1 Mb, 29% <1 Mb). The remaining studies contribute 1–3 patients each. The large variant-curation study (PMID: 39013458) included 209 individuals, 191 of them likely pathogenic or pathogenic, and is listed under biological evidence.
- Common phenotypes: global developmental delay and intellectual disability (core), autism spectrum disorder, hypotonia, distinctive facial features, epilepsy, motor impairment, cardiac defects, hearing loss and constipation (PMID: 39746677; PMID: 39696517). Psychiatric features are also reported: regression or regressive behaviors, and psychosis or schizophrenia with autism (PMID: 28361099; PMID: 36617745). Isolated speech phenotypes such as childhood apraxia of speech occurred with EHMT1 nonsense and de novo missense variants (PMID: 41530369).
- Autism-specific observations:
  - Autism was documented in the Boston cohort (PMID: 39746677), in individual case reports (PMID: 28361099; PMID: 36617745), and in an ASD patient with an exon 24 de novo intragenic deletion plus epilepsy (ASHG 2011 - Intragenic CNVs identified by exon-level microarray analysis in individuals with autism plus epilepsy).
  - Mosaic EHMT1 variants in 3 apparently unaffected parents were associated with ASD features and impaired cognitive flexibility (PMID: 29416845).
  - ASD exome cohorts reported EHMT1 missense variants of uncertain significance: p.Gly325Val in Türkiye (PMID: 41751633) and p.K327T in China (PMID: 40642607).
- Variant types: loss-of-function (splice, nonsense, deletions) predominates, with multiple examples:
  - Splice variants: c.2382+1G>T (PMID: 39696517) and c.2750-1G>T, with RNA-seq-confirmed exon skipping and frameshift (PMID: 28361099).
  - Nonsense variant: p.Gln1077* (PMID: 41530369).
  - Deletions: 9q34 deletions (PMID: 39746677; PMID: 39696517).
  - Missense variants are also seen: p.Pro809Leu was found in unrelated patients (PMID: 39696517; PMID: 28057753), and functional work showed it causes misfolding and aberrant histone mark binding (PMID: 28057753). p.Arg948Trp was de novo (PMID: 41530369).
- Cohorts: only RUMC (the mosaic-parent study, PMID: 29416845) and the AGRE/Emory cohort (ASHG 2011) are identifiable. No UDN, GREGoR, DDD or GEL cohorts were identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG for Kleefstra syndrome with ASD as a frequent component. Evidence for isolated ASD from EHMT1 variants is weaker, since the ASD-cohort missense variants are VUS.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse is the main model. The extracted data also include rat (cardiac work, PMID: 27893464) and Drosophila (listed under biological evidence).
- Key phenotypes:
  - Ehmt1+/- mice are viable. Reported phenotypes include anxiety, autistic-like traits and aberrant social interactions (background described in PMID: 36877418), and heightened defensive and offensive aggression toward unfamiliar conspecifics (PMID: 36877418).
  - Ehmt1+/- mice show enhanced pattern separation and increased hippocampal cell proliferation, with other touchscreen learning tasks unimpaired (PMID: 28071689).
  - Forebrain-specific haploinsufficiency (D6Cre/+) caused abnormal sensory-motor gating, absent object recognition memory, reduced auditory evoked potentials, reduced beta/gamma oscillations and reduced hippocampal Grin1 expression (PMID: 32954001).
  - Knockdown in cortical neurons impaired network development (PMID: 27767173).
  - Conditional knockouts outside the brain showed oocyte meiotic arrest with near-infertility in females (PMID: 40650982), reduced oligodendrocyte lineage cells and MBP (PMID: 40134232), and pathological cardiac hypertrophy (PMID: 27893464).
  - No Mendelian ratio data or sample sizes were provided.
- Consistency with human phenotype: good. Heterozygous mice reproduce the neurodevelopmental, cognitive, social and behavioral features of Kleefstra syndrome. The enhanced pattern separation is a notable divergence.
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE to STRONG.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: EHMT1/EHMT2 mediate H3K9me2-dependent transcriptional repression. EHMT1 sits in a chromatin-modification network with MBD5, MLL3, SMARCB1 and NR1I3, and these genes cooperate with it genetically in Drosophila (PMID: 22726846). Drosophila EHMT1 disruption caused neurodevelopmental and learning and memory defects (ASHG 2011 - Identification of three novel genes for Kleefstra syndrome associated phenotypes establishes a chromatin modification module of the EHMT1 gene network). EHMT1 also interacts with BRD4 (PMID: 40598222).
- Neurodevelopmental relevance:
  - EHMT1 is part of an epigenetic barrier that sets the pace of human neuronal maturation in hPSC-derived neurons (PMID: 38297124).
  - Ehmt1+/- mice show delayed maturation of PV+ interneurons and GABAergic transmission, shifting the excitatory-inhibitory balance toward overexcitability (PMID: 32975655).
  - Depletion in RPE1 cells altered Golgi, lysosome and adhesion structures and reduced migration (PMID: 37257768).
  - Specific brain expression data were not provided in the extracted evidence.
- Genotype-phenotype mechanism: analysis of 209 individuals showed that disrupting the ankyrin reader domain produces a KLEFS1-specific DNA methylation signature with a milder phenotype. Disrupting only the SET domain does not produce the signature or the typical phenotype, and N-terminal truncations are also milder (PMID: 39013458). This supports haploinsufficiency as the main mechanism, with a reader-function component.
- Non-neural findings: EHMT1 is upregulated in esophageal squamous cell cancer (PMID: 24649311) and supports alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma progression (PMID: 34897678). These are not relevant to ASD.
- Strength of biological evidence: STRONG.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: STRONG for EHMT1 haploinsufficiency as a cause of a neurodevelopmental disorder (Kleefstra syndrome) that includes ASD. For ASD as a standalone phenotype, the evidence is moderate.
- Key strengths:
  - A large clinical cohort (n=65) plus many independent case reports.
  - Recurrent LoF variants, de novo variants and deletions.
  - Functional validation of a missense variant (PMID: 28057753).
  - A genotype-phenotype and epigenetic signature study of 209 individuals (PMID: 39013458).
  - Concordant mouse and Drosophila phenotypes.
  - Mechanistic data on interneuron and network development.
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - Many variant details are not given in the abstracts.
  - Autism rates are not quantified in the extracted data.
  - Several ASD-cohort variants are VUS.
  - No model organism Mendelian ratio or sample size data were provided.
  - Few UDN, DDD or GEL-style cohorts were identified.
  - Some extracted studies cover non-neural biology.
- Conclusion: EHMT1 is a well-established autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental gene. Heterozygous LoF variants and deletions cause Kleefstra syndrome, in which autism is a frequent and core feature, and this is supported by consistent mouse, fly and cellular data. The evidence supports EHMT1 as an ASD-associated gene in the syndromic context. Missense variants found in ASD-only cohorts need individual functional or segregation evidence before they are considered pathogenic.
