PAPOLG LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
PAPOLG encodes poly(A) polymerase gamma (PAPγ), a canonical nuclear poly(A) polymerase that adds poly(A) tails to pre-mRNAs and contributes to mRNA 3'-end processing, stability and alternative polyadenylation. It lies at 2p16.1, adjacent to BCL11A, within the 2p15-p16.1 microdeletion region.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: Only one distinct patient is described, in two overlapping sources. These are Levy et al. 2017 (PMID: 28573701) and the corresponding ASHG 2015 abstract (ASHG 2015 - Molecular and clinical delineation of the 2p15-16.1 microdeletion syndrome). They are very likely the same individual. A third report, ASHG 2011 (ASHG 2011 - Refining the 2p15-p16 Microdeletion Syndrome), describes one more patient, but with a larger deletion.
- Total patients: About 2 unique patients, both with multigene deletions.
- Phenotypes:
  - The 427 kb de novo deletion patient had speech delay, autistic traits, motor stereotypies and brain structure abnormalities, without microcephaly (PMID: 28573701).
  - The 1.11 Mb deletion patient had developmental delay, microcephaly and dysmorphism (ASHG 2011).
- Variant type: Only large contiguous-gene deletions (427 kb and 1.11 Mb). The 427 kb deletion also encompasses BCL11A, a well-established neurodevelopmental gene, which most plausibly explains the phenotype. No PAPOLG-specific loss-of-function or missense variants are reported.
- Cohorts: None of the patients are from UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL. The reports come from French centers (Paris, APHP) and a separate ASHG abstract.
- Attribution: The authors explicitly attribute the phenotype to the contiguous genes, not to PAPOLG alone. The ASHG 2011 abstract places PAPOLG outside the narrowed critical region (KIAA1841, C2orf74, AHSA2, USP34), so it is not a leading candidate.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE to LIMITED. There is no gene-specific evidence for autism or neurodevelopmental disease.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- No model organism data (knockout or otherwise) were identified.
- Consistency with the human phenotype cannot be assessed.
- Strength of model organism evidence: NONE

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: PAPγ is a canonical poly(A) polymerase involved in mRNA polyadenylation and stability.
  - In SARS-CoV-2-infected THP-1 macrophages, PAPOLG was upregulated. Knockdown promoted NF-κB mRNA degradation and lowered TNF-α and IL-6, while overexpression had the opposite effect (PMID: 40607946).
  - A viral RNA element recruits PAPγ and PAPα to drive cytoplasmic polyadenylation, extending PAPγ function beyond nuclear processing (PMID: 42594865).
- Expression and disease associations are largely non-neurological:
  - Amplification and overexpression in follicular and transformed follicular lymphoma, with a proposed proto-oncogene role (PMID: 31410080).
  - High expression in hepatocellular carcinoma, associated with poor prognosis (PMID: 35482720).
  - Altered blood expression in ovarian cancer (PMID: 23551967).
  - Oocyte expression in rabbit, linked to oocyte competence (PMID: 35724452).
  - A nutrigenomic study in which PAPOLG SNPs affected the plasma metabolome, suggesting a link to mitochondrial function (PMID: 23964055; genotype P<0.001).
- No brain or neurodevelopmental expression data, neuronal functional studies or ASD-relevant mechanisms were found.
- Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED, and not specific to neurodevelopment.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT
- Key strengths: PAPOLG has a plausible general role in RNA processing, and its deletion has been observed de novo in an individual with autistic traits.
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - There are no single-gene variants (LoF or missense) and no cohort-level data.
  - The only patients carry multigene deletions in which BCL11A, or other genes in the region, are better candidates.
  - The authors themselves do not implicate PAPOLG.
  - There are no model organism data.
  - Biological studies concern cancer, immunity and reproduction rather than the nervous system.
  - No haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative mechanism has been demonstrated.
- Conclusion: There is currently no credible evidence that heterozygous pathogenic variants in PAPOLG cause autism spectrum disorder or another neurodevelopmental disorder. The sparse deletion data are confounded by BCL11A and neighboring genes. PAPOLG should be considered unsupported as an ASD gene unless de novo or loss-of-function variants are identified in additional affected individuals.
