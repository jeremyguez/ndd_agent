BRSK2 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER / AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER

1. GENE SUMMARY
BRSK2 (BR serine/threonine kinase 2, also called SAD-A) is an AMPK-related serine/threonine kinase. It is implicated in neuronal polarity, cell-cycle control, nutrient and energy sensing (AMPK/mTOR signaling), and pancreatic β-cell insulin secretion. The evidence summarized here supports heterozygous, mainly loss-of-function (LoF), de novo variants causing a neurodevelopmental disorder (NDD) with autism.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: 10 publications or abstracts. Seven are peer-reviewed case reports or series: Hiatt et al. 2019 (PMID: 30879638), the Chinese proband (PMID: 35754711), the catatonia/regression case (PMID: 41423339), the c.442del case (PMID: 37671287), the Italian family cohort (PMID: 38519481), the Indian cohort (PMID: 39534727) and the Brazilian cohort (PMID: 42721435). Three are SPARK-derived reports: the exome study of 457 families (PMID: 31452935), ASHG 2018 (Pilot study results from SPARK) and ASHG 2017 (SPARK: A large-scale genomic resource). The SPARK reports probably overlap, with the same two de novo LGD variants counted repeatedly. An additional publication (PMID: 42509346) describes 52 patients with heterozygous BRSK2 variants, but individual-level detail was not extracted.
- Patient numbers: about 22 individuals are tabulated, and fewer than that are likely unique given the SPARK overlap. Counting the 52-patient series, the total is much larger.
- Common phenotypes: autism spectrum disorder, speech/language delay, developmental delay, intellectual disability, motor delay and behavioral problems (PMID: 30879638). Single cases add catatonia and developmental regression (PMID: 41423339), and attention deficit, auditory hallucinations, limb tremor and abnormal EEG/brain electrical activity (PMID: 37671287).
- Variant type: predominantly LoF (nonsense, frameshift, splice). Examples are p.R222X (PMID: 35754711), p.L148Cfs*39 (PMID: 37671287) and c.954_955insT (PMID: 42721435). Six of the 9 variants in Hiatt et al. were LoF and 3 were missense. Missense variants also appear in PMID: 38519481. Most variants were de novo, with all 6 probands with parental data de novo in Hiatt et al.
- Statistical support: de novo enrichment p = 2.46 × 10^-6 in the NDD cohorts (3,429 probands) (PMID: 30879638). TADA analysis gave genome-wide significance for ASD (p = 2.3 × 10^-6) (PMID: 31452935).
- Known cohorts: SPARK (USA) contributes several reports. Hiatt et al. used a University of Washington/HudsonAlpha GeneMatcher collaboration. No UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL involvement was identified in the extracted data.
- Limitations: Some variants are described as LoF without notation. One variant (c.978G>C, PMID: 39534727) is labeled LoF but the notation suggests a missense or splice effect, so its classification is uncertain.
- Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: zebrafish (three studies) and mouse (one study). Drosophila work is covered under biological evidence.
- Zebrafish brsk2b-/- (PMID: 35754711): higher embryonic mortality, pericardial edema, developmental delay, depigmentation and growth retardation. Adults showed decreased activity, enhanced anxiety-like behavior and reduced social interaction. Brain expression of homer1b, mbpa, mpz and plp1b was increased and isl1a decreased.
- Zebrafish brsk2ab-/- (PMID: 38003696): catch-up growth, improving locomotion, and escalating social deficiency and anxiety-like behavior over time.
- Zebrafish brsk2ab-/- retina (PMID: 41596506): disrupted retinal lamination and enhanced light perception.
- Mouse (PMID: 37188647): β-cell-specific conditional knockout was viable with normal metabolism on chow and protection from high-fat-diet-induced metabolic disease. This is relevant to metabolism, not to neurodevelopment, and no neurological mouse phenotype was reported in the extracted data.
- Consistency with human phenotype: the zebrafish social, anxiety and developmental phenotypes are concordant with ASD and developmental delay in patients. Mendelian ratios, sample sizes and p-values were not reported in the abstracts. The models are full knockouts, which is consistent with a LoF mechanism, although the heterozygous state was not studied.
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Function and pathways: BRSK2 phosphorylates TSC2 and suppresses mTORC1 (PMID: 28591720). It drives AMPKα2 signaling and suppresses NRF2 and global protein synthesis (PMID: 32546533). It is also involved in autophagy through the PIK3C3/Vps34 complex (PMID: 41258112) and in cell-cycle control, with centrosomal localization in mitosis and G2/M arrest on overexpression (PMID: 23029325). Its levels are regulated by ubiquitin-proteasome degradation through Jab1 (PMID: 22609399) and APC/C-Cdh1 (PMID: 23029325). In β cells it phosphorylates PCTAIRE1 at Ser-12 and regulates insulin secretion (PMID: 22798068). Variants are also associated with type 2 diabetes-related traits (PMID: 37188647).
- Expression: the extracted data give no detailed expression data. The kinase is described as brain-specific or brain-enriched in its nomenclature, and it is abundant in pancreatic islets. The gene is intolerant to protein-altering variation (PMID: 30879638).
- Functional variant data: In Drosophila, pan-neuronal knockdown of the orthologue sff caused impaired locomotion and seizure susceptibility. Overexpression of human wild-type BRSK2 was toxic. Most kinase-domain and KA1 missense variants showed partial loss of function, and a few showed possible gain of function (PMID: 42509346). Patient iPSC/organoids with a frameshift variant showed reduced BRSK2 transcript and protein consistent with nonsense-mediated decay (PMID: 41423339).
- Limitations: much of the mechanistic work comes from cancer and metabolic contexts, with limited neuronal data in the extracted material.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: STRONG.
- Key strengths: multiple independent de novo LoF variants across several countries and cohorts (USA, China, Italy, India, Brazil), statistical enrichment of de novo variants (p = 2.46 × 10^-6 and 2.3 × 10^-6), a large 52-patient series (PMID: 42509346), consistent ASD/NDD phenotypes, concordant zebrafish behavior and Drosophila neuronal phenotypes, and evidence of NMD in patient-derived cells.
- Key gaps: overlapping SPARK reports inflate the apparent patient number. Several variant notations are missing from the abstracts. Genotype-phenotype correlations for missense variants (loss versus gain of function) are unresolved. There is no neurodevelopmental mouse model, and the mechanistic work in neurons is limited. Phenotypes in the single-case reports are variable and may be affected by ascertainment.
- Conclusion: BRSK2 is a well-supported autism and NDD gene, with heterozygous, predominantly de novo LoF variants causing a syndrome of ASD, developmental delay, intellectual disability and speech/motor delay. Statistical, cohort and model-organism data converge on haploinsufficiency as the principal mechanism. Further work should clarify missense mechanisms and neuronal function.
