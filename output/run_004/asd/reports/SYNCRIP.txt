SYNCRIP LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER / NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER (HETEROZYGOUS, AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
SYNCRIP (hnRNP Q) is a predominantly cytoplasmic RNA-binding protein with RRM domains. It regulates mRNA stability, translation, transport and miRNA processing, and it is expressed ubiquitously, including in neurons. The extracted evidence links it to neuronal differentiation and cortical development.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: Four sources were found. Two are case-level or case-series reports with variant data: a 3-patient series (PMID: 34157790) and a genome sequencing study in autism (PMID: 30504930). Two are large cohort or meta-analytic de novo enrichment studies (PMID: 36350923; PMID: 33874999).
- Approximate patients: About 10 individuals are countable.
  - 3 in the dedicated series (PMID: 34157790).
  - 2 in the autism genome sequencing study (PMID: 30504930).
  - 5 in the 46,612-trio analysis (PMID: 36350923): 1 missense and 4 likely gene-disrupting variants (2 DD, 2 ASD).
  - The HNRNP-family study (PMID: 33874999) did not report a SYNCRIP-specific count in the abstract.
  - Some overlap between studies is possible, because the trio meta-analysis may include previously published cases. The count is therefore approximate and may not be fully independent.
- Common phenotypes: Intellectual disability and developmental delay are the most common, followed by autism or autistic features (2 of 3 in PMID: 34157790). Single cases had myoclonic-atonic epilepsy, periventricular nodular heterotopia or widened subarachnoid spaces (neuroradiological features reported for the cohort).
- Genotype-phenotype pattern: In PMID: 34157790, the two frameshift carriers were more severely affected than the carrier of an RRM2-domain missense variant.
- Variant types: Predominantly de novo. Loss-of-function (frameshift or gene-disrupting) variants are the most common type, with a smaller number of missense variants. Exact variant notations were not available in the abstracts.
- Cohorts: DDD is named in PMID: 36350923. PMID: 33874999 is an international collaboration. UDN, GREGoR, RUMC and GEL were not identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE. De novo LoF variants recur and there is statistical enrichment in combined NDD cohorts (PMID: 36350923). The numbers are small, however, and the autism-specific phenotype is less well defined than ID/DD.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: Mouse (conditional knockouts and shRNA knockdowns) and Drosophila (in the biological evidence section).
- Neurological phenotypes: Syncrip conditional knockout in radial glial cells disrupted neuronal differentiation and localization. It caused superficial cortical layer defects, stalled late-stage neurogenesis, dysregulated Notch signaling and impaired learning and memory (PMID: 39776340). This is consistent with a neurodevelopmental disorder with ID.
- Other mouse work was non-neurological. Hematopoietic conditional knockout impaired stem cell maintenance (PMID: 37085479), and knockdown studies in leukemia models showed context-dependent effects (PMID: 28436985; PMID: 30266814).
- Lethality, Mendelian ratio and fertility: Not reported. Ubiquitous syp knockdown in Drosophila caused semi-lethality (PMID: 33617535).
- No autism-relevant behavioral phenotypes such as social behavior were reported. Heterozygous models were not described.
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE. The cortical conditional knockout is relevant, but no germline heterozygous model exists.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: SYNCRIP is involved in mRNA stabilization, translation control and pri-let-7a/miRNA processing through its RRM2-3 domains (PMID: 31907208). It also acts in phase-separated RNP complexes, and pathogenic human variants weaken its phase separation (PMID: 39776340). It controls the translation of CDC42 in hematopoietic stem cells (PMID: 37085479).
- Expression and neuronal relevance:
  - It is ubiquitously expressed and predominantly cytoplasmic (PMID: 10734137).
  - It is found in granules in rat hippocampal neuron dendrites that co-transport with Staufen1 and IP3R1 mRNA (PMID: 15475564).
  - iCLIP in rat cortical neurons identified hundreds of target mRNAs enriched for neurogenesis, migration and neurite outgrowth, including Dcx. SYNCRIP acts with miR-9 to promote early neuronal differentiation (PMID: 33030396).
- Drosophila: Syncrip affects synaptic transmission and BMP signaling at the NMJ (PMID: 25171887) and starvation-induced hyperactivity (PMID: 33617535).
- Other: Roles in APOBEC3B regulation in prostate cancer (PMID: 37478850) and in HCV replication (PMID: 19232660) are not relevant to neurodevelopment.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE to STRONG for a role in neuronal development and RNA regulation. Direct functional testing of patient variants is limited to the phase-separation data.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: MODERATE (approaching STRONG for a broad neurodevelopmental disorder; MODERATE for autism specifically).
- Key strengths:
  - Recurrent de novo LoF variants.
  - Significant de novo enrichment in large NDD meta-analyses (PMID: 36350923).
  - A dedicated case series with a possible genotype-phenotype pattern (PMID: 34157790).
  - A cortical conditional knockout that matches the human phenotype (PMID: 39776340).
  - Neuronal RNA targets and a plausible haploinsufficiency mechanism (PMID: 33030396).
- Key gaps:
  - Small numbers of individuals with detailed phenotyping.
  - Variant notations and inheritance details are missing from the abstracts.
  - Possible cohort overlap.
  - Autism is often secondary to ID/DD.
  - No heterozygous germline mouse model.
  - Little functional validation of individual variants.
  - Missense pathogenicity is unclear (PMID: 30504930).
- Conclusion: SYNCRIP is a well-supported neurodevelopmental disorder gene, with heterozygous de novo, mainly LoF, variants causing intellectual disability, with autistic features in some individuals. Evidence for autism as a distinct primary phenotype is more modest, but the combined clinical, statistical, animal and molecular data support SYNCRIP as an autosomal dominant ASD/NDD gene. More phenotyped patients and heterozygous model data would strengthen the case.

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. Further evidence for de novo variants in SYNCRIP as the cause of a neurodevelopmental disorder (PMID: 34157790)
2. Genome sequencing identifies multiple deleterious variants in autism patients with more severe phenotypes (PMID: 30504930)
3. Integrated gene analyses of de novo variants from 46,612 trios with autism and developmental disorders (PMID: 36350923)
4. Rare deleterious mutations of HNRNP genes result in shared neurodevelopmental disorders (PMID: 33874999)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. Posttranscriptional Control of Neural Progenitors Temporal Dynamics During Neocortical Development by Syncrip (PMID: 39776340)
2. RNA binding protein SYNCRIP maintains proteostasis and self-renewal of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (PMID: 37085479)
3. Functional screen of MSI2 interactors identifies an essential role for SYNCRIP in myeloid leukemia stem cells (PMID: 28436985)
4. Deletion 6q Drives T-cell Leukemia Progression by Ribosome Modulation (PMID: 30266814)

### Biological Evidence
1. RNA-binding protein syncrip regulates starvation-induced hyperactivity in adult Drosophila (PMID: 33617535)
2. SYNCRIP, a new player in pri-let-7a processing (PMID: 31907208)
3. SYNCRIP, a cytoplasmic counterpart of hnRNP R, interacts with ubiquitous synaptotagmin isoforms (PMID: 10734137)
4. SYNCRIP (hnRNP Q1/NSAP1) is a component of mRNA granule transported with IP3R1 mRNA in neuronal dendrites (PMID: 15475564)
5. The cytoplasmic SYNCRIP mRNA interactome of mammalian neurons (PMID: 33030396)
6. Syncrip/hnRNP Q influences synaptic transmission and regulates BMP signaling at the Drosophila neuromuscular synapse (PMID: 25171887)
7. Loss of SYNCRIP unleashes APOBEC-driven mutagenesis, tumor heterogeneity, and AR-targeted therapy resistance in prostate cancer (PMID: 37478850)
8. SYNCRIP is a host factor involved in hepatitis C virus RNA replication (PMID: 19232660)
