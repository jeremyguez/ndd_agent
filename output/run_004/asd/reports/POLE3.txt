POLE3 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
POLE3 encodes the small histone-fold accessory subunit (p17) of DNA polymerase epsilon. It forms a heterodimer with POLE4 and takes part in leading-strand replication, parental histone transfer and DNA repair. No literature links it to autism spectrum disorder (ASD) or any other human Mendelian neurodevelopmental disorder.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent case reports/case series: 0 found.
- Total patients: 0.
- Phenotypes: none reported. No ASD, intellectual disability or other neurodevelopmental phenotypes are attributed to POLE3 variants.
- Variant type: no patient variants. The only human variant mentioned is a somatic colorectal cancer CHRAC1 D121Y mutation, which is in a different gene and was studied biochemically (PMID: 37682991).
- Cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse only.
- Key findings: Pole3-null mice survive to late embryonic stages, so Pole3 is dispensable for DNA replication. Hypomorphic and neomorphic alleles show a stage-specific role for the histone fold domain in T and B cell development. Adding positive charges to the acidic C terminus causes peripheral lymphopenia of increasing severity (PMID: 32553171). The study reports no neurological or behavioral phenotypes, and no Mendelian ratio data are given in the summary beyond late embryonic survival of nulls.
- Consistency with human phenotype: the phenotype is immunological and has no demonstrated relationship to ASD.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED, and not specific to ASD or neurodevelopment.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and molecular function:
  - POLE3-POLE4 "grips" newly synthesised dsDNA to support Polε processivity. It acts in parallel with CHTF18-RFC2/5, and loss of both is synthetic lethal (PMID: 41339636). POLE3 is also required for survival in DSCC1-knockout cells (PMID: 36622344).
  - The POLE3-POLE4 pathway takes part in transferring parental H3.1 and H3.3 histones after replication. Mutants defective in this transfer show chromosome segregation defects (PMID: 35523900).
  - POLE3 or POLE4 deficiency causes loss of asymmetric H3K9me3 inheritance and increased LINE-1 expression (PMID: 37938774).
  - POLE3 interacts with CHRAC1 to promote DNA double-strand break repair (PMID: 37682991).
  - Loss of POLE3 or POLE4 causes marked hypersensitivity to ATR inhibitors (PMID: 31506018).
- Expression: in mouse NIH3T3 cells, Pole3 peaks at S-phase entry and is regulated by E2F1/4 and MYC through a bidirectional promoter shared with C9orf46 (PMID: 16403426). No brain or neurodevelopmental expression data were found.
- Cancer-related findings: POLE3 is linked to cisplatin resistance in testicular germ cell tumors (PMID: 29618620). In pancreatic cancer cells, USP15 stabilizes POLE3 and promotes DNA repair (PMID: 41950365).
- Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED for ASD. The function is well characterized, but it is not connected to neuronal or neurodevelopmental mechanisms, and nothing in the evidence addresses haploinsufficiency or dominant effects.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT.
- Strengths: POLE3 has a well-defined role in replication, chromatin inheritance and DNA repair. The epigenetic inheritance roles are conceptually compatible with, though not evidence for, a neurodevelopmental mechanism.
- Gaps and limitations:
  - There are no human patients or variants.
  - There are no neurological phenotypes in mice.
  - There are no brain expression data.
  - There is no evidence for a heterozygous or dominant mechanism.
  - The existing literature focuses on immunology, replication and cancer.
- Conclusion: There is currently no clinical, model organism or biological evidence supporting POLE3 as an autosomal dominant ASD gene. Its established functions are in DNA replication, histone inheritance and repair, with a mouse phenotype restricted to lymphocyte development. Gene-disease association cannot be assessed until de novo or heterozygous variants are reported in individuals with ASD or neurodevelopmental disorders.
