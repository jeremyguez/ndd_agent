GABRB3 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER (heterozygous / autosomal dominant variants)

1. GENE SUMMARY
GABRB3 encodes the β3 subunit of the pentameric GABA-A receptor, the main ligand-gated chloride channel mediating fast inhibitory neurotransmission in the brain. It spans about 250 kb with 9 exons, in the imprinted 15q11-q13 Prader-Willi/Angelman region, in a cluster with GABRA5 and GABRG3 (PMID: 9126483).

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: three entries were extracted. They are one multi-center trio exome study of epileptic encephalopathy (PMID: 23934111), and two ASD-focused conference abstracts: ASHG 2016 (Whole-genome sequencing provides additional insight into the genetic architecture of autism) and ASHG 2009 (A High Resolution CNV Survey of the GABA Gene Family Candidate Genes in Autism).
- Patients: about 6 in total. Four had de novo variants and epileptic encephalopathy (PMID: 23934111). One had a de novo missense variant and ASD (ASHG 2016). One had a heterozygous 3-exon intragenic deletion and autism, absent in 250 controls (ASHG 2009).
- Phenotypes: epileptic encephalopathy (infantile spasms, Lennox-Gastaut syndrome) predominates. Autism appears in only two patients, both from abstracts.
- Variant type: predominantly de novo missense, plus one intragenic deletion. Specific variants were not given in the abstracts.
- Cohorts: none are from UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL. The cohorts are EPI4K/Epilepsy Phenome/Genome Project and the Simons Simplex Collection.
- Statistical support: GABRB3 showed a significant excess of de novo mutations in epileptic encephalopathy (P = 4.1 × 10^-10) and is intolerant to variation (PMID: 23934111). This supports a dominant, de novo mechanism, but the evidence is for epilepsy rather than ASD specifically.
- Related human data: 22 heterozygous carriers were identified among 416 epilepsy patients, with phenotypes from febrile seizures to epileptic encephalopathy (PMID: 28053010). These support the gene-disease relationship for epilepsy, not ASD.
- Strength of clinical evidence for ASD: LIMITED. Only two ASD cases appear, both from abstracts with unspecified variants, and the strong statistical signal is for epileptic encephalopathy.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse (full, heterozygous, conditional and large-deletion models) and zebrafish.
- Full knockout mice show spontaneous seizures, EEG abnormalities, increased mortality and Angelman-like behavior (PMID: 10515160). They also show hypotonia, increased risk-assessment behavior and an expanded locus coeruleus dendritic zone (PMID: 17156762).
- The Ube3a-to-Gabrb3 homozygous deletion is perinatally lethal because of cleft palate from the Gabrb3 null. Maternal-deletion mice are viable with seizures, impaired learning and memory, and altered ultrasonic vocalization (PMID: 20808828).
- Heterozygous knockout mice show increased seizure susceptibility, tactile and heat hypersensitivity, and altered prepulse inhibition, with sex and parent-of-origin effects (PMID: 20699105). Wild-type γ2 subunits are reduced and less clustered at inhibitory synapses (PMID: 31435640).
- Conditional endothelial knockouts show forebrain vascular defects, impaired interneuron migration and persistent behavioral deficits (PMID: 29086765). They also show hypertension, altered vessel density and cardiac histology, reduced short-term memory and altered prepulse inhibition (PMID: 35318369). Prenatal NAD+ treatment rescued angiogenesis and behavior (PMID: 33036972).
- Zebrafish gabrb3 CRISPR mutants show unprovoked electrographic seizures (PMID: 34083748).
- Consistency with humans: seizures and the autism-relevant features of altered sensory processing, social and anxiety-like behavior and impaired learning are concordant with the human epilepsy/ASD/neurodevelopmental phenotype. Heterozygous mice show a phenotype, which is consistent with dominant haploinsufficiency. No Mendelian ratio data were reported.
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE to STRONG. Models are consistent across species and approaches, but the specific autism-like behavioral phenotypes are partly confounded by the Angelman deletion and by endothelial or parent-of-origin effects.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Function: GABRB3 is a core subunit of GABA-A receptors mediating inhibitory signaling. In oocytes, 5 of 7 epilepsy-associated mutations reduced GABA-induced current or sensitivity, consistent with loss of function (PMID: 28053010). Some variants such as P253L/P253S instead caused gain of function, with 3- to 23-fold increased GABA sensitivity, and were associated with developmental and epileptic encephalopathy (PMID: 39642202). Both loss and gain of function may therefore be pathogenic.
- Expression and neurodevelopment: Gabrb3 is enriched in contralaterally projecting pyramidal neurons. Its ablation reduces GABAergic synapses and alters network synchrony and connectivity, and GABRB3 expression correlates with atypical connectivity in human ASD datasets (PMID: 36446382). Deleting Gabrb3 in peripheral somatosensory neurons removes presynaptic inhibition and causes tactile hypersensitivity. Deletion during development, but not in adulthood, produced social deficits and anxiety-like behavior (PMID: 27293187).
- Other mechanisms: a 15q duplication neuronal model showed reduced GABRB3 transcript and disrupted 15q11-q13 pairing that depends on MeCP2 and CTCF (ASHG 2011 - Neuron-specific impairment of inter-chromosomal pairing and transcription in a novel model of human 15q-duplication syndrome). Heterozygous mice showed maternal immune activation effects, including altered fetal cortical development (ASHG 2017 - Multi-system contributions to Gabrb3-related neurodevelopmental risk in utero).
- Association studies are mixed. Rare variants, especially in the 5' regulatory region, were enriched in 356 ASD patients vs 386 controls (OR = 3.37, P = 0.007). Reporter assays showed increased expression for several of these variants (PMID: 24999380). A meta-analysis of two promoter SNPs found no association with ASD (PMID: 30074174).
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE to STRONG for a role in neurodevelopment and inhibitory signaling. Support is moderate for ASD specifically.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level for ASD: LIMITED to MODERATE. The evidence is strong for GABRB3 as a dominant de novo epilepsy/developmental and epileptic encephalopathy gene, but ASD-specific evidence is more limited.
- Key strengths: a highly significant de novo excess and intolerance to variation (PMID: 23934111); concordant mouse and zebrafish models, including heterozygous phenotypes; functional electrophysiology showing altered receptor function (PMID: 28053010); and mechanistic data linking Gabrb3 to somatosensory and social/anxiety phenotypes (PMID: 27293187).
- Key gaps: only two ASD cases, both from abstracts, with unspecified variants (ASHG 2016; ASHG 2009); no ASD cohort from UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL; ASD phenotypic details are absent; common-variant association is negative (PMID: 30074174); and the imprinting and 15q context complicates interpretation of the models.
- Conclusion: GABRB3 is a well-supported neurodevelopmental disease gene, with heterozygous (largely de novo missense) variants causing epilepsy and developmental and epileptic encephalopathy. Autism is plausibly part of the phenotypic spectrum, supported by model and biological data, but direct human ASD evidence remains sparse. ASD as a standalone gene-disease assertion is best rated as limited to moderate until detailed ASD case-level data with defined variants are published.
