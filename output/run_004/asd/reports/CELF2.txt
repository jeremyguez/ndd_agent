CELF2 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
CELF2 (CUGBP Elav-like family member 2) is an RNA-binding protein that regulates alternative splicing, mRNA stability and translation. It is broadly expressed and has roles in neurodevelopment and in cancer as a tumor suppressor. Its best-documented human disease association is a developmental and epileptic encephalopathy (DEE) with intellectual disability, with autism as a less well-documented feature.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: 3 were found. Only 2 are meaningful for a neurodevelopmental phenotype; the third is a VUS.
- Total patients: about 7 (5 in PMID: 33131106; 1 in PMID: 42036727; 1 in PMID: 39737487).
- Case series (PMID: 33131106): 5 individuals with heterozygous variants.
  - p.(Pro520Ser) in two unrelated individuals, both de novo.
  - p.(Arg506Gly), inherited from a mosaic mother.
  - p.(Tyr521Ter), de novo, which removes the last amino acid and may escape NMD.
  - c.272-1G>C canonical splice variant, de novo, predicted to cause NMD. This individual had intellectual disability and autistic features.
  - The missense and terminal variants cluster in the C-terminal putative nuclear localization signal. Functional studies showed extranuclear mislocalization of the mutant protein.
- ASD-specific case (PMID: 42036727): one Chinese child with ASD carried a heterozygous frameshift, NM_006561.3:c.1500delT, p.(Gln501Argfs*4). It was detected by WGS from newborn dried blood spots in a cohort of 92 children with autism and classified as likely pathogenic. No inheritance or de novo status was reported in the extracted data.
- VUS (PMID: 39737487): CELF2 c.1456G>A (missense) was an incidental, monoallelic VUS in a TAOK2-NDD individual and is not considered diagnostic.
- Common phenotypes: DEE (3 of 5 in the series), intellectual disability and developmental delay, and autistic features or ASD (2 individuals in total, one in each of PMID: 33131106 and PMID: 42036727).
- Variant types: mixed. There are 3 LoF variants among the pathogenic reports (frameshift, nonsense, splice) and 3 missense variants (p.Pro520Ser in two individuals, p.Arg506Gly). The overall pattern is de novo, with one maternal mosaic inheritance.
- Known cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none identified. One Chinese ASD WGS cohort was identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence for ASD: LIMITED. The evidence supports a neurodevelopmental disorder gene, mainly DEE and ID. Autism is reported in only about 2 individuals, and the small numbers and lack of cohort-level statistics restrict the ASD-specific conclusion.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse and zebrafish.
- Mouse full knockout (PMID: 42189331):
  - Perinatal lethality.
  - Impaired neuronal maturation and disrupted cortical organization, with thinning of upper cortical layers.
  - Selective loss of upper-layer II/III excitatory neurons.
  - Markedly reduced Camk2a transcript and protein.
  - Widespread splicing dysregulation of neurodevelopmental transcripts.
- Mouse Nestin-Cre heterozygous conditional knockout (PMID: 38829512):
  - Viable, with autism-like behavior (social impairment and anxiety).
  - No repetitive behavior or learning impairment.
  - Reduced cortical thickness, reduced dendritic spine density and impaired synaptic maturation.
  - Dysregulation of dendrite- and synapse-related genes.
  - This is the most directly relevant model for heterozygous loss and ASD.
- Zebrafish celf2+/- and morpholino knockdown (PMID: 40066778): epilepsy-like behavior and epileptic EEG activity, rescued by wild-type CELF2 mRNA, with altered neural marker gene expression. This is consistent with the DEE phenotype.
- Other zebrafish findings: a germline celf2 mutant showed no hindbrain rhombomere or neuronal defects (PMID: 29945667), a negative or limited finding. Triclosan-related knockdown work (PMID: 33621777) suggests neurotoxicity but is indirect.
- Other mouse findings: Celf2 mutant mice have defective axon regeneration (PMID: 27253061). Hematopoietic conditional knockout mice are viable and develop enhanced leukemogenesis (PMID: 38514854), which is not relevant to ASD.
- Consistency with human phenotype: good. Heterozygous loss produces autism-like behavior and cortical and synaptic abnormalities in mice, and epilepsy in zebrafish.
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE. The data are convergent, but there is no mendelian ratio data, sample sizes were not extracted, and one zebrafish mutant was normal.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Molecular function: CELF2 binds AU/U-rich elements to regulate alternative splicing and mRNA stability. Examples include FAM198B stabilization (PMID: 33335801), CD44 splicing (PMID: 35941702), ULK1 and CARD10 splicing (PMID: 31409895) and TREM2 exon 3 skipping (PMID: 33093587). Most of this work is in cancer and is not neurodevelopmental.
- Neural pathways: CELF2 regulates Map2k7/MKK7 splicing in the JNK pathway and affects LZK-induced Purkinje cell degeneration (PMID: 33475086). Dleu2-driven Celf2 transcription supports synaptic plasticity and dendritic spine number in the mouse infralimbic cortex (PMID: 41628728).
- Expression: CELF2 is broadly expressed in developing tissues, with nuclear and cytoplasmic localization and tissue-specific isoforms (PMID: 23468433). Normal cells show predominantly nuclear localization, and cytoplasmic mislocalization is seen in colon cancer (PMID: 23795348). This parallels the extranuclear mislocalization of the human NLS variants.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE. The molecular role in splicing and synaptic regulation is well supported, but most of the literature is in cancer.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level for CELF2 as an ASD gene with heterozygous variants: LIMITED. Evidence for a neurodevelopmental disorder (DEE and ID) is stronger than for ASD specifically.
- Key strengths:
  - Recurrent de novo variants, including a recurrent p.Pro520Ser in two unrelated individuals.
  - A clustered C-terminal NLS mechanism with functional mislocalization data (PMID: 33131106).
  - Both LoF and missense variants are reported.
  - A heterozygous conditional knockout mouse shows autism-like behavior (PMID: 38829512).
  - A zebrafish epilepsy model.
  - A well-defined role in neuronal splicing and synaptic function.
- Key gaps:
  - Few patients overall (about 7), with autism documented in only about 2.
  - Inheritance for the ASD frameshift case is not specified.
  - No cohort-level statistical enrichment.
  - No UDN, DDD or similar cohort data.
  - The ASD case comes from a single small WGS study.
  - Model organism sample sizes and Mendelian ratios are lacking.
  - Some LoF variants are located in the last exon or near the NLS, so mechanism (haploinsufficiency versus dominant-negative) is unclear.
- Conclusion: CELF2 is a credible neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous, mostly de novo variants causing DEE and ID, with autistic features in a subset. Support from mouse and zebrafish models and from known splicing and synaptic function is consistent. Evidence for ASD as a distinct primary phenotype remains limited and needs additional cases with detailed phenotyping and segregation or de novo confirmation.
