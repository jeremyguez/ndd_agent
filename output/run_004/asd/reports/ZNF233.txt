ZNF233 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
ZNF233 (19q13.31) encodes a 670-amino-acid Krüppel-type C2H2 zinc finger protein, a family usually associated with transcriptional regulation. The only functional data identified concern a pro-proliferative role in hepatocellular carcinoma (PMID: 30179682). No neurodevelopmental function has been described.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent case reports/series: 0
- Approximate total number of patients: 0
- Phenotypes: none reported. No individuals with ASD or any other neurodevelopmental phenotype are linked to ZNF233.
- Variant type: no pathogenic variants reported, so no LoF versus missense pattern can be assessed.
- Cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms studied: none identified. No knockout or knock-in models were found in the evidence provided.
- Key phenotypes (neurological, lethality, Mendelian ratio deviations): no data.
- Consistency with human phenotype: cannot be assessed.
- Strength of model organism evidence: NONE

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and molecular function: ZNF233 is predicted to be a transcription factor by virtue of its C2H2 zinc finger domains. Experimentally, it promotes G1/S transition and cell growth in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) cells (PMID: 30179682). No target genes or neurodevelopmental pathways are defined in the evidence.
- Expression: ZNF233 mRNA is markedly upregulated in HCC tissue compared with matched non-tumorous liver. Its expression correlates with tumor grade, stage and prognosis (PMID: 30179682). It is also overexpressed in hepatoblastoma versus normal liver and contributes to a tumor-identity signature in a single-cell classifier (signature-level AUC 0.99, F1 0.97, not specific to ZNF233) (PMID: 42385356). No brain or neurodevelopmental expression data were provided.
- Key experimental findings: Overexpression in SMMC-7721 cells promoted proliferation. Knockdown in QGY-7701 cells reduced proliferation in vitro and in xenografts, with fewer Ki-67-positive cells (PMID: 30179682). All of this is oncology-related and has no direct bearing on ASD.
- Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED for general function; NONE for relevance to ASD or neurodevelopment.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT

- Key strengths: ZNF233 is a zinc finger protein with experimentally supported roles in cell proliferation in liver cancer models (PMID: 30179682). Its overexpression in hepatoblastoma has been replicated in a second tumor type (PMID: 42385356).

- Key gaps and limitations: There are no human cases, no heterozygous variants, no segregation or de novo data, and no model organisms. There is no brain expression or neuronal functional data. The only available literature is cancer-focused, and sample sizes and significance values were not reported.

- Conclusion: There is currently no evidence supporting ZNF233 as an autosomal dominant autism spectrum disorder gene. The existing literature addresses only its role in liver tumor biology. Any association with ASD would require identification of de novo or heterozygous variants in affected individuals, together with neurodevelopmental functional data.
