TAOK1 EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER / NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
TAOK1 encodes thousand-and-one amino acid kinase 1, a Ste20-like serine/threonine kinase. It regulates MAPK signaling (p38, ERK, JNK), microtubule dynamics, and neuronal migration and maturation. It also acts in innate immune signaling and the DNA damage response.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: Six clinical reports were provided. Five are directly relevant to neurodevelopmental disorder (NDD) or ASD:
  - a large international series (PMID: 39737487)
  - a family and matchmaking series (PMID: 35091509)
  - a single case report (PMID: 38443934)
  - the ASHG 2017 abstract (ASHG 2017 - Novel de novo TAOK1 variants associated with a neurodevelopmental phenotype, macrocephaly, and joint hypermobility)
  - an ASD proband in a mouse study (PMID: 37656623)
  
  A sixth report, a single craniosynostosis patient, is of uncertain relevance (PMID: 42607197). The biological evidence also describes a 23-person cohort (PMID: 33565190).
- Patient numbers: The clinical reports total about 64 patients (50 + 4 + 1 + 7 + 1 + 1). Adding the 23-person cohort (PMID: 33565190) gives roughly 87, though overlap between cohorts is likely and cannot be excluded. The 50-patient study carried 37 unique variants (PMID: 39737487).
- Common phenotypes: All 50 patients in the large series had neurodevelopmental abnormalities, 83% had macrocephaly, and 58% had hypotonia (PMID: 39737487). Other frequent features were:
  - feeding difficulties
  - joint hypermobility or laxity
  - facial dysmorphism
  - behavioral problems and ASD
  - GI issues
  - occasional male genital anomalies and hypoglycemia
  
  Seizures were absent in the ASHG 2017 series. Single reports describe periventricular nodular heterotopia (PMID: 38443934) and craniosynostosis (PMID: 42607197).
- Variant types: Variants are mixed LoF (nonsense, frameshift, splice) and missense. Missense variants cluster in the kinase domain at missense-intolerant residues (PMID: 39737487). Most variants are de novo. One frameshift was inherited from a mildly affected mother, which suggests incomplete penetrance and variable expressivity (PMID: 35091509).
- ASD specifically: ASD is reported in the ASHG 2017 series and in a de novo frameshift proband (PMID: 37656623). Most evidence is for broader NDD, with ASD as one component.
- Known cohorts: GeneDx (USA) was the source of de novo variants identified among 9,302 NDD exomes, with GeneMatcher recruitment (ASHG 2017 - Novel de novo TAOK1 variants associated with a neurodevelopmental phenotype, macrocephaly, and joint hypermobility). UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC and GEL are not explicitly named in the abstracts. The other studies were international matchmaking or multicenter efforts.
- Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: The model organism findings include Xenopus tropicalis (knockdown) and mouse (knockdown or deficiency models). The mouse Taok1 haploinsufficiency study, which reports autistic-like behaviors via the dorsal raphe nucleus, was cited only in the clinical findings (PMID: 37656623).
- Key phenotypes:
  - Xenopus: TAOK1 knockdown impaired motile cilia formation and heart development, and TAOK1 perturbation impaired primary cilia formation in neural progenitors in vitro (PMID: 40552535).
  - Mouse: Taok1 haploinsufficiency produced autistic-like behaviors (PMID: 37656623). TAOK1 deficiency exacerbated colitis (PMID: 29400705). AAV-mediated silencing was protective in doxorubicin cardiomyopathy (PMID: 39964965).
  
  Lethality, Mendelian ratios and fertility data were not reported.
- Consistency with human phenotype: The haploinsufficiency mouse autism-like phenotype matches the human ASD/NDD and supports a LoF mechanism. The Xenopus ciliary and cardiac findings are tangential. The immune and cardiac mouse phenotypes are unrelated to the human NDD.
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE. The one directly relevant haploinsufficiency mouse study is supportive, but there is little neurodevelopmental characterization in the provided data and no systematic Mendelian ratio data.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: TAOK1 is a MAPK-pathway kinase.
  - It regulates ERK1/2 through TRAF6/TPL2 in TLR4 signaling (PMID: 32344244).
  - It dampens IL-17 signaling by binding IL-17RA (PMID: 29400705).
  - It promotes TBK1-IRF3 antiviral signaling, using microtubule/dynein trafficking (PMID: 36649698).
  - It phosphorylates ETS2 (PMID: 40983701) and USP7 (supporting RAD51 and HR repair) (PMID: 40106350).
  - It modulates p38 MAPK in cardiomyocyte death (PMID: 39964965).
- Expression and neurodevelopmental relevance: The neuronal findings come from the 23-person cohort study (PMID: 33565190). Altered TAOK1 levels disrupted neural migration in embryonic mouse brain in vivo and neuronal maturation in vitro. Missense variants act through loss of function or dominant-negative effects. Other expression data concern liver (62 participants; PMID: 36930872) and tumors, not brain.
- Other findings: TAOK1 is upregulated in cancers (ESCC, NSCLC) (PMID: 38376345; PMID: 36158126), and silencing it protects against hepatocellular lipotoxicity (PMID: 36930872). These are largely unrelated to NDD.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE. The neurodevelopmental functional data are strong but come from essentially one study, and the other pathway data are not neuro-specific.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: STRONG.
- Key strengths:
  - Many unrelated patients, roughly 64 to 87, with mostly de novo variants.
  - A consistent phenotype of NDD, macrocephaly, hypotonia, feeding difficulties and joint hypermobility.
  - Recurrent kinase-domain missense clustering together with truncating variants.
  - Functional support from in vivo neuronal migration and maturation assays (PMID: 33565190).
  - A haploinsufficiency mouse model with autistic-like behavior (PMID: 37656623).
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - ASD is part of a broader NDD phenotype and is not the sole or primary feature in most patients.
  - Specific variant notations were unavailable in the abstracts.
  - Cohort overlap cannot be excluded.
  - Penetrance is incomplete in at least one family (PMID: 35091509).
  - Two ASHG 2017 patients carried additional de novo variants.
  - The craniosynostosis report is of uncertain relevance.
  - Mouse and Xenopus data lack Mendelian ratio, lethality and detailed neuroanatomical data.
  - Missense mechanisms (LoF versus dominant-negative) are not fully resolved.
- Conclusion: TAOK1 is a well-established autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene. Heterozygous de novo LoF and kinase-domain missense variants cause a syndromic NDD with frequent ASD, macrocephaly, hypotonia and joint hypermobility. The evidence is supported by functional neuronal data and a haploinsufficiency mouse model. For ASD specifically, the association is strong within the context of syndromic NDD.
