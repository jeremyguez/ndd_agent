NUDCD2 EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) MODEL

1. GENE SUMMARY
NUDCD2 (NudC domain containing 2) encodes a protein described as an Hsp90 co-chaperone. It associates with LIS1 (PAFAH1B1), HERC2 and cohesin subunits, and it is implicated in cell migration, microtubule extension and chromosome segregation (PMID: 37272762; PMID: 39172275).

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- The clinical evidence extraction returned no entries. The only disease-relevant observations come from the biological evidence set, which I summarize here for context.
- Independent reports: One peer-reviewed report describes two unrelated patients with biallelic NUDCD2 variants (PMID: 37272762). A conference abstract describes NUDCD2 as a candidate gene in patients with ataxia, dystonia and intellectual disability (ASHG 2014 - The utility of clinical exome sequencing in identifying the genetic origins of eight unclassified developmental disorders in unique Canadian populations). That abstract covers 19 patients by WES across 8 disorders, and the number of NUDCD2 patients, the variants and the inheritance are not specified.
- Total patients: About 2 are documented with biallelic variants. The number in the ASHG abstract is unclear.
- Phenotypes: The biallelic patients had dysmorphic facies, midline brain hypoplasia, hypothyroidism, valve stenosis, liver and kidney failure, hypotonia and early death, with ciliopathy-like features (PMID: 37272762). The ASHG abstract reports ataxia, dystonia and intellectual disability.
- Variant type: The biallelic patients had loss-of-function variants, and one patient's fibroblasts lacked NUDCD2 protein (PMID: 37272762). No heterozygous variants are reported.
- Cohorts: No UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL involvement is documented.
- Autism: No patient with autism spectrum disorder is reported. No heterozygous or de novo variants are reported.
- Strength of clinical evidence for the ASD/autosomal dominant hypothesis: NONE. The disease association that exists is recessive and multisystem.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- No model organism data were identified.
- No knockout phenotypes, lethality data or Mendelian ratio data are available, so consistency with human phenotype cannot be assessed.
- Strength: NONE.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: NUDCD2 acts as an Hsp90 co-chaperone. It interacts with LIS1, a lissencephaly-associated protein relevant to neuronal migration (PMID: 37272762; ASHG 2014). In mouse ESCs it interacts with Smc1a and with RetSat, and RetSat deletion reduced Smc1a, Smc3 and Nudcd2 loading onto mitotic chromosomes. This links Nudcd2 to chromosome segregation (PMID: 39172275).
- Expression: No brain or neurodevelopmental expression data were provided. Expression changes were reported in unrelated contexts. These include downregulation in osteoporosis (PMID: 38715824), in bovine blastocysts from cows with subclinical endometritis (PMID: 22781929), and an association with HPV+ head and neck squamous cell carcinoma survival (PMID: 31310629).
- Other experimental findings: Proteomic screens in cell culture detected altered NUDCD2 levels in homocysteine-treated vascular smooth muscle cells (PMID: 41644998) and in dinutuximab beta-treated insulinoma cells (PMID: 41123852). These are incidental and not relevant to neurodevelopment.
- Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED. The LIS1 and cohesin links are plausible for neurodevelopment, but there is no direct functional evidence for NUDCD2 in autism or in haploinsufficiency.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level for NUDCD2 as an autosomal dominant ASD gene: INSUFFICIENT.
- Strengths: There is a plausible mechanism through LIS1, microtubule and cohesin biology. There is a recessive loss-of-function disorder with protein loss confirmed in patient fibroblasts (PMID: 37272762).
- Gaps and limitations:
  - No heterozygous variants have been reported, and no ASD cases or de novo variants have been described.
  - Only two patients with biallelic variants are published.
  - The ASHG abstract lacks detail.
  - There are no model organism data and no brain-specific expression data.
  - Most of the other biological citations are incidental expression or proteomic observations.
- Conclusion: The available evidence supports NUDCD2 as a candidate gene for a severe recessive multisystem syndrome, not as an autism gene acting through heterozygous pathogenic variants. Establishing an autosomal dominant ASD association would require the identification of heterozygous (ideally de novo) variants in ASD cohorts, together with functional or animal model support.
