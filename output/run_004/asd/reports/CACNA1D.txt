CACNA1D LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
CACNA1D encodes the pore-forming α1 subunit of the Cav1.3 L-type voltage-gated Ca2+ channel. Cav1.3 supports cardiac pacemaking, hearing and hormone secretion, and is expressed postsynaptically in neurons, where it shapes firing and plasticity (PMID: 26842699).

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: 9 entries were found. These comprise a multi-patient review/series, several single-variant case reports, a twin pair, a two-patient report, a family study, a Japanese cohort abstract and a Russian ASD cohort report.
- Total patients: roughly 25 or more. This includes 12 individuals in Hofer/Wang et al. (PMID: 32583268), 2 with G1169D (PMID: 39246741), 1 with V401L (PMID: 28472301), 2 with A749G and G407R (PMID: 25620733), 2 twins with S652L (PMID: 31921405), 1 with Q558H (PMID: 30054272), 2 in the Japanese cohort, 1 in the Russian cohort, and an unspecified number in a three-generation R930H family (PMID: 36430690). The 12-patient series may overlap with some individually reported cases.
- Common phenotypes: ASD, neurodevelopmental disorder or developmental delay, intellectual disability, and seizures/epilepsy. Hypotonia, congenital tremor, sensorineural or moderate hearing impairment, and primary aldosteronism or other endocrine features occur in some patients. Sinus node dysfunction and ADHD were reported in the R930H family.
- Variant type: predominantly de novo missense variants. Electrophysiology shows gain-of-function (GOF) gating changes in V401L, A749G, G407R, S652L and G1169D (PMID: 28472301; PMID: 25620733; PMID: 31921405; PMID: 39246741).
- Exceptions to the GOF pattern: Q558H reduced current density (PMID: 30054272). R930H showed isoform-specific GOF and LOF (PMID: 36430690).
- Weaker data: a Japanese cohort abstract reported a p.C1471* nonsense variant and a p.E1675del in schizophrenia/ASD, without a specific diagnosis (ASHG 2015 - Identification of rare disruptive variants in voltage-gated channel genes). Two VUS were reported in a Russian child with ASD (PMID: 41255692).
- Cohorts: S652L was reported from the DDD study (PMID: 31921405). No UDN, GREGoR, RUMC or GEL cohorts were identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE to STRONG for a neurodevelopmental disorder, including ASD, caused by de novo GOF missense variants. ASD is a core feature in only some patients (A749G, G407R, V401L), and the phenotype is often broader (seizures, developmental delay). The evidence for ASD specifically is MODERATE.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse (mostly full and conditional Cav1.3 knockouts) and zebrafish.
- Mouse knockouts are viable. Reported phenotypes include:
  - Reduced Purkinje cell dendritic complexity, with normal eyeblink conditioning (PMID: 41075059).
  - Impaired dentate gyrus neurogenesis and hippocampus-dependent memory (PMID: 26459417).
  - Deficits in motor learning and associative learning, with no change in anxiety-like, depression-like or social preference behaviors (PMID: 35044095).
  - Profound hearing loss with inner hair cell differentiation arrest in cochlear conditional knockouts (PMID: 31178698).
  - Loss of 43-47% of superior olivary complex volume in brainstem conditional knockouts (PMID: 22678062).
- Zebrafish: heterozygous cacna1da splice mutants (haploinsufficiency) showed hyperlocomotion and psychosis-like behaviour, reversed by antipsychotics. There were no epileptiform discharges, and homozygotes had low viability and sub-Mendelian ratios (PMID: 33057948).
- Consistency with human disease: partial. The knockouts model loss of function, whereas the human ASD/neurodevelopmental variants are mostly GOF. Deafness and cognitive or learning deficits overlap somewhat with human features (hearing impairment in Q558H and G1169D). Autism-like social deficits were not seen in the knockout mice. No GOF knock-in neurodevelopmental model was identified. The only knock-in (rat D307G) addresses blood pressure (PMID: 35142739).
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Function: Cav1.3 is a low-voltage-activated L-type channel. Somatic GOF variants cause aldosterone-producing adenomas, and germline GOF variants cause neurodevelopmental disease. In idiopathic hyperaldosteronism, somatic CACNA1D mutations were found in 58% (57 of 99) of aldosterone-producing cell clusters (PMID: 30354720).
- Neuronal expression: Cav1.3 is expressed postsynaptically in neurons and shapes firing and plasticity. Loss of function in mice and humans does not cause neuropsychiatric disease, whereas GOF variants are linked to ASD and seizures (PMID: 26842699).
- Mechanism: S652L shifted activation and inactivation by about 13-17 mV to more negative voltages and increased window current, and sensitivity to isradipine rose 3-4-fold. A benign-population variant, S652W, shifted gating the opposite way (PMID: 31921405). L-type channel blockers are proposed as possible therapy (PMID: 32583268).
- Other contexts: roles in prostate cancer calcium handling (PMID: 35193095; PMID: 35496494) and macrophage inflammation (PMID: 41223220). These are not relevant to ASD.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE to STRONG for GOF as the disease mechanism. Evidence on brain expression during development is thin in the supplied data.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: MODERATE (approaching STRONG for a GOF neurodevelopmental disorder; MODERATE for ASD specifically).
- Key strengths: recurrent de novo missense variants in multiple independent reports; consistent GOF electrophysiology across several variants, with a plausible mechanism and therapeutic angle; a DDD-cohort case; and a paralogous somatic GOF disease, primary aldosteronism, supporting the mechanism.
- Key gaps: ASD is rarely the sole phenotype, and ASD diagnoses were often not independently detailed. Mouse knockouts do not model the GOF mechanism and lack an autism-like phenotype. Truncating and in-frame variants (C1471*, E1675del) and the Russian VUS are weakly supported. There are no large case-control or burden analyses in the supplied data, and individual variants in the 12-patient series are not listed. Q558H and R930H show non-GOF or mixed effects, which complicates a simple GOF model.
- Conclusion: CACNA1D is a well-supported gene for a de novo, autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder caused by GOF missense variants, with ASD as a recurrent feature alongside seizures, intellectual disability and variable endocrine, cardiac and hearing involvement. Evidence for ASD specifically is moderate, and LOF variants are not established as a cause. A GOF animal model and larger genotype-phenotype series would strengthen the case.
