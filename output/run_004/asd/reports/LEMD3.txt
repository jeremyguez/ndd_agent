LEMD3 AND AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER: LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT

1. GENE SUMMARY
LEMD3 (also called MAN1) encodes an inner nuclear membrane protein that antagonizes BMP/TGF-β signaling by binding R-SMADs, and it contributes to nuclear lamina-chromatin organization. Heterozygous loss-of-function variants cause the established sclerosing bone disorders osteopoikilosis, Buschke-Ollendorff syndrome and, in some families, melorheostosis-like lesions (PMID: 15489854). No established association with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) was found in the evidence provided.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: only one clinical report was extracted, a 12q14 microdeletion series (PMID: 21267005). Of its 6 patients, 2 had deletions that included LEMD3 and HMGA2.
- Total patients: about 2 with LEMD3 deletion (6 in the full series).
- Phenotypes: short stature (attributed to HMGA2), developmental delay, significant speech delay and subtle facial dysmorphism. Congenital heart defects occurred in the overall series. Neither patient with LEMD3 deletion had osteopoikilosis. ASD was not reported in the abstract.
- Variant type: multigenic interstitial deletions. These are not gene-specific, and the phenotypes cannot be attributed to LEMD3.
- Cohorts: no UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL patients were identified. The cases came from diagnostic testing centres.
- Bone-disease literature in the biological evidence (PMIDs: 17087626, 16470551, 26694706, ASHG 2011 - A novel mutation in the gene LEMD3 in a patient with sub-clinical Osteopoikilosis) describes loss-of-function variants (p.L478X, p.Y441X, p.R655X, p.Y871X) and one missense variant (R901W). These variants are linked to osteopoikilosis/BOS, not to neurodevelopmental or ASD phenotypes.
- Strength of clinical evidence for ASD: NONE.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: a mouse conditional knockout in vascular smooth muscle cells (PMID: 41044070), a zebrafish model used to test a LEMD3 variant from a brain arteriovenous malformation patient (PMID: 30120215), and Xenopus XMAN1 work cited as prior evidence of BMP antagonism (PMID: 15489854). The heterozygous Lemd3 gene-trap mouse is described in PMID: 19862465.
- Key phenotypes: the VSMC-specific knockout lost the contractile phenotype and showed exacerbated intimal hyperplasia (PMID: 41044070). Heterozygous gene-trap mice showed no osteopoikilosis-like lesions (PMID: 19862465). Lethality, Mendelian ratios and neurological or behavioural phenotypes were not reported.
- Consistency with human phenotype: the mouse haploinsufficiency model does not reproduce the human bone phenotype (PMID: 19862465). No neurobehavioural or ASD-relevant phenotypes have been assessed.
- Strength of model organism evidence for ASD: NONE (LIMITED for non-neurological functions).

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: LEMD3 is an inner nuclear membrane protein that antagonizes BMP/TGF-β signaling via R-SMADs (PMID: 19862465). It interacts with CBX3 (HP1γ) to anchor heterochromatin at the nuclear periphery, and its loss increases inter-TAD interactions, reduces chromatin accessibility and represses contractile genes in VSMCs (PMID: 41044070).
- Other findings: LEMD3 is a target of miR-223-3p in hepatic stellate cell activation and liver fibrosis (PMID: 32003753). The intragenic SNP rs138916004 was associated with venous thromboembolism in African-Americans (OR=3.2; p=1.3E-08; 393 cases, 4,941 controls) (PMID: 28203683). LEMD3 is absent from human spermatids (PMID: 28684548).
- Brain expression: no brain or neurodevelopmental expression data were provided.
- Strength of biological evidence for ASD: LIMITED. The function is plausible, since BMP/TGF-β signaling and chromatin organization are relevant to neurodevelopment, but this is indirect and untested in neural contexts.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level for LEMD3 as an ASD gene: INSUFFICIENT.
- Strengths: LEMD3 loss of function is well established in autosomal dominant sclerosing bone dysplasias, with multiple families and truncating variants (PMIDs: 17087626, 16470551, 26694706). It has well-characterized roles in BMP/TGF-β signaling and chromatin architecture.
- Gaps: there are no ASD or neurodevelopmental cases with intragenic LEMD3 variants. The only neurodevelopmental-related observation is developmental and speech delay in multigene 12q14 deletions, which are confounded by HMGA2 and other genes (PMID: 21267005). Those deletion patients also lacked osteopoikilosis. There are no neural models, no brain expression data and no de novo variant or cohort-level enrichment data.
- Conclusion: the available evidence does not support LEMD3 as an autosomal dominant ASD gene. Its established disease association is with osteopoikilosis/Buschke-Ollendorff syndrome. Any neurodevelopmental link would require new evidence, such as de novo or segregating LoF variants in ASD cohorts and neural functional studies.

### Model Organism Evidence
1. Hellemans et al. 2004 - Loss-of-function mutations in LEMD3 result in osteopoikilosis, Buschke-Ollendorff syndrome and melorheostosis (PMID: 15489854)
2. LEMD3 organizes the 3D chromatin architecture to maintain vascular smooth muscle cell identity (PMID: 41044070)
3. Perturbations of BMP/TGF-β and VEGF/VEGFR signalling pathways in non-syndromic sporadic brain arteriovenous malformations (BAVM) (PMID: 30120215)

### Biological Evidence
1. Deactivating germline mutations in LEMD3 cause osteopoikilosis and Buschke-Ollendorff syndrome, but not sporadic melorheostosis (PMID: 17087626)
2. Germline LEMD3 mutations are rare in sporadic patients with isolated melorheostosis (PMID: 16470551)
3. Identification of a novel LEMD3 Y871X mutation in a three-generation family with osteopoikilosis and review of the literature (PMID: 26694706)
4. The heterozygous Lemd3 +/GT mouse is not a murine model for osteopoikilosis in humans (PMID: 19862465)
5. Hsa_circ_0070963 inhibits liver fibrosis via regulation of miR-223-3p and LEMD3 (PMID: 32003753)
6. Identification of unique venous thromboembolism-susceptibility variants in African-Americans (PMID: 28203683)
7. LEM-domain proteins are lost during human spermiogenesis but BAF and BAF-L persist (PMID: 28684548)
