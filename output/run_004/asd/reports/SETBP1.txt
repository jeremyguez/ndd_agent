SETBP1 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) PATHOGENIC VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
SETBP1 encodes SET binding protein 1, a nuclear protein with AT-hook DNA-binding motifs and a degron motif. It acts as a transcriptional regulator and epigenetic hub that recruits a HCF1/KMT2A/PHF8 complex to AT-rich promoters (PMID: 29875417). It also stabilizes SET, which inhibits PP2A and P53. Distinct heterozygous mechanisms produce distinct disorders. Haploinsufficiency (SETBP1-HD) causes a neurodevelopmental disorder, and gain-of-function degron missense variants cause Schinzel-Giedion syndrome (SGS).

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Number of reports: Nine clinical sources were extracted. Two are large-scale enrichment/discovery studies (PMID: 25217958; ASHG 2013 - Discovery of Genes Responsible for Neurocognitive Disease by Large Scale Integration of Sequence and Copy Number Data), and one is a registry study. The rest are small case series or single diagnostic findings from exome or panel cohorts.
- Patient numbers: Explicit counts total about 40 (34 in the Simons Searchlight registry (PMID: 38923504); 3 in an Italian panel study (PMID: 33391157); 2 in an Australian speech cohort (PMID: 41530369); 1 each in PMID: 30977854 and PMID: 33739554). Several large studies gave no counts (PMID: 25217958; PMID: 39931922; PMID: 37086723), so the true total is higher.
- Common phenotypes: Intellectual disability and developmental delay, speech and language impairment (including childhood apraxia of speech, PMID: 41530369), ADHD, autism spectrum disorder, seizures, hypotonia, and feeding difficulties. The Simons Searchlight cohort lists ASD and ADHD among its main features (PMID: 38923504). The ASHG 2013 abstract lists autism among the phenotypes of LoF carriers. Other reported features include craniosynostosis (PMID: 37086723), motor speech disorder (PMID: 39931922), and epilepsy (PMID: 33391157).
- Variant types: Predominantly LoF (nonsense, frameshift, deletions), with a few missense variants. One example is p.Glu858Lys in a patient with severe epilepsy (PMID: 33391157). De novo occurrence is documented (PMID: 33391157; PMID: 41530369).
- Cohorts: Simons Searchlight registry (USA) is the only named cohort. None are from UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL as extracted. CHOP (PMID: 30977854) is a single-center study.
- ASD-specific limitations: Autism is reported as one feature among several, usually from registry or abstract-level data. Individual ASD diagnoses are not tied to specific variants, and several abstracts lack variant-level detail.
- Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG for a SETBP1 haploinsufficiency neurodevelopmental disorder. For ASD specifically it is MODERATE, since ASD is a recurrent but not universal feature.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: Mouse only. A newly developed heterozygous Setbp1 knockout was studied (PMID: 41640626).
- Key phenotypes: Hyperactivity in the open field, decreased social interest in the 3-chamber test, and increased olfactory exploration. Hypoexcitability of mPFC layer-5 extratelencephalic projection neurons and altered apical dendritic branching in prefrontal pyramidal neurons were also seen. Lethality, Mendelian ratio, and fertility data were not reported.
- Consistency with human phenotype: Good. The behaviors parallel the ADHD and ASD features of SETBP1-HD patients (PMID: 41640626).
- Strength: LIMITED to MODERATE. It is a single, recent study with no sample size reported, but it is directly relevant to ASD-like behavior and shows a circuit-level correlate.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: SETBP1 binds AT-rich promoters via its AT-hooks and recruits an epigenetic complex (HCF1/KMT2A/PHF8). Its targets are enriched for brain morphogenesis and organ development genes. In utero electroporation of mutant SETBP1 impaired mouse neurogenesis and delayed neuronal migration (PMID: 29875417). SETBP1 accumulation stabilizes SET, inhibits P53, and causes DNA damage and apoptosis resistance in human SGS neural progenitors (PMID: 34193871).
- Expression: Specific expression data were not provided, though the target enrichment and neurogenesis findings support neurodevelopmental relevance.
- Other findings: Somatic degron mutations are found in myeloid malignancies, for example 17% of secondary AML and 15% of CMML (PMID: 23832012). They are the same sites as the SGS germline mutations, a gain-of-function mechanism distinct from haploinsufficiency. A non-degron variant, D874V, also causes SGS (PMID: 38711130). A de novo variant in a moderate-to-severe ID trio cohort (PMID: 25356899) placed SETBP1 and SET in an ID gene network. Cancer, AML therapy, and disordered eating findings (PMID: 35057575) are not relevant to ASD.
- Limitations: Most mechanistic work concerns gain-of-function and leukemia rather than haploinsufficiency and ASD.
- Strength: MODERATE. Gene function and neurodevelopmental relevance are well supported, but ASD-specific mechanisms are not.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: STRONG for SETBP1 haploinsufficiency as an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder in which ASD is a recurrent feature. For ASD as an isolated phenotype, it is MODERATE.
- Key strengths: Multiple independent LoF and de novo findings, with enrichment in cases over controls (PMID: 25217958). A 34-patient registry reports ASD and ADHD (PMID: 38923504). A heterozygous mouse model recapitulates social and hyperactivity phenotypes (PMID: 41640626). The protein's role in neurogenesis and epigenetic regulation is established (PMID: 29875417).
- Key gaps: Autism prevalence in carriers is not quantified in the extracted data. Many variants are unspecified, and the mouse evidence rests on one study. No UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL data were found. Missense variants outside the degron are of unclear significance, and the gain-of-function (SGS) and loss-of-function mechanisms must be kept separate.
- Conclusion: SETBP1 heterozygous loss-of-function variants cause a well-established neurodevelopmental disorder (SETBP1-HD) in which autism spectrum disorder, ADHD, ID, and speech impairment are prominent. Human genetic evidence and supportive mouse and functional data justify classifying SETBP1 as a gene associated with ASD in the context of this syndrome. Further work should quantify ASD frequency among variant carriers and provide additional model organism validation.
