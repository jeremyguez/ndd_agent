SLC39A8 EVIDENCE REPORT: ASSESSMENT AS A HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

1. GENE SUMMARY
SLC39A8 encodes ZIP8, a plasma membrane divalent metal transporter. It functions as an electroneutral Zn2+/(HCO3-)2 symporter (PMID: 21658371) and also transports Mn, Fe and Cd. Its key physiological role is Mn uptake, including at the blood-brain barrier and the intestine, and Mn is a cofactor for glycosylation enzymes such as β-1,4-galactosyltransferase. The established disease association is autosomal recessive SLC39A8-CDG (congenital disorder of glycosylation), not a dominant disorder.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: 3 were extracted. One is a true case series of recessive SLC39A8-CDG, and two are association or population studies.
- Approximate patients: 3 CDG patients in the case report (PMID: 34246313). The scoliosis association study had 1,298 cases (PMID: 30301978). The schizophrenia/metal-transport study (PMID: 32753748) did not report a patient count in the extraction, though it also profiled 2 SLC39A8-CDG patients.
- Phenotypes in CDG patients (PMID: 34246313):
  - severe developmental disability
  - dystonia and dyskinesia
  - Leigh syndrome-like basal ganglia, thalamic and white matter changes
  - cerebral and cerebellar atrophy
  - variable or near-normal transferrin IEF, with abnormal serum N-glycans
- Variant types and inheritance:
  - The CDG cases are biallelic missense variants: two siblings are compound heterozygous for p.Ser44Trp and p.Gly350Arg, and one unrelated patient is homozygous for p.Ala391Thr (PMID: 34246313).
  - No patients with heterozygous pathogenic variants and a neurodevelopmental disorder were identified.
  - Heterozygous carriers of the common hypofunctional allele p.Ala391Thr (rs13107325) show lower serum Mn, altered plasma N-glycans, MRI signal changes and schizophrenia risk (PMID: 32753748). They are also at increased risk of severe adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (OR 1.94, combined P = 7.02e-14; PMID: 30301978). These are common-variant susceptibility associations, not Mendelian neurodevelopmental disease.
- Cohorts: none of the studies were from UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL. The CDG report is from Italy.
- Strength of clinical evidence for a heterozygous neurodevelopmental disorder: NONE. The evidence for a recessive disorder is established but outside the question asked.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse (the majority) and zebrafish.
- Severe or complete loss of function:
  - The full knockout is embryonic lethal with a left ventricular noncompaction-like cardiac phenotype (PMID: 29337306).
  - The hypomorph (10-15% of wild-type ZIP8) shows neonatal lethality, stunted growth, multi-organ dysmorphogenesis and severe anemia (PMID: 30013175).
- Tissue-specific and inducible models:
  - Inducible global knockout is viable but has markedly reduced brain Mn, with other brain metals unaffected (PMID: 37482277).
  - Neuron-specific knockout is viable and shows cerebellar defects, abnormal Purkinje cell dendrites, increased apoptosis and motor dysfunction (PMID: 39435657).
  - Intestine-specific knockout impairs dietary Mn absorption and increases colitis susceptibility via ACER1 (PMID: 38839750).
  - Kidney-specific knockout and A391T knock-in mice are protected from kidney injury (PMID: 40956895).
- Zebrafish: mutants show vertebral abnormalities, impaired growth and decreased motor activity (PMID: 30301978).
- No heterozygous knockout phenotype, and no Mendelian ratio deviation for heterozygotes, was reported in the extracted data. Heterozygous animals appear unaffected or were not characterized.
- Consistency with human: the neuronal and cerebellar phenotypes, motor dysfunction and brain Mn depletion are consistent with the recessive human CDG (dystonia, cerebellar atrophy, Mn deficiency). They do not support dominant inheritance.
- Strength: MODERATE for the gene's role in neurodevelopment and Mn homeostasis under biallelic or severe loss of function. NONE for a heterozygous mechanism.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function:
  - ZIP8 is a Mn/Zn/Fe/Cd transporter. Loss of function causes Mn deficiency and failure of Mn-dependent glycosylation enzymes (PMID: 28749473).
  - High-dose MnSO4 in 2 patients normalized the glycosylation defects and improved motor, hearing and neurological function (PMID: 28749473).
- Brain relevance:
  - ZIP8 mediates blood-brain barrier Mn uptake (PMID: 37482277).
  - Plasma membrane abundance in brain microvascular endothelial cells is regulated by Ca2+ and SPCA1 (PMID: 35787370).
  - A391T knock-in mice show region-dependent altered brain N-glycosylation, with about one-third of cortical glycoproteins affected, including cell adhesion molecules and neurotransmitter receptors (PMID: 35260802).
- Spectrum of hypofunction: A391T carriers show a milder version of the glycosylation and Mn changes seen in CDG patients, suggesting a dose-response relationship (PMID: 32753748). This is a gradient of hypofunction across genotypes, but it has not been linked to a dominant Mendelian phenotype.
- Other roles: zinc-dependent effects in kidney disease (PMID: 40956895), IPF via ZIP8/SIRT1 (PMID: 35389887), Cd toxicity (PMID: 15722412), and osteoarthritis (PMID: 23688035).
- Strength: STRONG for the gene's biological role in Mn homeostasis and glycosylation, and its relevance to neurodevelopment. This applies to recessive loss of function, not to dominant disease.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level for SLC39A8 as a heterozygous (autosomal dominant) neurodevelopmental disorder gene: INSUFFICIENT.
- Key strengths:
  - The gene has a well-defined function and a clear recessive disorder (SLC39A8-CDG) with a treatable Mn mechanism.
  - Mouse models are concordant with the recessive neurological phenotype.
  - Common-variant data show a dose-dependent effect of reduced function.
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - No heterozygous pathogenic variant carriers with a neurodevelopmental disorder were reported, and de novo variants were not described.
  - No large neurodevelopmental cohorts (DDD, UDN and others) were represented.
  - The only clinical case series is small (n = 3) and recessive.
  - Heterozygous animal phenotypes are not reported.
  - The A391T associations (schizophrenia, scoliosis) are polygenic risk effects rather than Mendelian disease.
- Conclusion: SLC39A8 is a well-validated autosomal recessive CDG gene with neurodevelopmental and neurological features, supported by mouse and biochemical data. There is currently no evidence that heterozygous variants cause a monogenic neurodevelopmental disorder. The common A391T allele confers modest risk for complex traits. A dominant neurodevelopmental classification is not supported.

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. Clinical, molecular and glycophenotype insights in SLC39A8-CDG (PMID: 34246313)
2. A missense variant in SLC39A8 is associated with severe idiopathic scoliosis (PMID: 30301978)
3. The schizophrenia risk locus in SLC39A8 alters brain metal transport and plasma glycosylation (PMID: 32753748)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. Generation of a Slc39a8 hypomorph mouse: markedly decreased ZIP8 Zn2+/(HCO3-)2 transporter expression (PMID: 21658371)
2. Lin et al. 2018 - Zinc transporter Slc39a8 is essential for cardiac ventricular compaction (PMID: 29337306)
3. In utero gene expression in the Slc39a8(neo/neo) knockdown mouse (PMID: 30013175)
4. Metal-ion transporter SLC39A8 is required for brain manganese uptake and accumulation (PMID: 37482277)
5. Neuronal SLC39A8 deficiency impairs cerebellar development by altering manganese homeostasis (PMID: 39435657)
6. The manganese transporter SLC39A8 links alkaline ceramidase 1 to inflammatory bowel disease (PMID: 38839750)
7. SLC39A8-mediated zinc dyshomeostasis potentiates kidney disease (PMID: 40956895)

### Biological Evidence
1. SLC39A8 deficiency: biochemical correction and major clinical improvement by manganese therapy (PMID: 28749473)
2. Calcium and the Ca-ATPase SPCA1 modulate plasma membrane abundance of ZIP8 and ZIP14 to regulate Mn(II) uptake in brain microvascular endothelial cells (PMID: 35787370)
3. The schizophrenia-associated variant in SLC39A8 alters protein glycosylation in the mouse brain (PMID: 35260802)
4. The ZIP8/SIRT1 axis regulates alveolar progenitor cell renewal in aging and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (PMID: 35389887)
5. Identification of mouse SLC39A8 as the transporter responsible for cadmium-induced toxicity in the testis (PMID: 15722412)
6. MicroRNA-488 regulates zinc transporter SLC39A8/ZIP8 during pathogenesis of osteoarthritis (PMID: 23688035)
