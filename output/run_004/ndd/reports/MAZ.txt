MAZ LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: MAZ AND AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER

1. GENE SUMMARY
MAZ (MYC-associated zinc finger protein) encodes a 477-amino-acid Cys2His2 zinc finger transcription factor. It binds GA-box (GGGAGGG) elements and regulates transcriptional initiation and termination, for example at the c-MYC P2 promoter (PMID: 1502157). MAZ lies within the 16p11.2 recurrent CNV region. It also acts as a cofactor in CTCF-mediated insulation at Hox clusters (PMID: 35145304).

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: Four were found, and none is a conventional case report or case series with defined heterozygous variants in individuals with a neurodevelopmental disorder (NDD).
- Patient numbers: Most entries do not give a patient count. The only stated figure is 15 patients in the genitourinary CNV abstract (ASHG 2014 - The role of MAZ in the regulation of genitourinary development via modulation of WNT signaling). That abstract reports MAZ CNVs in about 6% of 258 patients with genitourinary abnormalities versus 0% of 57 controls. The ocular study mentions human MAZ CNVs and SNVs with abnormal eye development, without numbers or specific variants (PMID: 32571845).
- Phenotypes: These are genitourinary malformations (CAKUT, hypospadias, cryptorchidism, bladder exstrophy-epispadias) and ocular malformations. Intellectual disability appears only in the Iranian reanalysis cohort (PMID: 39748273; ASHG 2022 - The benefits and yields of whole exome sequencing data reanalysis in Iranian undiagnosed patients with intellectual disability). There MAZ is a novel candidate gene in a consanguineous family with a likely recessive, probably homozygous, variant. This is not evidence for a heterozygous (autosomal dominant) mechanism, and the variant is not specified.
- Variant type: Predominantly CNVs (16p11.2 deletions/duplications) and unspecified SNVs. No characterized heterozygous LoF or missense variants in NDD patients are described.
- Cohorts: None from UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL. The data come from an Iranian ID cohort and a Baylor GU cohort.
- Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED for any disease association, and effectively NONE for a heterozygous NDD. The 16p11.2 CNVs encompass many genes, so MAZ-specific effects cannot be isolated.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organism: Mouse only.
- Conditional Maz knockout causes eye defects, with abnormal peripheral eye and ciliary margin formation, reduced progenitor proliferation, and dysregulated Wnt signaling (increased Sfrp2, Wnt2b and Fzd4; reduced TCF-Lef1 reporter) (PMID: 32571845).
- CRISPR full knockout is perinatally lethal, with sub-Mendelian survival that depends on Maz copy number. Homozygous mutants show a high rate of CAKUT, and heterozygotes show haploinsufficiency for bladder development (PMID: 29432158). This indicates dosage sensitivity.
- Maz motif deletions at the Hox cluster derepress posterior Hox genes and cause homeotic transformations (PMID: 35145304).
- Consistency with human phenotype: Mouse eye and urinary tract phenotypes match the human CNV-associated ocular and GU phenotypes. No neurological or behavioral phenotypes are reported in mice.
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE for dosage-sensitive developmental roles (GU, eye). LIMITED for neurodevelopment specifically.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Function and pathways: MAZ is a transcriptional regulator that acts in several contexts:
  - WNT signaling modulation (ASHG 2014 - The role of MAZ in the regulation of genitourinary development via modulation of WNT signaling).
  - Erythropoiesis and fetal hemoglobin repression through a MAZ-MYB axis (PMID: 34351390; PMID: 41331317).
  - p53 regulation, where Akt phosphorylation at Thr385 releases MAZ from the p53 promoter (PMID: 26902421).
  - KRAS and AKT-NF-κB signaling in lung adenocarcinoma (PMID: 39424990).
  - FTH1 and ferroptosis in glioma (PMID: 36164395).
  - Competition with Sp1 at the PNMT promoter (PMID: 10085109).
  - CTCF-mediated insulation at Hox clusters (PMID: 35145304).
- Expression: MAZ is broadly expressed. Maz is ubiquitous in mouse, including kidney, ureter, testis, bladder and genital tubercle. The original cloning found mRNA in all human tissues tested except kidney (PMID: 1502157). Brain-specific expression is not described.
- Human genetic association: An exome collapsing analysis found a burden of dominant missense variants in MAZ in congenital obstructive uropathy (292 cases, 8,541 controls; rare P=3.58x10-5; joint P=4.6x10-7) (ASHG 2018 - An exome-wide association study identifies candidate susceptibility genes for congenital obstructive uropathy).
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for a role in developmental gene regulation. LIMITED for a neurodevelopmental role.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT for MAZ as a heterozygous (autosomal dominant) neurodevelopmental disorder gene. There is LIMITED evidence for a developmental disorder gene affecting GU and eye development.

Key strengths:
- MAZ is the only gene covered by all mapped 16p11.2 CNVs in GU patients.
- Dosage-sensitive mouse phenotypes, including haploinsufficiency for bladder development, are consistent with human GU and eye findings.
- There is a dominant missense burden signal in congenital obstructive uropathy.
- MAZ is a well-characterized transcription factor with wide regulatory roles.

Key gaps:
- No published patients with characterized heterozygous MAZ SNVs and NDD.
- The only ID link is a probable recessive candidate in one Iranian family without specified variants.
- The CNV data are confounded by other 16p11.2 genes.
- Evidence consists mostly of conference abstracts and lacks statistics for the CNV frequencies.
- No mouse neurobehavioral data.
- No UDN, DDD or similar cohort data.

Conclusion: Current evidence supports MAZ as a dosage-sensitive developmental regulator relevant to genitourinary and ocular malformations, not as an established autosomal dominant NDD gene. The only neurodevelopmental signal is a candidate recessive ID family (PMID: 39748273). Establishing a heterozygous NDD association would require de novo or heterozygous LoF and missense variants in well-phenotyped cohorts, with functional validation.
