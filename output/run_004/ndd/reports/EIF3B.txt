EIF3B LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) LoF VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
EIF3B encodes eIF3b, a peripheral subunit of the eukaryotic translation initiation factor 3 (eIF3) complex. It takes part in 43S/48S pre-initiation complex assembly, mRNA scanning, start-codon recognition and the timing of ribosomal subunit joining. A cryo-EM study of the 48S complex showed that eIF3b relocates to the 40S intersubunit face after mRNA attachment (PMID: 27373335).

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: Only one primary clinical report was found, describing a matchmaking-based cohort (PMID: 41033306). A second citation (PMID: 41679311) carries the same title and appears to be a duplicate record of that report, with no additional data. Two single-patient 7p22.3 microdeletion reports (PMID: 21671376; PMID: 39152504) are indirect and non-specific for EIF3B. So there is effectively one genuine EIF3B case series plus two contiguous-deletion case reports.
- Patient numbers: The main report describes 14 individuals with EIF3B variants, along with 4 with EIF3A variants (PMID: 41033306). The two deletion reports add 1 patient each. In the first, EIF3B is not clearly deleted, and at most regulatory elements may be involved (PMID: 21671376). In the second, EIF3B is one of several candidate genes, and the authors describe the evidence as controversial (PMID: 39152504).
- Common phenotypes: Congenital cardiac defects, craniofacial dysmorphisms, mild developmental delay and behavioral abnormalities (PMID: 41033306). The deletion patients had tetralogy of Fallot or other congenital heart defects. One also had epilepsy, moderate developmental delay, hypotonia and dyspraxia (PMID: 39152504).
- Variant type: Predominantly loss-of-function (de novo or LoF). Individual variants are not given in the available abstract. Some may be microdeletions. The cohort's inheritance and variant details could not be verified. The gene is described as highly constrained against LoF.
- Known cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): None are identified. The report is an international matchmaking collaboration with centers unspecified.
- Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE. A 14-person LoF series with a consistent phenotype is notable, but the evidence rests on a single study whose variant-level data are unavailable here. Neurodevelopmental delay is described as mild, and cardiac and craniofacial features predominate.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: Zebrafish only (PMID: 41033306).
- Key phenotypes: eif3ba mutants (and eif3s10 [EIF3A] mutants) show developmental abnormalities, thin heart tubes, absent craniofacial cartilage and embryonic lethality. Mendelian ratios, sample sizes and neurological phenotypes were not reported. No mouse data are available.
- Consistency with human phenotype: The cardiac and craniofacial findings match the human features. Neurodevelopmental relevance cannot be assessed. Homozygous full knockouts are lethal, and heterozygous zebrafish phenotypes are not described, so modeling of the dominant, haploinsufficient state is incomplete.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED to MODERATE. The findings are concordant with the human phenotype, but they come from a single zebrafish study with sparse quantitative detail.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: eIF3b is a core translation-initiation component (PMID: 27373335). It also interacts with regulatory proteins and has been studied extensively in cancer and other contexts:
  - PUS1 stabilizes it against ubiquitin-mediated degradation (PMID: 39247811).
  - It stabilizes PTGS2 by counteracting MDM2 (PMID: 36050601).
  - It forms a complex with METTL3 (PMID: 40576144).
  - It is recruited by YTHDF2 to promote translation of m6A-modified ETV5 mRNA (PMID: 38247171).
  - It is a TIPARP target linked to mitochondrial dysfunction in stroke (PMID: 39547463).
- Expression: EIF3B is overexpressed in several cancers, such as pancreatic cancer (PMID: 33459066), and is differentially expressed in rheumatoid arthritis and osteoarthritis synovium (PMID: 40592131). No brain-specific or neurodevelopmental expression data were provided.
- Key experimental findings: Knockdown reduces viability and induces apoptosis in cancer cells (PMID: 33459066; PMID: 28360085). These findings support the gene's importance for cell growth and survival, but they are cancer-focused and not directly relevant to neurodevelopment.
- Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED to MODERATE. Its essential role in translation is well supported, but disease-specific mechanistic data are lacking, and the 7p22.3 cardiac region association is only suggestive.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: LIMITED to MODERATE (leaning LIMITED for a neurodevelopmental disorder specifically).
- Key strengths: A 14-individual LoF series with a recurrent cardiac, craniofacial and developmental phenotype (PMID: 41033306). Gene constraint against LoF. Concordant zebrafish mutant phenotypes. EIF3B lies within the 7p22.3 region associated with cardiac malformations. The gene has a well-defined role in translation initiation (PMID: 27373335).
- Key gaps: Only one primary clinical study, with variant-level data, inheritance details and segregation unavailable. The neurodevelopmental phenotype is mild and secondary to cardiac and craniofacial features. The microdeletion reports cannot attribute the phenotype to EIF3B (PMID: 21671376; PMID: 39152504). There are no mouse or neuron-specific functional data and no known large cohort replication. Most biological literature is cancer-related.
- Conclusion: Heterozygous LoF variants in EIF3B are plausibly associated with a syndromic disorder with cardiac, craniofacial and mild neurodevelopmental features, based mainly on one recent matchmaking series with supportive zebrafish data (PMID: 41033306). Independent replication, detailed variant data and neurodevelopmental functional studies are needed before the gene can be considered an established neurodevelopmental disorder gene.
