SLC18A3 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: heterozygous (autosomal dominant) neurodevelopmental disorder association

1. GENE SUMMARY
SLC18A3 encodes the vesicular acetylcholine transporter (VAChT), which loads acetylcholine into synaptic vesicles in cholinergic neurons. The gene sits within the first intron of CHAT (choline acetyltransferase). The established human disease association is autosomal recessive presynaptic congenital myasthenic syndrome, not a heterozygous neurodevelopmental disorder.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: Two sources were found, and neither provides gene-specific evidence for heterozygous pathogenic SLC18A3 variants.
  - (a) A cohort of recurrent 10q11.21q11.23 deletions (PMID: 21948486). It included 24 unrelated individuals with deletions of 0.3-12 Mb, of whom 20 were clinically evaluated. A separate group of 17 individuals had reciprocal duplications.
  - (b) A large neurodevelopmental disorder (NDD) epilepsy cohort (PMID: 37645600). SLC18A3 is named only in the full text, as one of 13 "NDD-genes" without definite seizure-related reports. No patient count, zygosity or variant details are given.
- Total patients: About 24 with deletions, plus 17 with duplications, all from a single CNV study. SLC18A3 cannot be separated from CHAT and other genes in these deletions. The exome cohort gives no per-gene numbers.
- Common phenotypes in the deletion cohort: developmental delay, intellectual disability, hypotonia, sleep apnea, chronic constipation, gastroesophageal reflux, vesicoureteral reflux, epilepsy, ataxia, dysphagia, nystagmus and ptosis. Nine individuals also carried additional copy number changes. Deletions showed variable expressivity and incomplete penetrance, including apparently normal carrier parents.
- Variant type: Large multigene deletions (mostly NAHR-mediated) that include CHAT and SLC18A3. No heterozygous SLC18A3 point variants (LoF or missense) are described in the clinical evidence.
- Known cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): None identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED, bordering on NONE for SLC18A3-specific heterozygous disease. The CNV data are confounded by multiple genes, incomplete penetrance and additional CNVs.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- No model organism data were provided. Knockout phenotypes, lethality and Mendelian ratio deviations are therefore not available from this evidence set. Consistency with human phenotype cannot be assessed.
- Strength of model organism evidence: NONE (within the evidence provided).

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Function and pathway: VAChT packages acetylcholine into vesicles, and acts together with CHAT and the high-affinity choline transporter SLC5A7 in the cholinergic synapse pathway. Several studies show co-regulation of these genes. SLC18A3, CHAT, SLC5A7 and ACHE were downregulated in arsenic-exposed human iPSC-derived motor neurons (PMID: 37624150). Slc18a3, Chat and Slc5a7 were downregulated with reduced acetylcholine and cognitive dysfunction in microplastic-exposed mice (PMID: 38484610).
- Expression: SLC18A3 marks cholinergic neuronal populations in human sympathetic ganglia (PMID: 40667173), is enriched in kisspeptin neurons of the rat arcuate nucleus (PMID: 35063020), and is also found in non-neuronal rat testis cells (PMID: 21482687). Brain and neurodevelopmental expression is only indirectly supported.
- Key functional finding: The most relevant study is a recessive one. Homozygous p.Gly360Arg was found in two brothers with severe hypotonia, arthrogryposis and respiratory failure (presynaptic congenital myasthenic syndrome) (PMID: 28188302). The mutant VAChT had normal mRNA but undetectable protein in PC12 cells, indicating loss of function. The carrier frequency is 1:30 in Yemeni Jews. Heterozygous carriers are not reported as affected.
- Other findings are of low relevance to NDD. These include roles in renal cancer through ACh/cAMP signaling (PMID: 36225635), in bronchioalveolar carcinoma (PMID: 23222296), an expression difference in obstructive sleep apnea blood cells (PMID: 24732660), and no common-variant association with heart rate variability in 6,740 individuals (PMID: 25384021).
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for a role in cholinergic neurotransmission and for recessive loss of function. LIMITED for a heterozygous NDD mechanism.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level for SLC18A3 as a heterozygous NDD gene: INSUFFICIENT (LIMITED at best).
- Key strengths:
  - SLC18A3 has a clear, well-defined biological role in cholinergic neurotransmission.
  - Recessive loss of function is supported by a functional study (PMID: 28188302).
  - Recurrent 10q11.21q11.23 deletions that include CHAT and SLC18A3 are associated with developmental delay, intellectual disability, hypotonia and other features (PMID: 21948486).
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - There are no reported heterozygous SLC18A3 SNVs or indels in NDD patients.
  - The deletion phenotype cannot be attributed to SLC18A3 because the deletions are multigene and show incomplete penetrance.
  - The NDD cohort mention (PMID: 37645600) lacks any variant, zygosity or patient detail.
  - No model organism data were provided.
  - Heterozygous carriers of the recessive allele appear unaffected.
- Conclusion: The evidence does not support SLC18A3 as an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene. The only gene-level Mendelian association is the autosomal recessive congenital myasthenic syndrome. Haploinsufficiency of the 10q11.21q11.23 region may contribute to a neurodevelopmental phenotype with incomplete penetrance, but this cannot be attributed to SLC18A3 alone. Heterozygous de novo or inherited variants in well-phenotyped cohorts, with functional validation, would be needed to reassess.
