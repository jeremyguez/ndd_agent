ATRN LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: ATRN AS A CANDIDATE AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

1. GENE SUMMARY
ATRN encodes attractin, a large transmembrane protein (with a secreted isoform) that acts in the agouti/melanocortin pigmentation pathway and in secretory vesicle and proteoglycan deposition pathways. Its loss in rodents causes spongiform neurodegeneration, hypomyelination and dark coat color. Human attractin lacks intrinsic dipeptidyl peptidase 4 activity, so it likely acts as a receptor or adhesion protein (PMID: 17261078).

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Number of reports: 1. It is an array-CGH cohort study of children with developmental delay/intellectual disability (PMID: 36249514).
- Patients: 1 (case 30, F-4).
- Phenotype: developmental delay/intellectual disability, which was the cohort inclusion criterion. No individual features were given.
- Variant type: a de novo multi-gene CNV, with copy-number type not stated. It spans ATRN plus PTPRA, GNRH2, MRPS26, OXT, AVP, LZTS3, ITPA, SLC4A11, ADAM33, SIGLEC1 and HSPA12B. It was classified as a VUS, and ATRN was not singled out as causal. There are no LoF or missense single-gene variants.
- Cohort: not UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL. The center is not stated (possibly Turkey).
- A separate psychiatric study reports 14 ATRN mutation carriers with abnormal testosterone, among 9,008 psychiatric patients and 247 controls (PMID: 34869343). This is not a neurodevelopmental disorder cohort, and the variants and inheritance are not described.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE to very LIMITED. Strictly, there is no gene-specific evidence for a heterozygous neurodevelopmental disorder.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse (spontaneous mg, mg-3J and mg-L alleles, and full knockout), rat (zitter, myelin vacuolation and G505C knock-in) and zebrafish (full knockout, morpholino and maternal mutant).
- Mouse: Atrn-deficient mice are viable. They show age-dependent spongiform neurodegeneration with mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress (PMID: 17720281). Brain vacuolation occurs without prion-type pathology (PMID: 23787699). Other findings are hypomyelination, tremor and reduced adiposity (PMID: 18064672). Atrn mutants also lose extracellular proteoglycan in kidney and liver (PMID: 32580758). Combined Aspa and Atrn loss causes absence-like and tonic seizures (PMID: 17460362). Mutants suppress agouti pigmentation effects (PMID: 11041357).
- Rat: the zitter rat (splice donor deletion) shows neurological impairment, neurodegeneration, myelination abnormalities and darkened coat (PMID: 36058846). The mv rat carries an Atrn exon 1 deletion with tremor and CNS myelin vacuolation (PMID: 12379762). G505C knock-in rats show impaired spatial learning and memory and reduced activity (PMID: 34000322; PMID: 36621889).
- Zebrafish: atrn-/- fish are viable, with increased aggression and elevated testosterone (PMID: 34869343). Maternal Atrn regulates epiboly and actin demethylation (PMID: 28924386).
- Consistency with humans: the models show neurodegeneration, myelin defects, cognitive impairment and behavioral change, which are broadly relevant to neurological disease. However, the phenotypes are recessive or homozygous loss-of-function and are neurodegenerative or myelin-related rather than classic neurodevelopmental. Heterozygous phenotypes are not reported in the data provided. Mendelian ratios and sample sizes were not provided.
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE for a neurological role of ATRN, and LIMITED for a dominant neurodevelopmental disorder mechanism.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: attractin participates in agouti/MC1R signaling together with mahogunin, including a cAMP-independent branch (PMID: 19493315). It also contributes to secretory vesicle and proteoglycan deposition (PMID: 32580758).
- Expression: most mouse dopaminergic neurons express Atrn, and expression declines with age (PMID: 16860906). Brain expression is relevant to myelination and neuronal survival.
- Key experiments: Atrn knockdown worsens, and overexpression protects against, neuronal death induced by MPP+ and lactacystin, with effects on cytochrome c and AIF release (PMID: 16860906). The paralog Atrnl1 partially rescues Atrn-null phenotypes when overexpressed (PMID: 18064672). Allelic severity tracks Atrn mRNA levels, which is consistent with a dosage-sensitive loss-of-function mechanism (PMID: 11514456). Knock-in rats show reduced MBP and BDNF (PMID: 36621889; PMID: 34000322).
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE, mainly for neuroprotection, myelination and mitochondrial function. It does not directly support a neurodevelopmental mechanism.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT (clinical evidence none; supportive non-human evidence moderate).
- Key strengths: consistent rodent and zebrafish loss-of-function phenotypes affecting the nervous system (neurodegeneration, hypomyelination, cognitive and behavioral changes). There is a plausible biological role in myelination and mitochondrial protection, and a dosage-sensitive effect of mRNA levels.
- Key gaps: there are no reported human individuals with single-gene heterozygous ATRN variants and a neurodevelopmental phenotype. The only case is a de novo multi-gene CNV VUS in which ATRN is not implicated (PMID: 36249514). The rodent phenotypes are recessive and neurodegenerative, and heterozygous animals are not characterized. There is no segregation, functional validation of human variants, or Mendelian ratio data. The psychiatric carrier data lack variant detail (PMID: 34869343).
- Conclusion: ATRN has solid animal and cellular evidence of a neurological role, but no clinical evidence supports it as a heterozygous autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene. Its status should remain unestablished until additional patients with well-characterized de novo or loss-of-function ATRN variants are reported. A recessive or other inheritance model may be more consistent with the existing animal data.
