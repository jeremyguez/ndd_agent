PPP6R3 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER (HETEROZYGOUS/AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
PPP6R3 (also called SAPS3) encodes a regulatory subunit of protein phosphatase 6 (PP6), a serine/threonine phosphatase holoenzyme with the catalytic subunit PPP6C. It helps direct PP6 substrate specificity and acts in pathways including RIPK1-dependent cell death signaling, AMPK regulation, and translational control. Its function is ubiquitous, and it has not been established as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: 1 (Zhang et al. 2015, PMID: 26666178), a trio exome study of Chinese schizophrenia patients.
- Total patients: 1.
- Phenotype: schizophrenia only. No intellectual disability, developmental delay, autism, epilepsy, or congenital anomalies are reported.
- Variant type: a single de novo missense variant (chr11:68370926, G/C). The protein change was not given. It was predicted damaging in silico. The authors did not prioritize PPP6R3; their prioritized genes were LRP1, MACF1, DICER1, and ABCA2.
- Cohorts: none of the known cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL) are represented.
- Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED. This is a single de novo missense variant in an adult-onset psychiatric phenotype, with no recurrence, no functional validation, and no segregation or constraint data. It is close to NONE for a neurodevelopmental disorder.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse (germline conditional knockout, full knockout, liver-specific knockout) and zebrafish (full knockout and microexon deletion).
- Mouse:
  - Germline-specific Ppp6r3 deletion caused abnormal spermatogonial differentiation and male infertility through dysregulated EIF3C/EIF4G1 phosphorylation and translation (PMID: 40721635).
  - Whole-body and liver-specific Saps3 knockouts were viable. They were protected from high-fat-diet metabolic dysfunction through AMPK activation, with no effect on a normal diet (PMID: 36914647).
  - Ppp6r3 deletion decreased bone mineral density, as noted in a review (PMID: 40806370).
- Zebrafish:
  - ppp6r3 full knockouts were viable but developed only as males, had a spermatogenesis block, and were infertile (PMID: 40962474).
  - A ppp6r3 microexon deletion caused only mild larval neural phenotypes (brain activity, morphology, and behavior), and most of the 45 microexon mutants had minimal or no phenotypes (PMID: 41252186).
- Consistency with human phenotype: poor. The dominant phenotypes are reproductive, metabolic, and skeletal. Knockouts are viable, with no reported lethality, Mendelian ratio deviation, or overt neurodevelopmental phenotype. The mild neural phenotype in the microexon mutant is the only neurological signal, and it is of uncertain relevance.
- Strength of model organism evidence for a neurodevelopmental disorder: LIMITED.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function:
  - PP6 regulates RIPK1 phosphorylation, promoting S166 autophosphorylation and reducing S321 phosphorylation. PPP6R3 acts redundantly with PPP6R1 and PPP6R2 in TAK1 inhibitor-induced PANoptosis (PMID: 38807188).
  - PPP6R3 co-purifies with the influenza polymerase together with PPP6C and PPP6R1 (PMID: 25187537).
  - It regulates AMPK (PMID: 36914647) and translation in spermatogonia (PMID: 40721635).
- Expression: described as ubiquitously expressed, including in bone and muscle (ASHG 2012 - The PPP6R3/LRP5 locus influences lean mass in children of different ethnic background and highlights pleiotropic effects on muscle-bone interactions). No brain-specific or developmental expression data were provided.
- Human genetic associations are with musculoskeletal traits, not neurodevelopment:
  - The 11q13 locus near LRP5/PPP6R3 is associated with hip geometry (rs7102273, GWS p≤2.6×10-8) with an eQTL linking the SNP to PPP6R3 expression (PMID: 30888730).
  - The locus is associated with lean mass and BMD in children (10,414 children; PMID: 28743860).
  - TWAS/colocalization (PMID: 36416764) and SMR (PMID: 42260878) support PPP6R3 as a causal gene for BMD and osteoporosis.
  - LD at the locus makes it hard to attribute the signal specifically to PPP6R3.
- Somatic oncology findings (PPP6R3-TENM4-NRG1 in breast cancer, PMID: 33413557; PPP6R3-USP6 in nodular fasciitis, PMID: 27113271) are not relevant to germline neurodevelopmental disease.
- Strength of biological evidence for a neurodevelopmental disorder: LIMITED. The gene has well-characterized phosphatase biology, but there is no direct neurodevelopmental link.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT.
- Key strengths: PPP6R3 has a well-defined molecular role as a PP6 regulatory subunit. Multiple knockout models are viable and show consistent reproductive, metabolic, and bone phenotypes, and one de novo missense variant has been reported in a psychiatric cohort.
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - There is only one patient with a de novo missense variant of unspecified protein change, and the authors did not prioritize it.
  - There are no LoF variants, no recurrent variants, and no case series.
  - The phenotype is schizophrenia, not a neurodevelopmental disorder.
  - There is no functional validation of the variant.
  - Animal models show no robust neurodevelopmental phenotype.
  - The human genetic associations concern bone and muscle, and functional redundancy with PPP6R1 and PPP6R2 may buffer loss of the gene.
- Conclusion: The evidence does not support PPP6R3 as a heterozygous neurodevelopmental disorder gene. A single unprioritized de novo missense variant in a schizophrenia patient is insufficient, and model organisms point to reproductive, metabolic, and skeletal roles rather than neurodevelopment. Additional patients with de novo or LoF variants and consistent neurodevelopmental phenotypes, together with functional studies, would be needed to reassess.
