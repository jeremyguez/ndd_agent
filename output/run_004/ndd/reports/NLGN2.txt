NLGN2 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: NEUROLIGIN 2 AND AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER

1. GENE SUMMARY
NLGN2 encodes neuroligin 2, a postsynaptic cell adhesion molecule. It is enriched at inhibitory (GABAergic) synapses, where it binds presynaptic neurexins and scaffolds gephyrin and GABA-A receptors to support inhibitory synapse formation and function.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: Four were found, but only two are clearly informative. These are one de novo nonsense variant (PMID: 27865048) and one de novo missense variant reported in an ASHG 2019 abstract (ASHG 2019 - PgmNr 1344: Catatonia is a clinical feature of the natural progression of NLGN2-related autism spectrum disorder). The other two are weak. One is a title-only report of febrile and atypical absence seizures attributed to an NLGN2 variant, with no variant details or abstract available (PMID: 37506563). The other is an ASHG 2017 abstract in which NLGN2 carried an additional de novo variant in a TAOK1 patient, with unclear contribution (ASHG 2017 - Novel de novo TAOK1 variants associated with a neurodevelopmental phenotype, macrocephaly, and joint hypermobility).
- Total patients: About 4, with 1 patient per report. Only about 2 have a reasonably attributable NLGN2 phenotype.
- Common phenotypes: Autism, anxiety, hyperphagia, and developmental delay or intellectual disability recur. The nonsense case also had severe anxiety, obsessive-compulsive behaviors, obesity, macrocephaly, and dysmorphic features (PMID: 27865048). The missense case had language impairment, developmental regression, and catatonia that responded to benzodiazepines (ASHG 2019 - PgmNr 1344). Seizures were reported in the title-only report (PMID: 37506563).
- Variant types: One de novo heterozygous LoF variant, c.441C>A p.(Tyr147Ter) (PMID: 27865048). One de novo missense variant with the change unspecified. Two variants of unspecified type. Heterozygous de novo status is documented for the first two.
- Cohorts: No patients are identified as coming from UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL. Centers named are Cincinnati Children's Hospital Medical Center for the catatonia report and GeneDx for the TAOK1 report.
- Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED. There are very few cases, no cohort-level data, no recurrent variants, and no segregation or functional validation of the human variants. Two of the four reports are largely uninformative.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: Mouse only.
- Key phenotypes: Nlgn2 knockout mice are viable, with no lethality and no reported Mendelian ratio deviation.
  - Anxiety: Full knockouts show a robust anxiety phenotype with impaired inhibitory transmission in the basal amygdala (PMID: 26142252). Altered theta and beta synchrony in the vHPC-mPFC-BLA circuit was also reported (PMID: 33960026). Anxiety-related behavior was reported in Nlgn2 knockouts with IgSF9b rescue (PMID: 30573727). Adult mPFC conditional knockout caused impaired anxiety behavior, fear memory, and social interaction (PMID: 25824299).
  - Development and motor: Knockouts show developmental delays, reduced pup ultrasonic vocalizations, reduced exploration, and impaired rotarod performance, but normal sociability and no stereotypies (PMID: 22820233).
  - Sleep and EEG: Knockouts show altered sleep architecture and hypersynchronized EEG events (PMID: 30231918; PMID: 38570872).
  - R215H knock-in: Homozygotes lack NLGN2 protein and show growth retardation, anxiety, increased prepulse inhibition, and impaired spatial learning and memory (PMID: 29230184).
  - Other: Immature retinal vasculature (PMID: 29709479).
- Heterozygous data: Heterozygous phenotypes are largely uncharacterized. Heterozygotes were included in one study (PMID: 22820233) without detailed reporting here, so haploinsufficiency modeling is incomplete.
- Consistency with human phenotype: Anxiety, developmental delay, cognitive impairment, and possibly seizure-like or hypersynchronous EEG activity are consistent with the patients. Normal sociability in knockouts only partially matches the autism seen in patients. Hyperphagia, obesity, and macrocephaly are not modeled.
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE. Many independent studies show consistent neurobehavioral phenotypes, but mostly in homozygous knockouts rather than heterozygotes, and the phenotypes are narrow.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: NLGN2 organizes GABAergic postsynaptic specializations through neurexin and gephyrin. It is regulated by MDGA1 (PMID: 39897557; PMID: 39284869) and by PSD-95/GSK3β signaling (PMID: 32818520). It also interacts with the serotonin transporter in the midbrain (PMID: 26793096).
- Expression: Nlgn2 is present at most inhibitory synapses in the basal amygdala (PMID: 26142252). Expression is reduced in the nucleus accumbens of patients with major depressive disorder (PMID: 29339486). Peripheral blood NLGN2 expression predicted escitalopram response (PMID: 27461515).
- Key findings: The schizophrenia-associated R215H variant is a loss-of-function allele. It is retained in the ER and fails to support GABAergic synaptogenesis, and its penetrance was incomplete in the family (PMID: 21551456). In knock-in mice it reduces GABAergic transmission and produces schizophrenia-like behaviors (PMID: 29859117). Cryo-EM structures of human NLGN2 and NLGN3 have been solved (PMID: 36479216).
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE. The function is well established and is relevant to the E/I balance mechanisms implicated in neurodevelopmental disorders. However, the evidence points more to psychiatric phenotypes (anxiety, depression, schizophrenia) than to neurodevelopmental disorder specifically, and it does not directly validate the human neurodevelopmental variants.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: LIMITED.
- Key strengths:
  - A de novo heterozygous nonsense variant in a patient with a complex neurodevelopmental and neuropsychiatric phenotype (PMID: 27865048).
  - A second de novo missense case with overlapping features (ASHG 2019 - PgmNr 1344).
  - Consistent mouse knockout phenotypes of anxiety and altered inhibitory synapse function.
  - Well-characterized synaptic biology.
- Key gaps:
  - Only about 2 informative patients, with no cohort-level or statistical evidence.
  - Incomplete variant details, since one case is abstract-only and one is title-only.
  - No functional testing of the patient variants.
  - Heterozygous mouse models are poorly characterized.
  - The variant's mechanism and its phenotype (including hyperphagia and obesity) are not recapitulated in the models.
  - The related psychiatric literature, including R215H with incomplete penetrance, suggests variable expressivity or a risk-allele model rather than a fully penetrant dominant disorder.
- Conclusion: NLGN2 is a biologically plausible candidate for a heterozygous neurodevelopmental disorder with autism, anxiety, hyperphagia, and intellectual disability, supported by two de novo cases and consistent mouse and synaptic data. The clinical evidence is currently too sparse for a definitive gene-disease assertion. More patients with well-characterized LoF or missense variants, and functional validation, are needed to establish the association.
