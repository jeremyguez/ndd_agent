RARA LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: RARA AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
RARA encodes retinoic acid receptor alpha, a ligand-activated nuclear receptor. It heterodimerizes with RXR proteins and regulates transcription of retinoic acid (RA)-responsive genes involved in differentiation, cell cycle control, and reproduction. It is best known in disease as the partner in the PML-RARA fusion of acute promyelocytic leukemia (APL), a somatic rearrangement and not a germline neurodevelopmental mechanism.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent case reports/case series: 0 were found.
- Total patients: 0.
- Phenotypes: none reported, so no neurodevelopmental phenotype can be described.
- Variant type: not applicable, because no germline heterozygous variants were identified.
- Known cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse only. The studies are conditional knockouts (germ cell-, Sertoli cell-, and dendritic cell-specific) plus a review of global Rara knockout.
- Key phenotypes:
  - Germ cell-specific Rara deletion reduced epididymal sperm counts and disorganized the germ cell layers of the seminiferous tubules, worsening with age. It also impaired synaptonemal complex integrity and meiotic progression, and reduced spermatogonial proliferation after busulfan treatment (PMID: 30099545).
  - Sertoli cell RARA mediates RA effects on spermatogonial differentiation, and Sertoli cell-derived RA is required for spermiation (PMID: 23012458).
  - A review notes that global Rara knockout mice are viable but male infertile. It also reports that RARA-selective and pan-RAR antagonists cause reversible spermatogenic disruption (PMID: 32671394).
  - Dendritic cell-specific Rara knockout reduced Th2 and IgE/IgG1 responses in food allergen sensitization (PMID: 34365653) and in allergic airway inflammation (PMID: 32622265).
- Lethality: the models are viable. Mendelian ratios were not reported.
- Consistency with human phenotype: no neurological or neurodevelopmental phenotypes were reported in any of these models, and there is no human phenotype to compare against. The findings concern male reproduction and immune function.
- Strength of model organism evidence for a neurodevelopmental disorder: LIMITED. The mouse work is informative about RARA function but not about neurodevelopment.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: RARA is a transcription factor in the RA signaling pathway. In APL, the PML-RARA fusion represses RAR targets by recruiting N-CoR, SMRT, and HDACs, and RA and arsenic relieve this repression and promote degradation of the fusion (PMID: 24433507, PMID: 39017850). RXRA acts as a coreceptor that promotes PML/RARA DNA binding (PMID: 25258343).
- Regulation of RARA: miR-27a targets RARA and RXRA in rhabdomyosarcoma cells, and its overexpression increased proliferation and reduced the fraction of cells in G1 (PMID: 25915942). The m6A demethylase FTO regulates RARA mRNA and inhibits ATRA-induced differentiation in AML cells (PMID: 28017614). RARA regulates surface CD38 in myeloma cells (PMID: 40453054). ALDH1A1-driven RA synthesis supports RARA function in resistance to KRAS inhibitors (PMID: 39317105).
- Expression: the provided data cover hematopoietic expression (PMID: 23056333) and no brain or neurodevelopmental expression data.
- Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED for a neurodevelopmental role. The evidence is almost entirely oncologic (APL, AML, other cancers) and does not address neuronal function.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT.
- Key strengths: RARA's function in RA signaling is well characterized. Mouse conditional knockouts consistently show a requirement in spermatogenesis and immune responses.
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - There are no clinical reports of germline heterozygous RARA variants and no patient cohorts.
  - No neurological phenotypes were reported in the model organisms.
  - The biological data come from somatic leukemia and cancer contexts, not from neurodevelopment.
  - No variant-level functional data (e.g., missense or LoF effects) were provided.
- Conclusion: The available evidence does not support RARA as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous pathogenic variants. Its known roles are in RA-dependent transcription, spermatogenesis, immune modulation, and somatic leukemogenesis via PML-RARA. A gene-disease association would need independent patients with de novo or heterozygous RARA variants and supporting functional or neurodevelopmental model data.
