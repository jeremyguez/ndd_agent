SERPINA9 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: Evidence for a Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene (Heterozygous/Autosomal Dominant)

1. GENE SUMMARY
SERPINA9 (centerin, GCET1) encodes a serine protease inhibitor (serpin) expressed predominantly in germinal centre B cells. Recombinant centerin undergoes the stressed-to-relaxed conformational change needed for inhibitory activity. It rapidly inhibits trypsin (ka = 1.9x10^5 M-1 s-1) and weakly inhibits thrombin and plasmin (PMID: 17447896).

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent case reports/series: 0 found.
- Total patients: 0.
- Phenotypes: none reported. No neurodevelopmental phenotype has been linked to SERPINA9.
- Variant type: no germline variants reported in patients. The only human variant data are somatic SERPINA9 mutations found by exome sequencing in extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma (10 paired CD30+/CD30- samples), which is unrelated to neurodevelopment and not germline (PMID: 39187207).
- Cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms studied: no knockout or loss-of-function models were reported. Two indirect observations exist: Serpina9 was up-regulated in the hippocampus of 12-month-old APPsw-transgenic Alzheimer's disease model mice, confirmed by RT-PCR (116 DEGs in total) (PMID: 16933151). A canine GWAS in German shepherds identified a genome-wide significant IgA-level locus (p<0.0002) containing SERPINA9 and KIRREL3 as candidates (PMID: 26225558).
- Knockout phenotypes (neurological, lethality, Mendelian ratio deviations): none available.
- Consistency with human phenotype: cannot be assessed. The mouse finding concerns neurodegeneration-associated expression changes, not developmental effects, and the canine finding concerns immune phenotypes.
- Strength of model organism evidence: NONE for neurodevelopmental relevance (the data are only tangential).

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Function and pathways: SERPINA9 is a trypsin-like protease inhibitor that also binds DNA and heparin, although these interactions did not alter the inhibition rate (PMID: 17447896). Its expression is restricted to B cells and peaks as B cells proliferate into plasma cells (PMID: 26225558). One bioinformatic study listed it among seven NFAT-related prognostic genes in oral squamous cell carcinoma, but RT-qPCR validation did not list SERPINA9 among the significantly changed genes (PMID: 42471772).
- Expression pattern: centerin protein is found in germinal centre B cells of tonsil, lymph node and GI lymphoid tissue, and in Burkitt lymphoma Raji cells. It is strongly expressed in most follicular lymphomas, and 14 of 30 (47%) DLBCLs were positive, correlating most closely with CD10 (PMID: 18550480). No evidence of brain or neurodevelopmental expression was reported, apart from the up-regulation in the APPsw mouse hippocampus (PMID: 16933151).
- Key experimental findings are oncologic and immunologic. High SERPINA9 mRNA correlated with better overall survival in CD5+ DLBCL (24 CD5+ and 23 CD5- cases; p<0.05) (PMID: 36081566). SERPINA9 is part of a 17-gene signature associated with progression after axi-cel CAR T therapy (HR 6.12, 95% CI 3.57-10.50, for the signature as a whole) (PMID: 40083872). SERPINA9 expression responds to dexamethasone in lymphoma cells (PMID: 32048780).
- Strength of biological evidence for a neurodevelopmental role: LIMITED to NONE. The biology is well characterized for B-cell function but does not point to a neurodevelopmental mechanism.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT.
- Key strengths: the protein's biochemical function is well defined (PMID: 17447896), and its B-cell/germinal centre expression is well characterized (PMID: 18550480).
- Key gaps: there are no patients with germline variants, no segregation or de novo data, no cohort reports, no knockout models, and no evidence of expression in the developing brain or of a neuronal function. The reported associations are with lymphoma biomarkers, canine IgA levels, and an Alzheimer's mouse model. Most findings are correlative.
- Conclusion: there is no clinical, model organism or mechanistic support for SERPINA9 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene acting through heterozygous pathogenic variants. Its known biology lies in B-cell and germinal centre function. Without human genetic evidence, the gene cannot currently be considered a candidate for a dominant neurodevelopmental disorder.
