ANK3 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER

1. GENE SUMMARY
ANK3 encodes ankyrin-G, a scaffold protein that links membrane proteins (e.g., voltage-gated Na+ channels, cell adhesion molecules) to the β-spectrin/actin cytoskeleton. It organizes the axon initial segment (AIS) and nodes of Ranvier. The gene has several isoforms (480, 270 and 190 kDa, plus minor isoforms such as Ank3-1b), and common variants are associated with bipolar disorder and schizophrenia.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: Four clinical reports were captured. Two are case series of heterozygous LoF variants: PMID 38988293 (16 monoallelic and 11 biallelic individuals, 13 newly described) and PMID 34218362 (4 individuals). One is an ASD exome study (PMID: 22865819), and one is a balanced chromosomal abnormality report (PMID: 22521361). A further report (PMID: 40879451) describes biallelic disease and is relevant mainly to the recessive form.
- Patient numbers: About 24 heterozygous or monoallelic individuals are described, roughly 16 (PMID: 38988293) + 4 (PMID: 34218362) + 4 (PMID: 22865819), plus one patient with a chromosomal breakpoint (PMID: 22521361). Some overlap between series cannot be excluded. A further 11 biallelic individuals were also reported (PMID: 38988293), and 5 more in PMID: 40879451.
- Common phenotypes (monoallelic): language delay, autism spectrum disorder, intellectual disability, hypotonia, motor delay, ADD/ADHD, sleep disturbance, aggression/self-injury and epilepsy. Ataxia was absent in monoallelic individuals and occurred only with biallelic variants (PMID: 38988293).
- Variant types: Predominantly truncating LoF (frameshift/nonsense) in monoallelic cases, with a minority of missense variants. The ASD exome study reported missense variants, one confirmed de novo (p.Ser1569Ala) (PMID: 22865819). One balanced chromosomal rearrangement disrupted ANK3 (PMID: 22521361).
- Cohorts: No UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL cohort membership was identified in the extracted data. Center and country were largely not reported, apart from China for PMID: 22865819.
- Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE. Multiple unrelated patients with a consistent nonspecific neurodevelopmental phenotype and mostly truncating variants support the association. Limits include the small numbers, the nonspecific phenotype, incomplete inheritance and de novo data in the abstracts, and weak evidence for the missense variants.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: Mouse (most studies) and zebrafish.
- Key findings in mouse:
  - Heterozygous knockout of the large isoforms increased anxiety-like behavior and reduced the size of several brain regions (PMID: 28411148).
  - Heterozygous knockout also increased psychiatric-like behaviors that lithium ameliorated, with altered axonal transport and glutamate signaling proteomes (PMID: 28109561).
  - Forebrain heterozygous knockout showed increased sensitivity to subthreshold social defeat stress (PMID: 35928299).
  - Ank3-1b isoform-specific knockout caused absence epilepsy, hyperactivity, repetitive behaviors, increased slow gamma EEG power and reduced REM sleep (PMID: 38123552).
  - Loss of the giant 480-kDa isoform abolished AIS assembly and disrupted node of Ranvier formation. These mice were viable to weaning (PMID: 25552556).
  - Adult forebrain excitatory neuron conditional knockout caused hyperactivity, reduced neuronal activity, and Mbp downregulation, which lithium reversed (PMID: 42640795).
  - Oligodendrocyte-specific knockout destabilized paranodal junctions only in aged mice (PMID: 39738113).
  - Deletion of Ank3 from ependyma disrupted the SVZ niche and neurogenesis (PMID: 21745638).
- Zebrafish: ank3 knockdown produced coloboma and/or microphthalmia (PMID: 35034853).
- Lethality and Mendelian ratios: Models were reported as viable, and no Mendelian ratio data were extracted. Sample sizes and significance values were not provided.
- Consistency with humans: Heterozygous mice show behavioral and psychiatric-like phenotypes (anxiety, hyperactivity, epilepsy, repetitive behavior) that partly overlap with the human ADHD, ASD, epilepsy and behavioral features. Cognitive and developmental phenotypes are less well characterized. Loss of the cerebellar isoform fits the biallelic ataxia phenotype (PMID: 40879451).
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE. Many models and a consistent neurobehavioral picture support the gene's role, but they often use isoform-specific or conditional alleles, and heterozygous models mostly show psychiatric-like rather than overt neurodevelopmental phenotypes.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: Ankyrin-G anchors Na+ channels, β4-spectrin and cell adhesion molecules at the AIS and nodes, and it regulates axonal polarity, excitability, GABAergic inhibition and myelination. The BD-associated p.W1989R variant disrupts the ankyrin-G/GABARAP interaction, causing inhibitory deficits and pyramidal neuron hyperexcitability. Lithium acting through GSK-3β inhibition rescued these deficits (PMID: 40849089). A curated ankyrin-G interactome of 17 proteins showed an excess of de novo variants in 1009 trios (14 variants, p=0.007) (ASHG 2018 - Genetic variation in the AnkyrinG interactome causes a range of neurological disorders). A neuron-type-specific microexon (E35a) modulates interneuron excitability via an InsP3R complex (PMID: 41688438).
- Expression: Splice forms and transcription start sites differ between brain regions. The BD-associated isoform is higher in corpus callosum than frontal tissue (fold change 1.80, p<1e-4) and higher in adolescents than children (fold change 1.97, p=5e-3), consistent with a role in myelin maturation (PMID: 35091539). Novel intron-retaining isoforms are differentially expressed in BD (ASHG 2015 - RNA-sequencing identifies differentially expressed novel gene isoforms in bipolar disorder). Cerebellar expression is high (PMID: 40879451).
- Genetic association: A splice-site variant (rs41283526) in a minor isoform protects against BD (OR=0.31) and SCZ (OR=0.21) (PMID: 26682468). The risk SNP rs10994336 acts as a meQTL linked to executive function in BD (PMID: 34421516).
- Isoform-specific mechanisms are suggested by the different localization of mono- and biallelic variants (PMID: 38988293).
- Strength of biological evidence: STRONG for the role of ankyrin-G in neuronal function. It is more limited for the specific mechanism of heterozygous LoF in neurodevelopmental disorder (haploinsufficiency is plausible but not directly demonstrated).

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: MODERATE
- Strengths: Multiple unrelated individuals carry predominantly heterozygous truncating variants and share a consistent neurodevelopmental phenotype (PMID: 38988293, PMID: 34218362). Mouse heterozygous and conditional models show neurobehavioral phenotypes, and the protein has well-defined roles in AIS and neuronal excitability. Strong psychiatric genetic associations and a recessive ataxia phenotype add support.
- Gaps and limitations:
  - Cohort sizes are small and the phenotype is nonspecific.
  - Variant-level details, de novo status and segregation were not available in the abstracts.
  - Missense variants are of uncertain significance.
  - Isoform-specific consequences complicate predictions of LoF effect, since truncating variants affecting only some isoforms may behave differently.
  - No cohort provenance (UDN, DDD, etc.) was identified.
  - Model organism studies lack Mendelian ratio and sample-size data, and overt developmental phenotypes in heterozygous mice are mild.
- Conclusion: ANK3 has moderate evidence as a gene for a neurodevelopmental disorder with heterozygous, mostly truncating, variants. Patients show language delay, ASD, ID, ADHD, hypotonia and sometimes epilepsy. Mouse models and extensive biological data support a role for ankyrin-G dosage in neuronal function. Additional well-phenotyped cohorts with confirmed de novo variants and functional data on isoform-specific effects would be needed to upgrade the evidence to strong.
