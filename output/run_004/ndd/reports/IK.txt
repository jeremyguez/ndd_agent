IK GENE EVIDENCE REPORT: NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
IK (also known as RED, RER; the protein is a splicing-associated factor) was originally described as a 19 kDa cytokine that down-regulates HLA class II expression (PMID: 7970704). It maps to chromosome 2p15-p14 in that report. It is now recognized as a component of the spliceosome and a mitotic/cell-cycle factor, and has been linked to DNA repair signaling (PMID: 34065218). The evidence extracted for this report does not describe a role in neurodevelopment.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent case reports/case series: 0 found.
- Total patients: 0.
- Phenotypes: none reported.
- Variant type: no germline variants reported in any individual with a neurodevelopmental disorder. The only variant data are somatic IK-inactivating mutations (mostly frameshift and stop-gain) in endometrial cancer, from a TCGA-derived set of 547 patients (PMID: 34065218). These are unrelated to neurodevelopment.
- Cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organism: mouse only. Heterozygous Ik knockout lines were studied in the IMPC ocular phenotyping screen (PMID: 40323269).
- Key phenotypes: abnormal cornea morphology in late-adult mice (49+ weeks), combined significance p=6.89E-05. Ik was one of 14 genes linked to late-adult corneal phenotypes. Ik is also expressed in human corneal T/NK cells (66.67%) by single-cell transcriptomics. No data were extracted on lethality, Mendelian ratios, fertility, sample size, or neurological or behavioral phenotypes.
- Consistency with human phenotype: not assessable. A late-onset corneal phenotype has no evident link to a neurodevelopmental disorder, and no human IK disease phenotype has been established.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED for any phenotype, and NONE for neurodevelopmental relevance.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: IK was first purified as a cytokine secreted by K562 and other cancer cells that inhibits IFN-gamma-induced HLA class II expression in Colo 205 cells (PMID: 7970704). The cDNA encodes a protein with no homology to known cytokines, detects a 2.1 kb mRNA, and yields a secreted, active 19 kDa protein on transfection in COS cells (PMID: 7970704). Later work describes IK as a mitotic factor. It interacts with Ku80 (by Co-IP and mass spectrometry), and its knockdown causes G2/M enrichment, inactivates Ku80/Ku70 DNA repair signaling, and sensitizes endometrial cancer cells to cisplatin (PMID: 34065218).
- Expression: IK transcripts are present in CD34+ human cord blood progenitors and rise during growth factor-induced proliferation and differentiation (PMID: 9160666). The mRNA is detected in various cell types (PMID: 7970704). No brain or neurodevelopmental expression data were extracted.
- Key experiments: an antisense oligonucleotide against IK reduced IK transcripts, increased HLA-DR, and inhibited colony formation by CD34+ progenitors, with a 70% reduction in mean colony size (PMID: 9160666). In endometrial cancer, somatic IK-inactivating mutations were associated with reduced IK expression, higher mutation burden, better chemotherapy response, and longer survival (PMID: 34065218).
- Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED. The evidence supports roles in proliferation, cell cycle, and DNA repair, which are plausible but nonspecific. None of it directly addresses neurodevelopment.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT.

Key strengths:
- IK has defined cell-cycle and DNA-repair functions, which gives a plausible but indirect biological rationale for a role in development.
- A heterozygous mouse knockout shows a phenotype (late-onset corneal abnormality) in a systematic screen (PMID: 40323269).

Key gaps and limitations:
- There are no human case reports and no germline variants.
- There is no evidence of intolerance to heterozygous loss of function in humans.
- No neurological phenotyping data exist for the mouse model, and the only mouse phenotype is ocular.
- The cell biology data derive from hematopoietic and cancer systems, and none are from neural tissue.
- Somatic cancer mutation data do not inform germline neurodevelopmental disease.

Conclusion: There is currently no clinical, model organism, or biological evidence supporting IK as a heterozygous neurodevelopmental disorder gene. The available data concern immune regulation, cell cycle, DNA repair, and a late-onset corneal phenotype in mice. IK should be considered a candidate with insufficient evidence until germline variants are identified in individuals with neurodevelopmental phenotypes and supported by functional or neural expression studies.
