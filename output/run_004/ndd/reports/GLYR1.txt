GLYR1 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
GLYR1 (glyoxylate reductase 1 homolog, also called N-PAC or NDF) encodes a chromatin-associated protein that reads H3K36me3 and acts as a nucleosome-destabilizing factor. It partners with histone demethylase KDM1A/LSD1 and regulates histone acetylation and transcription. In the evidence reviewed here, it also acts as a cofactor for transcription factors such as GATA4.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: Two were found, both large-cohort exome studies rather than dedicated case reports or case series.
- Total patients: About 2, each with a single de novo variant.
  - One is a congenital heart disease (CHD) proband with a de novo missense variant, identified from about 9,000 proband-parent trios (PMID: 35182466). The variant disrupted the GATA4 interaction and impaired function in vitro and in mice.
  - The other is a proband with primary complex motor stereotypies (pCMS) and a de novo "damaging" variant of unspecified type (PMID: 37788244). GLYR1 appears only in a table of 52 genes and is not a high-confidence risk gene.
- Phenotypes: These are CHD and motor stereotypies. No patient has a described neurodevelopmental phenotype such as intellectual disability, developmental delay, autism or epilepsy. Motor stereotypies are the closest neurological or neurodevelopmental feature, and the evidence for them is weak.
- Variant type: One missense and one unspecified damaging variant. No clear loss-of-function pattern or recurrent variants. Exact variants were not given in the available text.
- Cohorts: No UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL cohort was identified. The data came from CHD and pCMS trio sequencing cohorts.
- A homozygous GLYR1 variant in a uniparental disomy region in a CHD patient is reported (PMID: 42539127). This is recessive-type evidence and does not support a heterozygous mechanism.
- Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED for a neurodevelopmental disorder, close to NONE. There is no phenotypic series and no statistical gene-level association with neurodevelopmental disease.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: Mouse and zebrafish.
- Mouse: A conditional knockout in spermatogenic cells caused male subfertility, reduced sperm count and motility, increased head and neck sperm malformations, fewer spermatocytes across meiotic stages, and reduced histone acetylation (PMID: 38395189). No neurological, lethality or Mendelian ratio data are reported. A CHD-associated missense variant impaired function in mice (PMID: 35182466), but details are not given.
- Zebrafish: Knockdown or functional studies recapitulated patient-specific cardiac phenotypes (PMID: 42539127). Specific phenotypes are not detailed.
- Consistency with human phenotype: The cardiac data are partly consistent with the CHD cases. The reproductive phenotype is unrelated to the neurodevelopmental question. No brain or behavioral phenotypes have been assessed.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED, and primarily relevant to cardiac and reproductive biology rather than neurodevelopment.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Molecular function: GLYR1 is an H3K36me3 reader and chromatin regulator that affects histone acetylation. It interacts with GATA4 and TBX5 in cardiac progenitors, where GLYR1 and GATA4 co-occupy and co-activate cardiac developmental genes (PMID: 35182466). It binds promoters and regulates transcription of PER3 (PMID: 38642856) and the lncRNA HSD11B1-AS1 (PMID: 41217560). It interacts with MLH1 and regulates p21 through p38MAPK and PI3K/AKT signaling (PMID: 32370786).
- Disease relevance outside neurodevelopment: GLYR1 has a frequent frameshift mutation in microsatellite-unstable colorectal cancer (51%), consistent with a tumor suppressor-like role (PMID: 21544814). Fusions such as NCOR1::GLYR1 in MDS/MPN (PMID: 38565504) and GLYR1-SLC9A8 in prostate cancer cells (PMID: 24012641) have been reported.
- Brain expression: Direct neurodevelopmental data are lacking. Glyr1 was differentially expressed in the prefrontal cortex of chronic stress mice (PMID: 41765126), which is a non-specific finding. Glyr1 is widely expressed in mouse testis (PMID: 38395189).
- Functional plausibility: Chromatin regulation is a pathway commonly implicated in neurodevelopmental disorders, which gives some theoretical plausibility. The experimental evidence is cardiac, oncologic and reproductive.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for general function in chromatin and transcription regulation, but LIMITED for neurodevelopmental relevance.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level for a heterozygous neurodevelopmental disorder: INSUFFICIENT.
- Strengths:
  - GLYR1 has a well-characterized chromatin regulatory function.
  - A de novo missense variant has functional support for disrupting GATA4 binding (PMID: 35182466).
  - Animal models show that Glyr1 matters for spermatogenesis and possibly cardiac development.
- Gaps and limitations:
  - There are only two patients, each with a single de novo variant, and neither has a well-defined neurodevelopmental phenotype.
  - Variant details are missing for the pCMS case, and the pCMS gene is not high-confidence.
  - There are no multi-patient case series, no LoF enrichment and no recurrent variants.
  - No neurological phenotyping has been done in animal models.
  - The only recessive-type report (UPD, homozygous) involves CHD.
- Conclusion: Current evidence does not support GLYR1 as an established autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene. The limited human data point toward a possible role in congenital heart disease via disrupted GATA4 interaction, and a possible weak link to motor stereotypies. Additional patients with detailed phenotypes and variants, together with neurodevelopmental functional studies, would be needed to evaluate any association.
