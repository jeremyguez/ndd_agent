SEPTIN2 EVIDENCE REPORT: NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
SEPTIN2 (SEPT2) encodes a GTP-binding cytoskeletal septin. It forms hetero-oligomeric septin filaments (septin2-6-7 complexes and octamers) that interact with actin, membranes and microtubules, and it contributes to cytokinesis, cell migration, ciliogenesis and, in neurons, axon initial segment (AIS) organization.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: One study was identified (PMID: 41408595). It reports mutations in five unrelated individuals and one mother-daughter pair, which implies dominant inheritance or transmission in that family. This is a single report, and only the abstract-level data were available.
- Patients: About 7 individuals in total.
- Phenotypes: Cognitive impairment was present in most individuals, and the report describes an impaired higher cognitive function / neurodevelopmental phenotype. No further detail was available, such as seizures, dysmorphism, growth, MRI findings, or the severity or range of intellectual disability.
- Variant type: The variants are described as missense. This is inferred from the dominant-negative mechanism, and the report predicts they preclude Septin-2 homodimerization. Specific variant notations (HGVS), de novo status and segregation data were not available. No loss-of-function variants were reported.
- Cohorts: No cohort affiliation (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL) was identified, and the center and country were not reported.
- Functional support in the same study: Mutant constructs in neurons caused loss of AIS Ankyrin G, aberrant MAP2 in the distal axon, axonal shortening and hypoexcitability. The Ankyrin G interaction was largely ablated (PMID: 41408595).
- Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED. The report is a single study with few patients and no variant-level detail, and it has not been independently replicated. The functional data are supportive.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: Mouse only.
- Endocardial lineage-specific Septin2 conditional knockout mice developed normally, with no detectable cardiac or valve abnormalities and no lethality. This contrasts with Septin7 mutants, which had juvenile lethality and myxomatous valve disease (PMID: 42657464). This tissue-restricted model says little about the brain.
- Sept2 knockdown in mice impaired spermatogonial proliferation (G1-S arrest, reduced AKT phosphorylation via PP2A/B56γ stabilization) and phenocopied hypoxia-induced spermatogenesis defects, with n = 3-5 per group (PMID: 40487848).
- Disrupted folate-dependent methylation led to neural tube defects with failed septin2 methylation, which altered GTP binding and septin2-6-7 complex formation and compromised ciliogenesis (PMID: 28432198). This is indirect evidence, because the manipulation was of the pathway, not of Sept2 itself.
- No germline Sept2 knockout or neuron-specific model, and no neurobehavioral or cognitive phenotyping, was reported. Mendelian ratios were not reported.
- Consistency with human phenotype: Largely not assessable. The neural tube and ciliogenesis findings are suggestive of a role in neurodevelopment, but no direct neurodevelopmental phenotype has been shown in a Sept2 mutant animal.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Molecular function: Septin2 forms septin2-6-7 complexes and octamers, and its GTP binding and homodimerization are important for assembly. Septin2 stabilizes actin stress fibres (PMID: 15541171) and is required for podosome maturation in endothelial cells, where it forms a diffusion barrier and uses phosphoinositide-binding residues (PMID: 31865373). It is also required for chromosome congression in mouse oocytes and is SUMOylated there (PMID: 20372094).
- Pathways: Folate-dependent methylation regulates septin2 and ciliogenesis (PMID: 28432198). Sept2 acts in PP2A/AKT signaling in spermatogonia (PMID: 40487848). It limits ER stress and macrophage activation through HSPA5 modification (PMID: 37978190). It is S-nitrosylated at Cys111 in aortic dissection, which reduces its interaction with TIAM1 (PMID: 38357802). It is also stabilized by CDK2 and LH1 in cancer (PMID: 30444001, PMID: 36721170).
- Expression: Most of the supplied evidence concerns non-neuronal tissues, including endothelium, testis, macrophages, liver and oocytes. Neuronal relevance comes mainly from the AIS study, which shows a role in AIS maintenance and Ankyrin G interaction (PMID: 41408595). Brain expression data per se were not provided.
- Key findings: The septin assembly mechanism is well characterized, and the dominant-negative mechanism proposed for the human variants is biologically plausible, since homodimerization-deficient subunits could disrupt octamers.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE. The general function is well supported, but neurodevelopment-specific evidence is mostly confined to one study.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: LIMITED.
- Key strengths: Several affected individuals, including a familial transmission (mother-daughter), share cognitive impairment. A plausible dominant-negative mechanism is supported by neuronal functional data (loss of AIS Ankyrin G, axonal shortening, hypoexcitability). Septin2 biology is well characterized, and folate/neural tube data point to a developmental role.
- Key gaps: There is only a single clinical report, with no variant-level data, no de novo confirmation, and no phenotypic detail beyond cognitive impairment. There is no replication or cohort-level (e.g., DDD, UDN) evidence and no population constraint or burden analysis. No neuron-relevant or germline animal model is available, and the conditional knockout in endocardium was normal. Most functional literature is non-neuronal.
- Conclusion: SEPTIN2 is an emerging candidate for a dominant neurodevelopmental disorder, supported by one study (PMID: 41408595) that links presumed dominant-negative missense variants to cognitive impairment and to AIS dysfunction in neurons. Independent replication with detailed variant and phenotype data, along with neuronal in vivo models, is needed before the gene-disease relationship can be considered established.
