SUMO2 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: SUMO2 AND NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
SUMO2 encodes small ubiquitin-like modifier 2, the most abundantly expressed SUMO isoform. It is covalently conjugated to lysine residues on many target proteins, either as a single moiety or as poly-SUMO chains. This post-translational modification regulates transcription, chromatin organization, nuclear body assembly, protein stability and the stress response. SUMO2 is essential for mouse embryonic development (PMID: 24891386).

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Case reports/series: The structured clinical evidence contained no entries, so no peer-reviewed case reports or case series were identified. The only human observation is a conference abstract (ASHG 2022 - Maternally Inherited novel SUMO2 variant associated with a complex phenotype).
- Patients: About 2, from one family (a proband and his mother).
- Phenotypes: Both have limb malformations (tibial agenesis, syndactyly). The proband also has congenital heart defects. No neurodevelopmental phenotype is described in the available summary.
- Variant type: A heterozygous start-loss variant (c.2T>A, p.Met1?), which is predicted loss of function. It was maternally inherited, so it is not de novo.
- Cohorts: No UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL cohort membership is reported.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE to minimal. The single abstract-level family does not show a neurodevelopmental phenotype and cannot support gene-disease association.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: Mouse only, in both full and conditional knockouts.
- Full knockout: Sumo2-/- embryos are severely developmentally delayed and die at about E10.5 (embryonic lethal). Sumo2+/- mice, and Sumo2+/-;Sumo3+/- mice, have no overt phenotype (PMID: 24891386). Sumo3-/- mice are viable. Sumo2+/-;Sumo3-/- mice are nearly absent at birth (2 found across 35 litters), and the rare survivors are small (PMID: 24891386). The gene is therefore dosage-sensitive when SUMO3 is also lost, but single heterozygosity is tolerated.
- Genetic interaction: Reducing Sumo2 rescued the placental trophoblast defects and the heart defects (atrioventricular cushion and myocardium) of Senp2 knockouts, which shows that SUMO2 levels matter for extraembryonic and cardiac development (PMID: 31625212).
- Neurological: Conditional deletion of Sumo2 in forebrain neurons produced marked cognitive impairment, with episodic and fear memory deficits, impaired activity-dependent SUMOylation and a deficit in maintaining hippocampal long-term potentiation. Gene expression and neuronal morphology were not constitutively changed (PMID: 32910521).
- Other conditional models: T cell-specific knockout enhanced CD8+ T cell activation and antitumor activity (PMID: 42853933). Epithelial knockout showed that SUMO2 is required for epithelial differentiation (PMID: 42348677).
- Consistency with human phenotype: The heart and limb/developmental phenotypes are loosely compatible with the single human family. The cognitive and memory deficits in the conditional model support a role in neuronal function. Heterozygous knockout mice, however, have no overt phenotype, which does not match a dominant human disorder.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED to MODERATE. Essentiality in development and a neuronal memory phenotype are shown, but there is no heterozygous neurodevelopmental model.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: SUMO2/3 are conjugated to substrates by the E1/E2/E3 cascade and removed by SENP proteases. The SENP1–SUMO2 crystal structure (2.62 Å) shows the C-terminal QQTGG motif inserting into the SENP1 catalytic triad (PMID: 31045562). SUMO2 forms poly-SUMO chains, and K11 acetylation (deacetylated by SIRT1) shifts linkage preference toward K5 and K35 chains (PMID: 30201799). SUMO2 K11 can also be lactylated (PMID: 41057295).
- Neural relevance: SUMO2/3-ylation is modulated by guanosine in primary neurons and astrocytes (PMID: 32892251). SUMO2/3 modification of USP5 regulates Cav3.2 calcium channel binding in dorsal root ganglia (PMID: 31455361). SUMO2/3 modification of Drp1 affects mitochondrial fission in ischemia models (PMID: 38615367). SUMO2 attachment to TDP-43 targets it to PML nuclear bodies and limits aggregation (PMID: 40246979). SUMO2/3 silencing in B35 neuroblastoma cells altered the expression of 163 genes (105 up, 58 down) and suppressed cell division (PMID: 19275883).
- Cancer and other contexts: SUMO2 is a dependency in synovial sarcoma (PMID: 40804185). SUMO2 modification of hnRNPA2B1 and DDX39B drives bladder cancer metastasis (PMID: 37991737; PMID: 35395674). SUMO2/3 levels are elevated in IBD (PMID: 39565873).
- Expression: The provided evidence does not give detailed brain developmental expression data. SUMO2 is reported as the predominant SUMO isoform (PMID: 24891386).
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for a general role in neuronal function and chromatin regulation. It is LIMITED for any specific link to neurodevelopmental disease, because most studies are in cancer or other contexts.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT (for SUMO2 as an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene).
- Key strengths: SUMO2 is essential for mouse development. Conditional neuronal deletion causes memory deficits and impaired synaptic plasticity (PMID: 32910521). The gene has well-characterized molecular function, including in neural substrates. One heterozygous start-loss variant is reported in a family with limb and cardiac malformations (ASHG 2022 - Maternally Inherited novel SUMO2 variant associated with a complex phenotype).
- Key gaps: There are no peer-reviewed clinical reports and no cohort data. The only human family is described in an abstract, with an inherited variant and no neurodevelopmental phenotype documented. There is no de novo variant evidence, no functional validation of the variant, and no genetic constraint or gene-burden data in the provided material. Heterozygous knockout mice are unaffected, and a full knockout is embryonic lethal, so a dominant human disease mechanism is unestablished.
- Conclusion: Current evidence does not support SUMO2 as an established neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous pathogenic variants. Biological plausibility and the mouse neuronal phenotype are supportive, but human genetic evidence is a single abstract-level family with a limb and cardiac phenotype. Additional unrelated probands with de novo or well-phenotyped variants, plus functional studies, are needed before any gene-disease classification beyond "insufficient."

3. Small ubiquitin-like modifier 2 (SUMO2) is critical for memory processes in mice (PMID: 32910521)

5. Ubiquitin-like proteins NEDD8 and SUMO2 control epithelial homeostasis, regeneration, and inflammation (PMID: 42348677)

8. Gene expression and cell growth are modified by silencing SUMO2 and SUMO3 expression (PMID: 19275883)
