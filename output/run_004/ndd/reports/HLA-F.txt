HLA-F LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
HLA-F is a non-classical MHC class I gene. Its protein is expressed as a peptide-free "open conformer" on activated lymphocytes and other cells, where it acts as a ligand for NK cell receptors (KIR3DS1, KIR3DL2, KIR2DS4) and contributes to MHC-I cross-presentation. No established role in neurodevelopment has been described.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent case reports or case series: 0 found.
- Total patients: 0.
- Phenotypes: none reported. No neurodevelopmental phenotype has been linked to HLA-F variants.
- Variant type: no pathogenic variants (LoF, missense or other) have been reported in affected individuals. The only variation described is common regulatory polymorphism (SNPs rs1362126, rs2523405 and rs2523393 near HLA-F or in HLA-F-AS1) associated with expression level (PMID: 37166140). A polymorphic 2.6 kbp upstream deletion correlates with expression level and exon usage (ASHG 2018 - Illuminating the dark genome: Evaluation of technologies to assess medically relevant dark spots).
- Known cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms studied: none. No knockout or other model data were identified. HLA-F has no clear one-to-one rodent ortholog, which would limit modelling.
- Neurological phenotypes, lethality or Mendelian ratio deviations: no data.
- Consistency with human phenotype: cannot be assessed.
- Strength of model organism evidence: NONE.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: all findings concern immune function.
  - HLA-F and other MHC-I open conformers bind KIR3DS1 but not KIR3DL1. Immobilized HLA-F increased granule exocytosis in activated KIR3DS1+ cells (PMID: 27649529).
  - Open conformers also interact with KIR3DL2 and KIR2DS4 (PMID: 24018270).
  - HLA-F and MHC-I open conformers mediate TAP- and tapasin-independent cross-presentation of exogenous antigen (PMID: 23851683).
  - HLA-F is up-regulated on HCV-infected cells, and KIR3DS1–HLA-F interaction contributes to NK-mediated control of HCV replication (PMID: 30031767).
- Expression:
  - Expression is regulated by common genetic polymorphisms, with a double-dose effect (PMID: 37166140).
  - HLA-F is expressed in mid-secretory endometrium and endometrial stromal cells, and varies with genotype (PMID: 35689445).
  - Expression in tumors is associated with poor prognosis in glioma (593 samples; PMID: 30755240), NSCLC (83 patients, HR 5.12, p=0.017; PMID: 21561677) and gastric cancer (209 patients; PMID: 25862890).
  - The glioma study is the only brain-related finding, and it concerns tumor expression and prognosis, not neurodevelopment. No data on normal brain or developmental expression were provided.
- Key experimental findings: protein interaction, pathway and expression data are well supported for immune and oncologic contexts, but none address neuronal function or neurodevelopment.
- Strength of biological evidence for a neurodevelopmental role: LIMITED. The immune-function evidence is reasonable, but its relevance to neurodevelopmental disease is unsupported.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT.
- Key strengths: HLA-F's molecular function in immune regulation is reasonably characterized, and common regulatory variants affecting expression have been described.
- Key gaps:
  - No patients, no heterozygous pathogenic variants and no cohort data.
  - No model organism data.
  - No evidence of brain or neurodevelopmental expression or function.
  - Genetic findings are limited to common regulatory polymorphisms and a structural polymorphism, not disease-causing variants.
  - The HLA region's polymorphism and complexity complicate variant interpretation.
- Conclusion: There is currently no clinical, model organism or mechanistic evidence supporting HLA-F as an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene. The available literature supports only an immunological role and expression associations in cancer and reproductive contexts. Without affected individuals carrying rare heterozygous variants and functional data in neural systems, the gene–disease relationship cannot be assessed and should not be asserted.
