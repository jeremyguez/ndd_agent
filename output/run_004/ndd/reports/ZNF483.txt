ZNF483 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT MODEL)

1. GENE SUMMARY
ZNF483 encodes a KRAB-domain zinc finger protein (KRAB-ZNF), a family of transcriptional repressors. It acts with TRIM28 to repress differentiation genes through H3K9me3 and DNA methylation in human pluripotent stem cells (PMID: 29198826). It has also been proposed as a MeCP2-binding protein that influences neuronal differentiation (PMID: 34192606).

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: three were found. Two are ASD cohort studies and one is a familial microdeletion report. No classic case reports or case series describe heterozygous, ZNF483-specific variants.
- Approximate patients: the numbers are largely unquantified. The Chinese WGS study included 112 ASD trios (334 individuals), but the number of patients with ZNF483 variants is not stated (PMID: 39126614). The Omani cohort reported 1 individual (PMID: 39769462). The microdeletion report describes a family, with the number of affected members not extracted (PMID: 30847195).
- Phenotypes:
  - ASD is the main phenotype, seen in the two cohort studies.
  - The microdeletion family had fatigue, muscle cramps, somnolence, short stature, delayed puberty, sensorineural hearing loss, and mild developmental delay.
- Variant types:
  - The Chinese study gave no variant-level details, only de novo and inherited coding variants at gene level (PMID: 39126614).
  - The Omani case carried a homozygous nonsense variant, NM_133464.5 c.2047C>T, p.R683* (PMID: 39769462). This is a recessive-type observation, not heterozygous.
  - The 9q31.2q32 deletion is a multi-gene heterozygous deletion (chr9:109195179-113974353, hg18), so ZNF483 cannot be separated from other genes in the region (PMID: 30847195).
- Known cohorts: none of UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL are identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED. There is gene-level ASD association in one trio study, but no variant-level data. The one LoF variant is homozygous, and the deletion is non-specific.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- No knockout or mutant organism data were identified.
- The only related work is in a murine P19 embryonal carcinoma cell line, which is a cell model and is covered under biological evidence (PMID: 34192606).
- Consistency with the human phenotype cannot be assessed.
- Strength of model organism evidence: NONE

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function:
  - ZNF483 is one of three KRAB-ZNFs (with ZNF114 and ZNF589) that maintain pluripotency in human PSCs, working with TRIM28 to repress pro-differentiation genes (PMID: 29198826).
  - It is a candidate MeCP2-binding protein from cerebral cortex. It is broadly conserved in metazoans, whereas MeCP2 arose in chordates (PMID: 34192606).
- Key experiments (PMID: 34192606):
  - CRISPR-Cas9 ZNF483-knockout P19 cells showed decreased proliferation, altered aggregate formation, reduced NeuN expression and altered MeCP2 phosphorylation.
  - Overexpression enhanced cytosolic MeCP2 localization.
  - These findings support a role in neuronal differentiation.
- Expression:
  - Direct data on human brain expression are limited in this evidence set.
  - Znf483 mRNA was increased in the hippocampus of an Aβ-induced AD rat model and fell with treatment (PMID: 30641629).
  - Other expression findings are of uncertain relevance: down-regulation in equine blood after shock wave therapy (PMID: 34008346) and prostate cancer RNA-seq predictive value (PMID: 37298233).
- Human genetic data in a related trait: rare LoF variants in ZNF483 were associated with age at menarche in exomes from about 220,000 women, and they abolished the effect of polygenic risk (PMID: 38951643; PMID: 37503126). This is consistent with the delayed puberty seen in the microdeletion family and suggests a role in pubertal timing, but it does not directly bear on neurodevelopment.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for a plausible neurodevelopmental role (epigenetic regulation, MeCP2 interaction, neuronal differentiation). The evidence rests on few studies, mostly in cell lines.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: LIMITED
- Key strengths:
  - ASD gene-level association from a WGS trio study (P<0.001 for risk gene selection) (PMID: 39126614).
  - A LoF variant in an ASD individual (PMID: 39769462).
  - Mechanistic links to MeCP2, a well-known neurodevelopmental gene, and to neuronal differentiation (PMID: 34192606).
  - Independent population-level LoF association with pubertal timing (PMID: 38951643).
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - There are no well-characterized heterozygous variant cases with detailed phenotypes.
  - The only specific LoF variant is homozygous (PMID: 39769462).
  - The deletion evidence is confounded by multiple genes (PMID: 30847195).
  - No model organism data exist.
  - There is no segregation or functional validation of patient variants, and variant details from the Chinese study are not available.
  - The cell studies are in one murine line.
- Conclusion: ZNF483 is a plausible candidate neurodevelopmental/ASD gene, supported by gene-level ASD association and by functional links to MeCP2 and neuronal differentiation. Evidence for a heterozygous (autosomal dominant) disorder is currently limited. Additional cases with well-phenotyped heterozygous LoF or de novo variants, and in vivo models, are needed before a gene-disease relationship can be asserted.
