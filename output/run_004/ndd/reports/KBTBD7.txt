KBTBD7 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: Evidence for a heterozygous (autosomal dominant) neurodevelopmental disorder

1. GENE SUMMARY
KBTBD7 encodes a BTB-BACK-Kelch domain protein that acts as a substrate adaptor for CUL3-RING E3 ubiquitin ligase complexes (and also works with p97/VCP). It targets substrates such as TIAM1, DRD2, Vangl1/2 and PTEN for ubiquitination and degradation (PMID: 25684205; PMID: 32572597; PMID: 33990333).

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: three were found, and none is a dedicated KBTBD7 case series. All are cohort or large-scale studies that mention the gene.
- Approximate patients: about 3 are identifiable (1 autism case, 2 Moebius syndrome cases), plus an unspecified number of NDD cases with recurrent de novo missense variants.
- Phenotypes:
  - A single autism proband had ASD, atypical language and normal IQ. The variant was NM_032138.7 c.1849C>T, p.Arg617Cys, de novo, classified as VUS (PS2, PP3, BS4) (PMID: 38519481).
  - A large NDD meta-analysis listed KBTBD7 among 26 candidate genes based on recurrent de novo damaging missense variants at the same amino acid positions. Specific variants and patient numbers were not given in the extracted text, and the phenotype was a non-specific NDD (PMID: 35468861).
  - In a Moebius syndrome exome study, p.Pro499Arg was found in 2 individuals with congenital facial and abducens palsies. It was inherited from an unaffected parent and judged non-diagnostic (PMID: 28299356).
- Variant type: exclusively missense. No loss-of-function variants were reported.
- Known cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none are explicitly identified. The studies come from Italy and Japan, and the Japanese study was a unified analysis of NDD cohorts.
- Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED. There is a statistical signal from recurrent de novo missense variants and one de novo VUS, but there is no detailed phenotyping, no phenotypic homogeneity and no functional validation. The Moebius variant was inherited from an unaffected parent.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse (full CRISPR-Cas9 knockout) and zebrafish (knockdown).
- Key phenotypes:
  - Knockout mice had elevated DRD2 protein in the pituitary, thalamus and heart, supporting a role in DRD2 degradation in vivo (PMID: 32572597). No neurological, behavioral, viability or Mendelian ratio data were reported.
  - Zebrafish knockdown disrupted convergent extension during gastrulation, consistent with increased Vangl stability and altered Wnt/PCP signaling (PMID: 33990333).
- Consistency with human phenotype: only indirect. Wnt/PCP disruption and altered dopamine receptor signaling are plausibly relevant to neurodevelopment, but no neurodevelopmental or behavioral phenotype has been shown.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function:
  - KBTBD7 is a CUL3 adaptor that degrades TIAM1, which regulates RAC1 signaling, actin organization and cell adhesion. This depends on GABARAP binding (PMID: 25684205).
  - It promotes Vangl1/2 ERAD via p97/VCP (PMID: 33990333).
  - It ubiquitinates DRD2 at five lysines (PMID: 32572597).
  - It also promotes PTEN degradation in NSCLC (PMID: 35499228), activates MKK3/6-p38/NF-κB inflammatory signaling (PMID: 29991775), and ubiquitinates KLF15 in microglia (PMID: 39489209).
- Expression:
  - Kbtbd7 is strongly down-regulated in Gtf2i and Gtf2ird1 mutant mouse embryos. It is a likely direct TFII-I target, its expression suggests a role in brain development, and it is a candidate contributor to Williams syndrome (PMID: 19527686).
  - It is upregulated in several cancers, including NSCLC (102 paired specimens) (PMID: 35499228).
- Other associations: a TWAS linked KBTBD7 to uterine fibroids (PMID: 36174000), and it is part of an NSCLC prognostic signature (PMID: 31417870). Neither is relevant to neurodevelopmental disease.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for molecular function and neurodevelopment-relevant pathways (RAC1, Wnt/PCP, dopamine signaling), but LIMITED for a direct neurodevelopmental mechanism. Most data come from cancer and inflammation contexts, and no studies address the effects of the patient variants.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: LIMITED
- Key strengths:
  - Recurrent de novo damaging missense variants at the same residues were reported in a large NDD analysis (PMID: 35468861).
  - A de novo missense variant was found in an ASD proband (PMID: 38519481).
  - KBTBD7 has well-characterized roles in pathways relevant to neurodevelopment (Wnt/PCP, RAC1, DRD2).
  - It is a probable TFII-I target with brain-enriched expression (PMID: 19527686).
- Key gaps:
  - There are no detailed clinical case descriptions and no consistent phenotype.
  - No LoF variants have been reported.
  - The only diagnostic classification is a VUS, and the Moebius variant was inherited from an unaffected parent.
  - The animal models have no neurological characterization.
  - There is no functional testing of the patient missense variants.
  - The mechanism (dominant-negative, gain-of-function or haploinsufficiency) is unknown.
  - No known clinical cohorts are identified.
- Conclusion: KBTBD7 is a plausible candidate NDD gene, supported mainly by statistical enrichment of de novo missense variants and by relevant biology. The current evidence is insufficient to establish a definitive gene-disease relationship for a dominant neurodevelopmental disorder. More patients with detailed phenotypes, functional studies of the recurrent missense variants, and neurobehavioral analysis of knockout or variant-bearing models are needed.

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. Genomic analysis of 116 autism families strengthens known risk genes and highlights promising candidates (PMID: 38519481)
2. Large-scale discovery of novel neurodevelopmental disorder-related genes through a unified analysis of single-nucleotide and copy number variants (PMID: 35468861)
3. An exome sequencing study of Moebius syndrome including atypical cases reveals an individual with CFEOM3A and a TUBB3 mutation (PMID: 28299356)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. Wang et al. 2020 - The KBTBD6/7-DRD2 axis regulates pituitary adenoma sensitivity to dopamine agonist treatment (PMID: 32572597)
2. Regulation of Wnt/PCP signaling through p97/VCP-KBTBD7-mediated Vangl ubiquitination and endoplasmic reticulum-associated degradation (PMID: 33990333)

### Biological Evidence
1. Genau et al. 2015 - CUL3-KBTBD6/KBTBD7 ubiquitin ligase cooperates with GABARAP proteins to spatially restrict TIAM1-RAC1 signaling (PMID: 25684205)
2. KBTBD7 promotes non-small cell lung carcinoma progression by enhancing ubiquitin-dependent degradation of PTEN (PMID: 35499228)
3. MicroRNA-21 prevents excessive inflammation and cardiac dysfunction after myocardial infarction through targeting KBTBD7 (PMID: 29991775)
4. Targeting of ubiquitination and degradation of KLF15 by E3 ubiquitin ligase KBTBD7 regulates LPS-induced septic brain injury in microglia (PMID: 39489209)
5. New TFII-I family target genes involved in embryonic development (PMID: 19527686)
6. A transcriptome-wide association study of uterine fibroids to identify potential genetic markers and toxic chemicals (PMID: 36174000)
7. A Robust 8-Gene Prognostic Signature for Early-Stage Non-small Cell Lung Cancer (PMID: 31417870)
