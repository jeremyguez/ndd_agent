BRWD1 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: BRWD1 AND NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER (HETEROZYGOUS/AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
BRWD1 (bromodomain and WD repeat-containing protein 1) is a histone-reader chromatin regulator on chromosome 21 (HSA21). It binds the BAF (SWI/SNF) chromatin remodeling complex and shapes enhancer accessibility, cohesin dynamics and transcription, with best-characterized roles in B lymphopoiesis, germinal center B cells and gametogenesis. It is also implicated in brain chromatin regulation in a Down syndrome model.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent case reports/series: One relevant study was found, a large exome sequencing cohort study of obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) and chronic tic disorders (PMID: 42680906). It is a gene-level statistical report, not a case report or series of patients with a neurodevelopmental disorder.
- Number of patients: Not specified. No per-patient data are available.
- Phenotypes: OCD and chronic tic disorders. No intellectual disability, developmental delay, or other classic neurodevelopmental phenotypes are reported for BRWD1.
- Variant type: Not specified. The study reports an excess of de novo and rare protein-damaging variants in cases (FDR < 0.1), which suggests loss-of-function or damaging variants, but the abstract gives no details. BRWD1 also overlaps an OCD GWAS locus.
- Cohorts: No UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL cohort is identified.
- Related human data: Hypogammaglobulinemia patients with BRWD1 mutations are mentioned in the mouse B-cell work (PMID: 30250168), but these are immunological rather than neurodevelopmental. Rare variants in BRWD1 were also enriched in non-obstructive azoospermia (PMID: 25739334).
- Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED. The signal is a single gene-level association with OCD/tics, which are neuropsychiatric rather than classic neurodevelopmental phenotypes. There are no individual-level phenotype or variant data.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organism: Mouse only.
- Full Brwd1 knockout mice are viable but infertile in both sexes (PMID: 25547156). Spermatids show down-regulation of about 300 transcripts, including near-complete loss of protamine and transition protein transcripts. Oocytes show chromosome condensation and structural defects, abnormal telomeres, and >2-fold overexpression of MLL5 and LINE-1. The ENU repro5 allele gives a similar infertility phenotype (PMID: 18353305).
- Immune phenotypes: Full knockout causes a late B-cell developmental block with dysregulation of >7000 genes (PMID: 30250168). Conditional knockouts have impaired germinal center responses, class switching, affinity maturation and tolerance (PMID: 42864370).
- Neurological: No knockout neurological phenotype is reported and no Mendelian ratio data are given. In the Ts65Dn Down syndrome model, Brwd1 is upregulated in brain, and restoring it to two copies rescued hippocampal LTP, cognitive deficits, BAF localization, chromatin accessibility and gene expression (PMID: 36289231). This implicates BRWD1 dosage (overexpression) in neural function, but it is not a heterozygous loss-of-function model.
- Consistency with human phenotype: The reproductive and immune phenotypes are consistent with human BRWD1 data (azoospermia burden, hypogammaglobulinemia). A neurodevelopmental phenotype from loss of function is not demonstrated in models.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED for neurodevelopmental disorder. It is robust for fertility and B-cell biology, and the Ts65Dn data support a role in cognition when overexpressed.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: BRWD1 is a histone reader that interacts tightly with the BAF complex. It regulates enhancer accessibility and gene expression in pre-B cells (PMID: 30250168), targets recombination to the Igk locus (PMID: 26301565), and converts static cohesin to dynamic cohesin for long-range looping (PMID: 37985858). In Down syndrome models, excess BRWD1 mistargets BAF (PMID: 36289231).
- Expression: Broadly expressed (PMID: 18353305). It is brain-expressed and upregulated in Down syndrome iPSC-derived neurons and trisomic mouse brain (PMID: 36289231). In schizophrenia LCL profiling (446 cases, 457 controls), BRWD1 was one of eight transcripts differentially expressed after Bonferroni correction (ASHG 2011 - Analysis of transcriptional profiles in schizophrenia).
- Other: BRWD1 is linked to cisplatin resistance in cervical cancer (PMID: 30536307), which is not relevant to neurodevelopment.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE. The chromatin-regulatory mechanism is well supported and relevant to the chromatin-remodeling class of neurodevelopmental disorder genes (BAF/SWI-SNF). Direct neurodevelopmental mechanistic evidence for loss of function is limited.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: LIMITED (borderline INSUFFICIENT for a Mendelian autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder).
- Key strengths: A statistically significant excess of de novo and rare damaging variants in OCD/tic disorders (PMID: 42680906). Well-characterized BAF/cohesin chromatin-regulatory function. Brain-relevant dosage effects on cognition and LTP in a Down syndrome model (PMID: 36289231).
- Key gaps: No individual-level patient or variant data. No reported syndromic neurodevelopmental phenotype (ID, DD, autism, seizures). No defined variant spectrum or inheritance data beyond de novo enrichment. No neurological phenotype in knockouts, and the available mouse work focuses on fertility and immunity. Most brain data concern overexpression rather than haploinsufficiency.
- Conclusion: BRWD1 is a plausible chromatin-regulator candidate for neuropsychiatric and neurodevelopmental phenotypes, supported by gene-level de novo/rare variant enrichment in OCD/tic disorders and by brain-relevant BAF-related biology. However, there are no case-level clinical reports, and loss-of-function models do not show neurodevelopmental phenotypes. Evidence for a heterozygous BRWD1 neurodevelopmental disorder is currently limited, and additional patient cohorts with detailed phenotyping and functional validation of variants are needed.

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. Whole-exome sequencing in individuals with obsessive-compulsive disorder and chronic tic disorders identifies 36 large-effect risk genes (PMID: 42680906)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. BRWD1 orchestrates epigenetic landscape of late B lymphopoiesis (PMID: 30250168)
2. Mouse BRWD1 is critical for spermatid postmeiotic transcription and female meiotic chromosome stability (PMID: 25547156)
3. BRWD1 directs B cell epigenetic states required for germinal center initiation, maintenance and function (PMID: 42864370)
4. Rescue of deficits by Brwd1 copy number restoration in the Ts65Dn mouse model of Down syndrome (PMID: 36289231)

### Biological Evidence
1. Excess of rare variants in genes that are key epigenetic regulators of spermatogenesis in the patients with non-obstructive azoospermia (PMID: 25739334)
2. The dual bromodomain and WD repeat-containing mouse protein BRWD1 is required for normal spermiogenesis and the oocyte-embryo transition (PMID: 18353305)
3. Histone reader BRWD1 targets and restricts recombination to the Igk locus (PMID: 26301565)
4. BRWD1 orchestrates small pre-B cell chromatin topology by converting static to dynamic cohesin (PMID: 37985858)
5. MALAT1 promotes cisplatin resistance in cervical cancer by activating the PI3K/AKT pathway (PMID: 30536307)
