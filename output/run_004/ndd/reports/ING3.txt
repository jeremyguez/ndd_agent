ING3 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: ING3 AS A CANDIDATE AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

1. GENE SUMMARY
ING3 (Inhibitor of Growth 3) is a PHD-finger chromatin reader. It is a core subunit of the minimal NuA4/TIP60 histone acetyltransferase complex (EAF6, EPC1, ING3, TIP60), and it binds H3K4me3 to regulate transcription, cell cycle, apoptosis and p53 acetylation (PMID: 31905726; PMID: 29381681).

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent case reports or case series: 0. No clinical reports were retrieved.
- Total patients: 0.
- Phenotypes: none reported.
- Variant type: no human variants were reported, so no LoF or missense pattern can be assessed.
- Cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE. There is no human genetic evidence linking heterozygous ING3 variants to a neurodevelopmental disorder.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms studied: mouse (full and conditional knockouts) and zebrafish (knockdown).
- Mouse full knockout or null alleles: Homozygous Ing3-null mice die in utero and do not survive past E10.5. They show severe growth retardation and failure of neural tube closure (PMID: 39787145). An independent line with a UbC-mCherry cassette inserted into the Ing3 locus lacks ING3 protein. Its homozygous mutants die in utero with growth retardation, neural tube closure defects, no closed primary brain vesicles, and a lack of ectodermal differentiation (PMID: 31905726). Embryonic lethality was confirmed, and ectopic ING3 expression rescued it (PMID: 40149370).
- Mouse conditional knockout: Prostate-specific deletion (PB-Cre4, mosaic, ~15–30% recombination) caused increased γH2AX and 53BP1 but no gross abnormalities, no PIN and no malignant transformation (PMID: 40149370).
- Zebrafish: ing3 knockdown, alone or combined with brd7, ing2, ing4 and suz12b, raised tfpia mRNA and gave an antithrombotic phenotype. This is not neurodevelopmental (PMID: 34315984).
- Heterozygous mice: no phenotype data were provided, and no Mendelian ratio data beyond homozygous lethality were reported.
- Consistency with human phenotype: Homozygous null mice have neural tube and brain vesicle defects, so ING3 is essential for early neural development. No human disease phenotype exists for comparison, and the recessive-type lethality does not directly model a heterozygous dominant disorder.
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE for an essential developmental role, including neural development, but LIMITED for relevance to heterozygous human neurodevelopmental disease.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: ING3 is part of the NuA4/TIP60 HAT complex. It reads H3K4me3 at transcription start sites and controls a cell-cycle gene network (PMID: 29381681). It cooperates with p300 to acetylate p53 (PMID: 31886514). It promotes UV-induced apoptosis via the Fas/caspase-8 pathway independently of p53 (PMID: 16520380). It also acts as an androgen receptor co-activator, and a splice variant lacking the PHD (ING3Δex11) induces EMT (PMID: 34439818).
- Expression: ING3 is highest in rapidly renewing tissues (small intestine, bone marrow, epidermis) and is higher in proliferating than in quiescent cells (PMID: 25819753). It is mainly cytoplasmic in normal tissues, with strong staining in stomach, skin and cervix and weak staining in brain, liver, testis and prostate (PMID: 28454301). Brain expression is therefore low in adult tissue, and no developing-brain expression data were provided.
- Other functional findings: ING3 knockdown disrupts asymmetric division in mouse oocytes, possibly through mTOR (PMID: 24066152). It impairs osteoblast differentiation and is implicated as a bone mineral density GWAS effector gene (ASHG 2018 - Genome-scale capture C promoter interaction analysis implicates novel effector genes at GWAS loci for bone mineral density).
- Cancer links: Low ING3 mRNA predicts poor survival in head and neck cancer (n=71) (PMID: 18081876), whereas other work suggests an oncogenic role in prostate cancer (PMID: 29381681). Evidence on its role in cancer is therefore mixed.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for a fundamental role in chromatin regulation and growth, but LIMITED for neurodevelopment specifically.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT for ING3 as a heterozygous neurodevelopmental disorder gene.
- Key strengths: The mouse nulls consistently show embryonic lethality, with neural tube closure defects and brain vesicle abnormalities, in two independent lines and a rescue experiment (PMID: 31905726; PMID: 39787145; PMID: 40149370). ING3 is also a core component of a chromatin-modifying complex, a class of proteins frequently implicated in neurodevelopmental disorders.
- Key gaps: There are no human patients, variants, de novo data or cohort data. There are no heterozygous mouse phenotype data. Brain-specific expression and function are not characterized, and much of the biology comes from cancer studies. Complete loss is embryonic lethal in mice, so the human consequences of heterozygous variants are unknown.
- Conclusion: ING3 is biologically plausible as a developmental gene, given the neural tube and brain defects in null mice and its role in the NuA4/TIP60 complex. There is currently no clinical evidence for an association with a heterozygous neurodevelopmental disorder, so it should be regarded as an unsupported candidate until human cases with heterozygous (ideally de novo) variants are reported.
