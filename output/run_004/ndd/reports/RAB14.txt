RAB14 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER WITH HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) VARIANTS

1. GENE SUMMARY
RAB14 encodes a small GTPase of the Rab family that regulates membrane trafficking between the trans-Golgi network (TGN) and endosomes, including endosomal recycling and budding. The literature supplied also links it to mitophagy, cytokinesis, and lysosomal acidification.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent case reports or case series: 0. No clinical reports were identified.
- Total patients: 0.
- Phenotypes: none reported.
- Variant type: no data (no LoF, missense, or other variants described).
- Cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms studied: mouse. Drosophila appears only in the biological evidence, as a fat body RNAi model.
- Key phenotypes: the single mouse study did not knock out Rab14. Ueno et al. 2011 overexpressed dominant-negative Rab14 (Rab14-GDP) in mouse embryos (PMID: 21238925). This reproduced the Kif16b/FGFR2 deficiency phenotype, with impaired FGF receptor transport and FGF signaling. It also caused defective basement membrane assembly by the primitive endoderm lineage and impaired epiblast development. In the parallel Kif16b-/- knockout, embryos failed to develop epiblast and primitive endoderm lineages and died at peri-implantation. No Mendelian ratios, lethality data for Rab14 itself, sample sizes, or neurological phenotypes were reported.
- Consistency with human phenotype: cannot be assessed, because there are no human data. The early embryonic developmental phenotype points to an important developmental role. It is a dominant-negative, non-germline approach and does not model heterozygous human variants.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and molecular function: Rab14 acts in several trafficking and cellular processes.
  - Golgi-endosome trafficking: Vps26B is a Rab14 effector, and the RhoBTB3-SHIP164-Vps26B complex drives budding of Rab14+ endosomes at Golgi-endosome contacts (PMID: 38565878).
  - Mitophagy: Rab14 promotes Parkin-dependent mitophagy, requiring TBK1 and PI3K activity (PMID: 40737294).
  - Cytokinesis: Rab14 binds the MACF2/CAMSAP3 complex, and its depletion or knockout causes cytokinesis failure or delay (PMID: 33566684).
  - Pathogen restriction: the Rab14-CAMK2D-V-ATPase axis drives lysosomal acidification (PMID: 41771894).
  - Other roles: Rab14 supports Brucella infection (PMID: 42402044), and it interacts with CPT1A to promote fatty acid trafficking in nasopharyngeal carcinoma cells (PMID: 29721083).
  - Cationic substances take an endocytic route that requires Rab14 (PMID: 34731620).
  - Oocyte maturation: Rab14 is required for asymmetric division through the ROCK-cofilin pathway (PMID: 34323331).
  - Extracellular vesicles: in Drosophila, Rab14 with Klp98A functions in extracellular vesicle biogenesis and secretion (PMID: 36103151).
- Expression: the only expression data are from pig (PMID: 17380425). Porcine RAB14 encodes a 215-aa protein with 99% identity to human and mouse. It is highly expressed in fat, lung, spleen and kidney, and only weakly or barely in small intestine, muscle and liver. Brain expression was not reported, so neurodevelopmental relevance is not directly shown.
- Key findings: Rab14 has well-supported roles in trafficking, mitophagy, and cell division, all of which could plausibly matter in neurons. However, none of the provided studies tests neuronal function or neurodevelopmental phenotypes.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for general cell biology, but LIMITED for neurodevelopmental relevance.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT.
- Key strengths: Rab14 has well-characterized roles in Golgi-endosome trafficking and mitophagy. The mouse dominant-negative study shows a requirement for Rab14-dependent FGFR trafficking in early embryogenesis (PMID: 21238925).
- Key gaps: there are no patients, variants, or cohort data. There is no Rab14 germline knockout or heterozygous model and no neurological phenotyping. The supplied papers include no brain or neuron-specific expression or functional data. There are also no variant-level functional studies, and no evidence for haploinsufficiency or a dominant mechanism.
- Conclusion: The supplied literature does not support RAB14 as an established autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene. Its trafficking functions make it a plausible candidate, but the case is currently speculative. Establishing a gene-disease relationship would require multiple unrelated individuals with heterozygous (likely de novo) variants, supported by neuronal or animal-model functional data.
