MAEA GENE EVIDENCE REPORT: NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
MAEA (macrophage erythroblast attacher) encodes a core catalytic subunit of the CTLH multi-subunit E3 ubiquitin ligase complex. It targets substrates for ubiquitylation, with roles in erythroid/macrophage biology, haematopoietic stem cell maintenance, and the DNA replication stress response and homologous recombination (HR) (PMID: 40880485; PMID: 33947846).

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: Two companion publications were found, and they likely overlap in their patients (PMID: 41420108; PMID: 40880485). Neither is a conventional detailed case series in the abstract-level data available.
- Patient numbers: About 8 patients with MAEA variants are reported (PMID: 41420108). The other paper does not give a patient count (PMID: 40880485).
- Phenotypes: Neurodevelopmental disorder (named "DIADEM"), developmental delay and intellectual disability (PMID: 41420108). Microcephaly, craniofacial abnormalities, ocular defects and heart malformations are described for the disorder as a whole, not per patient (PMID: 40880485).
- Variant type: Four missense variants are reported: p.Arg34Cys, p.Glu349Gly, p.Tyr394Asp and p.Met396Arg (PMID: 40880485). No loss-of-function variants are described. Zygosity, inheritance and de novo status are not stated in the available data, so an autosomal dominant mechanism cannot be confirmed. The variants may instead be biallelic or recessive; this is unresolved.
- Cohorts: No UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL involvement is stated. Centers and countries are not reported.
- Functional support: Patient-derived cells show defective HR-dependent repair, reduced RAD51 loading, and impaired replication fork restart and protection (PMID: 41420108). The variants are defective in the replication stress response (PMID: 40880485).
- Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED. A small number of patients and four missense variants have supportive functional data. Inheritance and segregation are undocumented, and the work comes from a single research effort.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: Mouse (conditional knockouts) and Xenopus laevis (knockdown).
- Mouse: Constitutive Maea-deficient mice die perinatally with anemia and defective erythrocyte enucleation, as background cited in (PMID: 30674470). Macrophage-specific deletion severely reduces bone marrow macrophages, erythroblasts and erythroblastic islands. Erythroid-specific deletion has no such effect (PMID: 30674470). Conditional Maea deletion impairs HSC quiescence and causes a lethal myeloproliferative syndrome, with stabilised MPL/FLT3 and impaired autophagy (PMID: 33947846). Mendelian ratios and heterozygote phenotypes are not reported.
- Xenopus: Maea knockdown interferes with head formation. Maea.S ubiquitinates β-catenin and reduces Wnt target gene expression (PMID: 36444483). This is a possible, but indirect, link to craniofacial or head development.
- Consistency with human phenotype: Limited. The murine phenotypes are mainly haematological, and no brain or neurodevelopmental assessment is reported. Perinatal lethality of the constitutive knockout shows that MAEA is essential, and the Xenopus head phenotype loosely parallels the craniofacial and microcephaly features in patients. Heterozygous model data are absent.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: MAEA is a catalytic CTLH subunit. Reported substrates and pathways include:
  - Ku80, with MAEA loss impairing RAD51 recruitment and causing nascent strand degradation (PMID: 40880485).
  - MKLN1 and ZMYND19, which regulate mTORC1 (PMID: 41315365).
  - PARP1 (PMID: 39990651).
  - PHD3 (PMID: 36882523).
  - β-catenin (PMID: 36444483).
  - Cytokine receptors and autophagy in HSCs (PMID: 33947846).
- Expression: High expression in HSCs is documented (PMID: 33947846). Brain expression data are not provided in the available evidence. Osteosarcoma and glioma expression findings are cancer-related (PMID: 40989695; PMID: 36882523).
- Key findings: A screen for RAD51 regulators identified MAEA as an HR regulator, and patient variants are defective in the replication stress response (PMID: 40880485). Related DNA repair and replication defects are shown in patient cells (PMID: 41420108). CTLH complex formation depends on RANBP9/RANBP10 (PMID: 40883813).
- Not relevant to this disorder: A type 2 diabetes GWAS signal at the CTBP1-MAEA locus favored CTBP1, not MAEA (ASHG 2011 - Comprehensive evaluation of type 2 diabetes susceptibility in the Japanese population by genome-wide imputation analysis using the 1000 Genomes Project data).
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE. The molecular function and a plausible mechanism (DNA replication stress and genome stability) are well supported. Neural-specific evidence is lacking.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: LIMITED (for a heterozygous/autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder).
- Key strengths:
  - Multiple patients (~8) with a consistent neurodevelopmental and congenital anomaly phenotype.
  - Recurrent functional deficits in patient cells and in variant assays.
  - Well-characterised E3 ligase function and DNA repair mechanism.
  - Essential in mouse (perinatal lethality of the constitutive knockout).
- Key gaps:
  - Inheritance, zygosity and de novo status are unreported, so a dominant mode is unsupported.
  - Only missense variants are described, with no LoF variants.
  - The patient reports likely overlap, and there are no independent replication cohorts.
  - Mouse models lack neurodevelopmental characterisation and heterozygote data.
  - No brain expression data are provided.
- Conclusion: MAEA is an emerging candidate for a neurodevelopmental disorder with microcephaly, craniofacial, ocular and cardiac features, supported by patient-cell functional data implicating HR and replication fork defects. The evidence is currently limited and does not establish an autosomal dominant mechanism. Additional unrelated families with documented de novo or segregating variants, and neurodevelopmental animal models, are needed.

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. Pathogenic variants in MAEA disrupt DNA replication fork stability and are associated with developmental abnormalities in humans (PMID: 40880485)
2. Loss of CTLH component MAEA impairs DNA repair and replication and leads to developmental delay (PMID: 41420108)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. MAEA is an E3 ubiquitin ligase promoting autophagy and maintenance of haematopoietic stem cells (PMID: 33947846)
2. Maea expressed by macrophages, but not erythroblasts, maintains postnatal murine bone marrow erythroblastic islands (PMID: 30674470)
3. maea affects head formation through ß-catenin degradation during early Xenopus laevis development (PMID: 36444483)

### Biological Evidence
1. CTLH ubiquitin ligase substrates ZMYND19 and MKLN1 negatively regulate mTORC1 at the lysosomal membrane (PMID: 41315365)
2. The E3 ubiquitin ligase MAEA promotes macrophage phagocytosis and inhibits gastrointestinal cancer progression by mediating PARP1 ubiquitination and degradation (PMID: 39990651)
3. E3 ligase MAEA-mediated ubiquitination and degradation of PHD3 promotes glioblastoma progression (PMID: 36882523)
4. Identification of MAEA protein as a potential target for chemoresistance in osteosarcoma using bioinformatics and proteomic analysis (PMID: 40989695)
5. RANBP9 and RANBP10 cooperate in regulating non-small cell lung cancer proliferation (PMID: 40883813)
