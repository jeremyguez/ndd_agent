MSI1 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
MSI1 (Musashi-1) encodes a conserved neural RNA-binding protein that regulates translation of target mRNAs. It is best known as a marker and regulator of neural and other stem/progenitor cells. Several of the studies below that carry the "MSI1" name concern yeast or Arabidopsis MSI1, which are unrelated WD-repeat chromatin proteins. They are not relevant to the human gene.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: 1 case report (PMID: 40018479).
- Total patients: 1 (a female Saudi patient).
- Phenotypes in that patient: refractory epilepsy, spasticity in all limbs, global developmental delay, intellectual disability, hypertelorism, small nasal tip, congenital heart defects (tricuspid atresia, single ventricle), finger and toe clubbing, and overlapping toes.
- Variant type: a de novo heterozygous ~6.6 Mb deletion at 12q24.23q24.31 spanning 122 genes, including MSI1. This is a contiguous-gene deletion, and the authors attributed the phenotype to the whole region rather than to MSI1. No single-gene variants (LoF or missense) in MSI1 have been reported.
- Cohorts: none (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC and GEL are not represented).
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE to LIMITED. The single report is a multigene CNV and cannot be used to support MSI1 haploinsufficiency. I rate it NONE for gene-specific evidence.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse (knockout and conditional knockout), zebrafish (morpholino knockdown), and Xenopus (oocyte knockdown).
- Mouse:
  - Full Msi1 knockout causes photoreceptor degeneration and loss of RPE65 in RPE microvilli (PMID: 18662689).
  - Full knockout severely impairs midline crossing and migration of precerebellar neurons, through reduced Robo3 protein (mRNA unchanged), and phenocopies Robo3-deficient mice (PMID: 20696379).
  - Pan-retinal or rod-specific Msi1/Msi2 double conditional knockout abolishes the light response, disrupts outer segment morphology and cilia, and causes complete photoreceptor loss within 6 months. The mice were viable (PMID: 33168629). The phenotype depends on loss of both paralogs, which suggests redundancy.
  - Loss of MSI1 protein increases Dio2 expression in cortex, and knockdown in primary cortical astrocytes slowed proliferation by 56% (PMID: 38879014).
- Zebrafish: zmsi1 morpholino knockdown frequently caused aberrant CNS formation (PMID: 22429745).
- Xenopus: MSI1/2 knockdown abolished oocyte maturation (PMID: 40211036), which has no neurodevelopmental relevance.
- Not reported: Mendelian ratios, heterozygous phenotypes, and seizure or cognitive phenotypes. The studies are homozygous or null and give no evidence of dominant or haploinsufficiency effects.
- Consistency with human phenotype: partial. The neural wiring and CNS phenotypes are plausibly relevant to neurodevelopment. The retinal degeneration and the lack of epilepsy, ID or cardiac findings do not match the human case.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED to MODERATE for a role in neural development. For a heterozygous neurodevelopmental disorder it is LIMITED.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Function: MSI1 binds mRNA and controls translation and splicing of its targets. Examples are Robo3 in neurons (PMID: 20696379), ALDOA 5'UTR exon inclusion that raises mTOR activity in breast cancer (PMID: 39164523), and binding of pri-miR-147 to block its maturation (PMID: 36291177).
- Expression and cell biology: it marks cycling intestinal stem cells that are radioresistant (PMID: 32726617). It is overexpressed in Paget's disease, where it drives mTOR signaling (PMID: 32457396). It is highly expressed in glioblastoma, and ribosome profiling after knockdown in U251 cells was used to identify translational targets (ASHG 2012 - Simplified sample preparation and streamlined library preparation method for deep sequencing Ribosome Protected mRNA Fragments).
- Neural relevance: the mouse data above, plus the known role of MSI1 in neural stem cells. The supplied evidence does not include direct brain expression data.
- Non-orthologous studies: the yeast (PMID: 2554329, PMID: 11238915) and Arabidopsis/potato (PMID: 34807487, PMID: 19825648, PMID: 31427464) MSI1 papers concern different genes and should be disregarded.
- Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED to MODERATE. The mechanisms are well characterized but come mostly from cancer and non-neurodevelopmental contexts.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT.
- Strengths: well-defined RNA-binding function; mouse and zebrafish data showing roles in neural wiring and CNS formation (PMID: 20696379, PMID: 22429745); a de novo 12q24.23q24.31 deletion that includes MSI1 in a patient with a neurodevelopmental phenotype (PMID: 40018479).
- Gaps:
  - No patients with single-gene MSI1 variants.
  - The only human case is a 122-gene deletion.
  - No segregation or functional data for human variants.
  - Model phenotypes are mainly retinal and come from homozygous or double-paralog knockouts, with no heterozygous data.
  - Much of the "MSI1" literature concerns unrelated yeast and plant genes.
- Conclusion: There is currently no gene-specific clinical evidence linking heterozygous MSI1 variants to a neurodevelopmental disorder. The single contiguous-gene deletion case does not implicate MSI1 specifically, and the supportive model organism data show roles in neural development but not a dominant disease mechanism. MSI1 should be regarded as a candidate gene only, and additional patients with intragenic LoF or missense variants would be needed to establish an association.
