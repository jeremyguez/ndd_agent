XPO1 (exportin-1, also called CRM1) is the main nuclear export receptor. It binds leucine-rich nuclear export signal (NES) cargo, including proteins and ribonucleoprotein complexes, in a Ran-GTP-dependent manner and carries it through the nuclear pore complex to the cytoplasm. It lies within the minimal deleted region of the 2p15p16.1 microdeletion syndrome and has recently been proposed as a dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene.

1. GENE SUMMARY
XPO1 encodes the nuclear export receptor exportin-1/CRM1, which exports NES-bearing cargo (proteins, RNA-protein complexes) from the nucleus. Heterozygous deletion of XPO1 is implicated in 2p15p16.1 microdeletion syndrome, and de novo XPO1 variants have recently been reported in a neurodevelopmental disorder.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: 4 sources were found.
  - One variant-level case series: 22 individuals (PMID: 40819229).
  - Two microdeletion abstracts (ASHG 2015 - Molecular and clinical delineation of the 2p15-16.1 microdeletion syndrome; ASHG 2016 - Driver genes for 2p15p16.1 microdeletion syndrome identified using clinical, genomic and functional analysis).
  - One exome-based CAKUT study (PMID: 40774958).
- Patient numbers: about 22 individuals with de novo sequence variants. In addition, about 2 patients carry a 105 kb deletion involving only XPO1 and the distal end of USP34 (ASHG 2015), and 8 patients carry larger 2p15p16.1 deletions (ASHG 2016). The CAKUT study gave no patient count. The total is roughly 32 patients, and the deletion cases are not specific to XPO1.
- Phenotypes:
  - Sequence-variant cohort: a neurodevelopmental disorder with an "overlapping phenotype", but the abstract gives no details (PMID: 40819229).
  - Deletion cases: intellectual disability/cognitive impairment, microcephaly, hypotonia, growth delay, dysmorphic/craniofacial features, digital anomalies, and brain structural abnormalities (ASHG 2015; ASHG 2016).
  - CAKUT study: kidney and urinary tract malformations (PMID: 40774958).
- Variant types: In the 22-person series, about 11 have putative LoF variants (nonsense, frameshift, splice), 10 have missense variants, and 1 has an in-frame deletion. All are de novo. LoF and missense variants are therefore roughly balanced. Specific variant notations are not available in the abstract. The deletion cases are copy-number losses.
- Known cohorts: none of UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL is named. Cases were assembled through matchmaking (PMID: 40819229). The deletion cases come from France and from Canada/USA/UK collaborations.
- Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE. A 22-person de novo series with a LoF/missense mix is notable. However, only an abstract was available, with no phenotype details, variant notations, or statistical enrichment data. The deletion data are confounded by co-deleted genes (USP34, BCL11A, REL).

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms:
  - Drosophila (PMID: 40819229).
  - C. elegans (ASHG 2011 - Functional characterization of the 2p15-16 microdeletion).
  - Zebrafish (ASHG 2016 - Driver genes for 2p15p16.1 microdeletion syndrome identified using clinical, genomic and functional analysis).
  - Mouse: cultured neurons and pharmacological CRM1 inhibition only (PMID: 25706475).
  - Yeast: mechanistic work only.
- Key phenotypes:
  - Drosophila: ubiquitous and neuron-specific XPO1 knockdown impaired development, and GABAergic neuron-specific knockdown impaired habituation learning (PMID: 40819229).
  - Zebrafish: xpo1a knockdown caused microcephaly, a dysmorphic body, growth defects and brain abnormalities (ASHG 2016).
  - C. elegans: RNAi caused early embryonic lethality (ASHG 2011), which indicates an essential gene.
  - Mouse: Xpo1 knockdown in cultured neurons was neuroprotective, and pharmacological inhibitors were used in vivo (PMID: 25706475). No mouse knockout, Mendelian ratio or viability data are available, so this is not informative for neurodevelopment.
- Consistency with human phenotype: reasonably consistent. Microcephaly, growth defects and brain abnormalities in zebrafish, and learning and developmental defects in flies, parallel the human neurodevelopmental features. These are knockdown models only, with no patient-variant modelling.
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE. Several organisms show relevant phenotypes, but all are knockdown-based, and there is no mammalian genetic model and no functional testing of missense variants.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function:
  - XPO1 mediates Ran-GTP-dependent nuclear export of NES cargo, including rpS5, mutant NPM1c (PMID: 30205049), and R-loops through a DDX1 complex (PMID: 42303864).
  - It also acts as an adaptor docking transcription factors and chromatin at the nuclear pore (yeast Crm1/Xpo1-Gcn4-Nup2; PMID: 40068679).
  - Nup98 fusion proteins negatively regulate XPO1-mediated export (PMID: 28392395).
  - Nucleocytoplasmic transport defects, including XPO1 mislocalization, occur in SOD1-ALS (PMID: 41691309).
- Expression: XPO1 is ubiquitously expressed. XPO1::GFP is expressed throughout development in C. elegans, and the gene is expressed in adult mouse brain, gut and lung. In human fetal brain, expression is mild in immature ependyma and Cajal-Retzius cells (ASHG 2011 - Functional characterization of the 2p15-16 microdeletion). The same abstract reported no copy-number-related reduction of XPO1 expression in patient samples.
- Key findings:
  - XPO1 is dosage sensitive. Patient lymphoblastoid lines carrying a deletion showed reduced protein and nuclear accumulation of rpS5 (ASHG 2016).
  - Much of the remaining literature concerns cancer, where XPO1 inhibitors (KPT-330, eltanexor, KPT-335) are used, for example in DEK::NUP214 leukemia (PMID: 40148556) and canine lymphoma (PMID: 40872651). This is of limited relevance to neurodevelopment.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE. Function is well established, and there is evidence of dosage sensitivity. Brain-specific, developmental and variant-level functional data are lacking.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: MODERATE (trending toward strong if the 22-person cohort is confirmed in full publication).
- Key strengths:
  - A cohort of 22 individuals with de novo LoF and missense/in-frame variants (PMID: 40819229).
  - Convergent support from the 2p15p16.1 microdeletion literature, including small deletions limited to XPO1 and USP34 (ASHG 2015).
  - Concordant fly and zebrafish knockdown phenotypes.
  - Evidence of dosage sensitivity.
  - An essential, highly conserved function.
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - Phenotype and variant details are absent from the available abstracts.
  - There are no named cohorts and no statistical enrichment data.
  - The deletion cases are confounded by neighbouring genes.
  - There are no mouse knockout data and no functional assays of patient missense variants.
  - The CAKUT association (PMID: 40774958) lacks case-level data.
  - Most biological literature is cancer-focused.
  - The mechanism (haploinsufficiency vs. dominant-negative or gain-of-function for the missense variants) is unresolved.
- Conclusion: XPO1 has moderate support as an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene. De novo LoF and missense variants in 22 individuals, together with microdeletion and animal knockdown data, point to haploinsufficiency as a likely mechanism. Full phenotypic characterization, variant-level functional validation and independent replication are needed to firm up the gene-disease relationship.
