MIDEAS (Mitotic deacetylase-associated SANT domain protein; also ELM2 and SANT domain-containing protein 1, ELMSAN1) is a scaffold and corepressor subunit of the MiDAC histone deacetylase complex. MiDAC also contains HDAC1/2 and DNTTIP1, and it regulates chromatin acetylation and gene expression, including a neurodevelopmental program (PMID: 25653165; PMID: 32297854).

1. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: one (PMID: 41290615). It describes the same de novo heterozygous missense variant, p.Tyr654Ser, in two unrelated individuals.
- Total patients: about 2.
- Most common phenotypes: delayed speech development, joint contractures, dysmorphic features, and gut dysmotility. The authors describe a multisystem neurodevelopmental syndrome. The abstract gives no further detail, such as intellectual disability severity, seizures, or brain imaging.
- Variant type: missense only. No loss-of-function variants were reported. The variant lies in a conserved auto-inhibitory loop covering the HDAC active site, and the proposed mechanism is a gain of function, with increased MiDAC deacetylase activity.
- Cohorts: no UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL affiliation is stated. Center and country were not reported in the extracted data.
- Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED. The recurrent de novo variant in two unrelated individuals, together with structural and functional support, is notable. However, only two patients are reported, from a single study, with a single variant.

2. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organism: mouse only. The full knockout is embryonic lethal in late gestation (PMID: 32591534). Mendelian ratio data and sample sizes were not provided.
- Key phenotypes: heart malformation, haematopoietic failure, and perturbed embryonic gene expression. The phenotype is identical to that of the Dnttip1 knockout, which supports a shared complex function (PMID: 32591534).
- Consistency with human phenotype: only partial. Complete loss causes severe, lethal, multisystem developmental failure. This is not directly comparable to the human syndrome, which involves a proposed gain-of-function missense variant in surviving patients. Heterozygous knockout phenotypes were not described, and neurological phenotypes were not reported in mice.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED to MODERATE. It shows that MIDEAS is essential for development, but it has little neurodevelopmental or heterozygous relevance. I rate it LIMITED for this specific disease hypothesis.

3. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function: MIDEAS is a corepressor in the MiDAC complex with HDAC1/2 and DNTTIP1. The complex is upregulated in mitosis and associated with cyclin A. DNTTIP1 dimerization mediates assembly of the HDAC1:MIDEAS complex, and its C-terminal domain binds DNA and nucleosomes (PMID: 25653165). CryoEM shows four HDAC1 copies at the periphery of the complex with outward-facing active sites (PMID: 32591534). Structure-guided HDAC1 surface mutations (Y48E, E63R) disrupt MiDAC or NuRD assembly and alter gene expression and histone acetylation (PMID: 40966515). MiDAC is also important for chromosome alignment in mitosis in cancer cell lines (PMID: 32591534).
- Neurodevelopmental relevance: MiDAC regulates a neurodevelopmental gene expression program. It upregulates SLIT3 and NTN1, probably by removing H4K20ac, and it represses negative regulators of neurogenesis by reducing H3K27ac. Loss of MiDAC causes neurite outgrowth defects that SLIT3 and/or NTN1 supplementation rescues (PMID: 32297854). Specific brain expression data were not provided in the extracted evidence.
- Patient-derived data: fibroblasts from patients carrying p.Tyr654Ser show gene expression changes reciprocal to those seen after rapid MiDAC degradation. This is consistent with increased complex activity (PMID: 41290615).
- Other findings of limited relevance: MiDAC is involved in HIV core promoter regulation (PMID: 38781120). MIDEAS-AS1 is a lncRNA, not the protein, and it is relevant to breast cancer (PMID: 37770991). MIDEAS is among differentially methylated genes in Alzheimer's disease blood datasets (PMID: 40343304). None of these supports a neurodevelopmental disorder.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE. Mechanistic and structural data are good, and the neurite outgrowth data are relevant. However, there is no in vivo neuronal validation of the variant.

4. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: LIMITED
- Key strengths:
  - A recurrent de novo missense variant in two unrelated patients with overlapping multisystem neurodevelopmental features.
  - A structurally plausible mechanism, with the variant in an auto-inhibitory loop, and supportive patient fibroblast data.
  - Mouse knockout lethality showing that the gene is essential.
  - MiDAC's role in neurodevelopmental gene regulation and neurite outgrowth.
- Key gaps:
  - Only two patients and one variant, and no loss-of-function cases.
  - Limited phenotypic detail in the available data.
  - No cohort provenance.
  - No heterozygous mouse or neuronal model of the variant.
  - No independent replication.
  - Uncertainty about the disease mechanism: the proposed gain of function conflicts with the loss-of-function lethality in mice, so the relationship between dosage and phenotype remains unclear.

Conclusion: MIDEAS has preliminary but credible evidence as a dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene, resting on a recurrent de novo p.Tyr654Ser variant that is proposed to increase MiDAC deacetylase activity (PMID: 41290615). Support from mouse lethality (PMID: 32591534) and neurodevelopmental gene regulation (PMID: 32297854) is consistent with this, but it does not independently establish the disease association. Additional unrelated cases, including other variant types, and in vivo functional studies are needed before the gene-disease relationship can be considered moderate or strong.

### Clinical Evidence
1. A de novo missense variant in MIDEAS results in increased deacetylase activity of the MiDAC HDAC complex causing a neurodevelopmental syndrome (PMID: 41290615)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. Bantscheff/Millard et al. 2020 - The MiDAC histone deacetylase complex is essential for embryonic development and has a unique multivalent structure (PMID: 32591534)

### Biological Evidence
1. 2015 - Structural and functional characterization of a cell cycle associated HDAC1/2 complex reveals the structural basis for complex assembly and nucleosome targeting (PMID: 25653165)
2. 2020 - The histone deacetylase complex MiDAC regulates a neurodevelopmental gene expression program to control neurite outgrowth (PMID: 32297854)
3. 2025 - Mutations on the surface of HDAC1 reveal molecular determinants of specific complex assembly and their requirement for gene regulation (PMID: 40966515)
4. 2024 - Mitotic deacetylase complex (MiDAC) recognizes the HIV-1 core promoter to control activated viral gene expression (PMID: 38781120)
