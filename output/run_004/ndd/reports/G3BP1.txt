G3BP1 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
G3BP1 (Ras-GTPase-activating protein SH3-domain-binding protein 1) is an RNA-binding protein and a core nucleator of stress granules (SGs). It acts through liquid-liquid phase separation with RNA, Caprin-1 and USP10. It also takes part in mTORC1 regulation, innate immune sensing and RNA decay.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: Two were found. One is a large NDD cohort de novo variant (DNV) enrichment analysis (PMID: 35977029). The other is a single-patient report with a cardiac phenotype (PMID: 42107065). No dedicated NDD case reports or case series were identified.
- Patients: The exact number is not stated for the NDD study. It reported a nominally significant excess of G3BP1 de novo variants among 40,853 NDD individuals, with no specific variants or counts in the abstract. The other report describes 1 patient with a de novo missense variant, p.E411G.
- Phenotypes: The NDD cohort gives only a generic "neurodevelopmental disorder" label. The single case had dilated cardiomyopathy and heart failure, with no neurodevelopmental phenotype described.
- Variant type: This cannot be determined for the NDD cohort. The one described variant is missense (p.E411G), reported to disrupt succinylation at K413 and drive excess PI3K-AKT-mTOR signaling.
- Cohorts: No UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL membership is reported in the extracted data. The NDD study was a large meta-analysis-type cohort, and its specific source cohorts were not detailed here.
- Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED. The DNV excess is only nominally significant and has no phenotypic detail. The only variant-level case has a cardiac rather than neurodevelopmental phenotype.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: Zebrafish and mouse.
- Zebrafish: g3bp1 knockdown disturbed neuronal development and function, with white matter heterotopia, neuronal hyperactivity and phenotypes consistent with mTORC1 hyperactivity (PMID: 33497611).
- Mouse: Hepatocyte-specific G3BP1 conditional knockout worsened MASLD/MASH in males, with impaired autophagosome-lysosome fusion, increased de novo lipogenesis and impaired TFE3 nuclear translocation (PMID: 40813380). Cell-based G3BP1 deficiency reduced cGAS-dependent IFN production. The G3BP1 inhibitor EGCG, a pharmacological rather than genetic manipulation, blunted autoinflammation in an Aicardi-Goutières syndrome model (PMID: 30510222).
- Not reported: Mendelian ratios, lethality, fertility and sample sizes. No constitutive or brain-specific mouse knockout phenotype is summarized.
- Consistency with human phenotype: The zebrafish neuronal findings are loosely consistent with an NDD. The mTORC1 hyperactivity theme also parallels the mTOR mechanism proposed in the cardiac case. The mouse data concern liver and immune phenotypes, which are not directly relevant to NDD.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED. The only neurodevelopmental data come from zebrafish knockdown, which is not a stable germline model.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function:
  - SG nucleation and dynamics, regulated by ubiquitination. TRIM21 adds K63-linked ubiquitin, which inhibits G3BP1 phase separation (PMID: 36692217). USP10, DCAF7 and hnRNPA2B1 affect G3BP1 stability and SG disassembly (PMID: 38973296; PMID: 38363675).
  - Tethering of the TSC complex to lysosomes, which suppresses mTORC1 (PMID: 33497611).
  - Enhancement of cGAS-DNA binding (PMID: 30510222).
  - Dampening of excessive RLR/dsRNA innate immune responses (PMID: 37028415).
  - Structure-mediated RNA decay with UPF1 (PMID: 32017897).
  - Modulation of the CUL3-SPOP ligase (PMID: 35252555).
- Expression: No expression data were provided in the extracted evidence. Brain and neurodevelopmental expression is not characterized here.
- Key experimental findings:
  - G3BP1 is an essential gene in the core SG interaction network. NDD-associated de novo variants in SG-network genes, tested collectively rather than for G3BP1 specifically, disrupted network interactions and SG formation (PMID: 35977029).
  - G3BP1 loss causes mTORC1 hyperactivity (PMID: 33497611). Dysregulated mTOR signaling is a recognized NDD mechanism, which makes this plausible but indirect.
  - Small-molecule SG inhibitors bind a G3BP1/2 pocket and dissolve SGs (PMID: 38284934).
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for G3BP1 function and for plausibility in neurodevelopment through SGs and mTORC1. No variant-specific functional data exist for NDD-associated variants.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: LIMITED
- Key strengths:
  - A nominally significant DNV excess in a very large NDD cohort.
  - Well-characterized roles in SG biology and mTORC1 regulation.
  - Zebrafish neuronal phenotypes.
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - No phenotyped NDD patients or specific variants are described.
  - The DNV signal is only nominal.
  - The only variant-level case is cardiac.
  - There is no mouse neurological phenotype.
  - There are no functional studies of patient variants in an NDD context.
  - The mechanism (LoF vs missense or dominant-negative) is unresolved.
  - Segregation, recurrence and cohort-overlap data are missing.

Conclusion: G3BP1 is a biologically plausible NDD candidate gene, supported by a nominal de novo variant excess, a role in SG and mTORC1 pathways, and zebrafish neuronal phenotypes. However, there are no detailed clinical case series establishing a consistent neurodevelopmental syndrome. Additional unrelated patients with de novo variants, detailed phenotyping and variant-level functional studies are needed before G3BP1 can be considered an established autosomal dominant NDD gene.
