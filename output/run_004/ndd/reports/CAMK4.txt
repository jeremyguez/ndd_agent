CAMK4 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER, HETEROZYGOUS (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
CAMK4 encodes calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase IV, a serine/threonine kinase. It acts downstream of CAMKK2 and phosphorylates CREB (Ser133) and other substrates, linking calcium signaling to transcription. It is expressed in neurons (including cerebellar Purkinje cells), T cells, and the testis. Its C-terminal regulatory domain controls kinase activity.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: Six publications mention CAMK4. Only about three to four describe a sequence-level CAMK4 variant in a neurodevelopmental or movement phenotype, and these appear to overlap or be derivative of each other.
- Total patients: The count is small. The index case is a single de novo patient (PMID: 30262571). A later report describes "clustering CAMK4 variants" in a neurodevelopmental disorder with dystonia and chorea (PMID: 33211350). Its patient count and variant details were not available in the extracted text. A single-case hyperkinetic movement disorder report (PMID: 37070062) and a dystonia with anarthria/aphonia report (PMID: 42817183) may refer to previously published cases, so the number of unique patients may be fewer than the number of reports. Two further patients carry large contiguous deletions. A realistic estimate is about 1 to 2 patients with fully documented single-nucleotide variants, plus an unspecified number in the clustering series.
- Variant types:
  - The best-characterized variant is c.981+1G>A (PMID: 30262571). It is a de novo essential splice-site change that produces a stable transcript with a premature stop codon in the last exon. It loses the C-terminal regulatory domain (p.Lys303Serfs*28). Patient-derived cells showed increased CaMKIV activity, so the mechanism is gain of function despite the nominal "LoF" label.
  - The clustering variants (PMID: 33211350) are inferred to be missense, but this is not confirmed in the text.
  - Other reports involve CNVs: a rare CAMK4-containing CNV classified as a VUS in an autism cohort (PMID: 34664255), and two de novo 5q deletions of 15.4 Mb and 9.8 Mb that include both CAMK4 and APC (PMID: 39780213). These patients had mild intellectual disability and polyposis. CAMK4 is one of 22 genes in the narrowed common region, and causality is not established.
- Common phenotypes: Intellectual disability and developmental delay, autism, ataxia, and hyperkinetic movement disorders (dystonia, chorea). The index patient had severe generalized dystonia. One report describes dystonia with anarthria/aphonia and developmental and epileptic encephalopathy (PMID: 42817183).
- Cohorts: No UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL cohort membership is identified. The center information available is Toronto (PMID: 42817183) and a Brazilian ASD cohort (PMID: 34664255).
- Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED. There is a consistent phenotype (neurodevelopmental disorder with dystonia/chorea) and a mechanistically supported de novo variant, but few unique patients, scarce variant detail in the extracted data, and a likely gain-of-function mechanism confined to a narrow variant class.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: Mouse only. Models are mainly full Camk4 knockouts, plus macrophage-specific and podocyte-specific conditional knockouts.
- Neurological phenotypes: Camk4-/- mice show locomotor defects consistent with altered cerebellar function. They have normal overall cerebellar cytoarchitecture but fewer mature Purkinje neurons, reduced calbindin D28k and CREB Ser133 phosphorylation, and immature Purkinje cell synapses (multiple climbing fiber innervation, enhanced parallel fiber currents) (PMID: 11069976). Knockout also disrupts transferrin turnover and iron homeostasis in cerebellum and liver (PMID: 32460794).
- Other phenotypes: Male infertility with impaired spermiogenesis (PMID: 10932193); hematopoietic stem cell failure (PMID: 16020540); fewer dendritic cells (PMID: 17909078); impaired osteoclast differentiation (PMID: 17128269); reduced Th17 and autoimmune disease (PMID: 24667640); reduced psoriasis (PMID: 35869084); smaller, more stable atherosclerotic lesions (PMID: 40336480).
- Lethality and Mendelian ratios: All knockouts are viable. No Mendelian ratio deviations or sample sizes were reported.
- Consistency with human disease: Partial. The cerebellar and locomotor defects are loosely consistent with ataxia and motor features. However, the human disease appears to be driven by gain of function (loss of the regulatory domain), whereas these are loss-of-function models. No heterozygous or truncation/gain-of-function knock-in model has been reported, and no models show a clear intellectual disability or dystonia phenotype.
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE for a role in cerebellar and neuronal function. It is LIMITED for directly modeling the human gain-of-function disorder.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways: CAMKK2 → CAMK4 → CREB is the core pathway (PMID: 39076120; PMID: 32460794). CAMK4 also acts through AKT/mTOR to promote Th17 expansion (PMID: 32738170), and through NLRP3 inflammasome activation (PMID: 35734175).
- Expression: CAMK4 overlaps with CAMKK2 in mouse brain (PMID: 32460794). It is expressed in cerebellar Purkinje neurons (PMID: 11069976). It is upregulated in psoriatic skin, atherosclerotic plaque macrophages, and anti-PLA2R-stimulated podocytes (PMID: 35869084; PMID: 40336480; PMID: 35333675).
- Key findings: In CRISPR-edited HEK293 cells, CAMKK2-CAMK4 deficiency altered Ca2+ homeostasis and transferrin/TFRC interactions (PMID: 32460794). CAMK4 inhibition or knockdown reduces endothelial angiogenic potential (PMID: 34586897). It regulates cryptococcal uptake in an siRNA screen of 868 genes with 79 hits (PMID: 28970273). A common variant, rs10491334, was associated with hypertension in a Uygur population (OR 2.200, 95% CI 1.473-3.285, P<0.001; 1224 cases, 967 controls) (PMID: 26909912). Patient-derived cells carrying the c.981+1G>A variant show increased CaMKIV activity (PMID: 30262571).
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE. The kinase function and pathway are well established, and there is a direct functional readout in patient cells. Most other work is in non-neuronal contexts and does not address neurodevelopmental mechanisms.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: LIMITED (low end of the range), with an emerging gain-of-function mechanism.
- Key strengths: A de novo variant in a trio with functional validation showing increased kinase activity (PMID: 30262571); a recurring phenotype of dystonia, chorea, and neurodevelopmental disorder across multiple reports (PMID: 33211350; PMID: 37070062; PMID: 42817183); well-characterized cerebellar and Purkinje cell phenotypes in knockout mice (PMID: 11069976); and a clear biological pathway.
- Key gaps: Very few unique patients, and some reports are likely overlapping; variant details and counts for the clustering series are unavailable; CNV cases are confounded by multiple genes (including APC) and are VUS-level; no replicated functional data for other variants; no mouse model of the proposed gain-of-function mechanism; no cohort-level (DDD, UDN, etc.) or statistical burden evidence; and the mechanism for missense variants is unclear.
- Conclusion: CAMK4 is an emerging candidate gene for an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder with dystonia and chorea. The mechanism is probably gain of function through loss of the C-terminal regulatory domain, not simple haploinsufficiency. The evidence rests on a small number of de novo cases with supportive functional data and biologically plausible pathways. It is currently limited, and additional unrelated patients with defined variants and functional studies are needed to establish the gene-disease relationship.
