NCL (nucleolin) literature evidence report: NCL as a candidate heterozygous (autosomal dominant) neurodevelopmental disorder gene

1. GENE SUMMARY
NCL encodes nucleolin, an abundant multifunctional nucleolar phosphoprotein that shuttles between the nucleus, cytoplasm and cell surface. It is involved in rRNA transcription and ribosome biogenesis, and it binds and stabilizes mRNAs. In the evidence supplied, it also acts as a cell-surface receptor (for example, for midkine) and as an aptamer target (AS1411).

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent case reports or case series: 0 were found.
- Total patients: 0.
- Phenotypes: none reported.
- Variant type: no heterozygous variants of any type (LoF, missense or other) have been reported.
- Known cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE. There is no human genetic evidence linking NCL variants to a neurodevelopmental disorder.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- The extracted model organism evidence was empty. A single zebrafish study is classed as functional and biological evidence (see section 4), and it is the only organism-level data available.
- That study reports ncl-/- zebrafish with craniofacial anomalies, reduced rRNA synthesis and p53-dependent apoptosis (PMID: 35762670). Loss of NCL shortens fgf8a mRNA half-life and disrupts Fgf signaling, Sox9a-mediated chondrogenesis and Runx2-mediated osteogenesis. Exogenous FGF8 rescued the phenotype.
- Neurological phenotypes, lethality and Mendelian ratios were not described in the supplied data. No mouse data were provided.
- Consistency with human phenotype: it cannot be assessed, because there is no human phenotype. Craniofacial anomalies are a recognized feature of some neurodevelopmental syndromes, but this is only a loose parallel. The zebrafish phenotype was in homozygous knockouts, so it does not directly model a heterozygous state.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED (a single zebrafish study, with no neurodevelopmental readouts).

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Molecular function and pathways:
  - NCL stabilizes mRNAs, including fgf8a in zebrafish (PMID: 35762670) and Sp1 mRNA in cell culture (PMID: 36575197). In the latter study, miR-494 targets the NCL 3'-UTR (PMID: 36575197).
  - NCL forms a complex with MDM2 (PMID: 38657608).
  - NCL forms phase-separated nuclear condensates that support CD44 transcription (PMID: 41120080).
  - NCL acts as a midkine receptor (MDK-NCL signaling) in tumor microenvironments, in endometrial carcinoma (PMID: 37081869), colorectal cancer (PMID: 41246334) and lung adenocarcinoma (PMID: 40396179).
  - NCL has been placed in an NCL/miR-221/NF-κB/DNMT1 axis in AML (PMID: 29554483).
- Expression:
  - NCL is upregulated in lung adenocarcinoma tumors, at both mRNA and protein level (PMID: 40396179).
  - NCL protein is elevated in PBMCs of prostate cancer patients (mean 626.1 vs 198.5 pg/mL; 158 individuals) (PMID: 33676639).
  - No brain or neurodevelopmental expression data were provided.
- Genetic association: an eQTL links a heart rate-associated variant to NCL transcript levels in blood (p<1.6x10^-3) (ASHG 2011 - Genome-wide association analyses in up to 181,171 individuals reveal 14 new loci associated with heart rate). This is not relevant to neurodevelopment.
- Key points: nearly all of the evidence comes from oncology and cell biology. The zebrafish study is the only developmental one, and it shows a role in craniofacial development.
- Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED for a neurodevelopmental disorder. The gene's function is well established, but there is no neurodevelopmental-specific data.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT.
- Key strengths: NCL has well-characterized roles in ribosome biogenesis and mRNA stabilization. Zebrafish loss of function produces a developmental phenotype (craniofacial) through a defined mechanism (PMID: 35762670).
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - There are no human cases, no variant data and no cohort evidence.
  - There are no mouse or other model organism data, and no heterozygous or neurological phenotyping.
  - There are no brain expression or neuronal functional data.
  - Most biological studies concern cancer and are not informative for neurodevelopment.
  - Because NCL is essential for cell viability and ribosome biogenesis, it may be intolerant to variation. That is speculation and would need to be tested with constraint and genetic data.
- Conclusion: there is currently no clinical or genetic evidence that heterozygous NCL variants cause a neurodevelopmental disorder. The available biological and zebrafish data show an important developmental role but do not support a disease association. NCL should be considered an unsupported candidate until de novo or otherwise segregating variants are reported in affected individuals.
