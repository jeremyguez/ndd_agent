GENE EVIDENCE REPORT: ARPC4-TTLL3 (neurodevelopmental disorder, heterozygous/autosomal dominant hypothesis)

1. GENE SUMMARY
ARPC4-TTLL3 is a read-through locus. It joins the neighboring genes ARPC4 (actin related protein 2/3 complex subunit 4, a component of the Arp2/3 actin-nucleation complex) and TTLL3 (tubulin tyrosine ligase-like 3, a tubulin glycylase). Its own distinct function is not established. Any function is presumably inherited from the parent genes, and the read-through transcript is not an established disease gene.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent case reports/case series: 0 found.
- Total patients: 0.
- Phenotypes: none reported.
- Variant type: no variants reported, so no LoF versus missense pattern can be assessed.
- Known cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE.
Because this is a read-through locus that overlaps ARPC4 and TTLL3, variants in the region would be attributed to the parent genes in most clinical pipelines. This may explain the absence of ARPC4-TTLL3-specific reports.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms studied: none identified for this locus.
- Knockout phenotypes (neurological, lethality, Mendelian ratio deviations): no data.
- Consistency with human phenotype: cannot be assessed.
- Strength of model organism evidence: NONE.
Any model organism data would need to come from the individual orthologs of ARPC4 and TTLL3. Those data were not part of the evidence supplied here and are not evaluated.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and molecular function: no function specific to the read-through transcript is described in the evidence provided. By inference, it relates to Arp2/3-mediated actin branching (ARPC4) and tubulin glycylation (TTLL3).
- Expression pattern: no brain or neurodevelopmental expression data are provided.
- Key experimental findings: one study, a systematic meta-analysis with a multi-source integrated network, identified ARPC4-TTLL3 as one of five novel osteoarthritis-associated proteins (PMID: 35457215). It was ranked within a network of 1689 genes built from differentially expressed mRNAs and miRNAs, miRNA targets and SNP datasets. Mass spectrometry of superficial, middle and deep zones of human osteoarthritic cartilage versus normal controls confirmed it at the protein level, alongside PPIB, ASS1, LHDB and TPI1. The abstract gives no direction of change and no mechanism. The finding concerns cartilage and osteoarthritis, not the nervous system.
- Strength of biological evidence for a neurodevelopmental disorder: LIMITED. The evidence is a single association-type proteomic and network observation in an unrelated tissue and disease.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT.
- Key strengths: the only finding is a protein-level confirmation in human cartilage by mass spectrometry, supported by multi-source network prioritization (PMID: 35457215).
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - There are no clinical cases, no variant data and no segregation or de novo evidence.
  - There are no model organism data.
  - There is no neurodevelopmental expression or functional data.
  - The only biological evidence concerns osteoarthritis and lacks directionality or mechanism.
  - The read-through nature of the locus complicates attribution of any variant to this gene rather than to ARPC4 or TTLL3.

Conclusion: There is currently no evidence supporting ARPC4-TTLL3 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous pathogenic variants. The sole available finding is an osteoarthritis-related proteomic association in cartilage (PMID: 35457215), which is not relevant to neurodevelopment. Any future investigation should focus on the parent genes ARPC4 and TTLL3 and on whether variants affect the read-through transcript specifically.
