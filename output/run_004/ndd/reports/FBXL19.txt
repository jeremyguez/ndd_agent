FBXL19 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
FBXL19 (CXXC11) encodes a 674-aa F-box/leucine-rich repeat protein at 16p11.2. It carries a CxxC zinc finger domain and is paralogous to FBXL10 and FBXL11 (PMID: 15547684). Its best-characterized function is binding CpG islands in ES cells and recruiting CDK-Mediator to developmental gene promoters, which primes them for activation during lineage commitment (PMID: 29809150).

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: only one was found, a conference abstract (ASHG 2018 - Systematic scoring of candidate genes for neurodevelopmental disorders has a higher yield than large meta-analyses). It lists FBXL19 among the top-scoring candidate genes (top 10% of a 13-parameter score) in trio exome sequencing of undiagnosed NDD cases.
- Patient numbers: not specified. No individual patient, variant, inheritance or phenotype-level data are given beyond the general label "neurodevelopmental disorder."
- Phenotypes: no detail beyond NDD.
- Variant type: not reported, so no LoF versus missense assessment is possible.
- Cohorts: German centers (Leipzig, with exome sequencing by Centogene and CeGaT). None of the named cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL) is indicated.
- Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED, bordering on NONE. The single abstract is a candidate-gene prioritization without variant-level data, and there is no segregation, de novo confirmation, or phenotypic series.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organism: mouse only.
- Constitutive loss: CpG island recognition by FBXL19 through its CxxC domain is reported to be essential for mouse development, with an embryonic lethal outcome (PMID: 29809150). The stage of failure, Mendelian ratios and neurological phenotypes are not provided. In ES cells, loss of CpG island binding impairs CDK-Mediator recruitment and priming of developmental genes.
- Brain-specific manipulation: PVN-targeted FBXL19 knockdown in naive mice mimicked chronic stress and produced depression-like/neuropsychiatric behaviors. FBXL19 knockout aggravated stress-induced symptoms, and overexpression reversed them (PMID: 41028568). The mechanism involves the USP14-CBP-CREB axis and CRH biosynthesis in the HPA axis.
- Consistency with human phenotype: this is only partly informative. Embryonic lethality suggests an essential developmental role, but heterozygous phenotypes are not described. The stress and behavior data are adult-onset neuropsychiatric findings, not a neurodevelopmental model.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED to MODERATE. Essentiality is supported, but there is no characterization of a heterozygous or neurodevelopmental phenotype.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Molecular function: an epigenetic/chromatin-associated CpG island reader that links to CDK-Mediator and transcriptional priming of developmental genes (PMID: 29809150). Human and mouse proteins are 97.5% identical (PMID: 15547684). It is also implicated in ubiquitin-mediated regulation of CBP (PMID: 41028568).
- Expression: no brain or neurodevelopmental expression data are in the provided evidence. A GR-dependent eQTL study reported cortisol-responsive FBXL19 expression linked to obesity SNPs (ASHG 2022 - Glucocorticoids unmask silent non-coding genetic risk variants for common diseases).
- Other associations: these are largely non-neurodevelopmental. They include psoriasis GWAS support (ASHG 2011 - Meta-analysis of genome-wide association studies identifies additional susceptibility loci for psoriasis; 4011 cases, 7721 controls, p~8x10-7), innate immune/NF-κB pathway placement (PMID: 23369832), and endothelial antiviral protection in influenza (PMID: 39150394). Several papers concern the antisense lncRNA FBXL19-AS1 in cancer and pancreatitis, not the protein, and are not relevant to NDD.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for a plausible role in developmental gene regulation, but LIMITED specifically for neurodevelopment.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT (at most LIMITED) for FBXL19 as an autosomal dominant NDD gene.
- Strengths: a biologically plausible developmental chromatin/transcriptional regulatory role, mouse essentiality (embryonic lethality), and a brain-relevant mouse stress/behavior phenotype. Candidate-gene prioritization in an NDD exome cohort adds only weak support.
- Gaps: no published case reports or series with variant-level data, no de novo or segregation evidence, no defined phenotype, and no information on variant type or mechanism (haploinsufficiency versus other). Mouse data do not describe heterozygous or neurodevelopmental phenotypes, and there are no brain expression data. Many associations (psoriasis, immunity, cancer lncRNA) are unrelated to NDD.
- Conclusion: FBXL19 is a plausible but unproven NDD candidate gene. Current evidence rests on a single non-detailed conference abstract and general developmental biology. Additional patients with well-phenotyped, preferably de novo, variants and functional validation are needed before gene-disease validity can be established.
