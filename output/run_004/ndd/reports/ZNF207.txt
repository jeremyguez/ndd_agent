ZNF207 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
ZNF207 (also called BuGZ) encodes a microtubule-associated zinc finger protein. It stabilizes Bub3 and promotes its loading onto kinetochores, which supports mitotic chromosome alignment (PMID: 24462186). It also has reported roles in pluripotency and in splicing (U1 snRNP-associated).

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: One case series was found (PMID: 29130599). It describes three unrelated patients with de novo 17q11.2 interstitial microdeletions.
- Total patients: About 3.
- Common phenotypes: Developmental delay and intellectual disability (mild to severe), hypotonia (significant in two patients), excessive fatigue with daytime somnolence (two females), and a subtle facial phenotype. The facial features were a broad, wide forehead, long palpebral fissures, a bulbous nasal tip, and thick lips.
- Variant type: All were multi-gene deletions (0.97–1.18 Mb; smallest region of overlap 863 kb, 7 genes), not ZNF207-specific variants. The overlap includes ZNF207, CDK5R1, PSMD11, RHOT1 and SUZ12. The authors proposed CDK5R1 and RHOT1 as the main candidates, so ZNF207 is not specifically implicated.
- Cohorts: No known cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL) were identified. The patients came from three different countries.
- No ZNF207 intragenic LoF or missense variants were reported.
- Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED. This is contiguous-gene deletion evidence only, with no gene-level attribution.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- No model organism data were identified in the provided evidence.
- No knockout phenotypes (neurological, lethality, or Mendelian ratio data) are available, so consistency with the human phenotype cannot be assessed.
- Strength of model organism evidence: NONE

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Molecular function and pathways:
  - Mitosis: ZNF207 binds spindle microtubules and directly binds and stabilizes Bub3 through its GLEBS motif. This enhances Bub3 kinetochore loading, chromosome alignment, and the metaphase-to-anaphase transition (PMID: 24462186).
  - Splicing: A CRISPR splicing-reporter screen identified ZNF207 as a regulator of LMNA/progerin splicing. Its zinc finger interacts with U1 snRNP factors (PMID: 40568141).
  - Pluripotency and fate: A specific isoform was identified as an OCT4 enhancer-associated protein. It partners with pluripotency transcription factors in human embryonic stem cells, and isoform switching directs ectoderm commitment through neuronal transcription factors such as OTX2 (PMID: 30349051). ZNF207 also opposes cell fate reprogramming by keeping chromatin closed at reprogramming-factor motifs (PMID: 36973293).
- Neurodevelopmental relevance: The ectoderm and neuronal-lineage role (OTX2) is relevant to neurodevelopment (PMID: 30349051). No direct brain expression data or neuronal functional studies were provided.
- Cancer literature: Most publications concern hepatocellular carcinoma, where ZNF207 is overexpressed and linked to poor prognosis (PMID: 31113916). It regulates glycolysis genes ENO1 and GAPDH (PMID: 40684964), and it drives regorafenib resistance through PRDX1 lactylation and NRF2 activation (PMID: 40680452). It is also linked to immune exhaustion (PMID: 35246476). A ZNF207-HER2 fusion occurs in gastric cancer (PMID: 25889497). These findings show broad functional importance but are not informative for neurodevelopmental disease.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for a fundamental cellular role (mitosis, splicing, stem cell fate), but LIMITED for neurodevelopment-specific mechanisms.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT (at most LIMITED)
- Key strengths:
  - ZNF207 has well-characterized roles in chromosome segregation, splicing, and pluripotency/ectoderm commitment.
  - It lies within a recurrent 17q11.2 deletion region that is associated with hypotonia, intellectual disability, fatigue, and subtle dysmorphism, and the deletions were de novo in three unrelated patients (PMID: 29130599).
- Key gaps:
  - No intragenic ZNF207 variants have been reported in patients.
  - The deletions span several genes, and the authors favored other genes (CDK5R1, RHOT1) as candidates.
  - There are no model organism data and no segregation or functional studies of patient variants.
  - Most biological literature concerns cancer.
- Conclusion: ZNF207 has not been established as a monogenic autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene. The only clinical link is its inclusion in a multi-gene 17q11.2 deletion in three patients, where it is one of five candidate haploinsufficient genes. Further evidence would require de novo or LoF variants within ZNF207 in unrelated individuals with a consistent phenotype, plus supporting functional or animal model data.
