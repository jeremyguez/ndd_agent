BRSK1 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: HETEROZYGOUS VARIANTS AND NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER

1. GENE SUMMARY
BRSK1 (brain-specific kinase 1, also called SAD-B, with Drosophila ortholog sff) is an AMPK-related serine/threonine kinase that is activated by LKB1 phosphorylation of its T-loop (Thr189). It is preferentially expressed in the brain and is implicated in axon development, synaptic function and mitochondrial dynamics.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: three were found. Two are peer-reviewed case series (PMID: 41035394; PMID: 42805187). The third is a conference abstract (ASHG 2022 - An emirati cohort of autism spectrum disorders).
- Total patients: about 18 (7 + 10 + 1). Overlap between the series is not described, but the cohorts appear independent.
- Phenotypes:
  - Epilepsy and neurodevelopmental disorders were reported in a cohort of 394 epilepsy probands (PMID: 41035394).
  - Developmental delay, autism, ADHD, anxiety and variable seizures were reported in 10 individuals (PMID: 42805187).
  - Autism spectrum disorder was reported in a single Emirati proband (ASHG 2022 - An emirati cohort of autism spectrum disorders).
- Variant types:
  - PMID: 41035394 reported six novel variants in seven probands, found by trio exome sequencing. Five were de novo, one was inherited and one was recurrent. They were mostly frameshift/nonsense variants with one missense variant. Truncating variants abolished detectable BRSK1 protein and the missense variant reduced it.
  - PMID: 42805187 reported missense variants (p.Ile202Val, p.Arg237Cys, p.Thr406Ile) plus other missense and nonsense variants. The three tested missense alleles were partial loss-of-function in Drosophila.
  - The ASHG 2022 abstract reported one de novo variant of unspecified type. It was proposed as a candidate and not classified as likely pathogenic.
  - Overall, LoF variants are prominent and missense variants show partial loss of function, which supports a haploinsufficiency mechanism.
- Known cohorts: none of UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL is explicitly named. PMID: 42805187 used the Texome Project (Texas) and GeneMatcher. PMID: 41035394 used an epilepsy cohort that was probably Chinese, although the abstract does not state this.
- Limitations: specific variant notations were not available for most variants, and the ascertainment methods for the cohorts differ.
- Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE. There are two concordant series with de novo and LoF variants and functional support, but the numbers are modest and the reports are recent.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: mouse and Drosophila.
- Mouse (PMID: 41035394): the Brsk1Δ4-7 full knockout was studied in heterozygous and homozygous animals. Both showed increased seizure susceptibility, neuronal hyperexcitability and neurobehavioral impairments. Proteomics and phosphoproteomics showed dysregulation of axonal development and synaptic pathways. Heterozygous animals were affected, which supports haploinsufficiency. Mendelian ratios, lethality and fertility were not reported.
- Drosophila (PMID: 42805187): sff null flies showed locomotor defects, bang/heat sensitivity, short lifespan, NMJ overgrowth and elevated Futsch (MAP1B). Human BRSK1 cDNA rescued these phenotypes. The Ile202Val, Arg237Cys and Thr406Ile alleles gave only partial rescue.
- Consistency: the phenotypes (seizures, hyperexcitability, neurobehavioral deficits) match the human epilepsy and neurodevelopmental phenotype, and the fly assay provides variant-level functional validation.
- Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE to STRONG. Two species give concordant results, with a heterozygous mouse phenotype and a rescue assay. Quantitative details (sample sizes, Mendelian ratios) were not available.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function:
  - LKB1/STRAD/MO25 phosphorylates the BRSK1 T-loop and raises activity more than 50-fold (PMID: 14976552).
  - Ca2+/calmodulin binds the kinase domain and blocks LKB1-mediated Thr189 phosphorylation, with an IC50 of about 7 µM (PMID: 41905287).
  - BRSK1 is palmitoylated and associates with lipid rafts, where raft-like lipid vesicles increase its activity about 3-fold (PMID: 22020259).
  - SadA/B (BRSK2/BRSK1) regulate axonal mitochondrial fission/fusion through tau phosphorylation in cortical neurons (PMID: 34936879).
- Expression: BRSKs are preferentially expressed in the brain after E12. NGF induces Brsk1 through MAPK/Sp1 and is regulated by promoter methylation (PMID: 30196145).
- Non-neuronal roles: these include tumor-related functions, such as a PI3K/Akt–Jab1–p27 axis in breast cancer (PMID: 25036402), YAP/TAZ regulation (PMID: 40869130) and cisplatin resistance via mitochondrial respiration (PMID: 37140697). They are of limited relevance to neurodevelopment.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE. Brain-enriched expression, a well-defined regulatory cascade and neuronal axonal roles are established. Mechanistic links to the human disorder come mainly from the recent disease papers.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: MODERATE (approaching STRONG if replicated).
- Key strengths:
  - About 18 patients across independent reports, with de novo and LoF variants.
  - Protein loss was demonstrated in patient variants.
  - Heterozygous knockout mice reproduce the phenotype.
  - Fly rescue assays show partial loss of function for missense alleles.
  - Brain-enriched expression and a known role in axon and synapse biology.
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - Most variant notations were not available.
  - Phenotypes are variable, including seizures that are present only in some patients.
  - No named large cohorts (UDN, DDD, etc.) were identified.
  - The Emirati case is only a candidate.
  - Segregation data are limited, with one inherited variant in PMID: 41035394.
  - Details on mouse sample sizes and Mendelian ratios are lacking.
  - Missense pathogenicity rests on in silico predictions and fly assays.
- Conclusion: BRSK1 haploinsufficiency is supported as a cause of an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder with or without epilepsy (PMID: 41035394; PMID: 42805187). Two independent patient series, concordant mouse and fly models and established neuronal biology back this, though the evidence is recent and the cohorts are small. Further independent cases, detailed phenotyping and functional characterization of missense variants would strengthen the gene–disease relationship.

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. Haploinsufficiency of brain-specific kinase BRSK1 causes epilepsy and neurodevelopmental disorders (PMID: 41035394)
2. Monoallelic variants in BRSK1 are associated with a neurodevelopmental disorder with or without epilepsy (PMID: 42805187)

### Biological Evidence
1. LKB1 is a master kinase that activates 13 kinases of the AMPK subfamily, including MARK/PAR-1 (PMID: 14976552)
2. Regulation of brain-specific kinases 1 and 2 (BRSK1/2) by Ca(2+)/calmodulin (PMID: 41905287)
3. Brain specific kinase-1 BRSK1/SAD-B associates with lipid rafts: modulation of kinase activity by lipid environment (PMID: 22020259)
4. The balance of mitochondrial fission and fusion in cortical axons depends on the kinases SadA and SadB (PMID: 34936879)
5. Neuronal Growth Factor regulates Brain Specific Kinase 1 expression by inhibiting promoter methylation and promoting Sp1 recruitment (PMID: 30196145)
6. Decreased expression and prognostic role of cytoplasmic BRSK1 in human breast carcinoma: correlation with Jab1 stability and PI3K/Akt pathway (PMID: 25036402)
7. Integrative High-Throughput RNAi Screening Identifies BRSK1, STK32C and STK40 as Novel Activators of YAP/TAZ (PMID: 40869130)
8. BRSK1 confers cisplatin resistance in cervical cancer cells via regulation of mitochondrial respiration (PMID: 37140697)
