ANXA9 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER (AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT) ASSOCIATION

1. GENE SUMMARY
ANXA9 encodes annexin A9, a member of the annexin family of calcium-dependent phospholipid-binding proteins. The retrieved literature is almost entirely oncology-focused. It describes ANXA9 as a pro-tumorigenic factor that influences proliferation, migration, invasion and apoptosis through several signaling pathways. No physiological role in neurodevelopment is described in the evidence provided.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent case reports or case series: 0 were found.
- Total patients: 0.
- Phenotypes: none reported. No neurodevelopmental phenotypes are linked to ANXA9 in the evidence.
- Variant type: not applicable, because no germline variants in patients were reported.
- Cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): no patients were identified from any of these.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms studied: no germline knockout or mutant models were reported. The only in vivo work was xenograft experiments in mice using human breast cancer cells, in which ANXA9 knockdown suppressed tumor growth and lung metastasis (PMID: 38609357). Silencing also halved xenograft tumor size (PMID: 37294149). These are cancer models, not organismal loss-of-function models.
- Neurological phenotypes, lethality and Mendelian ratio deviations: none have been assessed.
- Consistency with human phenotype: cannot be assessed, because there is no human phenotype.
- Strength of model organism evidence: NONE.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and molecular function: in cancer cell systems, ANXA9 has been linked to several pathways:
  - TGF-β/SMAD signaling and EMT markers in gastric cancer (PMID: 34587407).
  - Wnt signaling in colorectal cancer, where knockdown caused cell cycle arrest and Wnt activation reversed the effect (PMID: 37349657).
  - Regulation of ADAM17, MMP9, TIMP-1 and E-cadherin in colorectal cancer cells (PMID: 29393380).
  - AKT/mTOR/STAT3 and p53/Bcl-2 pathways in breast cancer, through an interaction with S100A4 (PMID: 38609357).
  - Regulation by miR-186-5p in breast cancer (PMID: 37841425) and by miR-105-1 in ovarian cancer, where it affects cisplatin resistance (PMID: 33680718).
- Protein interactions: ANXA9 binds SULT2B1 by co-immunoprecipitation in ovarian cancer cells (PMID: 38777329). ER-β binds chromatin at the ANXA9 locus in MCF7 cells (PMID: 35872983).
- Expression pattern: ANXA9 is upregulated in gastric, colorectal, breast and ovarian cancers. In breast cancer it is about 1.5-2-fold higher than in normal tissue and highest in luminal subtypes (PMID: 37294149). It is downregulated in 42% of HPV-negative head and neck squamous cell carcinomas (372 patients, P<0.001) (PMID: 30744186). No brain or neurodevelopmental expression data are present in the evidence.
- Key experimental findings: knockdown reduces proliferation, migration and invasion across multiple cancer cell types, and re-expression rescues the effects. All of this is in a cancer context.
- Strength of biological evidence for a neurodevelopmental role: LIMITED. Functional data exist, but they are non-neural and cancer-specific, and they do not bear on a neurodevelopmental mechanism.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT.
- Strengths: ANXA9 function is reasonably well characterized in cancer biology, with consistent findings across several tumor types and some rescue experiments.
- Gaps and limitations:
  - There are no patients, no variants and no genotype-phenotype data.
  - There are no germline animal models.
  - There are no brain expression or neuronal functional studies.
  - The cancer-derived findings concern somatic overexpression and do not inform the effects of heterozygous germline loss-of-function or missense variants.
- Conclusion: there is no evidence supporting ANXA9 as an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene. The available literature addresses only its role in cancer. Any gene-disease association would require identifying de novo or heterozygous variants in affected individuals, together with neural expression and functional studies.
