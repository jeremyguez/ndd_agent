CBX5 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: CBX5 as a candidate autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene

1. GENE SUMMARY
CBX5 encodes heterochromatin protein 1 alpha (HP1α), a chromodomain protein that reads H3K9 methylation marks and dimerizes to crosslink chromatin. It supports heterochromatin organization, transcriptional repression, and mitotic chromosome mechanics and segregation (PMID: 34106828). No disease association with neurodevelopmental disorders is established in the evidence provided.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent case reports or case series: 0. No clinical reports were retrieved.
- Total patients: 0.
- Common phenotypes: none can be defined.
- Predominant variant type: not assessable (no LoF, missense, or other variants reported).
- Cohort membership (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none identified.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms studied: mouse only. One study used HP1α-null mouse embryonic fibroblasts (PMID: 31130069). The other used a mouse MLL-AML leukemia stem cell model with knockdown (PMID: 19200802). A Drosophila screen appears in the biological evidence, but it addresses collagen uptake and not neurodevelopment (PMID: 29792348).
- Key phenotypes:
  - HP1α-null MEFs showed mitotic chromosomal instability, anaphase/telophase chromatin bridges, and micronuclei (PMID: 31130069). These defects were also seen with the non-phosphorylatable S92A mutant and were rescued by wild-type HP1α or phosphomimetic S92D.
  - Cbx5 is required to maintain self-renewing leukemia stem cells (PMID: 19200802).
- No whole-organism knockout data were provided. There is no information on neurological phenotypes, lethality, Mendelian ratios, or fertility.
- Consistency with human phenotype: cannot be assessed, because there is no human phenotype and no neurodevelopmental readout in the models. The cellular chromosomal instability phenotype is only loosely relevant.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED, and not specific to neurodevelopment.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and molecular function:
  - HP1α acts as a chromatin crosslinker through dimerization. Acute degradation did not alter transcription, local compaction, or histone methylation, but it reduced chromatin and nuclear stiffness and impaired mitotic chromosome alignment and segregation. The dimerization-deficient I165E mutant fails to crosslink (PMID: 34106828).
  - CBX5 works with G9a and H3K9me to repress PPARGC1A in lung fibroblasts (PMID: 31095524).
  - BRD4 represses CBX5 in the DNA damage response (PMID: 28881656).
  - RBMX/RBMXL1 control CBX5 transcription, and loss of this control causes chromatin changes and chromosomal breaks (PMID: 34458856).
- Expression:
  - CBX5 is enriched in pathogenic fibroblasts in IPF lungs (PMID: 39642371).
  - It is downregulated in metastatic breast cancer, and it shares a bidirectional promoter with hnRNPA1 (PMID: 26791953).
  - It is differentially expressed in the blood of drug-naïve sporadic Parkinson's disease patients (40 PD and 20 controls in the discovery set, 12 in validation) (PMID: 26510930). This is the only neurological link, and it is a peripheral blood association in a different disease.
  - No brain or neurodevelopmental expression data were provided.
- Key findings: most studies address fibrosis, cancer, and periodontitis, for example (PMID: 39642371) and (PMID: 22900142). They support a role for CBX5 in chromatin regulation, genome stability, and cell-state control, but not in neurodevelopment.
- Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED to MODERATE for general function, and LIMITED for relevance to neurodevelopmental disorders.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT.
- Key strengths:
  - CBX5 has a well-characterized molecular role in chromatin organization and mitotic fidelity (PMID: 34106828, PMID: 31130069).
  - It is functionally linked to the H3K9me pathway, which includes genes implicated in other neurodevelopmental disorders. This is an inference and was not shown in the evidence provided.
- Key gaps:
  - No patients, variants, or segregation or de novo data.
  - No animal model with neurological assessment, and no whole-organism knockout phenotyping.
  - No brain expression or neuronal functional studies.
  - Existing data come from cancer, fibrosis, and other non-neurological contexts.
- Conclusion: There is currently no clinical or neurodevelopmental-specific experimental evidence supporting CBX5 as a heterozygous neurodevelopmental disorder gene. The available data establish only a general chromatin and mitotic function. CBX5 should be regarded as an unvalidated candidate until patients with likely pathogenic variants and supporting functional or model data are reported.
