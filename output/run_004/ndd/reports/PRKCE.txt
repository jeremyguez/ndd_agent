PRKCE (PKCε) REPORT: EVIDENCE FOR A HETEROZYGOUS NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

1. GENE SUMMARY
PRKCE encodes protein kinase C epsilon, a novel (calcium-independent) serine/threonine kinase of the PKC family. It signals through PDK1 and mTORC2-dependent AKT activation, and it regulates metabolism, cell survival, and nicotinic receptor and dopamine signaling. A de novo PRKCE variant has been linked to SHORT syndrome, and PRKCE has been proposed as a neurodevelopmental gene.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: Five clinical entries were provided, and none is a convincing case report of a neurodevelopmental disorder (NDD) with a pathogenic PRKCE variant. All are copy number variant (CNV) or cohort-level observations.
- Patients: Roughly 2 individuals are explicitly described. One has a rare CNV involving PRKCE in rolandic epilepsy (PMID: 24372385). One has a 4.8 Mb 2p16.3-p21 deletion spanning MSH2, MSH6, EPCAM, NRXN1 and PRKCE, with Lynch syndrome and intellectual disability (PMID: 28555354). The other entries do not give patient counts.
- Phenotypes: These include epilepsy, intellectual disability, and dysmorphism or short stature in the deletion patient. Other phenotypes are OCD traits, undiagnosed developmental disorder (UDD)/autism, and NDD. PRKCE was named among genes driving an OCD-trait CNV/SNV association signal (ASHG 2018 - A copy number variation map of pediatric neuropsychiatric traits). It was a critical-exon gene within CNVs in a Bangladeshi NDD cohort, but it was not prioritized (PMID: 36959829). A de novo variant in PRKCE was counted in published UDD/ASD cohorts (DDD among them), with about 1 per category and no variant or phenotype details (PMID: 37930872).
- Variant type: Predominantly CNVs that include many other genes, so the specific contribution of PRKCE cannot be separated. No clear LoF or missense series is described in the clinical data.
- Cohorts: DDD is referenced in the de novo analysis (PMID: 37930872). The Spit for Science cohort (Toronto) is also referenced. No UDN, GREGoR, RUMC or GEL data were reported.
- Notably, a single de novo PRKCE variant in a child with SHORT syndrome (without a PIK3R1 mutation) is reported in the biological evidence (PMID: 28934384). It is not part of the clinical evidence above. SHORT syndrome can include developmental features, but it is not primarily a NDD.
- Clinical evidence strength: LIMITED (bordering on none for NDD specifically). Evidence is non-specific, there are no recurrent de novo variants with consistent phenotypes, and the CNVs are multigenic.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organism: Mouse only.
- Neurological phenotypes: A 2025 conditional knockout study reports cerebral and cerebellar atrophy, motor, social and cognitive deficits, abnormal brain development, and reduced VEGF/PI3K-induced AKT activation (PMID: 40914752). This is the most relevant finding and it is consistent with the mTORC2/AKT mechanism in SHORT syndrome.
- Other full knockouts are viable, with no reported lethality or Mendelian ratio deviation. Phenotypes include reduced nicotine self-administration and altered dopamine reward signaling (PMID: 21911393) and prolonged nicotinic receptor desensitization (PMID: 26103136). Metabolic phenotypes include protection from high-fat-diet glucose intolerance (PMID: 21347625) and altered fasting ketogenesis (PMID: 22814763). Maternal Prkce loss impairs the first cleavage, with a 2-cell rate of 32.4% vs 80.1% in wild type (PMID: 35582855). Conditional myeloid knockout increases atherosclerosis (PMID: 40703062).
- Consistency with human phenotype: Partial. The brain atrophy and cognitive and motor deficits in the conditional knockout support a neurodevelopmental role. The mice are knockouts, however, and the human data involve heterozygous missense or CNV events with uncertain mechanism. Sample sizes and statistics were not provided.
- Strength: MODERATE (one recent relevant neurodevelopmental study, with viable full knockouts and supportive neuronal signaling data).

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways: PKCε acts in mTORC2-dependent AKT (S473) activation and in PDK1/SIN1 interactions. It also acts in glutaminolysis, where it phosphorylates GAC and so drives the MTOR-autophagy axis (PMID: 34720024). Other contexts are nicotinic receptor and dopamine signaling, insulin/lipid metabolism, and cell cycle regulation in oocytes.
- Key functional data: The de novo SHORT syndrome variant showed partial loss of kinase function, higher SIN1 binding, and impaired mTORC2-dependent pAKT-S473. Patient lymphoblasts showed reduced pFOXO1-S256 and pS6-S240/244 (PMID: 28934384). This is a single proband plus parents.
- Expression: Hippocampal Prkce expression was higher in high-performing Morris water maze mice, with grouping alongside AMPA/glutamatergic/LTP genes (PMID: 33328525). Other data are from cancer, metabolic and cardiac tissues, including epigenetic repression after prenatal cocaine (PMID: 18945988) and altered liver methylation in NAFLD (PMID: 23931760). The brain-specific developmental expression data provided are limited.
- Other associations: PRKCE variants are associated with diabetic nephropathy (PMID: 28616206). Several cancer expression studies also exist, such as KIRC (PMID: 36186442) and thyroid (PMID: 23457043), but they are not relevant to NDD.
- Strength: MODERATE for a general role in mTORC2/AKT signaling relevant to growth and development. It is LIMITED for neurodevelopmental disease mechanisms specifically.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: LIMITED for PRKCE as an autosomal dominant NDD gene.
- Strengths: A functionally characterized de novo variant gives a plausible mechanism through impaired mTORC2/AKT signaling (PMID: 28934384). A recent conditional knockout shows brain atrophy and cognitive and motor deficits (PMID: 40914752). PRKCE recurs in multiple NDD/epilepsy/OCD CNV and de novo datasets.
- Gaps: There is no case series of heterozygous LoF or missense variants with consistent NDD phenotypes. The CNV evidence is multigenic and non-specific. The de novo variant data in cohorts lack detail, and there are no statistically significant de novo enrichment results for PRKCE. Human phenotypes are not clearly mapped to knockout phenotypes, and the dominant inheritance mechanism (haploinsufficiency vs dominant-negative) is unresolved. The SHORT syndrome link rests on a single case.
- Conclusion: PRKCE is a biologically plausible candidate whose roles in mTORC2/AKT signaling and brain development are supported by a mouse conditional knockout. The human genetic evidence is currently sparse, consisting of one functionally studied de novo SHORT syndrome variant and non-specific CNV and cohort associations. It should be regarded as a candidate gene with limited evidence, and additional unrelated individuals with de novo variants and consistent phenotypes are needed before it can be classified as an established NDD gene.
