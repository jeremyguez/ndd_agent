H2AX LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: H2AX AS A CANDIDATE AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

1. GENE SUMMARY
H2AX encodes a histone H2A variant that is phosphorylated at S139 (γH2AX) by ATM, ATR and DNA-PK at DNA double-strand breaks and stalled replication forks. γH2AX serves as a platform for recruiting DNA repair and checkpoint factors. The gene lies at 11q23.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent case reports or case series: 0 found.
- Total patients: 0.
- Phenotypes: none reported. No human neurodevelopmental phenotype is described in the supplied evidence.
- Variant type: no patient variants were reported, so a predominant type (LoF vs missense) cannot be determined.
- Cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL): none identified.
- One indirect observation exists. MDS patients with 11q23 deletions, a region that includes H2AX, show increased dyserythropoiesis (PMID: 26791933). This is a large somatic deletion in a haematological setting. It is not evidence of a germline heterozygous neurodevelopmental disorder.
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- No structured model organism evidence was supplied. The mouse data below come from the biological-evidence papers.
- H2AX knockout mice show cell-autonomous anaemia and erythroid dysplasia with decreased enucleation. H2AX-deficient haematopoietic stem and progenitor cells lose quiescence and engraft poorly (PMID: 26791933).
- H2AX knockout mice show increased neuronal death after 3-nitropropionic acid challenge. H2AX loss in MEFs and mouse striatum reduces mitochondrial respiratory complex subunits and represses PGC-1α (PMID: 30910969). This points to a neuroprotective role, but it is a stress-induced phenotype, not a developmental one.
- No data were provided on lethality, Mendelian ratios, heterozygous phenotypes, or neurodevelopmental or behavioural phenotypes.
- Consistency with human phenotype cannot be assessed, because no human phenotype has been described.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED, and only weakly relevant to neurodevelopment.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function:
  - γH2AX recruits FANCD2 to stalled forks in a BRCA1-dependent manner. H2AX-deficient cells (knockout or S136A/S139A mutant) show an FA-like phenotype with MMC hypersensitivity (PMID: 17304220).
  - The γH2AX C-terminal linker recruits 53BP1 independently of the MDC1-RNF8-RNF168 pathway (PMID: 38821984).
  - TIP60-FACT acetylation of H2AX at K5 drives H2AX exchange and MDC1 degradation. Disrupting this exchange leads to pathological senescence (PMID: 41494686).
  - In BRCA-deficient tumours, γH2AX promotes fork degradation by suppressing CtIP-mediated fork protection (PMID: 38789420).
  - γH2AX is induced downstream of ATM during viral DNA damage responses (PMID: 31401636).
- Expression:
  - Mouse ESCs, iPSCs and inner cell masses have high basal γH2AX, which falls on differentiation. Human ESCs have low basal levels. This suggests a role beyond DNA damage signalling in pluripotency (ASHG 2010 - γH2AX in pluripotent stem cells: more than a DNA damage response signal?).
  - H2AX overexpression is linked to poor prognosis in breast cancer (n=3594) (PMID: 35261802) and oropharyngeal squamous cell carcinoma (mRNA HR 4.77, p=0.04; protein HR 2.24, p=0.03) (PMID: 39756240).
  - No brain-specific expression data were provided.
- Neural relevance: H2AX supports mitochondrial homeostasis in neurons and striatum (PMID: 30910969). Disease-relevant developmental data are absent.
- Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE for a core role in DNA damage response and genome stability. It is LIMITED for neurodevelopmental relevance specifically.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT for H2AX as a heterozygous neurodevelopmental disorder gene.
- Key strengths:
  - H2AX has a well-characterised role in DNA damage response and replication stress.
  - There is mechanistic plausibility, given the many neurodevelopmental disorders caused by DNA repair genes, and neuroprotective and mitochondrial roles have been described in mice (PMID: 30910969).
  - Roles in stem cell maintenance are suggested (PMID: 26791933; ASHG 2010 - γH2AX in pluripotent stem cells: more than a DNA damage response signal?).
- Key gaps:
  - There are no patients, no variants and no cohort data.
  - There are no developmental neurological phenotypes in knockout or heterozygous models.
  - There are no brain expression or constraint analyses in the supplied evidence.
  - Much of the literature is cancer-focused.
- Conclusion: No clinical evidence links H2AX to a neurodevelopmental disorder. Model organism and biological data support a role in genome maintenance, haematopoiesis and neuronal mitochondrial health, but not a developmental phenotype. H2AX should be considered an unestablished candidate until de novo or segregating heterozygous variants in affected individuals are reported.
