GPS2 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: GPS2 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
GPS2 (G protein pathway suppressor 2) encodes a subunit of the NCOR/SMRT-HDAC3 corepressor complex. It acts as a transcriptional coregulator and as an inhibitor of Ubc13-mediated K63 ubiquitination, with roles in metabolism, inflammation, mitochondrial signaling and cell survival. It is located at 17p13.1, next to DLG4, GABARAP and KCTD11.

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: Two independent reports describe patients, and a third entry duplicates one of them. The ASHG 2010 abstract covers the same patient as PMID: 20111057.
- Patient numbers: About 5 unique patients. These are 4 from a Brazilian cohort of 300 individuals with intellectual disability (PMID: 19617690) and 1 from Japan (PMID: 20111057).
- Variant type: All were large contiguous deletions (type "other"), not GPS2-specific variants.
  - PMID: 19617690 describes de novo 17p13.1 microdeletions with an overlapping ~180 kb region covering 18 genes, including KCTD11, DLG4, GPS2 and GABARAP. Two of the four deletions also included TP53.
  - PMID: 20111057 describes a 2.3-Mb deletion at 17p13.2p13.1 that includes GPS2.
- Phenotypes: Intellectual disability or developmental delay (moderate in the Japanese patient), congenital abnormalities, dysmorphic features and intractable epilepsy.
- Cohorts: None are from UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL.
- Limitations: No single-gene GPS2 LoF or missense variants are reported. GPS2 is one of many candidate genes in the deleted region, and the authors and functional work favored DLG4 and GABARAP (PMID: 20111057). No individual causal role for GPS2 is established.
- Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED, bordering on NONE for GPS2 specifically. The data support a contiguous-gene deletion syndrome at 17p13.1, not a GPS2 monogenic disorder.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organism: Mouse only. Most studies use conditional knockouts, and some use xenograft knockdown. No germline knockout or neurodevelopmental analysis was reported.
- Lethality: Endothelial-specific Gps2 deletion causes embryonic lethality at E16.5 from defective vascularization. TNFR1-mediated endothelial death is the mechanism, and TNFR1 deletion rescues the lethality (PMID: 41507360).
- Other conditional knockouts are viable and show tissue-specific phenotypes:
  - Adipocyte knockout: maladaptive adipose expansion, inflammation, and insulin and glucose dysregulation (PMID: 30208320; PMID: 32937117).
  - Macrophage knockout: pro-inflammatory activation and insulin resistance (PMID: 27270589).
  - B cell knockout: defects at multiple stages of B cell development (PMID: 28039360).
  - Hepatocyte knockout: protection from NASH (PMID: 30975991).
- Xenograft knockdown or deletion in cancer cells promotes tumor growth and metastasis (PMID: 35941228; PMID: 33490071).
- Consistency with human phenotype: No neurological, behavioral or brain phenotypes were reported, and no heterozygous or constitutive models were described. The phenotypes are metabolic, immune, vascular and oncologic, so they do not match the neurodevelopmental features seen in the deletion patients.
- Strength of model organism evidence for a neurodevelopmental disorder: LIMITED. The studies show that Gps2 is biologically important, with at least one essential developmental role, but they do not address the nervous system.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and molecular function: GPS2 is a component of the NCoR/SMRT corepressor complex (PMID: 26070566). It inhibits Ubc13-mediated K63 ubiquitination, including of mitochondria-associated translation proteins (PMID: 39094987). It restricts TRAF6-mediated ubiquitination to license HDAC3 recruitment to chromatin (PMID: 40305047). GPS2 also stabilizes HOIP/LUBAC to support NF-κB signaling and cell survival (PMID: 41507360), stabilizes ATF4 (PMID: 40693356), and restrains HIF1A activity in adipocytes (PMID: 30208320). Its stability is regulated by Siah2, TBL1 and PRMT6 (PMID: 26070566).
- Expression pattern: The supplied data contain no brain or neurodevelopmental expression data.
- Key experimental findings:
  - GPS2 supports erythroid differentiation through NCOR1 (PMID: 38814500).
  - GPS2 has antiviral activity against influenza (PMID: 33658351).
  - GPS2 acts in colorectal cancer through LLPS with LATS1 (PMID: 40715488).
- Relevance: The NCoR/SMRT-HDAC3 axis is plausibly relevant to neurodevelopment, because related genes such as NCOR1 and TBL1XR1 are linked to neurodevelopmental disorders. This link is inferential and not directly supported by the supplied data.
- Strength of biological evidence for a neurodevelopmental disorder: LIMITED to MODERATE. The molecular function is well characterized, but the neurodevelopmental relevance is indirect.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT (as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous pathogenic variants).
- Key strengths: GPS2 has well-defined molecular roles in the NCoR/SMRT-HDAC3 corepressor complex and in ubiquitin signaling. A conditional mouse model shows an essential developmental role in the endothelium. GPS2 lies within a recurrent 17p13.1 deletion region associated with intellectual disability and epilepsy.
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - There are no patients with intragenic GPS2 variants and no de novo LoF or missense variants.
  - The deletion patients (about 5, multigenic) cannot be attributed to GPS2, and DLG4 and GABARAP are stronger neuronal candidates.
  - There are no neuronal or brain mouse models, no heterozygous or constitutive knockout data, and no brain expression data in the supplied evidence.
  - No cohort-level data (UDN, DDD, GEL and similar) are provided.
- Conclusion: Current evidence does not support GPS2 as an established autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene. Its presence in 17p13.1 microdeletions associated with intellectual disability and epilepsy (PMID: 19617690; PMID: 20111057) makes it at most a candidate contributor to a contiguous-gene syndrome. Establishing a role would require identification of de novo or intragenic GPS2 variants in individuals with neurodevelopmental disorders and neuronal functional studies.
