ATF2 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: ATF2 AS A CANDIDATE AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

1. GENE SUMMARY
ATF2 (activating transcription factor 2) is a basic leucine zipper transcription factor of the ATF/CREB family. It is phosphorylated by stress-activated kinases (JNK, p38 MAPK) and regulates transcriptional programs for stress response, apoptosis, and inflammation, often with JUN. It also has a mitochondrial role in some contexts.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
Pathways and molecular function:
- ATF2 acts downstream of MAP kinase signaling (JNK, p38) as a stress-responsive transcription factor.
- A genome-wide CRISPRi screen in human neurons identified ATF2, DLK (MAP3K12), and JUN as required for neuronal death. ATF2 phosphorylation by MAP3 kinases drives the pro-apoptotic response and JUN upregulation, whereas JUN phosphorylation is dispensable. ATF2 knockdown prevented neuron death in culture and was neuroprotective in vivo (PMID: 40654834).
- Further neuronal apoptosis mechanisms:
  - RCAN1.1L stabilizes ATF2 mRNA through its 3'UTR (nucleotides 2915-2935). This raises mitochondrial ATF2, which upregulates FIS1 and drives mitochondrial fission and intrinsic apoptosis in ischemic neurons (PMID: 42129133).
  - ATF2 binds the BAP1 promoter, and BAP1 stabilizes P53, forming an ATF2/BAP1/P53 apoptotic axis after subarachnoid hemorrhage (PMID: 38965653).
  - ATF2 transcriptionally activates TLR4, and ATF2 overexpression promoted neuronal degeneration in oxygen-glucose deprivation models (PMID: 34591732).
- Neurodevelopmental and plasticity links:
  - In mouse visual cortex after monocular deprivation, p38 MAPK inhibition increased ATF2 and PSD-95 expression and improved visual function and synaptic plasticity, placing ATF2 downstream of p38 in cortical plasticity (PMID: 37393009).
  - In SH-SY5Y cells, knockdown of MBD5, RAI1, and TCF4 altered expression of circadian genes including ATF2 and ATF4 (ASHG 2014 - Disruption of FMR1, RAI1, MBD5, and TCF4 results in abnormal expression of circadian rhythm genes and sleep disturbances). MBD5 knockdown similarly dysregulated ATF2 (ASHG 2013 - MBD5 deletion disrupts circadian gene expression and is associated with sleep disturbance in the 2q23.1 deletion syndrome). These findings connect ATF2 to pathways disrupted in known neurodevelopmental syndromes, though only at the level of expression changes.
- Cancer studies show ATF2 is upregulated in gastric cancer, where it acts through HSPH1 and SLC7A11 to suppress ferroptosis (PMID: 36473315). It is also elevated in NSCLC, where it contributes to cisplatin resistance (PMID: 25359574). In osteosarcoma, KIF18B controls ATF2 nuclear aggregation and thereby β-catenin transcription (PMID: 32587775). These studies show general regulatory roles but are not relevant to neurodevelopment.
- Expression pattern: the evidence provided includes no systematic brain or developmental expression data. ATF2 is clearly expressed and functional in neurons, but its developmental expression pattern is not characterized here.
- Key limitation: the neuronal findings concern ATF2 activation or overexpression in adult injury and death paradigms. They do not address haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative variant effects during development.
- Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED. The neuronal function is well supported, but the evidence is indirect for a neurodevelopmental disorder mechanism.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT
- Key strengths:
  - Multiple independent studies place ATF2 centrally in neuronal stress signaling and apoptosis.
  - A human neuron CRISPRi screen provides direct functional evidence (PMID: 40654834).
  - There are suggestive links to synaptic plasticity and to circadian genes dysregulated in neurodevelopmental syndromes.
- Key gaps and limitations:
  - There are no reported patients, no variants, and no segregation or de novo data.
  - There are no germline knockout or knock-in models with neurodevelopmental phenotypes.
  - Biological data come mainly from injury, ischemia, and cancer contexts, and often rely on pharmacological or overexpression approaches.
  - The circadian data come from conference abstracts, show only expression changes, and have no stated sample sizes.
  - No functional studies of patient-derived variants exist.
- Conclusion: ATF2 is biologically plausible as a neuronal gene because of its role in MAPK-driven neuronal apoptosis and its possible role in plasticity. However, there is currently no clinical or model organism evidence supporting heterozygous ATF2 variants as a cause of a neurodevelopmental disorder. Gene-disease association cannot be supported without identification of affected individuals with likely pathogenic variants and supporting functional or model organism data.

5. Regulatory role of the p38 MAPK/ATF2 signaling pathway in visual function and visual cortical plasticity in mice with monocular deprivation (PMID: 37393009)
6. Increased ATF2 expression predicts poor prognosis and inhibits sorafenib-induced ferroptosis in gastric cancer (PMID: 36473315)
7. ATF2 contributes to cisplatin resistance in non-small cell lung cancer and celastrol induces cisplatin resensitization through inhibition of JNK/ATF2 pathway (PMID: 25359574)
8. KIF18B promotes tumor progression in osteosarcoma by activating β-catenin (PMID: 32587775)
