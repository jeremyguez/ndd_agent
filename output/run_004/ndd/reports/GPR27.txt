GPR27 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER (HETEROZYGOUS / AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT)

1. GENE SUMMARY
GPR27 (SREB1) encodes a "super-conserved" orphan G protein-coupled receptor. It is an intronless gene whose 379-aa product is expressed predominantly in the CNS (PMID: 9479505). Its best-supported functions are in pancreatic beta-cell insulin production and in glucose and lipid metabolism. Surrogate agonists drive β-arrestin 2 recruitment without detectable G protein activation (PMID: 28314853).

2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Independent reports: 3 were found, all contiguous-gene or copy-number deletions. No intragenic GPR27 variants (LoF or missense) were reported.
- Patients: about 3 in total, one per report.
  - A de novo ~15 Mb 3p13p11 deletion in a boy with developmental delay, psychomotor retardation, craniofacial dysmorphism and cleft lip (PMID: 12836054).
  - A 4.8 Mb 3p13p12.3 deletion spanning 19 genes in a Saudi patient with intellectual disability, language impairment, possible autistic features, regression and epilepsy (PMID: 39539796).
  - A 785 kb 3p14.1 deletion in a patient with gross motor delay, hypertonia, contractures, blepharophimosis and speech delay (PMID: 22736078).
- Common phenotypes: developmental and psychomotor delay and speech/language impairment.
- Variant type: exclusively large multigenic heterozygous deletions ("other"). In all three, GPR27 is a co-deleted passenger gene. The phenotype is attributed mainly to FOXP1 (PMIDs: 39539796, 22736078) or is not attributed to any single gene (PMID: 12836054).
- Cohorts: none of the patients are from UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC or GEL. One was from a single Saudi center (King Saud Medical City).
- Strength of clinical evidence: NONE for GPR27 as a causal gene. The deletions are confounded by FOXP1 and other genes, and no GPR27-specific variants have been described.

3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Organisms: zebrafish and mouse, both full knockouts.
- Zebrafish gpr27 knockout (PMID: 31914600):
  - Viable.
  - Potentiated glucose elevation after perturbation.
  - Elevated medium-chain acylcarnitines.
  - Abrogated insulin-dependent Akt phosphorylation, with insulin resistance.
- Aged zebrafish knockout (PMID: 37509577): exacerbated skeletal muscle atrophy and reduced myofiber cross-sectional area.
- Mouse knockout (PMID: 32221326):
  - Viable, with Mendelian ratios and no gross abnormalities.
  - Islet insulin and Pdx1 mRNA reduced by about 30%.
  - Slightly worse glucose tolerance.
  - Reduced neighboring Eif4e3 expression, a confounder.
- Neurological phenotypes were not reported in either model, and no neurodevelopmental assessment is described. The phenotypes are metabolic and muscular, so they are not consistent with the human neurodevelopmental presentations.
- Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED for a neurodevelopmental role. Knockouts are viable, with only mild metabolic phenotypes.

4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY
- Pathways and function:
  - Gq/11-linked signaling and Pdx1-dependent insulin promoter regulation in beta cells (PMID: 22253604).
  - Atypical, arrestin-biased signaling with surrogate agonists (PMID: 28314853). Optimized benzamide agonists have since been developed (PMID: 38060818).
  - Proposed links to lipid metabolism, neuronal plasticity and L-lactate homeostasis (PMID: 38060818).
  - MAPK/ERK pathway involvement in hepatocellular carcinoma (PMID: 35330739).
- Expression: transcripts are found in several areas of human and rat CNS (PMID: 9479505). Gpr27 mRNA is decreased in the cortex and striatum of Shank3-overexpressing mice and is modulated by neuronal activity in cultured neurons (PMID: 30233305).
- Disease association: clustered small deletions in the GPR27 enhancer-rich region were associated with Parkinson's disease in Korean cohorts (310 PD and 100 controls; replication in 100 PD and 100 controls), with SNCA upregulation (PMID: 36869069). This is not a neurodevelopmental phenotype and is not a variant-level causal demonstration.
- GPR27 expression correlates negatively with glioma grade (PMID: 38638156).
- Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED to MODERATE for general function. It is LIMITED for neurodevelopment specifically.

5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT
- Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous pathogenic variants.
- Strengths:
  - Brain-enriched expression.
  - Well-characterized roles in insulin and metabolic biology.
  - Viable knockouts in two species.
  - Emerging pharmacology.
- Gaps:
  - No intragenic pathogenic variants and no de novo or segregation data for GPR27.
  - Only three multigenic deletions, each with better-supported candidate genes (notably FOXP1).
  - No neurodevelopmental phenotype in knockout models.
  - Mouse knockout phenotype confounded by Eif4e3 reduction.
  - No evidence of haploinsufficiency or dominant mechanism.
- Conclusion: GPR27 has no supportive clinical evidence as a cause of autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disease. Its appearance in the patient deletions reflects its location within the 3p13-p14 region alongside FOXP1 and PROK2. Model organisms and functional studies point to metabolic and possibly neuronal-modulatory roles, but without GPR27-specific human variants, the gene–disease relationship cannot be supported at present.
