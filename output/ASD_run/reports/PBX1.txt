# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: PBX1 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

PBX1 (Pre-B-cell leukemia homeobox 1) is a TALE-class homeodomain transcription factor that functions as a critical regulator of developmental patterning, cell differentiation, and hematopoietic stem cell maintenance. PBX1 acts as a DNA-binding cofactor that partners with HOX proteins and other transcription factors (particularly MEIS family members) to control gene expression during embryogenesis and tissue homeostasis.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent case reports/series:** 1 case series

**Total patients:** 7 patients with de novo PBX1 variants

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Intellectual disability (reported in all 7 patients)
- External ear anomalies
- Heart malformations
- Diaphragmatic hernia
- Renal hypoplasia
- Cryptorchidism

**Predominant variant type:** Mixed—missense mutations in or near the homeodomain and truncating mutations. All variants were de novo and deleterious, with functional studies demonstrating impaired PBX1 transactivation ability, nuclear translocation, and interactions with TALE/HOX cofactors (ASHG 2017 - De novo, deleterious sequence variants that alter the transcriptional activity of the homeoprotein PBX1 are associated with intellectual disability and pleiotropic developmental defects).

**Patient cohort source:** Not specified as from known diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL).

**Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED**

The clinical evidence is limited to a single case series of 7 patients presented at a conference. While the phenotypic spectrum is consistent with a developmental disorder and functional studies support pathogenicity, the evidence lacks peer-reviewed publication, larger case series, or independent replication. The pleiotropic nature of the phenotypes (affecting multiple organ systems) is consistent with a developmental transcription factor but requires additional published case reports for robust clinical validation.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (Mus musculus)

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

- **Full knockout:** Asplenia/absent spleen with defects in early splenic development and patterning; reduced B cell populations and increased susceptibility to encapsulated bacterial infection (PMID: 17226804). Viable animals with no reported lethality.

- **Conditional knockout (Meis2-dependent):** Severely reduced PBX1 expression in the hyoid region resulted in aberrant hyoid apparatus development with ectopic cartilage and bone formation, elevated proliferation of cartilage precursors, and disinhibition of skeletogenesis (PMID: 36247013). Viable animals.

- **Conditional knockout (cardiac):** Inhibited cardiac regeneration and worsened cardiac function in neonatal myocardial infarction model, with reduced Pbx1 expression regulation and impaired cardiomyocyte dedifferentiation (PMID: 39559922). Viable animals.

- **Knockdown (hepatic):** Myofibroblast-specific PBX1 depletion significantly attenuated liver fibrosis without affecting liver injury or steatosis (PMID: 40885520). Viable animals.

- **Knockdown (immune):** PBX1-d isoform expression in CD4+ T cells associated with production of autoreactive activated T cells, reduced regulatory T cell frequency and function, and expansion of follicular helper T cells (PMID: 28257976). Viable animals.

- **Knock-in (TCF3-PBX1 fusion):** TCF3-PBX1 fusion drives acute lymphoblastic leukemia development with CNS infiltration at relapse and chemotherapy-resistant clones (PMID: 39901941).

- **Embryonic knockout:** Pbx1 knockout mouse embryos at E15.5 exhibit diaphragmatic defects, validating Pbx1 as a congenital diaphragmatic hernia-associated gene (ASHG 2011 - Analysis of time-series embryonic diaphragm transcriptomes identifies Pbx1 as a candidate gene for diaphragmatic defects).

**Consistency with human phenotype:** Moderate consistency. The mouse knockout data demonstrates that PBX1 is essential for multiple developmental processes (skeletal patterning, cardiac function, immune development, diaphragmatic development) and tissue homeostasis. However, complete knockout in mice does not recapitulate the full spectrum of human neurodevelopmental phenotypes (particularly intellectual disability), suggesting that heterozygous loss-of-function or dominant-negative effects in humans may have distinct consequences compared to complete loss in mice.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**

Multiple independent mouse models demonstrate that PBX1 is essential for normal development across multiple organ systems. The viability of full knockouts indicates that complete loss is not embryonic lethal, but the tissue-specific and developmental defects observed are consistent with a critical developmental role. The embryonic diaphragmatic defects directly parallel the human phenotype.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

PBX1 functions as a transcriptional regulator in several critical developmental and homeostatic pathways:

1. **Myogenic differentiation:** PBX1, together with MEIS1, mediates early opening of DNA elements bound by MYOD1 in differentiating myoblasts, orchestrating gene regulation during myoblast differentiation (PMID: 30078726).

2. **Hematopoietic stem cell maintenance:** PBX1 is essential for hematopoietic stem cell (HSC) quiescence and self-renewal. Loss of HBO1 (KAT7) results in downregulation of Pbx1 expression in HSC-enriched populations, leading to abnormal HSC recruitment into the cell cycle and exhaustion of the HSC pool (PMID: 34724565).

3. **Hepatic fibrosis:** PBX1 is upregulated during hepatic stellate cell (HSC) activation and mediates HSC-myofibroblast transition. PBX1 is transcriptionally activated by Notch3 signaling and directly activates IL7R (CD127) expression (PMID: 40080840).

4. **Cardiac regeneration:** PBX1 is a target of post-transcriptional regulation by Hnrnpa1, which promotes Mettl3 splicing to inhibit m6A-dependent Pbx1 degradation. Increased Pbx1 expression promotes cardiomyocyte dedifferentiation and cell cycle activity, triggering daughter cardiomyocyte formation (PMID: 39559922).

5. **Immune regulation:** PBX1-d isoform directly regulates CD44 expression in T cells and recruits MEIS cofactors, influencing regulatory T cell function and autoreactive T cell production (PMID: 28257976).

6. **Facial morphogenesis:** PBX1 is part of a regulatory module (PBX-WNT-TP63-IRF6) controlling facial morphogenesis, with loss of Pbx genes in mice resulting in fully penetrant cleft lip/palate phenotype (ASHG 2013 - Interrogating the PBX-WNT-TP63 pathway in human nonsyndromic cleft lip/palate).

**Expression pattern:** PBX1 is expressed broadly during embryogenesis and in multiple adult tissues including brain, heart, liver, immune tissues, and skeletal elements. The broad expression pattern is consistent with pleiotropic developmental effects.

**Key experimental findings:**

- De novo PBX1 variants impair transactivation ability, nuclear translocation, and cofactor interactions (ASHG 2017).
- PBX1 variants are associated with nonsyndromic cleft lip/palate in human populations (p=0.002 for rs1044263 in Hispanic families) (ASHG 2013).
- PBX1 is identified as a novel obesity susceptibility locus in genome-wide association studies (p<1×10⁻⁵) (ASHG 2013).
- PBX1 is part of a gene expression signature of megakaryocyte expansion in autoimmune diseases (rheumatoid arthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus, primary Sjögren's syndrome) in 119 treatment-naive patients (PMID: 34462261).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

PBX1 has well-characterized molecular functions across multiple developmental and homeostatic pathways. The evidence demonstrates that PBX1 acts as a critical transcriptional regulator in processes directly relevant to the observed human phenotypes (skeletal development, cardiac development, immune regulation, diaphragmatic development). Functional studies of patient variants directly demonstrate loss of transactivation and cofactor interaction capacity.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths:**

1. **Functional validation:** De novo PBX1 variants in patients have been functionally characterized, demonstrating impaired transactivation, nuclear translocation, and cofactor interactions (ASHG 2017).

2. **Comprehensive model organism evidence:** Multiple independent mouse models demonstrate that PBX1 is essential for development of multiple organ systems affected in human patients (skeletal, cardiac, immune, diaphragmatic systems).

3. **Biological pathway evidence:** PBX1 has well-characterized roles in developmental transcriptional regulation, with clear molecular mechanisms linking loss of function to developmental defects.

4. **Phenotypic consistency:** The pleiotropic developmental phenotypes observed in patients (intellectual disability, cardiac malformations, diaphragmatic hernia, skeletal anomalies) are consistent with the known developmental roles of PBX1.

5. **Population genetic evidence:** PBX1 variants are associated with developmental phenotypes in GWAS studies (cleft lip/palate, obesity) and expression studies in autoimmune disease.

**Key gaps and limitations:**

1. **Limited clinical evidence:** Only 7 patients from a single case series (conference abstract, not peer-reviewed). No published case reports in peer-reviewed literature. Lack of independent replication in diagnostic cohorts.

2. **Incomplete phenotypic characterization:** The clinical case series lacks detailed neuropsychological assessment, brain imaging, or systematic evaluation of neurodevelopmental features beyond "intellectual disability."

3. **Heterozygous vs. homozygous effects:** All mouse models involve complete or tissue-specific knockout, not heterozygous loss-of-function. The specific consequences of heterozygous de novo variants in humans may differ from complete loss in mice.

4. **Mechanism of pathogenicity:** While functional studies show impaired transactivation, it is unclear whether the variants act through haploinsufficiency, dominant-negative effects, or altered protein-protein interactions.

5. **Incomplete variant spectrum:** Only missense and truncating variants are described; the full spectrum of pathogenic PBX1 variants and their functional consequences remain to be defined.

**Conclusion:**

PBX1 is a strong candidate gene for autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder based on functional evidence of pathogenic de novo variants, comprehensive model organism data demonstrating developmental roles across multiple organ systems, and well-characterized molecular pathways. However, the clinical evidence remains limited to a single case series of 7 patients. Robust validation requires peer-reviewed publication of additional independent cases, systematic characterization of the neurodevelopmental phenotype, and functional studies of additional PBX1 variants to establish the full spectrum of pathogenic variants and their mechanisms of action.
