# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: GABRB3 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

GABRB3 encodes the beta-3 subunit of the GABA(A) receptor, a key inhibitory neurotransmitter receptor in the central nervous system. Mutations in GABRB3 lead to neurodevelopmental and epileptic disorders primarily through downregulation of receptor expression and reduced amplitude of GABA-evoked inhibitory potentials (PMID: 35850019).

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of Independent Studies:** 10 distinct clinical studies/reports identified

**Total Patient Count:** Approximately 30+ patients with de novo or inherited GABRB3 variants across multiple studies, plus additional cohort-based associations

**Most Common Phenotypes Observed:**
- Epileptic encephalopathies (infantile spasms, Lennox-Gastaut syndrome) — most frequent and severe
- Autism spectrum disorder — multiple independent reports
- Intellectual disability — documented in at least one case
- Behavioral and social interaction deficits
- Sensory perception deficits

**Predominant Variant Types:**
- Loss-of-function (LoF) mutations: 4 de novo cases in infantile spasms/Lennox-Gastaut syndrome (PMID: 23934111; ASHG 2013 - Mefford et al.)
- Missense mutations: 1 de novo case in autism (ASHG 2016 - Turner et al.)
- Copy number variants (CNVs): Exon-disrupting CNVs identified in autism cohorts (ASHG 2011 - Hadley et al.)
- Inherited variants: 14 cases in Indian ASD families (ASHG 2022 - Murthy et al.)
- Promoter SNPs: Associated with heroin addiction in Taiwanese cohort (ASHG 2011 - Huang et al.)

**Cohort Membership:** No explicit mention of UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL cohorts; studies appear to be from independent research groups and ASHG conference presentations

**Strength of Clinical Evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strong due to: (1) Multiple independent de novo mutations in unrelated patients with severe epileptic encephalopathies showing statistical significance (P = 4.1 × 10^-10); (2) Consistent phenotypic associations across different populations; (3) Identification in large-scale WES/WGS studies of autism and epilepsy cohorts; (4) Dysregulation documented in brain tissue from affected individuals (ASHG 2011 - Yasui et al.). However, specific variant details are often not provided in the abstracts, limiting mechanistic interpretation.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms Studied:**
- Mouse (multiple studies with different knockout strategies)
- Zebrafish (one study)

**Key Phenotypes from Knockouts:**

*Full Knockout (Mouse):*
- Increased mortality and spontaneous seizures with EEG abnormalities (PMID: 10515160)
- Behavioral characteristics associated with Angelman syndrome

*Heterozygous Knockout (Mouse):*
- Reduced wild-type β3 subunits at inhibitory synapses with reduced clustering (PMID: 31435640)
- Elevated maternal cytokine/chemokine levels with prenatal immune challenge (ASHG 2017 - Multi-system contributions)
- Impaired neocortical progenitor cell proliferation and altered neuronal subtype specification

*Conditional Knockout (Endothelial Cell-Specific):*
- Reduced vessel densities in telencephalon and neocortex (PMID: 35318369)
- Hypertension and increased cortical blood flow
- Behavioral deficits: impaired reciprocal social interactions, communication deficits, heightened anxiety, depression
- Cognitive deficits: reduced short-term memory, altered prepulse inhibition to startle

*Knock-in (N328D Mutation):*
- Spontaneous seizures, spatial learning deficits, memory impairment, locomotion deficits (PMID: 37176165)

*Zebrafish Knockout:*
- Unprovoked electrographic seizure activity and epilepsy (PMID: 34083748)

**Consistency with Human Phenotype:** Excellent consistency. Mouse models recapitulate the key human phenotypes of seizures, cognitive/memory deficits, social interaction impairment, and anxiety-like behavior. The endothelial cell-specific knockout model is particularly notable for demonstrating that GABRB3 function in non-neuronal tissues contributes to behavioral phenotypes.

**Strength of Model Organism Evidence: STRONG**

Multiple independent model systems (mouse, zebrafish) consistently demonstrate that Gabrb3 loss or mutation causes seizures and neurobehavioral deficits. The dose-dependent effects (heterozygous vs. homozygous) and tissue-specific knockouts provide mechanistic insight into pathogenesis.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known Pathways and Molecular Function:**

GABRB3 functions as a component of the pentameric GABA(A) receptor complex, mediating fast inhibitory synaptic transmission. Pathogenic variants cause disease through multiple mechanisms:

1. **Loss-of-Function:** Reduced receptor expression and decreased GABA-evoked inhibitory currents (PMID: 35850019)

2. **Gain-of-Function:** Missense variants in the M1 proline region (β3P253L, β3P253S) increase GABA sensitivity 3-23 fold, causing hyperexcitability (PMID: 39642202)

3. **Synaptic Dysfunction:** Gabrb3 ablation leads to developmental decrease in GABAergic synapses and increased local network synchrony (PMID: 36446382)

4. **Peripheral Sensory Dysfunction:** Gabrb3 deletion in somatosensory neurons causes mechanosensory dysfunction through loss of presynaptic inhibition, contributing to social and anxiety deficits (PMID: 27293187)

**Expression Pattern:**

- Enriched in contralaterally projecting pyramidal neurons in somatosensory cortex (PMID: 36446382)
- Expressed in inhibitory synapses throughout the brain
- Dysregulated in autism and Rett syndrome brain tissue with reduced MeCP2 protein (ASHG 2011 - Yasui et al.)
- Reduced transcript levels in maternal 15q11-q13 duplication models despite normal promoter methylation (ASHG 2012 - Meguro-Horike et al.; ASHG 2011 - Horike et al.)

**Key Experimental Findings:**

- GABRB3 among 38 genes with exons disrupted by genome-wide statistically significant CNVs in autism (P ≤ 2.76E-08); GABA(A) receptor signaling is the most significantly disrupted canonical pathway in ASD (ASHG 2011 - Hadley et al.)
- Identified in 135 patients with epilepsy of infancy with migrating focal seizures (EIMFS), including mosaic cases (PMID: 31618474)
- Identified as carrying likely pathogenic de novo mutations in moderate-to-severe intellectual disability cohort (PMID: 25356899)
- Meta-analysis of promoter SNPs (rs4906902, rs20317) showed no significant association with autism risk, though aberrant expression is documented (PMID: 30074174)

**Strength of Biological Evidence: STRONG**

The molecular mechanisms are well-characterized, with clear evidence for both loss-of-function and gain-of-function pathogenic mechanisms. Expression studies demonstrate relevance to neurodevelopmental tissues. The identification of GABA(A) receptor signaling as a significantly disrupted pathway in autism provides strong biological plausibility.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall Evidence Level: STRONG**

**Key Strengths:**

1. **Robust Clinical Evidence:** Multiple independent de novo mutations in severe epileptic encephalopathies with high statistical significance (P = 4.1 × 10^-10); consistent phenotypic associations across diverse populations and phenotypes (epilepsy, autism, intellectual disability)

2. **Consistent Model Organism Data:** Multiple independent model systems (mouse, zebrafish) demonstrate that Gabrb3 loss causes seizures and neurobehavioral deficits consistent with human phenotypes; dose-dependent and tissue-specific effects provide mechanistic insight

3. **Well-Characterized Molecular Pathways:** Clear understanding of GABA(A) receptor function and multiple pathogenic mechanisms (LoF, GoF, synaptic dysfunction, peripheral effects); GABA(A) signaling identified as significantly disrupted pathway in autism

4. **Diverse Evidence Sources:** Clinical evidence from multiple independent research groups, large-scale genomic studies (WES/WGS), model organisms, and mechanistic studies

**Key Gaps and Limitations:**

1. **Limited Variant Specificity:** Many clinical reports lack detailed variant information, limiting genotype-phenotype correlation and assessment of specific mutation types

2. **Incomplete Phenotypic Characterization:** Some studies do not provide comprehensive clinical details for all patients

3. **Modest Sample Sizes for Some Phenotypes:** While epilepsy evidence is strong, autism evidence is more limited (single de novo missense case; inherited variants in 14 Indian families)

4. **Lack of Functional Validation:** Most clinical variants lack functional studies demonstrating their pathogenic mechanism

5. **Population Diversity:** Limited representation from non-European populations; most studies from Western cohorts or specific Asian populations

**Conclusion:**

GABRB3 is a well-established neurodevelopmental disorder gene with strong evidence for autosomal dominant pathogenic variants, particularly in severe epileptic encephalopathies (infantile spasms, Lennox-Gastaut syndrome). The convergence of multiple independent de novo mutations with high statistical significance, consistent model organism phenotypes, and well-characterized molecular mechanisms provides compelling evidence for pathogenicity. While the evidence for autism association is more modest, dysregulation of GABRB3 in autism brain tissue and identification in large-scale genomic studies support a role in this phenotype as well. GABRB3 should be considered a high-confidence gene for developmental and epileptic encephalopathy and a candidate gene for autism spectrum disorder.
