# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AP2S1 AND NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

AP2S1 encodes the sigma subunit of the adaptor protein 2 (AP2) complex, a heterotetrameric protein essential for clathrin-mediated endocytosis (PMID: 29479578). The AP2 complex is pivotal for cellular internalization of membrane proteins and has critical roles in embryonic development, organogenesis, synaptic function, and behavioral plasticity.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Case Reports/Series Found:** 0 independent case reports or case series specifically documenting neurodevelopmental phenotypes associated with heterozygous AP2S1 variants.

**Total Patients:** No clinical data available for neurodevelopmental presentations.

**Clinical Phenotypes Observed:** The clinical literature on AP2S1 focuses exclusively on familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia type 3 (FHH3), a calcium homeostasis disorder. Three recurrent AP2S1 mutations were identified across 19 families with FHH3 (PMID: 26963950), and approximately 6% of CASR-negative FHH patients carried AP2S1 mutations (PMID: 27913609). No neurodevelopmental, behavioral, or neurological phenotypes are documented in the clinical literature.

**Variant Type:** Missense variants at codon 15 (p.Arg15Leu, p.Arg15His) are the predominant pathogenic variants identified in FHH3 patients (PMID: 27913609).

**Known Cohorts:** No mention of recruitment from UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL cohorts.

**Strength of Clinical Evidence for Neurodevelopmental Disorder: NONE**

The clinical evidence base does not support AP2S1 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene. All identified human pathogenic variants are associated with calcium homeostasis abnormalities, not neurodevelopmental phenotypes.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms Studied:** Mouse (two independent knock-in studies) and zebrafish (three studies examining behavioral and sensorimotor phenotypes).

**Key Phenotypes from Knockouts:**

*Mouse Studies:*
- Homozygous Ap2s1 mutations are embryonic lethal (E3.5-E9.5 or perinatal), indicating critical roles in early development and organogenesis (PMID: 29479578, PMID: 33729479).
- Heterozygous mice show normal appearance, growth, and normal plasma electrolytes, calcium, phosphate, magnesium, and PTH levels (PMID: 29479578).
- One knock-in model exhibits marked hypercalcaemia and hypermagnesaemia with impaired AP2σ-CaSR protein-protein interaction (PMID: 33729479), consistent with FHH3 pathophysiology rather than neurodevelopmental dysfunction.
- Both studies show sub-Mendelian ratios, consistent with homozygous lethality.

*Zebrafish Studies:*
- AP2 complex knockdown impairs acoustically evoked habituation learning and alters escape behavior choice (PMID: 38550987).
- AP2 knockdown antagonizes dopamine signaling in habituation regulation and impairs behavioral plasticity (PMID: 36972301).
- CaSR (a known AP2S1 interaction partner) knockout causes deficits in sensorimotor choice modulation and impaired behavioral response selection (PMID: 29681477).

**Consistency with Human Phenotype:** The zebrafish behavioral phenotypes (habituation deficits, altered sensorimotor responses) suggest potential neurodevelopmental relevance, but no human neurodevelopmental cases have been documented to validate this connection. The mouse heterozygotes show no obvious neurological phenotypes, and homozygous lethality precludes assessment of neurodevelopmental function.

**Strength of Model Organism Evidence: MODERATE**

Zebrafish studies provide suggestive evidence that AP2 complex dysfunction affects learning and behavioral plasticity, but the absence of documented human neurodevelopmental cases and the lack of detailed neurological phenotyping in heterozygous mice limit the strength of evidence for AP2S1 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known Pathways and Molecular Function:**

AP2S1 is a core component of clathrin-mediated endocytosis (CME), forming a heterotetrameric complex with AP2α, AP2β, and AP2μ subunits (PMID: 29479578). The AP2 complex is essential for:
- Internalization of membrane receptors, including the calcium-sensing receptor (CaSR) (PMID: 33729479)
- Viral entry pathways (AP2S1 cooperates with HAVCR1 for Zika virus internalization) (PMID: 40156834)
- EGFR endocytosis regulation (PMID: 39893205)
- Leptin and LIF receptor signaling (PMID: 29560723)

**Expression Pattern and Neurodevelopmental Relevance:**

AP2S1 is identified as a hub protein in the synaptic vesicle cycle pathway and is significantly downregulated in synaptosomes of vascular dementia rats, suggesting a role in synaptic dysfunction and cognitive decline (PMID: 38990251). This finding indicates AP2S1 has relevance to synaptic function and cognitive processes, though the study examined acquired rather than developmental pathology.

**Key Experimental Findings:**

- AP2S1 interacts with multiple signaling proteins (RNF41, HAVCR1, GPN3, CLTA) across diverse cellular contexts (PMID: 29560723, PMID: 40156834, PMID: 39893205).
- AP2S1 mutations impair protein-protein interactions critical for CaSR function (PMID: 33729479).
- AP2 complex dysfunction affects behavioral plasticity and learning in zebrafish (PMID: 38550987, PMID: 36972301).

**Strength of Biological Evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence demonstrates that AP2S1 has well-characterized molecular functions in endocytosis, synaptic vesicle cycling, and behavioral plasticity. However, the evidence is primarily focused on calcium homeostasis and viral entry rather than neurodevelopmental processes.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall Evidence Level: LIMITED**

**Key Strengths:**
1. AP2S1 has established roles in synaptic function and behavioral plasticity based on zebrafish studies and synaptosomal proteomic data.
2. The gene is essential for embryonic development, as demonstrated by homozygous lethality in mice.
3. AP2S1 is a well-characterized component of clathrin-mediated endocytosis with multiple documented protein interactions.

**Key Gaps and Limitations:**
1. **Absence of clinical evidence:** No case reports or case series document neurodevelopmental phenotypes in patients with AP2S1 variants. All identified human pathogenic variants are associated exclusively with FHH3.
2. **No heterozygous neurological phenotypes in mice:** Heterozygous Ap2s1 mice show normal development and no documented neurological abnormalities, contrasting with the behavioral deficits observed in zebrafish knockdowns.
3. **Lack of functional validation in human neurons:** No studies demonstrate that pathogenic AP2S1 variants impair neuronal function, synaptic transmission, or learning in human-derived systems.
4. **Species-specific effects:** The behavioral phenotypes observed in zebrafish knockdowns have not been replicated or validated in mammalian models.
5. **No segregation data:** No families with neurodevelopmental phenotypes and AP2S1 variants have been reported.

**Conclusion:**

While AP2S1 has documented roles in synaptic function and behavioral plasticity in model organisms, the current evidence does not support AP2S1 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene in humans. The clinical literature exclusively associates AP2S1 pathogenic variants with familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia type 3, a calcium homeostasis disorder. To establish AP2S1 as a neurodevelopmental gene, independent case reports or case series documenting neurodevelopmental phenotypes in patients with heterozygous AP2S1 variants would be required, along with functional studies demonstrating impaired neuronal function.
