# SETBP1 LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT
## Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene with Heterozygous Pathogenic Variants

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

SETBP1 (SET-binding protein 1) is an epigenetic regulator that functions as a SET-binding protein and has been identified as the causative gene of Schinzel-Giedion syndrome (PMID: 37489294). Pathogenic variants in SETBP1, particularly hotspot mutations that disrupt a degron sequence in exon 4, prevent proteasomal degradation and lead to pathogenic protein accumulation, with both SET-dependent and SET-independent functions implicated in neurological disease pathogenesis.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of Independent Studies:** 6 independent case reports/case series with clinical phenotyping data

**Total Patient Count:** Approximately 33 patients with neurodevelopmental phenotypes across clinical studies (27 patients in PMID: 38923504; 3 patients in PMID: 33391157; plus additional cases in PMID: 25217958 and PMID: 37086723)

**Most Common Phenotypes Observed:**
- Intellectual disability (reported in all studies)
- Developmental delay (universal finding)
- Speech and language delays (highly prevalent)
- Autism spectrum disorder (frequent)
- Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (frequent)
- Seizures (reported in multiple studies)
- Hypotonia (common)
- Sleep issues and vision impairment (recurring)
- Restricted interests and repetitive behaviors (autism-related features)

**Predominant Variant Type:** Loss-of-function variants predominate, including nonsense mutations (p.Arg589*, p.Glu545Ter, p.Tyr1066Ter), frameshift deletions (p.Glu734Alafs19*), and gene deletions (haploinsufficiency). Missense variants are less common but reported (e.g., p.Glu858Lys associated with severe phenotype; p.Thr1387Met in PMID: 39350244).

**Cohort Source:** The largest study (PMID: 38923504) utilized Simons Searchlight, a well-established neurodevelopmental disorder cohort. Additional cases identified through targeted sequencing of developmental delay/autism cohorts (PMID: 25217958 analyzed 4,716 additional cases with DD/autism; PMID: 33391157 screened 600 ID cases and 56 pediatric epilepsy cases).

**Strength of Clinical Evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) multiple independent case series with consistent phenotyping; (2) large-scale enrichment studies demonstrating statistical significance of SETBP1 loss-of-function mutations in developmental delay cohorts compared to controls (PMID: 25217958); (3) identification of de novo variants in exome sequencing studies; (4) association with syndromic craniosynostosis reaching genome-wide significance (PMID: 37086723); and (5) comprehensive phenotypic characterization through established research networks.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms Studied:** Mouse models exclusively

**Key Neurological Phenotypes from Knockouts:**

A heterozygous knockout mouse model (Setbp1 +/-) demonstrated behavioral and neurobiological phenotypes directly relevant to human neurodevelopmental disorders (PMID: 41640626):
- Hyperactivity and increased olfactory exploration
- Decreased social interest (autism-relevant)
- Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder-like behaviors
- Hypoexcitability in layer-5 extratelencephalic projection neurons in medial prefrontal cortex
- Altered dendritic arbor morphology in prefrontal pyramidal neurons
- Viable with no reported lethality

**Consistency with Human Phenotype:** Excellent concordance. The mouse model recapitulates core behavioral features observed in SETBP1-HD patients, including ADHD and autism spectrum disorder traits. The prefrontal cortex circuit deficits provide mechanistic insight into the cognitive and behavioral phenotypes.

**Additional Model Findings:** Knock-in models carrying pathogenic SETBP1 mutations (not simple haploinsufficiency) demonstrated severe phenotypes including hydrocephalus (>50% penetrance, perinatal lethal in some cases; PMID: 41612697) and myeloid neoplasm development, indicating that mutation-specific effects beyond simple dosage reduction contribute to pathogenesis.

**Strength of Model Organism Evidence: STRONG**

The heterozygous knockout model provides direct evidence that reduced SETBP1 dosage causes neurodevelopmental and behavioral phenotypes consistent with human disease. Circuit-level mechanistic insights strengthen the evidence. However, the absence of intellectual disability phenotyping in the mouse model (which is difficult to assess in rodents) represents a minor limitation.

---

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known Pathways and Molecular Function:**

SETBP1 functions as an epigenetic regulator with multiple mechanisms of action:

1. **Protein Stability and Degradation:** Pathogenic variants disrupt a degron sequence in exon 4, preventing proteasomal degradation and causing SETBP1 protein accumulation (ASHG 2016). Different mutations show varying effects on protein stability, with functionally stronger mutations correlating with more severe phenotypes.

2. **Histone Modification and Chromatin Remodeling:** SETBP1 mutations promote leukaemogenesis through repression of TGF-β pathway genes via histone deacetylation (PMID: 30367089), indicating roles in chromatin accessibility and gene regulation.

3. **SET-Dependent and SET-Independent Functions:** SETBP1 interacts with the SET protein complex and has independent functions in transcriptional regulation (PMID: 37489294).

4. **MAPK Pathway Activation:** Mutant SETBP1 enhances NRAS-driven MAPK pathway activation (PMID: 34002029), relevant to both oncogenic and potentially neurodevelopmental contexts.

**Expression Pattern:** SETBP1 was identified as a candidate gene in tissue-specific co-expression networks of the thalamic reticular nucleus, a brain region implicated in autism spectrum disorders (ASHG 2018, single-nucleus RNA sequencing of 694 cells from 6 adult mice; AUC = 0.80 for ASD prediction).

**Key Experimental Findings:**

- iPSC-derived neural models from SETBP1-HD patients showed impaired neurogenesis and altered neural progenitor fate choice (PMID: 36805818), providing mechanistic evidence for developmental delay and intellectual disability.
- Truncation mutations result in removal of SKI and/or SET domains, disrupting critical protein-protein interactions (PMID: 39350244).
- Protein network analysis demonstrated that SETBP1 products interact with other intellectual disability-associated genes (PMID: 25356899), supporting its role in a broader ID pathogenesis network.

**Strength of Biological Evidence: STRONG**

Multiple independent lines of evidence establish SETBP1's role in epigenetic regulation, chromatin remodeling, and transcriptional control. iPSC-based disease modeling provides direct evidence of impaired neurogenesis in patient-derived cells. The identification of specific molecular mechanisms (degron disruption, histone deacetylation, MAPK pathway effects) strengthens mechanistic understanding.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall Evidence Level: STRONG**

**Key Strengths:**

1. **Robust Clinical Evidence:** Multiple independent case series and large-scale enrichment studies (>29,000 cases with developmental delay) demonstrate statistically significant association between SETBP1 loss-of-function variants and neurodevelopmental phenotypes.

2. **Consistent Phenotypic Spectrum:** Intellectual disability, developmental delay, speech/language impairment, autism spectrum disorder, and ADHD are consistently reported across independent cohorts, establishing a recognizable clinical syndrome (SETBP1-HD).

3. **Mechanistic Validation:** iPSC-derived neural models, mouse behavioral studies, and molecular pathway analysis provide complementary evidence that SETBP1 haploinsufficiency impairs neurogenesis and prefrontal cortex circuit development.

4. **De Novo Variants:** Multiple studies identified de novo pathogenic variants in unaffected parents, supporting autosomal dominant inheritance with high penetrance.

5. **Functional Specificity:** Distinction between haploinsufficiency effects (neurodevelopmental) and gain-of-function effects (myeloid malignancy) clarifies the pathogenic mechanism for neurodevelopmental disease.

**Key Gaps or Limitations:**

1. **Limited Genotype-Phenotype Correlation:** While missense variants are reported, the relationship between specific variant types and phenotypic severity requires further characterization. The p.Glu858Lys missense variant (PMID: 33391157) was associated with severe phenotype, but sample sizes are small.

2. **Incomplete Phenotypic Characterization:** Some studies report "unspecified variants" without detailed molecular characterization. Standardized phenotyping protocols across cohorts would strengthen genotype-phenotype associations.

3. **Neuropsychiatric Phenotypes:** While autism and ADHD are well-documented, detailed characterization of anxiety, mood disorders, and other psychiatric features is limited.

4. **Incomplete Penetrance and Variable Expressivity:** The degree of incomplete penetrance and factors modifying expressivity remain incompletely characterized.

---

## CONCLUSION

SETBP1 is a well-established neurodevelopmental disorder gene with strong evidence supporting autosomal dominant inheritance of heterozygous loss-of-function variants. The clinical syndrome (SETBP1-HD) is characterized by intellectual disability, developmental delay, speech/language impairment, autism spectrum disorder, and ADHD, with additional features including seizures, hypotonia, and sleep disturbance. Mechanistic evidence from iPSC models and mouse studies demonstrates that SETBP1 haploinsufficiency impairs neurogenesis and prefrontal cortex circuit development. The convergence of large-scale genetic association studies, consistent clinical phenotyping across independent cohorts, and mechanistic validation through cellular and animal models provides compelling evidence for SETBP1's role in neurodevelopmental disease pathogenesis.
