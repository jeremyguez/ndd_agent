# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: PDE4C AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

PDE4C (Phosphodiesterase Type 4C) is a member of the phosphodiesterase type 4 enzyme family that specifically hydrolyzes cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) in intracellular signaling systems (PMID: 37499270). PDE4C exhibits a restricted tissue distribution pattern and is the least characterized of the four PDE4 subfamilies (A, B, C, D), with emerging roles in multiple disease pathways including cancer and potentially neurodevelopmental processes.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

PDE4C functions as a cAMP-degrading enzyme that regulates intracellular cAMP levels across multiple biological contexts. The available evidence identifies PDE4C as a node in several signaling pathways:

- **cAMP/PKA pathway:** PDE4C is a direct target of miR-615-3p; suppression of PDE4C activates cAMP/PKA signaling, which modulates calcium signaling and reduces endoplasmic reticulum stress in spinal cord injury models (PMID: 41526970).

- **TCF21/cAMP-FOXO1 axis:** PDE4C is directly upregulated by transcription factor TCF21 and functions to inhibit intracellular cAMP levels, thereby suppressing FOXO1 expression in endometrial stromal cells (PMID: 39326465).

- **miR-505-5p/AKT/EMT pathway:** In papillary thyroid carcinoma, PDE4C is a target of miR-505-5p; relief of miR-505-5p suppression activates P-AKT and EMT signaling pathways (PMID: 40427523).

- **p53-mediated apoptosis:** PDE4C functions as a tumor suppressor by promoting apoptosis through the wild-type p53 pathway in glioma cells (PMID: 24842301).

**Expression pattern:**

PDE4C exhibits a highly restricted tissue distribution. Notably, PDE4C mRNA and protein were not detected in adult rat urinary bladder tissue, in contrast to other PDE4 isoforms (PMID: 28687079), indicating selective expression in specific tissues. In the nervous system context, altered PDE4C phosphorylation status was observed in Alzheimer's disease plasma samples (PMID: 34182925), with increased frequency of tryptic phosphopeptides from PDE4C in AD patients compared to controls and other disease states. However, direct evidence of PDE4C expression in developing brain or neurodevelopmental tissues is not provided in the available literature.

**Key experimental findings:**

- PDE4C expression is significantly upregulated in thyroid carcinoma tissues and correlates with shorter progression-free survival (PMID: 38514754).

- PDE4C exhibits promoter hypermethylation and reduced expression in high-grade gliomas compared to low-grade gliomas, with hypermethylation associated with grade progression and overall survival (PMID: 24842301).

- PDE4C is transferred from M2 macrophages to osteosarcoma cells via exosomes and increases cell proliferation and mobility (PMID: 38774995).

- ELAVL1 directly interacts with PDE4C mRNA and increases PDE4C mRNA stability, promoting lymph node metastasis in papillary thyroid carcinoma (PMID: 41868681).

**Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE**

The biological evidence demonstrates robust mechanistic understanding of PDE4C function in cAMP signaling and multiple disease pathways, with well-characterized protein interactions and regulatory mechanisms. However, the evidence is predominantly derived from cancer biology and non-neurological contexts, with limited direct evidence of PDE4C function in neurodevelopmental processes or the developing nervous system.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
- Well-characterized molecular function as a cAMP-degrading enzyme
- Multiple validated signaling pathways and protein interactions identified
- Consistent experimental methodologies (ChIP-seq, qPCR, reporter assays, in vivo models)
- Evidence of altered PDE4C status in neurological disease (Alzheimer's disease)

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- Complete absence of clinical evidence: no case reports, case series, or patient cohorts with PDE4C variants and neurodevelopmental phenotypes
- No model organism studies examining PDE4C in neurodevelopmental contexts
- Limited direct evidence of PDE4C expression or function in developing brain tissue
- Existing biological evidence predominantly from cancer biology rather than neurodevelopmental disease
- No functional studies of heterozygous loss-of-function variants in neurodevelopmental models
- Restricted tissue distribution pattern not fully characterized in nervous system

**Conclusion:**

While PDE4C is a well-characterized cAMP-signaling enzyme with validated roles in multiple disease pathways and altered phosphorylation status in Alzheimer's disease, there is currently insufficient evidence to support PDE4C as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous pathogenic variants. The complete absence of clinical case reports, model organism studies, and direct neurodevelopmental functional evidence represents a critical gap. Future work would require identification of de novo or inherited PDE4C variants in neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts, functional characterization of such variants in neuronal models, and investigation of PDE4C expression and function during brain development.
