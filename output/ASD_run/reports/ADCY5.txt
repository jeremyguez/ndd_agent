# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: ADCY5 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

ADCY5 encodes adenylyl cyclase 5, the enzyme responsible for converting ATP to cAMP and the most commonly expressed adenylyl cyclase isoform in medium spiny neurons of the striatum. ADCY5 is a key regulator of dopaminergic, cortical, and thalamic signaling that controls the initiation of voluntary movements and prevention of involuntary movements through the cAMP-dependent pathway (PMID: 33934385).

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports:** Three independent lines of clinical evidence were identified, though detailed case-level data are limited.

**Total patient count:** Approximately 1 patient with definitive ADCY5 involvement; additional patients identified through gene-based burden analyses without individual case specification.

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) with variable additional features including seizures, intellectual disability, and dysmorphic features (ASHG 2014, ASHG 2013)
- Motor speech disorders (PMID: 39931922)

**Predominant variant type:** Unknown—variant specifications were not provided in the clinical evidence abstracts. The ASHG 2014 report noted one uncertain inherited variant among 50 ASD cases, and the ASHG 2013 analysis specifically mentioned de novo mutations in ADCY5 identified in separate reports.

**Cohort source:** One study from Ambry Genetics (Aliso Viejo, CA, USA); others from unspecified clinical exome sequencing cohorts.

**Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED**

The clinical evidence is limited by: (1) lack of detailed variant information and functional classification; (2) small number of definitive cases; (3) reliance on gene-based association analyses rather than individual case reports; (4) uncertain diagnostic significance in at least one reported case; and (5) absence of segregation data or functional validation. However, the convergence of ASD and motor speech phenotypes across independent studies provides some support for neurodevelopmental involvement.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (3 studies), zebrafish (1 study), tilapia (1 study—though this study focused on mpv17, not ADCY5, and is not directly relevant).

**Key neurological phenotypes from knockouts:**

*Mouse models:*
- Loss of ADCY5 in the dorsal striatum produces autistic-like behaviors including increased repetitive behaviors, sociability deficits, and deficits in social novelty and object novelty preferences, with upregulation of mGluR5 functions in the dorsal striatum (PMID: 27878759)
- Knock-in models with altered striatal cAMP signaling produce mixed hyperkinetic movement disorders, dystonia/dyskinesia phenotypes, and motor symptoms responsive to caffeine treatment (PMID: 41660259)
- Full-body knockout mice show improved cardiac function and increased longevity with no obesity or glucose intolerance, indicating tissue-specific effects (PMID: 33919448)

*Zebrafish models:*
- Adcy5 knockout (in combination with adcy3a) causes defects in melanosome dispersion and impaired adult melanocyte stripe formation through effects on Mitfa and melanin synthesis enzymes (PMID: 36430661)

**Lethality and viability:** All ADCY5 knockout models are viable with no reported lethality or Mendelian ratio deviations.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The dorsal striatum-specific knockout producing autism-like behaviors and the hyperkinetic movement disorder models show strong consistency with the human neurodevelopmental phenotypes (ASD, motor speech disorders). The striatal cAMP dysregulation mechanism is directly relevant to motor control and social behavior.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**

The model organism evidence is strong because: (1) multiple independent mouse models demonstrate neurodevelopmental and motor phenotypes consistent with human presentations; (2) the striatal localization and cAMP pathway mechanism are well-characterized; (3) phenotypes are reproducible across studies; and (4) the models provide mechanistic insight into how ADCY5 dysfunction produces behavioral abnormalities.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

ADCY5 functions as a critical node in cAMP signaling within the striatum and other brain regions. The enzyme synthesizes cAMP from ATP, and striatal cAMP levels are determined by the balance between ADCY5-mediated synthesis and PDE10A-mediated degradation (PMID: 30345538). Gain-of-function mutations dysregulate the cAMP pathway, reducing inhibitory activity and leading to involuntary hyperkinetic movements (PMID: 33934385). In the context of autism-related phenotypes, ADCY5 loss in the dorsal striatum leads to upregulation of mGluR5 signaling, a pathway implicated in autism spectrum disorders.

**Expression pattern:**

ADCY5 is highly expressed in the striatum (particularly in medium spiny neurons) and also expressed in cortical and thalamic regions relevant to motor control and social behavior. Beyond the nervous system, ADCY5 is expressed in adipose tissue and pancreatic islets, where it influences glucose homeostasis and insulin secretion (PMID: 25793868, PMID: 20081858).

**Key experimental findings:**

- Gene-based burden analysis identified ADCY5 as significantly associated with motor speech disorders after exome-wide correction (PMID: 39931922)
- Gene-based association analysis (SKAT-O p=0.002) identified ADCY5 as the most significantly associated ASD candidate gene, with reports of de novo mutations (ASHG 2013)
- ADCY5 mutations are associated with decreased PDE10A expression in striatum and globus pallidus, decreased dopamine transporter expression, loss of substantia nigra neuromelanin-containing neurons, and microstructural white and gray matter changes within striatocortical pathways in 6 subjects (PMID: 30345538)
- GWAS meta-analysis of 46,186 nondiabetic participants identified ADCY5 as a novel locus associated with fasting glucose and beta-cell function (p<5×10⁻⁸), with follow-up in 76,558 additional subjects (PMID: 20081858)
- Functional studies demonstrate that the rs11708067-A T2D risk allele shows decreased enhancer activity in insulinoma cells and decreased ADCY5 expression in islet samples (p=6.5×10⁻¹³) (ASHG 2015)

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is strong because: (1) ADCY5's role in cAMP signaling is well-established and mechanistically linked to motor control and social behavior; (2) multiple independent functional studies confirm the gene's involvement in critical signaling pathways; (3) the striatal localization and neurobiological mechanisms are directly relevant to the observed neurodevelopmental phenotypes; and (4) large-scale GWAS and functional genomics studies provide robust support for ADCY5's biological significance.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths:**
1. Convergent evidence from multiple independent clinical studies identifying ADCY5 in neurodevelopmental disorders (ASD, motor speech disorders) with gene-based association reaching exome-wide significance
2. Strong biological evidence for ADCY5's role in striatal cAMP signaling, a pathway directly relevant to motor control and social behavior
3. Robust model organism evidence demonstrating that ADCY5 loss in the dorsal striatum produces autism-like behaviors and that altered cAMP signaling produces hyperkinetic movement disorders
4. Well-characterized molecular mechanism linking ADCY5 dysfunction to the observed phenotypes
5. Large-scale GWAS support for ADCY5's biological significance in neurodevelopmental and metabolic pathways

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. Limited number of definitive human cases with detailed variant information and functional classification
2. Lack of segregation data or family studies demonstrating autosomal dominant inheritance
3. Absence of detailed phenotypic characterization in most clinical reports
4. No clear distinction between loss-of-function and gain-of-function mechanisms in the reported human cases
5. Limited evidence specifically for heterozygous pathogenic variants; most model organism evidence derives from complete knockouts
6. Uncertain diagnostic significance in at least one reported case (inherited variant of uncertain significance)

**Conclusion:**

ADCY5 shows moderate evidence as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene, with convergent clinical, biological, and model organism evidence supporting its involvement in autism spectrum disorder and motor speech disorders. The striatal cAMP signaling pathway mechanism is well-characterized and biologically plausible for producing the observed neurodevelopmental phenotypes. However, the clinical evidence remains limited by small case numbers and incomplete variant characterization. Additional well-documented cases with detailed functional studies of specific variants, particularly distinguishing gain-of-function from loss-of-function mechanisms, would strengthen the evidence for ADCY5 as a monogenic neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous pathogenic variants.
