# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: EBF3 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

EBF3 (Early B Cell Factor 3) is a transcription factor belonging to the early B cell factor family that plays critical roles in neuronal differentiation, cell fate specification, and transcriptional regulation of developmental genes. EBF3 contains a DNA-binding domain and a zinc finger domain essential for stabilizing DNA-binding interactions and transcriptional activation of target genes involved in neurodevelopment and skeletal development.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of Studies and Patient Cohort:**
Six independent clinical studies were identified, encompassing approximately 127 patients with EBF3-related neurodevelopmental disorders. The largest study included 83 patients (PMID: 35340043), followed by a Dutch cohort of 34 patients with progressive myoclonus ataxia (PMID: 38844245), and smaller case series ranging from 1-7 patients.

**Most Common Phenotypes:**
The clinical presentation is remarkably consistent across studies, with the following core features:
- Hypotonia (present in nearly all studies)
- Developmental delay / global developmental delay (universal finding)
- Ataxia (reported in 5 of 6 studies)
- Autism spectrum disorder (reported in 5 of 6 studies)
- Dysmorphic facial features and short stature (reported in multiple studies)
- Progressive myoclonus (noted in the Dutch cohort; PMID: 38844245)
- Cognitive impairment / intellectual disability (variable severity)
- Behavioral problems and epilepsy (in some patients)

**Phenotypic Severity Stratification:**
A critical finding from the largest study (PMID: 35340043) is that EBF3 missense variants affecting the zinc finger domain (ZNF) show significantly higher symptom severity with developmental delay and hypotonia, compared to missense variants outside the ZNF (e.g., p.Arg209Trp) which show lower severity. This establishes a clear genotype-phenotype correlation based on functional domain disruption.

**Predominant Variant Types:**
The evidence encompasses multiple variant classes:
- Loss-of-function variants (frameshift, nonsense, splice site) reported in 4 studies
- Missense variants reported in 5 studies, with domain-specific functional consequences
- Noncoding variants in the hs737 enhancer region (PMID: 34256850) associated with milder phenotypes and male predominance
- Copy number deletions (PMID: 33335013)

All variants identified were de novo heterozygous mutations, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance with haploinsufficiency as the primary mechanism (PMID: 29162653).

**Cohort Information:**
One study explicitly identified patients from a Dutch cohort (PMID: 38844245). Most studies do not specify enrollment from major diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL), though the large study of 83 patients (PMID: 35340043) appears to represent a meta-analysis combining newly identified cases with literature review.

**Diagnostic Confirmation:**
Variants were identified through whole-exome sequencing (WES) or whole-genome sequencing (WGS). Functional validation was performed in select studies: ZNF-disrupting variants showed impaired transcriptional activation in luciferase assays and failed to rescue viability in Drosophila models (PMID: 35340043). Noncoding variants were shown to reduce enhancer activity in neuronal cell lines (PMID: 34256850).

**Strength of Clinical Evidence: STRONG**

The clinical evidence is strong, supported by: (1) multiple independent case series totaling >120 patients; (2) consistent, recognizable phenotype across studies; (3) genome-wide significant p-values for de novo variants in neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts (missense p=8.12×10⁻³⁵, LoF p=2.26×10⁻¹³; PMID: 34256850); (4) clear genotype-phenotype correlations based on functional domain disruption; and (5) functional validation demonstrating pathogenic mechanisms.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms Studied:**
Mouse (primary model, 8 studies), zebrafish (1 study), Drosophila (referenced in clinical studies for functional validation), and Xenopus laevis (expression analysis only).

**Key Neurological and Developmental Phenotypes:**

*Perinatal Lethality:*
Full knockout of Ebf3 in mice results in perinatal lethality due to respiratory failure caused by lung failure to unfold at birth (PMID: 24786561). This is attributed to a hypercontractile diaphragm with impaired calcium efflux-related muscle functions, resulting from downregulated expression of the Ca²⁺ pump Serca1 (Atp2a1). This finding demonstrates that complete loss of Ebf3 is incompatible with postnatal survival, supporting the haploinsufficiency model in humans where heterozygous variants cause disease.

*Regulatory Region Knockouts:*
Heterozygous and homozygous knockouts of an EBF3 enhancer (Rr169617) in mice show sub-Mendelian ratios (p=0.02 for deviation from Mendelian expectation; p=5.8×10⁻⁴ for depletion of deletion allele), indicating reduced viability of homozygous deletion mice (PMID: 41081394, PMID: 39829799). These mice exhibit:
- Reduced Ebf3 expression (10% reduction in heterozygotes, 20% in homozygotes)
- Dysregulated genes including histone and brain development-related genes (Chd5, Ntng1) in E12.5 forebrain, midbrain, and hindbrain
- Sex-specific behavioral differences: homozygous males show reduced mobility, slower movement, and increased time in dark environments
- Altered open field, hole board, and light/dark transition test results

*Neuronal Development:*
Ebf3 is dynamically expressed during dopaminergic neuron development and acts as a target for miR218 (PMID: 25192643). Ebf3 overexpression at the neural precursor stage significantly increases TH+ dopaminergic neuron development, while suppression reduces dopaminergic neuron development, establishing Ebf3 as a critical regulator of dopaminergic neurogenesis.

Ebf3-expressing glutamatergic neurons are generated in the caudo-ventral pallium and medial amygdala; in Pax6 knockout mice, these Ebf3-expressing neurons fail to be generated (PMID: 29869132), indicating Ebf3's role in cell fate specification.

*Skeletal and Connective Tissue Development:*
Ebf3 is required for sternum ossification through a transient, lineage-restricted mechanism (PMID: 32398354). Ebf3 knockout mice show defective generation of Runx2+ pre-osteoblasts with stage-specific requirements at embryonic day 9.5-10.5 in lateral plate mesoderm cells. Ebf3 marks tenocytes and connective tissue cells, and its loss leads to upregulation of connective tissue markers (Egr1/2, Osr1) and downregulation of Shox2.

*Hematopoietic and Bone Marrow Niche Function:*
Conditional knockout of Ebf3 in CAR/LepR+ cells impairs hematopoietic stem cell numbers and leads to osteosclerosis, demonstrating Ebf3's role in maintaining the bone marrow niche (PMID: 29593065).

**Consistency with Human Phenotype:**
The mouse models show partial consistency with human neurodevelopmental phenotypes. The behavioral abnormalities (reduced mobility, altered light/dark preference) in enhancer knockout mice align with developmental delay and motor dysfunction in humans. However, the mouse models do not fully recapitulate the autism spectrum disorder, ataxia, or progressive myoclonus phenotypes observed in human patients, likely because heterozygous regulatory region knockouts produce only modest reductions in Ebf3 expression (10-20%), whereas human pathogenic variants may have more severe functional consequences.

**Strength of Model Organism Evidence: MODERATE**

The model organism evidence is moderate in strength. Strengths include: (1) multiple independent mouse studies demonstrating Ebf3's critical role in neurodevelopment and skeletal development; (2) clear demonstration that complete loss of Ebf3 is embryonic/perinatal lethal, supporting haploinsufficiency in humans; (3) identification of Ebf3's role in dopaminergic neuron development and cell fate specification; and (4) sub-Mendelian ratios in enhancer knockout mice suggesting dosage sensitivity. Limitations include: (1) lack of mouse models with heterozygous loss-of-function variants (frameshift, nonsense) that directly parallel human pathogenic variants; (2) incomplete recapitulation of human neurological phenotypes (autism, ataxia, myoclonus) in available models; and (3) limited neuroimaging or detailed behavioral characterization in most studies.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Molecular Function and Transcriptional Activity:**

EBF3 functions as a transcription factor with domain-specific roles. The zinc finger domain is critical for stabilizing DNA-binding interactions; missense variants disrupting this domain impair transcriptional activation and fail to rescue viability in Drosophila (PMID: 35340043). EBF3 variants cause mislocalization, reduced DNA-binding activity, and impaired transcriptional activation of target genes such as CDKN1A, as demonstrated by ChIP-seq and RNA-seq (PMID: 28017373).

**Known Target Genes and Pathways:**

1. *Ferroptosis Regulation:* EBF3 acts as a transcription factor binding to the ALOX15 promoter to enhance its expression, promoting ferroptosis-mediated cell death. In acute myeloid leukemia, the circadian clock protein Bmal1 suppresses EBF3 expression through EZH2-mediated promoter methylation, establishing EBF3 as a key node in ferroptosis regulation (PMID: 37271497).

2. *Muscle Relaxation and Calcium Handling:* EBF3 binds directly to the Atp2a1 (Serca1) promoter and synergizes with MyoD to induce its expression, controlling muscle relaxation via calcium efflux (PMID: 24786561). Loss of Ebf3 results in downregulated Serca1 expression and hypercontractile diaphragm.

3. *Epithelial-Mesenchymal Transition:* EBF3 directly binds the Vimentin promoter to upregulate its expression, promoting epithelial-mesenchymal transition and metastasis in nasopharyngeal carcinoma (PMID: 34906623). EBF3 is epigenetically silenced by the EGR1/EZH2/HDAC9 complex via H3K27-Me3 deposition.

4. *Notch Signaling:* EBF3 is identified as a downstream mediator of Notch signaling in skeletal stem and progenitor cells (PMID: 37752132), with dysregulation during aging contributing to skeletal aging phenotypes.

5. *Neurogenic Transcription:* EBF3 is a direct target of MYC transcription factors in accessible chromatin sites and is essential for the primitive neuronal component phenotype in glioblastomas with concurrent RB1 and TP53 pathway inactivation (PMID: 39821672).

**Expression Pattern and Developmental Relevance:**

EBF3 is expressed in multiple neurodevelopmentally relevant tissues:
- Dopaminergic neurons in the midbrain (PMID: 25192643)
- Cajal-Retzius cells and glutamatergic neurons in the medial amygdala (PMID: 29869132)
- Forebrain, midbrain, and hindbrain at embryonic stages (PMID: 41081394, PMID: 39829799)
- Tenocytes and connective tissue cells in skeletal muscle (PMID: 32398354)
- Cranial motor neurons (PMID: 39333082)

**Non-coding Variant Regulation:**

Single-cell epigenomic profiling of ~86,000 cranial motor neurons identified EBF3 as a disease gene in congenital cranial dysinnervation disorders (PMID: 39333082). Non-coding variants predicted to regulate EBF3 were nominated in unsolved cases, with 75% validation rate of predicted enhancers (44/59), establishing EBF3 as a key developmental regulator in motor neuron specification.

**Strength of Biological Evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is strong, supported by: (1) extensive characterization of EBF3 as a transcription factor with multiple validated target genes; (2) identification of domain-specific functional requirements (zinc finger domain critical for DNA-binding stability); (3) demonstration of EBF3's role in multiple neurodevelopmentally relevant pathways (dopaminergic neurogenesis, cell fate specification, motor neuron development); (4) single-cell epigenomic evidence linking EBF3 to motor neuron specification; and (5) functional validation of both coding and non-coding variants affecting EBF3 expression and activity.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall Evidence Level: STRONG**

EBF3 is a well-established neurodevelopmental disorder gene with compelling evidence across all three domains (clinical, model organism, and biological).

**Key Strengths:**

1. **Robust Clinical Evidence:** Over 120 patients across 6 independent studies with consistent, recognizable phenotype (hypotonia, developmental delay, ataxia, autism spectrum disorder). Genome-wide significant p-values for de novo variants in neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts (PMID: 34256850).

2. **Clear Genotype-Phenotype Correlation:** Missense variants affecting the zinc finger domain show significantly higher symptom severity than variants outside this domain (PMID: 35340043), with functional validation demonstrating impaired transcriptional activation.

3. **Comprehensive Biological Characterization:** EBF3 is a well-characterized transcription factor with multiple validated target genes and roles in dopaminergic neurogenesis, motor neuron specification, and cell fate determination. Domain-specific functional requirements are established.

4. **Supporting Model Organism Evidence:** Mouse studies demonstrate that complete loss of Ebf3 is perinatal lethal, supporting haploinsufficiency in humans. Regulatory region knockouts show sub-Mendelian ratios and behavioral abnormalities consistent with neurodevelopmental dysfunction.

5. **Multiple Variant Classes:** Evidence encompasses loss-of-function variants (frameshift, nonsense, splice), missense variants with domain-specific effects, and non-coding variants in regulatory regions, all showing de novo heterozygous inheritance.

**Key Limitations:**

1. **Incomplete Phenotypic Recapitulation in Models:** Available mouse models do not fully recapitulate autism spectrum disorder, progressive myoclonus, or severe ataxia observed in human patients. Most mouse models use regulatory region knockouts producing modest expression reductions (10-20%) rather than complete loss-of-function variants.

2. **Limited Mechanistic Understanding of Specific Phenotypes:** While EBF3's role in dopaminergic neurogenesis and motor neuron development is established, the molecular basis for autism spectrum disorder, progressive myoclonus, and ataxia remains incompletely understood.

3. **Neuroimaging Findings:** Only two patients have detailed neuroimaging (PMID: 34177436), limiting understanding of structural brain abnormalities associated with EBF3 variants.

4. **Cohort Representation:** Most studies do not specify enrollment from major diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD), limiting ascertainment bias assessment.

**Conclusion:**

EBF3 is a high-confidence neurodevelopmental disorder gene with autosomal dominant inheritance through heterozygous loss-of-function and missense variants. The clinical evidence is robust, supported by >120 patients with consistent phenotypes and genome-wide significant p-values for de novo variants. Functional studies demonstrate domain-specific pathogenic mechanisms, with zinc finger domain variants showing greater severity. While model organism evidence is supportive but incomplete, the comprehensive biological characterization of EBF3 as a transcription factor with critical roles in neuronal development provides strong mechanistic support for its role in neurodevelopmental disease.
