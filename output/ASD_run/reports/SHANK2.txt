# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: SHANK2 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

SHANK2 encodes a postsynaptic scaffolding protein localized at glutamatergic synapses in the central nervous system that plays a critical role in synaptogenesis, synaptic plasticity, and neuronal development (PMID: 28179641). The gene demonstrates extreme transcriptional complexity with approximately 500 isoforms in normal human brain, with 76% of transcripts retaining coding capability and showing tissue- and cell-type-specific diversity (ASHG 2022).

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of Studies and Patient Count:**
Seven independent clinical studies were identified, encompassing approximately 29 patients with SHANK2-related neurodevelopmental disorder across case reports, case series, and cohort analyses. These include 11 patients from a phenotypic spectrum study (PMID: 32987185), 10 patients from a protein-truncating variant analysis (PMID: 40307697), 6 patients from a Brazilian mutation screening cohort (ASHG 2018), and individual case reports from ASHG conferences, plus additional cases identified in large-scale genomic studies.

**Most Common Phenotypes:**
The core phenotypic features of SHANK2-related neurodevelopmental disorder include:
- Autism spectrum disorder (90% in the largest series; PMID: 40307697)
- Mild to moderate intellectual disability
- Speech delay and poor language skills
- Developmental delays and delayed motor milestones
- Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (50%; PMID: 40307697)
- Hypotonia
- Sensory abnormalities (hyperreactivity and seeking behaviors)
- Gastrointestinal abnormalities and recurrent ear infections

**Predominant Variant Types:**
Loss-of-function variants predominate in the clinical literature, including de novo point mutations resulting in early stop codons (PMID: 38704506), deletions (PMID: 40307697), and gene-disrupting variants identified in large cohorts (ASHG 2016). Missense variants have also been reported in association with autism (ASHG 2018), though their pathogenicity requires further characterization. One synonymous variant was identified in a patient with autism, though its significance is unclear.

**Cohort Membership:**
Cases were identified from the Simons Simplex Collection and MSSNG cohorts (ASHG 2016), as well as from clinical centers in Brazil and Europe. No cases were explicitly reported from DDD, UDN, GREGoR, RUMC, or GEL cohorts in the provided evidence.

**Inheritance Pattern:**
The majority of reported cases involve de novo variants, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. One case showed paternal inheritance of an 11q13.4 deletion disrupting SHANK2, with the sister also affected (ASHG 2015), suggesting potential familial transmission. Several missense variants were identified in parents with broad autism phenotype (ASHG 2018), indicating possible incomplete penetrance or variable expressivity in heterozygous carriers.

**Strength of Clinical Evidence: STRONG**

The clinical evidence is strengthened by: (1) consistent phenotypic features across multiple independent studies; (2) identification of de novo variants in large-scale genomic studies of autism cohorts; (3) functional validation of a pathogenic variant through patient-derived iPSC studies showing impaired dopamine neuron differentiation (PMID: 38704506); and (4) demonstration that genetic correction rescues the cellular phenotype. However, the total patient count remains modest (~29 patients), and detailed variant-level segregation data are limited.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms Studied:**
Mouse models are the primary organisms studied, with multiple independent Shank2 knockout lines generated and characterized.

**Key Phenotypes from Knockouts:**
Shank2-deficient mice demonstrate a broad spectrum of neuropsychiatric and behavioral phenotypes consistent with human neurodevelopmental disorders:

- **Autism-like behaviors:** Social interaction deficits, altered ultrasonic vocalizations, and deficits in sociability with intact social recognition memory (PMID: 30072871; PMID: 33126897)
- **Motor and cerebellar dysfunction:** Impaired motor coordination, reduced excitatory synapse density in cerebellar Purkinje cells with decreased GluD2 and PSD-93 levels (PMID: 27903723)
- **Behavioral inflexibility:** Impaired reversal learning and enhanced fear responses in males, with sex-dependent phenotypes (PMID: 36192805)
- **Repetitive and anxiety-like behaviors:** Enhanced repetitive behavior in hole-board tests and anxiety-like behavior in light-dark tests (PMID: 27903723)
- **Mania-like phenotype:** Significantly increased locomotor activity, abnormal reward-seeking behavior, anhedonia, and circadian rhythm perturbations that respond to mood stabilizers (lithium and valproate; PMID: 29046483)
- **Altered nociception:** Increased sensitivity to formalin pain, sensory-specific mechanical allodynia, and altered thermal preference due to decreased NMDAR in excitatory synapses on glycinergic interneurons (PMID: 37316943)
- **Skeletal phenotype:** Decreased bone mass and reduced osteoblastogenesis (PMID: 36751416)

**Lethality and Mendelian Ratios:**
All Shank2 knockout mice are viable and fertile, with no reported deviations from expected Mendelian ratios, indicating that complete loss of Shank2 is not embryonic lethal.

**Consistency with Human Phenotype:**
The mouse phenotypes show strong concordance with human clinical features: autism-like behaviors and social deficits parallel autism spectrum disorder in patients; motor coordination deficits align with developmental delays; and behavioral inflexibility and anxiety-like behaviors correspond to the neurodevelopmental and psychiatric features observed clinically. The identification of mania-like behavior in mice (PMID: 29046483) is particularly notable, as it suggests SHANK2 variants may contribute to mood dysregulation in addition to autism and intellectual disability.

**Strength of Model Organism Evidence: STRONG**

Multiple independent mouse studies consistently demonstrate neuropsychiatric and behavioral phenotypes relevant to human neurodevelopmental disorders. The viability of knockouts, combined with detailed characterization of molecular, electrophysiological, and behavioral alterations, provides robust support for SHANK2's role in neurodevelopment. The demonstration that mania-like phenotypes respond to mood stabilizers adds translational relevance.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known Pathways and Molecular Function:**

SHANK2 functions as a postsynaptic scaffolding protein at glutamatergic synapses, where it organizes the postsynaptic density and regulates synaptic strength and plasticity. The protein is involved in:

- **Synaptogenesis and synaptic architecture:** SHANK2 regulates excitatory synapse density and is critical for establishing proper synaptic organization (PMID: 27903723)
- **Stem cell fate determination:** SHANK2 plays a role in regulating self-renewal, proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, and metabolism in neural stem cells and iPSCs (PMID: 36257935)
- **Dopamine neuron development:** Patient-derived iPSCs carrying the SHANK2B Y29X mutation fail to differentiate into functional dopamine neurons, with impaired dopamine release in the ventral tegmental area (PMID: 38704506)
- **Auditory hair cell development:** SHANK2 localizes to the medial apical surface of developing hair cells and is essential for stereociliary bundle architecture and hearing (PMID: 40627398)
- **Nociceptive processing:** SHANK2 identifies a subset of glycinergic interneurons involved in pain processing, with loss of Shank2 leading to altered NMDAR composition and pain hypersensitivity (PMID: 37316943)

**Expression Pattern:**
SHANK2 is widely expressed in the brain with region- and cell-type-specific patterns. Brain region- and gene dosage-dependent transcriptomic changes occur in Shank2-mutant mice, with heterozygous mutants in prefrontal cortex showing increased synaptic gene expression (reverse-ASD pattern), while homozygous mutants show decreased synaptic gene expression and ASD-like patterns (PMID: 36311025). Sex hormones regulate SHANK2 expression, with both dihydrotestosterone and 17β-estradiol increasing expression in neuronal cells, potentially explaining sex bias in SHANK-associated autism (ASHG 2018).

**Key Experimental Findings:**

- **Functional validation:** Patient-derived iPSCs with SHANK2B Y29X mutation show impaired dopamine neuron differentiation, which is rescued by genetic correction (PMID: 38704506)
- **Regulatory interactions:** miR-137 directly targets the 3'UTR of SHANK2 and translationally represses SHANK2 protein levels without affecting mRNA (ASHG 2018)
- **Protein interactions:** SHANK2 is identified as an FMRP RNA binding target associated with high autism risk, with age- and brain region-dependent binding patterns (ASHG 2022)
- **Antisense regulation:** The autism-associated lncRNA Shank2-AS is upregulated in autism patients while SHANK2 expression is downregulated; overexpression of Shank2-AS decreases neurite numbers and lengths and promotes apoptosis (PMID: 30160788)
- **Signaling pathway involvement:** SHANK2 expression is upregulated in response to treatment in ischemic stroke models, suggesting involvement in synaptic plasticity through the cAMP/PKA/CREB signaling pathway (PMID: 39089658)

**Strength of Biological Evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is comprehensive and multi-faceted, demonstrating SHANK2's critical roles in synaptic organization, neuronal development, and neurotransmitter systems. Functional validation through patient-derived iPSC studies, identification of regulatory mechanisms (miR-137, Shank2-AS, sex hormones), and characterization of molecular alterations in knockout models provide strong mechanistic support for pathogenicity of SHANK2 variants.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall Evidence Level: STRONG**

SHANK2 meets criteria for a well-established neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous (autosomal dominant) pathogenic variants. The evidence is supported by:

**Key Strengths:**
1. **Consistent clinical phenotype:** Multiple independent studies document a recognizable phenotypic spectrum centered on autism spectrum disorder, intellectual disability, speech delay, and developmental delays in approximately 29 patients with SHANK2 variants
2. **Functional validation:** Patient-derived iPSC studies demonstrate that pathogenic variants impair neuronal differentiation and can be rescued by genetic correction (PMID: 38704506)
3. **Robust model organism evidence:** Multiple independent Shank2 knockout mouse lines consistently demonstrate autism-like behaviors, motor deficits, behavioral inflexibility, and mood dysregulation that parallel human phenotypes
4. **Mechanistic understanding:** Well-characterized molecular function as a postsynaptic scaffolding protein with identified regulatory mechanisms (miR-137, sex hormones, FMRP interactions) and involvement in critical developmental pathways
5. **Large-scale genomic support:** Identification of de novo SHANK2 variants in large autism cohorts (Simons Simplex Collection, MSSNG) with low false negative rates for gene-disrupting mutations

**Key Gaps and Limitations:**
1. **Limited patient cohort size:** Approximately 29 patients across all studies is modest compared to other established neurodevelopmental genes; larger case series would strengthen clinical characterization
2. **Incomplete segregation data:** Limited information on familial inheritance patterns and incomplete penetrance; most cases are de novo, limiting segregation analysis
3. **Missense variant classification:** Several missense variants have been reported in association with autism, but their pathogenicity relative to loss-of-function variants requires further characterization
4. **Phenotypic heterogeneity:** Variable expressivity is evident, with some patients showing mild features while others have moderate intellectual disability; genotype-phenotype correlations are not yet established
5. **Absence from major diagnostic cohorts:** No cases explicitly reported from DDD, UDN, GREGoR, or other major diagnostic sequencing initiatives, which may reflect ascertainment bias or true rarity

**Conclusion:**

SHANK2 is a well-supported neurodevelopmental disorder gene in which heterozygous loss-of-function variants cause a recognizable syndrome characterized by autism spectrum disorder, intellectual disability, speech delay, and developmental delays. The convergence of clinical evidence from multiple independent studies, functional validation through patient-derived cellular models, consistent phenotypes in multiple mouse knockout lines, and detailed mechanistic understanding of SHANK2's role in synaptic development and function collectively establish SHANK2 as a high-confidence autism and neurodevelopmental disorder gene. Future work should focus on expanding the clinical cohort, characterizing genotype-phenotype correlations, and determining the pathogenicity of missense variants.
