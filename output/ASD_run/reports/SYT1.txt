# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: SYT1 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

SYT1 (synaptotagmin-1) is the major isoform among 16 synaptotagmin proteins and serves as an essential synaptic vesicle protein that mediates calcium-triggered neurotransmitter release during synaptic vesicle exocytosis (PMID: 24692137). The protein interacts with target membranes and SNARE/complexin complexes, with the affinities of these Ca2+-dependent interactions correlating with the efficacy of exocytotic processes.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports:** 2 distinct clinical studies were identified.

**Total patient count:** Approximately 16 patients across studies (1 patient with detailed de novo missense mutation; 15 patients with deleterious SYT1 variations in an ASD cohort).

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Developmental delay
- Autism spectrum disorder (ASD)
- Autistic symptoms
- Motor delay and intellectual disability (referenced in review literature)

**Predominant variant type:** Mixed. One well-characterized de novo missense mutation (c.1201C>T, p.Pro401Leu) was identified with functional validation; the second study identified "deleterious variations" without specific variant details provided.

**Cohort source:** The larger ASD study (15 patients) was conducted in India but does not appear to be from established international cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL).

**Functional validation:** The p.Pro401Leu variant (PMID: 38321119) demonstrated a dominant-negative effect in cultured neurons with increased spontaneous and asynchronous neurotransmitter release, reduced dendrite outgrowth, and desynchronization of neurotransmission—directly linking the variant to impaired synaptic function.

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**

The evidence is strengthened by functional validation of one variant and identification of SYT1 in multiple independent patient cohorts with neurodevelopmental phenotypes. However, the total patient number is small, variant details are incomplete in one study, and patients are not from major international diagnostic cohorts.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (Mus musculus)

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

*Heterozygous knockout (PMID: 36031564):*
- Decreased Syt1-positive cells across multiple brain regions (prelimbic cortex, hippocampus, amygdala, accumbens nucleus, caudoputamen, ventral tegmental area)
- Reduced total movement distance in open field test
- Increased preference for peripheral areas (anxiety-like behavior)
- Reduced open-arm exploration in elevated plus maze
- Increased immobile time in forced swim test
- Elevated glutamate concentration in amygdala
- Overall phenotype: anxiety-like behavior and reduced exploratory activity (p<0.01 for most measures)

*Conditional knockout in retinal photoreceptors (PMID: 36280223):*
- Syt1 deletion from rods: reduced glutamate release evoked by short depolarizing steps
- Double deletion of Syt1 and Syt7: completely abolished depolarization-evoked release
- Syt1 deletion from rods and cones: abolished ERG b-waves
- Demonstrates isoform-specific and temporal regulation of neurotransmitter release

**Lethality and Mendelian ratios:** Both heterozygous and conditional knockouts are viable; no lethality reported. Mendelian ratios not detailed.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The heterozygous knockout phenotype (anxiety-like behavior, reduced exploratory activity, altered glutamate signaling) is consistent with neurodevelopmental and behavioral manifestations observed in human SYT1-associated disorder. The functional impairment in neurotransmitter release directly parallels the dominant-negative effects observed in human pathogenic variants.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**

Multiple independent mouse models demonstrate that reduced SYT1 function produces neurobehavioral phenotypes consistent with human disease. The heterozygous knockout model is particularly relevant to autosomal dominant inheritance, and the detailed electrophysiological characterization of release mechanisms provides mechanistic insight.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

SYT1 functions as a calcium sensor in synaptic vesicle exocytosis, with its C2A and C2B domains mediating Ca2+-dependent interactions critical for neurotransmitter release (PMID: 39481209). The protein is regulated through the cAMP/PKA/CREB signaling pathway, which modulates SYT1 expression during synaptic plasticity (PMID: 39089658).

**Expression pattern:**

SYT1 is prominently expressed in the brain, particularly in hippocampus, cortex, and other regions critical for learning and memory. The gene shows differential expression in autism spectrum disorder brain post-mortem tissues (ASHG 2023 - Investigating Immune Functions of neuronal genes burdening Autism Spectrum Disorder). SYT1 is also identified as a neuro-immune gene with dual neuronal and immune cell-specific functional networks relevant to ASD pathogenesis.

**Key experimental findings:**

- *Functional studies:* Cultured hippocampal neurons transfected with pathogenic SYT1 variants (L159R, T196K, E209K, E219Q, M303V, S309P, Y365C, G369D) showed graded, variant-dependent dominant-negative impairment in exocytosis that correlated with severity of motor and communication impairments (PMID: 39481209).

- *Synaptic plasticity:* SYT1 expression is positively correlated with BDNF expression in hippocampus and is involved in synaptic function related to cognitive decline in Alzheimer's disease pathology (PMID: 35130907).

- *Ischemic stress response:* SYT1 protein levels are altered in response to oxygen-glucose deprivation and ischemic stroke, indicating its role in synaptic ultra-structure maintenance (PMID: 33111641).

- *Genomic regulation:* SYT1 shows significant allelic expression imbalance in both European American and African American populations (p=0.05 to 7×10⁻⁸), suggesting cis-acting regulatory variants affect SYT1 expression (ASHG 2010 - Allelic Expression Imbalance Analysis).

- *Chromosomal context:* SYT1 is located within the 12q21.2-q21.33 region; deletions encompassing this region are associated with intellectual disability and facial dysmorphisms (ASHG 2012 - 12q21 deletion syndrome).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

Extensive functional characterization demonstrates that SYT1 variants impair calcium-dependent exocytosis in a dose-dependent manner, with direct correlation to clinical severity. Multiple independent studies confirm SYT1's critical role in synaptic function, plasticity, and neurodevelopmental processes.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths:**

1. **Functional validation:** The p.Pro401Leu variant has been functionally characterized in cultured neurons, demonstrating dominant-negative effects on neurotransmitter release and synaptic development (PMID: 38321119).

2. **Mechanistic clarity:** Multiple studies establish that pathogenic SYT1 variants impair calcium-triggered exocytosis in a variant-dependent manner that correlates with clinical severity (PMID: 39481209).

3. **Consistent animal models:** Heterozygous Syt1 knockout mice exhibit neurobehavioral phenotypes (anxiety, reduced exploration, altered glutamate signaling) consistent with human neurodevelopmental disorder (PMID: 36031564).

4. **Convergent evidence:** Independent clinical cohorts (de novo case and ASD cohort) identify SYT1 variants in patients with overlapping neurodevelopmental phenotypes.

5. **Biological plausibility:** SYT1's essential role in synaptic vesicle exocytosis and its differential expression in ASD brain tissue provide strong mechanistic support for its role in neurodevelopmental disease.

**Key limitations:**

1. **Limited clinical sample size:** Only ~16 patients total across studies; larger cohorts would strengthen clinical evidence.

2. **Incomplete variant characterization:** The ASD study (15 patients) does not provide specific variant details or segregation analysis.

3. **Lack of international cohort representation:** Patients are not from major diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD), limiting standardized phenotyping.

4. **Incomplete penetrance/expressivity data:** No systematic assessment of incomplete penetrance or variable expressivity in families.

**Conclusion:**

SYT1 is a well-characterized neurodevelopmental disorder gene with strong biological and functional evidence supporting pathogenic heterozygous variants as a cause of developmental delay, autism spectrum disorder, and related neurodevelopmental phenotypes. The convergence of functional studies demonstrating dominant-negative effects on synaptic exocytosis, animal models showing consistent neurobehavioral impairment, and independent clinical case reports provides compelling evidence for SYT1's role in autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disease. Future studies with larger patient cohorts and systematic phenotyping would further establish SYT1's clinical significance and refine genotype-phenotype correlations.
