# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: SMARCC2 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

SMARCC2 encodes a core subunit of the BAF (Brahma-associated factor) chromatin-remodeling complex, which is part of the larger SWI/SNF family of ATP-dependent chromatin remodelers. SMARCC2 plays a critical role in regulating chromatin accessibility and gene expression, with particular importance in neuronal differentiation and synaptic function.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent studies:** 5 publications reporting clinical cases and cohorts

**Total patient count:** Approximately 71 affected individuals across all studies (3 patients from PMID: 41532374; 1 patient from PMID: 41291750; 2 patients from PMID: 41168819; 65 individuals from PMID: 37551667; additional cases from functional studies PMID: 40492195 and PMID: 41183059)

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Developmental delay (mild to moderate)
- Intellectual disability (mild to moderate)
- Speech/language difficulties
- Autism spectrum disorder and autistic behaviors
- Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD)
- Behavioral issues and obsessive-compulsive disorder features
- Facial dysmorphism
- Hypotonia and feeding difficulties
- Brain abnormalities on imaging

**Predominant variant types:**
- Loss-of-function variants (splice site variants, structural variants with breakpoints disrupting SMARCC2)
- Missense variants, particularly at conserved residues (Arg116 identified as a hotspot in PMID: 41532374)
- Structural variants (233 kb inversion disrupting exons 16-17 in PMID: 41291750)

**Cohort source:** No patients from established research cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL) explicitly mentioned; cases appear to be from clinical diagnostic centers in China, France, and other institutions

**De novo status:** All reported variants are de novo, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance with reduced reproductive fitness

**Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) multiple independent case reports and case series from different geographic regions; (2) consistent phenotypic presentation across studies; (3) identification of recurrent variants at the same codon (Arg116) suggesting a functional hotspot (PMID: 41532374); (4) first structural variant in SMARCC2-related Coffin-Siris Syndrome 8 (PMID: 41291750); (5) large cohort study of 65 affected individuals demonstrating clinical and molecular spectrum (PMID: 37551667); and (6) functional validation showing altered protein interactions for missense variants.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** None reported in the provided evidence set

**Key findings:** No model organism studies (mouse knockouts, zebrafish, Drosophila, etc.) were identified in the extracted evidence

**Strength of model organism evidence: NONE**

This represents a significant gap in the evidence base. Model organism studies would be valuable for: (1) demonstrating that heterozygous loss of Smarcc2 recapitulates neurodevelopmental phenotypes; (2) characterizing the developmental timing and brain regions affected; and (3) testing potential therapeutic interventions.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

SMARCC2 functions as a core component of the BAF chromatin-remodeling complex, which regulates chromatin accessibility at regulatory regions controlling gene expression. The evidence demonstrates multiple layers of functional importance:

- **Chromatin remodeling and transcriptional regulation:** SMARCC2 is a validated member of the SWI/SNF complex with regulatory phosphosites identified in neuronal differentiation (PMID: 31819260)

- **Interaction with ASD risk genes:** ChIP-seq analysis in iPSC-derived neurons from ASD patients showed SMARCC2 binding to multiple autism risk genes involved in transcriptional regulation (PMID: 40492195)

- **Synaptic function:** SMARCC2 deficiency in mouse prefrontal cortex impaired working memory with downregulation of synaptic transmission genes, and iPSC-derived neurons showed impaired GABAergic and glutamatergic synaptic currents (PMID: 40492195)

- **Protein interactions:** Co-immunoprecipitation and proximity-ligation assays demonstrated that missense variants in SMARCC2 alter BAF-subunit interactions, with N-terminal missense variants showing decreased protein expression similar to loss-of-function variants (PMID: 37551667)

- **Regulatory domain conservation:** Variants in conserved DNA-interacting domains (SANT and SWIRM) presented more severe phenotypes, suggesting structure-function relationships (ASHG 2018)

**Expression pattern:**

SMARCC2 is expressed broadly but shows particular relevance to neurodevelopment. Transcriptomic analysis of patient fibroblasts identified differentially expressed genes (H19, SCRG1, RELN, CACNB4) involved in neuronal development (ASHG 2018). SMARCC2 is significantly diminished in iPSC-derived neurons from idiopathic ASD patients (PMID: 40492195) and shows differential expression in ASD samples (PMID: 41183059).

**Key experimental findings:**

- Missense variants cause altered protein-protein interactions within the BAF complex without necessarily causing complete loss of function, suggesting a dominant-negative mechanism (PMID: 37551667)

- SMARCC2 deficiency leads to impaired synaptic transmission and working memory deficits that can be rescued by histone deacetylase inhibition, suggesting potential therapeutic targets (PMID: 40492195)

- SMARCC2 interacts with multiple regulatory proteins (CNBP, FBXO28, ARID1B) that modulate its stability and function (PMID: 37186134, PMID: 37348029, PMID: 40890601)

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is comprehensive, including: (1) validated role in chromatin remodeling and neuronal differentiation; (2) direct binding to autism risk genes; (3) demonstration of synaptic dysfunction in patient-derived neurons; (4) mechanistic studies showing altered protein interactions for pathogenic variants; (5) identification of potential therapeutic targets; and (6) consistent findings across multiple experimental systems (cell culture, iPSC-derived neurons, mouse models).

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths:**

1. **Consistent clinical phenotype:** Multiple independent case reports and a large cohort study (65 individuals) demonstrate a recognizable neurodevelopmental syndrome with intellectual disability, developmental delay, autism spectrum features, and ADHD

2. **Molecular validation:** Functional studies confirm that both loss-of-function and missense variants disrupt SMARCC2 function through altered protein interactions and impaired chromatin remodeling

3. **De novo inheritance pattern:** All reported variants are de novo, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance with reduced reproductive fitness

4. **Mechanistic understanding:** Detailed molecular studies link SMARCC2 dysfunction to impaired synaptic transmission, altered expression of autism risk genes, and working memory deficits

5. **Recurrent variants:** Identification of hotspot mutations (Arg116) at the same codon in independent patients strengthens the evidence for pathogenicity

6. **Therapeutic potential:** Demonstration that HDAC inhibition can rescue Smarcc2-deficiency phenotypes suggests potential treatment approaches

**Key gaps and limitations:**

1. **Absence of model organism studies:** No mouse knockouts, zebrafish, or other model organism data were identified, limiting understanding of developmental timing and in vivo mechanisms

2. **Limited structural variant characterization:** Only one structural variant has been reported; more structural variants would help define the full spectrum of SMARCC2-related disease

3. **Incomplete variant specification:** One study (PMID: 41168819) reported SMARCC2 variants in 2 patients with specific learning disorders but did not provide detailed variant information, limiting interpretation

4. **Phenotypic heterogeneity:** While most patients present with intellectual disability and autism features, some patients have isolated learning disorders without intellectual disability, suggesting possible genotype-phenotype correlations that require further study

5. **Limited functional characterization of missense variants:** While some missense variants show altered protein interactions, systematic functional analysis of the full spectrum of missense variants is needed

**Conclusion:**

SMARCC2 is established as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous de novo pathogenic variants causing a recognizable syndrome characterized by intellectual disability, developmental delay, autism spectrum disorder, and ADHD. The evidence is supported by multiple independent clinical case reports, a large cohort study, and robust molecular and cellular studies demonstrating impaired chromatin remodeling, altered synaptic function, and disrupted expression of autism risk genes. While model organism studies are notably absent, the convergent evidence from clinical genetics, functional genomics, and cellular neurobiology provides strong support for SMARCC2 as a high-confidence neurodevelopmental disorder gene.
