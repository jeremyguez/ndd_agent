# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: ATP2B4 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

ATP2B4 encodes the plasma membrane calcium ATPase 4 (PMCA4), a critical calcium extrusion pump located in the cell membrane that maintains intracellular calcium homeostasis. ATP2B4 is expressed in multiple tissues including red blood cells, sperm, pancreatic acinar cells, neural tissue, and vascular smooth muscle, with particular importance in calcium-dependent cellular processes.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (6 studies) and zebrafish (1 study)

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

Mouse studies using full knockout or conditional knockout models revealed the following:

- **Viability and Mendelian inheritance:** ATP2B4 full knockout mice are viable with Mendelian inheritance ratios, indicating the gene is not essential for embryonic development (PMID: 15178683, PMID: 33098669).

- **Male infertility:** ATP2B4 knockout males show complete male infertility despite normal spermatogenesis and mating behavior, caused by inability to achieve hyperactivated sperm motility and calcium overload in sperm tails (PMID: 15178683). This is the most consistent and robust phenotype across studies.

- **Red blood cell abnormalities:** ATP2B4 knockout mice display reduced RBC size and reduced RBC hemoglobin content, with normal RBC maturation and reticulocyte counts (PMID: 34215257).

- **Smooth muscle dysfunction:** Impaired phasic contractions in portal vein smooth muscle in vitro, with strain-dependent apoptosis (PMID: 15178683).

- **Malaria susceptibility:** Slight protection against experimental cerebral malaria during Plasmodium berghei infection with minor reduction in antigen-experienced T cell accumulation in brain, though no significant alteration in peripheral parasite burdens (PMID: 34215257).

- **Normal B-cell development:** ATP2B4 knockout mice show normal B-cell development and calcium handling, with no PMCA4 expression detected in naive B cells (PMID: 33098669).

- **Pancreatic vulnerability:** PMCA4b-selective inhibition worsens pancreatitis-induced injury, indicating PMCA4 importance in acinar cell calcium handling (PMID: 29042438).

- **Renal dysfunction:** Conditional knockout of polycystin-1 (associated with Atp2b4 downregulation) causes reduced serum electrolytes and renal wasting of magnesium, calcium, and phosphate (PMID: 29767557).

- **Cardiovascular and developmental effects:** Zebrafish atp2b4 knockdown results in decreased heart rate, pericardial edema, vascular damage, thrombosis, and decreased expression of cardiovascular development genes (PMID: 38679130).

**Neurological phenotypes:** No specific neurological or neurodevelopmental phenotypes were reported in ATP2B4 knockout or knockdown models. The zebrafish study showed cardiovascular effects but did not assess neurological function.

**Consistency with human neurodevelopmental phenotype:** Not applicable, as no human neurodevelopmental cases exist for comparison.

**Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED**

While ATP2B4 knockout mice are viable and show tissue-specific phenotypes (particularly in sperm, RBCs, and smooth muscle), the absence of reported neurological or neurodevelopmental phenotypes in multiple knockout models limits the evidence for ATP2B4 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene. The model organism evidence does not support a primary neurodevelopmental role.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

ATP2B4 encodes PMCA4, the primary calcium extrusion pump in red blood cells and a critical regulator of intracellular calcium homeostasis across multiple cell types. The protein functions through ATP-dependent calcium transport, maintaining the steep calcium gradient essential for cellular signaling and preventing calcium overload.

**Expression pattern:**

ATP2B4 shows erythroid-specific expression with particular importance in red blood cells, where it is the main calcium ATPase (PMID: 28714864). The gene is also expressed in neural stem cells, where it plays a role in oxidative stress resistance (PMID: 38666459), and in adrenal tissue (PMID: 32002807). Expression in nasal epithelium has been validated as stable across different conditions (PMID: 22525089). However, no studies specifically characterized ATP2B4 expression in developing brain or neural tissue during critical neurodevelopmental windows.

**Key experimental findings:**

- **Malaria association:** Multiple GWAS studies identified ATP2B4 polymorphisms (particularly rs1541252 and rs10900585) as strongly associated with malaria resistance and red blood cell traits including mean corpuscular hemoglobin concentration (PMID: 28714864, PMID: 35563239, PMID: 36604655). SNPs in regulatory regions alter ATP2B4 expression and intracellular calcium levels, affecting parasitaemia (PMID: 35563239, PMID: 36604655).

- **Oxidative stress resistance:** ATP2B4 is significantly upregulated in Nfkbia-null neural stem cells, and overexpression of ATP2B4 increases NSC survival when exposed to H2O2, indicating a role in oxidative stress resistance in neural precursors (PMID: 38666459).

- **Calcium homeostasis in disease:** ATP2B4 downregulation is associated with calcium overload in multiple pathological contexts: in renal ischemia-reperfusion injury (PMID: 41241171), in multiple myeloma RBCs via exosomal miR-4261-mediated suppression (PMID: 36091107), and in polycystin-1 dysfunction models (PMID: 29767557).

- **Enamel development:** ATP2B4 expression is significantly decreased in enamel organs of Mmp20 null mice, correlating with reduced enamel thickness and attenuated pH changes during enamel development (PMID: 20929715).

- **Regulatory architecture:** Fine-mapping identified erythroid-specific enhancers containing GATA1 binding motifs that cooperatively regulate ATP2B4 expression, with a minimal 98 bp regulatory sequence identified (ASHG 2016).

**Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE**

ATP2B4 has well-characterized molecular function in calcium homeostasis with strong evidence for tissue-specific regulation and disease associations. However, the biological evidence is primarily focused on red blood cell biology and malaria susceptibility rather than neurodevelopmental processes. The evidence for ATP2B4 in neural development is limited to a single study showing oxidative stress resistance in neural stem cells, without evidence of broader neurodevelopmental roles.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
- ATP2B4 has well-established molecular function in calcium homeostasis with strong biological evidence from multiple GWAS studies and functional experiments
- The gene is viable in knockout models, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance patterns
- Evidence exists for ATP2B4 involvement in oxidative stress resistance in neural stem cells
- Multiple tissues show ATP2B4-dependent calcium handling with disease relevance

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- **Complete absence of clinical evidence:** No published case reports, case series, or patient cohorts with ATP2B4 variants and neurodevelopmental phenotypes exist
- **Lack of neurological phenotypes in model organisms:** ATP2B4 knockout mice do not exhibit neurological or neurodevelopmental abnormalities despite comprehensive characterization
- **Limited neural expression data:** No detailed characterization of ATP2B4 expression during critical windows of brain development
- **No functional studies in neurons:** The single neural study examined oxidative stress in stem cells but did not assess neuronal function, synaptic development, or neurodevelopmental processes
- **Tissue-specific focus:** Existing evidence concentrates on red blood cells, sperm, and smooth muscle rather than neural tissue

**Conclusion:**

Based on current evidence, ATP2B4 cannot be classified as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene. While ATP2B4 has important roles in calcium homeostasis and shows some involvement in neural stem cell oxidative stress resistance, there is no clinical evidence of pathogenic variants causing neurodevelopmental disorders, and knockout models do not exhibit neurological phenotypes. To establish ATP2B4 as a neurodevelopmental gene would require identification of affected patients with de novo or inherited ATP2B4 variants, functional characterization of variants in neuronal systems, and demonstration of neurodevelopmental phenotypes in model organisms.
