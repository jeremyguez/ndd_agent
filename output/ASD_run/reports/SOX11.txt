# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: SOX11 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

SOX11 is a transcription factor belonging to the SRY-box family that plays critical roles in neural development, neurogenesis, and neuronal differentiation. It functions as a direct target of developmental signaling pathways (including WNT/STOP signaling) and acts as a key regulator of neural fate specification, progenitor proliferation, and neuronal maturation across multiple developing tissues including the neocortex, cerebellum, and enteric nervous system.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent studies:** 3 case series/reports

**Total patient count:** Approximately 41 patients across studies (7 patients in PMID: 39333428; 13 patients in PMID: 37916390; 21 patients in PMID: 37924570)

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Neurodevelopmental delay and intellectual disability (present in all cohorts)
- Autism spectrum features and ADHD (reported in all three studies)
- Microcephaly (PMID: 39333428)
- Short stature (PMID: 39333428)
- Hypotonia (PMID: 39333428)
- Communication difficulties and language impairment (PMID: 37916390; PMID: 37924570)
- Pragmatic language impairment with social communication deficits (PMID: 37916390)
- Autistic traits in 90% of patients, with 62% in severe range (PMID: 37924570)
- Adaptive behavior impairment ranging from borderline to mild (PMID: 37924570)

**Predominant variant types:**
The clinical evidence includes missense variants (5 cases), loss-of-function variants including nonsense mutations (1 case), and whole-gene deletions (1 case) reported in PMID: 39333428. All variants were de novo, with most being novel. The variants are associated with IDDMOH (intellectual developmental disorder with microcephaly, with or without ocular malformations or hypogonadotropic hypogonadism) phenotype.

**Cohort information:** 
No indication that patients are from known international cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL). Studies appear to be from individual diagnostic centers.

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**

The evidence is strengthened by multiple independent case series with consistent phenotypic descriptions and de novo variant findings. However, the total patient count remains relatively modest (41 patients), and specific variant notations are not provided in the abstracts. The consistency of neurodevelopmental and behavioral phenotypes across all three studies is notable, particularly the high prevalence of autism spectrum features and communication difficulties.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse, Xenopus, and zebrafish

**Key neurological phenotypes from knockouts:**

*Mouse models:*
- Full knockout: Reduced neurogenesis in developing neocortex with drastically decreased basal progenitors and reduced asymmetric division in apical progenitors (PMID: 34431536)
- Heterozygous knockout: Hearing impairment with grossly normal inner ear structures; homozygous ablation results in reduction in sensory neuron survival and decreased axonal growth (PMID: 35642566)
- Full knockout: Lateral and posterior semicircular canal dysplasia with spatiotemporal misregulation of genes critical for ear morphogenesis (ASHG 2016)
- Full knockout: Pulmonary malformations during branching morphogenesis with defective epithelial-mesenchymal condensation (PMID: 40481144)

*Xenopus knockdown:*
- Promotes neural fate during neural development, neural differentiation, and neuron maturation in the central nervous system (PMID: 38397232)
- N-terminus including HMG domain necessary for protein interactions; novel role for N-terminal 46 amino acids in placodal progenitor specification

*Zebrafish knockdown:*
- Loss of sox11a and sox11b reduces number of neurons expressing inhibitory motor neuron marker adcyap1b with no effect on cell proliferation or death (PMID: 40789027)
- sox11a/b knockdown causes brain abnormalities potentially explaining intellectual disability (ASHG 2014)

**Lethality and Mendelian ratios:**
No Mendelian ratio deviations reported. Perinatal lethality noted in conditional Mettl3 knockout mice where Sox11 is downregulated (PMID: 40303284), but this is secondary to Mettl3 deficiency rather than primary Sox11 loss.

**Consistency with human phenotype:**
The model organism evidence shows strong consistency with human neurodevelopmental phenotypes. Reduced neurogenesis, impaired neural differentiation, and sensory neuron deficits in animal models align well with intellectual disability, developmental delay, and communication difficulties observed in patients. The inner ear developmental defects in mice correlate with potential hearing and vestibular involvement in human cases.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**

Multiple independent model systems (mouse, Xenopus, zebrafish) consistently demonstrate critical roles for SOX11 in neurogenesis, neural differentiation, and neuronal maturation. The phenotypes are specific and reproducible, with clear mechanistic insights into SOX11's function in neural development. The heterozygous knockout mouse showing hearing impairment is particularly relevant to understanding haploinsufficiency effects in humans.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

SOX11 functions as a key transcriptional regulator in multiple developmental and cellular contexts:

- **Neural development:** SOX11 is a direct target of WNT/STOP signaling during mitosis and is stabilized by GSK3 inhibition (PMID: 34431536). It cooperates with SOX4 and TFAP2D in specifying ventrolateral pallial excitatory neurons, with loss of SOX4 and SOX11 resulting in marked reduction in basolateral amygdala, claustrum, and piriform cortex size (PMID: 39814878).

- **Transcriptional regulation:** SOX11 co-localizes and interacts with Pou3f2 and Neurog2 (PMID: 38397232). SOX4 and SOX11 show cooperativity in controlling active enhancers of neuronal progenitors (ASHG 2023). SOX11 is downstream of the PAX6-BAF complex transcriptional network (ASHG 2014).

- **Organ development:** SOX11 regulates IGF2 signaling during lung development (PMID: 40481144) and is involved in inner ear bony labyrinth development, potentially mediating effects of CHD7 (ASHG 2016).

**Expression pattern:**

SOX11 shows robust expression in:
- Developing neocortex and cerebellar progenitors
- Neural progenitors and differentiating neurons
- Dermal papilla fibroblasts during skin development (PMID: 38072389)
- Interneuron progenitors with enriched SOX11 motifs in active enhancers (ASHG 2023)

**Key experimental findings:**

- SOX11 is essential for sensory neuron survival and maturation (PMID: 35642566)
- Sox11 deletion in kidney injury models did not worsen injury, suggesting context-dependent roles (PMID: 41279940)
- SOX11 is involved in neuronal subtype specification in the developing enteric nervous system through binding to enhancers of genes like adcyap1b (PMID: 40789027)
- Loss of SOX4 and SOX11 in post-mitotic excitatory neurons alters PFC connectivity and learned threat-response behaviors (PMID: 39814878)

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is comprehensive, with consistent findings across multiple experimental systems demonstrating SOX11's critical roles in neural development, neurogenesis, and neuronal differentiation. The identification of specific protein interactions, regulatory targets, and signaling pathway involvement provides strong mechanistic support for how SOX11 haploinsufficiency could lead to neurodevelopmental disorders.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths of the evidence:**

1. **Clinical consistency:** Three independent case series totaling 41 patients show remarkably consistent phenotypes centered on neurodevelopmental delay, intellectual disability, autism spectrum features, and communication difficulties. All reported variants are de novo, supporting pathogenicity.

2. **Model organism validation:** Multiple independent model systems (mouse, Xenopus, zebrafish) consistently demonstrate that SOX11 is essential for neurogenesis, neural differentiation, and neuronal maturation. Heterozygous knockout mice exhibit hearing impairment, providing evidence for haploinsufficiency effects.

3. **Mechanistic understanding:** Biological evidence establishes SOX11 as a key transcriptional regulator in neural development, with identified protein interactions, regulatory targets, and involvement in critical developmental signaling pathways (WNT/STOP, BAF complex).

4. **Phenotypic specificity:** The high prevalence of autism spectrum features (90% in one cohort) and communication/language difficulties across studies suggests a specific neurodevelopmental signature for SOX11 variants.

**Key gaps or limitations:**

1. **Limited patient numbers:** While 41 patients across three studies is meaningful, larger cohorts would strengthen genotype-phenotype correlations and allow assessment of variant-specific effects (missense vs. LoF).

2. **Incomplete variant characterization:** The clinical reports lack detailed variant notation and functional validation data for individual variants.

3. **Phenotypic heterogeneity:** While core features are consistent, there is variability in severity and specific manifestations (e.g., not all patients have microcephaly or short stature), limiting predictive power.

4. **Limited functional studies:** No patient-derived cellular or iPSC-based studies demonstrating functional consequences of specific SOX11 variants.

**Conclusion:**

SOX11 is a well-established neurodevelopmental disorder gene with strong evidence supporting autosomal dominant inheritance through heterozygous pathogenic variants. The convergence of consistent clinical phenotypes across 41 patients, robust model organism evidence demonstrating essential roles in neurogenesis and neural differentiation, and comprehensive biological characterization of SOX11's function in neural development collectively establish SOX11 as a high-confidence gene for intellectual developmental disorder with autism spectrum features and communication difficulties. The phenotypic signature—particularly the prominence of pragmatic language impairment and autistic traits—may help distinguish SOX11-related neurodevelopmental disorder from other genetic causes of intellectual disability.
