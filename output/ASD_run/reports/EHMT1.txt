# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: EHMT1 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

EHMT1 (euchromatic histone methyltransferase 1) encodes a histone methyltransferase that functions as a key component of the epigenetic machinery, containing both a 'reader' domain (ankyrin repeat) and a 'writer' domain (SET domain methyltransferase) for H3K9 methylation (PMID: 39013458). EHMT1 plays critical roles in transcriptional repression, neuronal maturation, and epigenetic regulation during development, with heterozygous loss-of-function variants causing Kleefstra syndrome-1 (KLEFS1), a neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, and distinctive dysmorphic features.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Study Count and Patient Numbers:**
Approximately 11 independent clinical reports/case series were identified, encompassing approximately 20-25 directly reported patients with EHMT1 variants. Additional patients were identified through large cohort studies and diagnostic panels, bringing the total documented cases to at least 30+ individuals across published literature.

**Most Common Phenotypes Observed:**
The predominant clinical features include:
- Intellectual disability and developmental delay (present in nearly all cases)
- Autism spectrum disorder (reported in 7+ studies)
- Dysmorphic features and craniofacial abnormalities
- Speech and language delay
- Behavioral problems and psychiatric symptoms (including autism, depression, psychosis)
- Seizures/epilepsy (in subset of patients)

Notable findings include a case of psychosis and autism without functional regression (PMID: 36617745), and documentation of EHMT1 mosaicism in apparently unaffected parents associated with autism spectrum disorder and neurocognitive dysfunction (PMID: 29416845), suggesting variable expressivity and potential germline mosaicism.

**Predominant Variant Type:**
Loss-of-function variants (deletions, splice site mutations, frameshift variants) are the most common pathogenic mechanism, consistent with haploinsufficiency. Missense variants have also been reported (e.g., c.2426 C > T, p.Pro809Leu in PMID: 39696517), though functional studies indicate that disruption of the reader domain (ankyrin repeat) is critical for the typical KLEFS1 phenotype, while disruption of only the writer methyltransferase activity may not produce the full phenotype (PMID: 39013458).

**Cohort Representation:**
Patients were identified through exome sequencing, targeted NGS panels for developmental delay/autism/epilepsy, and diagnostic flow-charts. One study explicitly referenced the Autism Genetic Resource Exchange (AGRE) cohort (ASHG 2011 - Moreno-De-Luca et al.). Large cohort studies identified EHMT1 variants in 2 Russian children with autism spectrum disorder (PMID: 41255692) and in pediatric epilepsy and intellectual disability cohorts with diagnostic yields of 45% and 23% respectively (ASHG 2022 studies).

**Strength of Clinical Evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strong because: (1) multiple independent case reports and case series consistently document EHMT1 variants in patients with Kleefstra syndrome and related neurodevelopmental phenotypes; (2) variants are identified through rigorous molecular methods (exome sequencing, targeted NGS, array CGH); (3) phenotypes are consistent across studies; (4) both loss-of-function and missense variants are documented; (5) functional studies have characterized the molecular consequences of variants; and (6) the gene is established in diagnostic panels for neurodevelopmental disorders.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms Studied:**
Mouse models are the primary organism studied, with one study examining C. elegans ortholog SET-6 (PMID: 32103178).

**Key Phenotypes from Knockouts:**

*Heterozygous knockout mice (Ehmt1+/-)* demonstrate a complex neurobehavioral phenotype consistent with human Kleefstra syndrome:
- Cognitive impairments: impaired fear extinction, impaired novel and spatial object recognition, complete absence of object recognition memory (PMID: 32954001)
- Enhanced pattern separation and increased adult hippocampal cell proliferation (PMID: 28071689)
- Behavioral abnormalities: decreased sociability, increased aggression, defensive postures, anxiety, and autistic-like traits with aberrant social interactions (PMID: 36877418)
- Neurophysiological deficits: abnormal sensory-motor gating, reduced auditory evoked potentials, marked reductions in beta- and gamma-band oscillatory activity, disrupted NMDA-R signaling (PMID: 32954001)
- Craniofacial abnormalities

*Conditional knockout mice* reveal critical roles in reproduction and early development:
- Meiotic defects and impaired oocyte maturation (PMID: 36690445)
- Mid-gestation embryonic lethality upon fertilization (Ehmt1 conditional knockout and Ehmt1/2 conditional double knockout)
- Infertility phenotype

*Pharmacological inhibition studies* demonstrate:
- Alleviation of synaptic damage and motor impairment in Parkinson's disease models (PMID: 38472451)
- Resensitization of therapy-resistant ovarian cancer cells to PARPi with low toxicity (PMID: 39136655)

**Consistency with Human Phenotype:**
The heterozygous knockout mouse phenotype shows strong consistency with human Kleefstra syndrome, particularly regarding intellectual disability, autism-like behaviors, social dysfunction, and cognitive impairments. The enhanced pattern separation finding is intriguing and suggests potential compensatory mechanisms in haploinsufficiency. The behavioral and neurophysiological abnormalities directly parallel the psychiatric and neurocognitive symptoms observed in human patients.

**Strength of Model Organism Evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strong because: (1) heterozygous knockout mice recapitulate core neurobehavioral features of human Kleefstra syndrome; (2) multiple independent studies confirm consistent phenotypes; (3) detailed neurophysiological and molecular characterization demonstrates disrupted NMDA-R signaling and altered histone methylation patterns; (4) conditional knockout studies establish critical developmental roles; and (5) the mouse model has been extensively characterized across cognitive, behavioral, and molecular domains.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known Pathways and Molecular Function:**

EHMT1 functions as a histone H3 lysine 9 (H3K9) methyltransferase that catalyzes both H3K9me1 and H3K9me2 modifications, which are associated with transcriptional repression. The gene has two functionally distinct domains:

1. **Reader domain (ankyrin repeat):** Critical for the typical Kleefstra syndrome phenotype; disruption of this domain produces a characteristic DNA methylation signature (PMID: 39013458)
2. **Writer domain (SET methyltransferase):** Catalyzes H3K9 methylation; disruption alone does not produce the full KLEFS1 phenotype

EHMT1 functions in multiple biological contexts:
- **Neuronal maturation:** Transient EHMT1 inhibition at the progenitor stage primes cortical neurons to rapidly acquire mature properties, indicating EHMT1 holds transcriptional maturation programs in a poised state (PMID: 38297124)
- **Epigenetic regulation:** EHMT1 and EHMT2 regulate H3K27me2 and H3K27me3 establishment in zygotes, contributing to epigenetic asymmetry (PMID: 33311485)
- **Transcriptional repression:** Forms heterodimers with EHMT2 and interacts with BRD4 to repress target genes (PMID: 40598222)
- **Aging and neurodegeneration:** EHMT1 expression increases with age in human frontal cortex and correlates with Alzheimer's disease progression; EHMT1 reduces mitochondrial function by repressing nuclear-encoded mitochondrial proteins (PMID: 32103178)
- **Cellular structure:** EHMT1 regulates subcellular morphology including Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, and centrosome functioning (PMID: 37257768)

**Expression Pattern:**
EHMT1 is expressed in the brain, particularly in cortical neurons and hippocampus, with expression increasing during development and aging. The gene is relevant to neurodevelopmental processes including neuronal differentiation, synaptogenesis, and cognitive function.

**Key Experimental Findings:**

- Comprehensive analysis of 209 individuals with EHMT1 variants revealed that disruption of the reader (ankyrin repeat) domain produces a characteristic KLEFS1-specific DNA methylation signature, while disruption of only the writer methyltransferase activity does not (PMID: 39013458)
- EHMT1 methylates histone and non-histone substrates; EHMT1-mediated methylation of DNA ligase 1 (LIG1) is critical for DNA replication fork progression (PMID: 37873068)
- EHMT1 depletion alters subcellular organelle distribution and reduces cell migration capacity (PMID: 37257768)
- De novo CNVs impacting EHMT1 show significant overlap between neurodevelopmental disorder and congenital anomaly patients (P-value = 0.01, OR = 1.58 for deletions; ASHG 2022 - Safizadeh Shabestari et al.)

**Strength of Biological Evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strong because: (1) EHMT1's molecular function as an H3K9 methyltransferase is well-established; (2) distinct functional domains have been characterized with differential contributions to disease phenotype; (3) multiple independent studies confirm roles in neuronal maturation, epigenetic regulation, and cellular structure; (4) expression patterns are consistent with neurodevelopmental relevance; (5) protein interactions and substrate specificity have been documented; and (6) mechanistic links between EHMT1 dysfunction and neurodegeneration/aging have been established.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall Evidence Level: STRONG**

EHMT1 is supported by strong, convergent evidence across clinical, model organism, and biological domains as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous (autosomal dominant) pathogenic variants causing Kleefstra syndrome-1.

**Key Strengths of the Evidence:**

1. **Clinical consistency:** Multiple independent case reports and case series (11+ studies) consistently document EHMT1 variants in 30+ patients with a coherent phenotype of intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, and dysmorphic features
2. **Molecular characterization:** Variants include both loss-of-function (deletions, splice site mutations) and missense variants; functional studies have identified distinct contributions of different protein domains to disease pathogenesis
3. **Model organism validation:** Heterozygous knockout mice recapitulate core neurobehavioral features of human disease, including intellectual disability, autism-like behaviors, and neurophysiological abnormalities
4. **Mechanistic understanding:** EHMT1's role in H3K9 methylation, neuronal maturation, and epigenetic regulation provides plausible biological mechanisms for neurodevelopmental dysfunction
5. **Diagnostic utility:** EHMT1 is established in diagnostic panels for developmental delay, autism, and epilepsy with documented diagnostic yield

**Key Gaps or Limitations:**

1. **Genotype-phenotype correlation:** While the reader domain disruption is associated with the characteristic KLEFS1 DNA methylation signature, detailed genotype-phenotype correlations for specific missense variants remain limited
2. **Incomplete penetrance and variable expressivity:** Documentation of EHMT1 mosaicism in apparently unaffected parents suggests incomplete penetrance or variable expressivity, but the frequency and clinical significance of these cases require further characterization
3. **Functional validation of missense variants:** While some missense variants have been identified, systematic functional characterization of missense variants is limited
4. **Neuroimaging and biomarker data:** Structural and functional neuroimaging findings are not well-documented in the clinical literature
5. **Long-term outcome data:** Limited longitudinal follow-up data on developmental trajectories and long-term outcomes in EHMT1-related neurodevelopmental disorder

**Conclusion:**

EHMT1 is a well-established neurodevelopmental disorder gene with strong evidence supporting heterozygous loss-of-function variants as a cause of Kleefstra syndrome-1, characterized by intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, and distinctive dysmorphic features. The convergence of consistent clinical phenotypes across multiple case series, recapitulation of disease features in heterozygous knockout mice, and detailed characterization of EHMT1's role in epigenetic regulation and neuronal maturation provide robust support for EHMT1's pathogenic role in neurodevelopmental disorders. Future work should focus on systematic functional characterization of missense variants, investigation of incomplete penetrance mechanisms, and longitudinal outcome studies to refine genotype-phenotype correlations and optimize clinical management.
