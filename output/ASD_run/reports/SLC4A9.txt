# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: SLC4A9 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

SLC4A9 (also known as AE4) encodes an electroneutral, cation-dependent chloride/bicarbonate exchanger that mediates Na+-dependent and K+-dependent Cl-/HCO3- exchange across cell membranes (PMID: 27114614). The gene is located on chromosome 5q31, contains 23 exons encoding a 990 amino acid protein with 12 transmembrane domains, and exhibits tissue-restricted expression predominantly in the kidney with alternative splicing generating at least 14 known isoforms (PMID: 11305939).

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (Mus musculus)

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

All identified mouse studies involved full or conditional knockouts of Slc4a9. The primary phenotypes observed were:

- **Salivary gland dysfunction:** Ae4(-/-) mice exhibited a 35% reduction in salivation with impaired Cl- uptake during β-adrenergic receptor activation (PMID: 25745107, PMID: 29209914). These mice remained viable and fertile with normal Mendelian ratios.

- **Renal ion transport:** Slc4a9 expression was induced as a compensatory response in pendrin-positive intercalated cells of the kidney, suggesting a role in salt reabsorption pathways (PMID: 25893600). Conditional knockout of Nedd4-2 in intercalated cells resulted in increased electroneutral Cl-/HCO3- exchange activity and approximately 10 mm Hg elevation in blood pressure, though AE4 abundance was not increased (PMID: 29773687).

- **Tissue-specific function:** In duodenal epithelium, AE4 contributed minimally (<5%) to basal Cl-/HCO3- exchange activity, with PAT-1 (SLC26A6) being the predominant apical anion exchanger (PMID: 17170027).

- **Lethality and viability:** All Slc4a9 knockout mice were viable with no reported lethality or developmental abnormalities.

**Neurological phenotypes:** No neurological, behavioral, or neurodevelopmental phenotypes were reported in any mouse knockout studies.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** Not applicable, as no human neurodevelopmental phenotypes have been documented.

**Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED**

While mouse knockouts demonstrate that Slc4a9 is not essential for viability and has specific roles in salivary gland secretion and renal ion transport, the complete absence of neurological or neurodevelopmental phenotypes in knockout animals provides no support for a role in neurodevelopmental disorders. The evidence is limited to peripheral organ function.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

SLC4A9 is a member of the SLC4 family of bicarbonate transporters involved in acid-base homeostasis, pH regulation, cell volume regulation, and salt balance (PMID: 38536494, PMID: 23506864). The protein functions as an electroneutral monovalent cation-dependent Cl-/HCO3- exchanger, mediating Na+-dependent Cl-/HCO3- exchange and K+-dependent Cl-/HCO3- exchange, with activity regulated by PKA-dependent phosphorylation at the S173 residue (PMID: 27114614, PMID: 34585968).

**Expression pattern:**

SLC4A9 expression is largely restricted to the kidney, with particular abundance in type B intercalated cells of the cortical collecting duct where it localizes to the basolateral membrane (PMID: 11305939, PMID: 29773687, PMID: 24990900). Expression is regulated by the transcription factor Foxi1, a master regulator of intercalated cell differentiation (PMID: 36603001). Notably, no brain or central nervous system expression has been documented in the literature reviewed.

**Key experimental findings:**

- SLC4A9 is induced by hypoxia in cancer cell lines (8 of 10 tested) in a HIF1α and HIF2α-dependent manner, and SLC4A9 disruption reduces tumor growth and increases apoptosis (PMID: 27197160).

- Differential methylation at an SLC4A9 locus (cg08215811) was associated with all-cause mortality in a cardiovascular cohort (CATHGEN) with 55 cases and 49 controls in the discovery set and 108 cases and 108 controls in the validation set (PMID: 31642367).

- SLC4A9 transcript levels are downregulated (66.1% reduction) in heat-stressed laying hens, impairing ion transport during eggshell mineralization (PMID: 29115887).

**Brain/neurodevelopmental relevance:** No evidence of SLC4A9 expression in brain tissue or involvement in neurodevelopmental processes was identified.

**Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE**

The biological evidence demonstrates robust characterization of SLC4A9 molecular function, regulation, and tissue-specific expression. However, the complete absence of documented brain expression and the restriction of known functions to peripheral organs (kidney, salivary gland, intestine) and cancer biology substantially limits relevance to neurodevelopmental disorders.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
- Well-characterized molecular function as a cation-dependent anion exchanger
- Consistent phenotypes across multiple mouse studies demonstrating tissue-specific roles
- Clear regulatory mechanisms (PKA phosphorylation, Foxi1 transcriptional control)
- Viable knockout animals with no developmental lethality

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- **Complete absence of clinical evidence:** No published case reports, case series, or patient cohorts with SLC4A9 variants exist in the literature.
- **No neurological phenotypes in model organisms:** Mouse knockouts show no neurological, behavioral, or neurodevelopmental abnormalities.
- **Restricted expression pattern:** SLC4A9 expression is documented only in peripheral tissues (kidney, salivary gland, intestine) with no evidence of brain expression.
- **No mechanistic link to neurodevelopment:** The known functions of SLC4A9 (ion transport in epithelial tissues) have no established connection to neurodevelopmental processes.
- **Lack of functional studies in neural contexts:** No cell culture or experimental evidence demonstrates SLC4A9 function in neurons or neural progenitor cells.

**Conclusion:**

There is currently insufficient evidence to support SLC4A9 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene. The complete absence of clinical case reports, combined with the lack of neurological phenotypes in animal models and the restriction of known expression and function to peripheral organs, provides no foundation for implicating heterozygous SLC4A9 variants in neurodevelopmental disease. Before SLC4A9 could be considered a candidate neurodevelopmental gene, human genetic evidence (case reports with detailed phenotyping) and functional evidence of brain expression and neuronal involvement would be required.
