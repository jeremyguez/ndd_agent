# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: SOX5 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

SOX5 is a member of the SoxD family of transcription factors containing a conserved high-mobility-group (HMG) DNA-binding domain and a group-specific coiled-coil domain that mediates protein dimerization (PMID: 19647094). SOX5 functions as a key regulator of cell fate determination and developmental processes, including chondrogenesis, neurogenesis, and neural crest development, and acts as a co-factor that enhances transactivation by SOX9 in multiple tissue contexts (PMID: 19647094, PMID: 32380234).

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of Studies and Patient Cohort:**
Six independent clinical studies were identified, comprising approximately 74 patients with SOX5-related neurodevelopmental disorders. The largest series included 41 patients (PMID: 31578471), followed by 20 Spanish patients (PMID: 37702321), 8 patients with deletions (ASHG 2009), and 3 patients in a ZBTB20-related study (ASHG 2013), with two additional single case reports (PMID: 40500800, PMID: 29477873).

**Most Common Phenotypes:**
The core clinical phenotype of Lamb-Shaffer syndrome (SOX5 haploinsufficiency) includes:
- Intellectual disability (reported in all major series)
- Developmental delay (universal finding)
- Language/speech delay (reported in all series)
- Autistic features (reported in multiple series)
- Facial dysmorphic features (PMID: 37702321)
- Visual problems including hypermetropia and strabismus (PMID: 37702321, PMID: 40500800)
- Pain insensitivity (PMID: 37702321)
- Seizures (reported in severe cases; PMID: 40500800)
- Spinal anomalies including butterfly vertebrae and scoliosis (ASHG 2009)

**Predominant Variant Types:**
All variant types are pathogenic:
- Loss-of-function variants (truncating mutations, microdeletions): reported across all studies
- Missense variants: particularly those in the HMG domain show severe phenotypes; variants outside the HMG domain show milder phenotypes (PMID: 31578471)
- Structural variants (CNVs, balanced translocations): represent approximately 53% of reported variants (PMID: 37702321)

Functional studies demonstrate that missense variants in the HMG domain prevent SOX5 from binding DNA and promoting transactivation in vitro, while variants outside the HMG domain do not show this effect (PMID: 31578471). A specific missense variant (c.1769T>C, p.Leu590Ser) showed reduced transcriptional activation activity in functional assays (PMID: 40500800).

**Cohort Source:**
No patients were identified as coming from large standardized cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL). Studies were primarily from individual centers or regional cohorts (Spain noted in PMID: 37702321; Japan in PMID: 29477873).

**Inheritance Pattern:**
Both de novo and inherited cases are documented. Variable expressivity is demonstrated, with a mother carrying a missense variant showing a milder phenotype than her severely affected son (PMID: 40500800). De novo balanced reciprocal translocations and de novo deletions are reported (PMID: 29477873, ASHG 2009).

**Strength of Clinical Evidence: STRONG**

The clinical evidence is strong, supported by multiple independent case series totaling 74 patients with consistent phenotypic features, documented pathogenic variants of multiple types, functional validation of pathogenicity, and clear demonstration of haploinsufficiency as the disease mechanism.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms Studied:**
Five model organisms have been used to study SOX5 function: mouse (full knockout and genetic ablation), Xenopus (knockdown), zebrafish (knockdown), and in vitro human cell culture systems.

**Key Neurological Phenotypes from Knockouts:**

Mouse studies reveal critical roles in neurodevelopment:
- Failed preplate partition and laminar inversion of early-born neurons (PMID: 18840685)
- Loss of layer 5 subcerebral axons and misrouting of subplate and layer 6 corticothalamic axons to the hypothalamus (PMID: 18840685)
- Abnormal postmitotic differentiation of subplate and deep-layer neurons with maintained immature differentiation state (PMID: 18840685)
- Impaired migration of early-born neurons (PMID: 18840685)

Additional developmental phenotypes:
- Abnormal lung development with delayed maturation and impaired branching morphogenesis (PMID: 21330457)
- Dramatically increased capacity to form glioma-like tumors with loss of antiproliferative function in brain stem cells (PMID: 28687615)

Xenopus knockdown studies demonstrate:
- Reduced myogenic transcription of muscle markers and altered slow muscle-specific gene expression (PMID: 30071218)
- Impaired antagonism of Meox2 inhibitory function on myogenic differentiation (PMID: 30071218)

**Lethality and Mendelian Ratios:**
No data on lethality or Mendelian ratio deviations were provided in the available studies.

**Consistency with Human Phenotype:**
The mouse knockout phenotypes showing impaired neuronal migration, abnormal cortical lamination, and defective postmitotic differentiation are highly consistent with the human neurodevelopmental phenotype of intellectual disability, developmental delay, and autism spectrum features observed in Lamb-Shaffer syndrome. The role in cell fate determination and differentiation aligns with the developmental delay and cognitive impairment seen in patients.

**Strength of Model Organism Evidence: STRONG**

The model organism evidence is strong, with multiple independent studies in different organisms demonstrating critical roles for SOX5 in neuronal migration, cortical development, and cell fate determination—processes directly relevant to the neurodevelopmental phenotypes observed in human patients.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known Pathways and Molecular Function:**

SOX5 functions as a member of the chondrogenic SOX Trio (SOX9, SOX5, SOX6) that regulates chondrogenesis in a stage-specific manner (PMID: 32380234). SOX5 enhances transactivation by SOX9 in chondrocytes but antagonizes SOX9 in oligodendrocytes and melanocytes, demonstrating context-dependent function (PMID: 19647094).

SOX5 is identified as a downstream target of the ZBTB20 transcriptional repressor, with mutations in SOX5 associated with autism spectrum disorder and intellectual disability (ASHG 2013). SOX5 is also implicated in transcriptional regulation of extracellular matrix genes by promoting SOX9 binding to distant enhancers through its HMG domain (PMID: 40500800).

**Expression Pattern and Brain Relevance:**

SOX5 is identified as a gene with genetic associations to brain-based traits and neuropsychiatric disorders, converging with other neurodevelopmental genes (MEF2C, SATB2, TCF4, TSHZ3) in specific coexpression modules and distinct cell types during prenatal and postnatal brain development (PMID: 30545854). This expression pattern during critical developmental windows is consistent with its role in neurodevelopmental disorders.

**Key Experimental Findings:**

- Functional studies demonstrate that pathogenic missense variants in the HMG domain prevent DNA binding and transactivation, while variants outside this domain show reduced but not absent activity (PMID: 31578471, PMID: 40500800)
- CRISPRa screening identified SOX5 as a top hit for antagonizing cellular senescence and rejuvenation through elevation of enhancer activity (PMID: 37832549)
- SOX5 is identified as a lineage-defining gene whose chromatin accessibility is controlled by T-bet in human B cell subsets (PMID: 35867801)
- GWAS meta-analysis identified SOX5 at genome-wide significant loci for suicide attempt risk (PMID: 37777856) and back pain susceptibility (ASHG 2022), suggesting broader neuropsychiatric and pain-related associations

**Strength of Biological Evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is strong, with clear demonstration of SOX5's role as a transcription factor with HMG domain-dependent DNA binding, documented involvement in neurodevelopmental pathways, expression during critical brain development periods, and functional validation showing how pathogenic variants impair transcriptional activity.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall Evidence Level: STRONG**

SOX5 is a well-established neurodevelopmental disorder gene with strong evidence across all three domains of evidence.

**Key Strengths:**

1. **Robust Clinical Evidence**: 74 patients across six independent studies with consistent phenotypic features (intellectual disability, developmental delay, language delay, autism spectrum features) and documented pathogenic variants of multiple types (LoF, missense, structural variants)

2. **Functional Validation**: Multiple studies demonstrate that pathogenic variants impair SOX5's transcriptional function, particularly those affecting the HMG domain, establishing a clear genotype-phenotype correlation

3. **Mechanistic Understanding**: Model organism studies clearly demonstrate SOX5's critical role in neuronal migration, cortical lamination, and cell fate determination—processes directly relevant to the observed human phenotypes

4. **Molecular Characterization**: SOX5 is identified as a key transcription factor in neurodevelopmental pathways, with documented interactions with other neurodevelopmental genes and expression during critical developmental windows

**Key Gaps and Limitations:**

1. **Limited Cohort Representation**: Patients are primarily from individual centers or regional cohorts; no representation from large standardized research cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD) limits generalizability

2. **Incomplete Penetrance and Expressivity Data**: While variable expressivity is documented, systematic data on penetrance and factors modifying phenotypic severity are limited

3. **Genotype-Phenotype Correlation**: While HMG domain variants show more severe phenotypes, the relationship between specific variant types and clinical severity could be more thoroughly characterized

4. **Lethality Data**: No information on whether SOX5 haploinsufficiency affects viability or whether homozygous loss-of-function is embryonic lethal

**Conclusion:**

SOX5 is a high-confidence neurodevelopmental disorder gene in which heterozygous loss-of-function and missense variants cause Lamb-Shaffer syndrome, a neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by intellectual disability, developmental delay, language impairment, and autism spectrum features. The evidence is supported by consistent clinical findings across 74 patients, functional studies demonstrating impaired transcriptional activity of pathogenic variants, and model organism studies showing critical roles in neuronal development and migration. The gene meets criteria for autosomal dominant inheritance with variable expressivity.
