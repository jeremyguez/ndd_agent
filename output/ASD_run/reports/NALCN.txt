# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: NALCN AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

NALCN (Sodium Leak Channel, Non-Selective) is a sodium leak channel that conducts depolarizing Na+ currents to regulate the resting membrane potential of neurons (PMID: 34929720). The channel functions as part of a ~1-MDa channelosome complex with FAM155A, UNC79, and UNC80, and plays critical roles in modulating neuronal excitability, pacemaking activity, respiration, circadian rhythm, and pain sensitivity (PMID: 34929720).

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports:** Three independent case reports/case series were identified.

**Total patient count:** Approximately 3 individual patients with NALCN variants associated with neurodevelopmental phenotypes.

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Autism spectrum disorder (identified in 2 of 3 cases)
- Intellectual disability / mental retardation (2 of 3 cases)
- Seizures / epilepsy (2 of 3 cases)
- Synaptic signaling imbalance (1 case)

**Predominant variant type:** The specific mutation types were not detailed in the extracted data, though variants were identified through exome sequencing and high-throughput sequencing approaches. One case involved a novel mutation co-segregating with a 14q11.2 microdeletion (PMID: 32034750).

**Cohort source:** One case was identified through diagnostic exome sequencing at Ambry Genetics (United States) (ASHG 2014); one from Bulgaria; one with unspecified origin. None appear to be from established research cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL).

**Notable finding:** One patient carried de novo mutations in both ANO3 and NALCN, suggesting potential oligogenic inheritance (ASHG 2014).

**Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED**

The clinical evidence is limited by: (1) very small sample size (n=3 patients); (2) lack of detailed variant nomenclature and functional characterization; (3) absence of segregation data in most cases; (4) no systematic screening of larger ASD or neurodevelopmental cohorts; and (5) lack of comparison to established NALCN-related syndromes (CLIFAHDD, IHPRF1/IHPRF2) described in the biological evidence.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (primary), rat, Xenopus oocytes, and rodent models.

**Key neurological phenotypes from knockouts:**

- **Neuronal excitability and pacemaking:** NALCN conditional knockout in dopaminergic neurons of the substantia nigra and ventral tegmental area reduced pacemaking rates by 31-71% depending on neuronal subtype (PMID: 37640554). NALCN blockade abolished pacemaking in substantia nigra dopaminergic neurons (PMID: 34409942).

- **Auditory system function:** Glycinergic neuron-specific NALCN knockout eliminated spontaneous firing in cartwheel interneurons and abolished α2-adrenergic receptor-mediated inhibition of spike generation (PMID: 37987013).

- **Pain sensitivity:** NALCN knockdown and conditional knockout prevented neuropathic pain symptoms in rodent models (PMID: 33766679). NALCN genetic deletion significantly reduced intrinsic excitability of spinal projection neurons involved in pain processing (PMID: 29746349).

- **Sleep regulation:** A gain-of-function NALCN mutation in mice reduced rapid eye movement sleep by increasing excitability of REMS-inhibiting neurons (PMID: 27806374).

- **Reproductive function:** Smooth muscle-specific NALCN knockout mice exhibited reduced myometrial excitability and prolonged/dysfunctional labor, though no lethality or infertility was reported (PMID: 30021195).

**Lethality and Mendelian ratios:** No embryonic lethality or Mendelian ratio deviations were reported in the conditional knockout models examined.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The model organism evidence demonstrates that NALCN dysfunction impairs neuronal excitability and pacemaking, which is consistent with the neurodevelopmental and seizure phenotypes observed in human cases. The role of NALCN in regulating neuronal excitability provides a plausible mechanistic link to autism, intellectual disability, and epilepsy.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**

Multiple independent conditional knockout and knockdown studies in mice and rats consistently demonstrate that NALCN loss-of-function impairs neuronal excitability and pacemaking, with no embryonic lethality, supporting the feasibility of heterozygous pathogenic variants in humans.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Molecular function and pathways:**

NALCN conducts background sodium leak currents that depolarize the resting membrane potential of neurons, thereby regulating intrinsic neuronal excitability (PMID: 34929720, PMID: 22476981). The channel is activated by M3 muscarinic receptors through Src kinase-dependent signaling (PMID: 19575010) and is modulated by α2-adrenergic receptors and GABAB receptors (PMID: 37987013). The channel functions as part of a multi-subunit channelosome complex with UNC79, UNC80, and FAM155A, with calmodulin binding to the carboxy-terminal domain of NALCN serving as a putative modulatory hub (PMID: 34929720).

**Expression pattern:**

NALCN is expressed in the central and peripheral nervous system, including dopaminergic neurons of the substantia nigra and ventral tegmental area, auditory neurons in the dorsal cochlear nucleus, spinal projection neurons, and dorsal root ganglion neurons. Expression is also documented in pancreatic β-cells and myometrial smooth muscle (PMID: 19575010, PMID: 30021195). In gastric cancer tissues, NALCN expression is significantly downregulated compared to normal tissue (PMID: 40013144).

**Key experimental findings:**

1. **Channelosome architecture:** Cryo-EM structural studies revealed the ~1-MDa NALCN channelosome complex with UNC79 and UNC80 forming an intertwined anti-parallel superhelical assembly that docks onto the NALCN-FAM155A pore-forming subcomplex (PMID: 34929720).

2. **Genotype-phenotype correlation:** A comprehensive analysis of 51 patients with NALCN and UNC80 variants identified distinct syndromes: gain-of-function variants cause CLIFAHDD syndrome (congenital lower limb contractures, facial involvement, arthrogryposis, and developmental delay), while loss-of-function variants cause IHPRF1 syndrome (infantile hypotonia, psychomotor retardation, and feeding difficulties) with 70.6% of loss-of-function patients presenting with refractory epilepsy (PMID: 40048676).

3. **UNC79 channelosome component:** Heterozygous loss-of-function variants in UNC79, a critical NALCN channelosome component, cause neurodevelopmental syndrome with intellectual disability, developmental delay, and epilepsy in humans, with corresponding seizure-like phenotypes in Drosophila knockdown models (PMID: 37183800).

4. **Hormone regulation:** In myometrial smooth muscle cells, progesterone increases NALCN expression 5.6-fold while estrogen decreases it 2.3-fold, demonstrating hormonal regulation of NALCN expression (PMID: 31935111).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is strong, with well-characterized molecular function, defined protein interactions, structural data, and established genotype-phenotype correlations linking NALCN variants to specific neurodevelopmental syndromes.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths:**

1. **Robust biological and functional evidence:** NALCN's role in regulating neuronal excitability is well-established through structural, biochemical, and electrophysiological studies. The channelosome complex architecture is defined, and the molecular mechanisms of channel activation and modulation are characterized.

2. **Consistent model organism evidence:** Multiple independent conditional knockout studies in mice demonstrate that NALCN loss-of-function impairs neuronal excitability and pacemaking without embryonic lethality, supporting the plausibility of heterozygous pathogenic variants in humans.

3. **Established disease associations:** The biological evidence clearly links NALCN variants to defined neurodevelopmental syndromes (CLIFAHDD with gain-of-function variants; IHPRF1/IHPRF2 with loss-of-function variants), with strong genotype-phenotype correlations and high penetrance of epilepsy in loss-of-function cases (PMID: 40048676).

4. **Mechanistic plausibility:** The phenotypes observed in human cases (autism, intellectual disability, seizures) are mechanistically consistent with impaired neuronal excitability caused by NALCN dysfunction.

**Key gaps and limitations:**

1. **Minimal clinical case data:** Only 3 individual patients with NALCN variants and neurodevelopmental phenotypes were identified in the clinical literature. This is substantially smaller than the 51-patient cohort described in the biological evidence (PMID: 40048676), suggesting that the clinical evidence extracted may be incomplete or that additional case reports exist in the literature.

2. **Lack of variant characterization:** The clinical cases lack detailed variant nomenclature, functional validation, and segregation analysis. It is unclear whether the variants identified are loss-of-function or gain-of-function, which is critical for predicting phenotype based on the established genotype-phenotype correlations.

3. **No systematic cohort screening:** The clinical cases appear to be incidental findings from exome sequencing rather than results of systematic screening of neurodevelopmental cohorts, limiting the ability to estimate prevalence or penetrance.

4. **Absence of functional studies:** None of the clinical cases included functional validation of the identified variants (e.g., patch-clamp electrophysiology, cell-based assays).

**Conclusion:**

NALCN is a well-characterized neurodevelopmental disorder gene with strong biological and model organism evidence supporting a role for heterozygous pathogenic variants in causing autism spectrum disorder, intellectual disability, and epilepsy. However, the clinical evidence base is currently limited, with only 3 reported cases lacking detailed variant characterization. The established genotype-phenotype correlations from larger cohort studies (PMID: 40048676) suggest that systematic re-evaluation of existing exome sequencing data and prospective screening of neurodevelopmental cohorts would likely identify additional NALCN-related cases and strengthen the clinical evidence base. Functional validation of newly identified variants is essential to confirm pathogenicity and predict phenotypic severity.
