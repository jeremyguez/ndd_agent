# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: TCF4 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

TCF4 (Transcription Factor 4) is a transcriptional regulator and key component of the canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, functioning as a DNA-binding partner of β-catenin to regulate transcription of developmental and neuronal genes. TCF4 plays critical roles in neural progenitor proliferation, neuronal differentiation, and cortical development, making it essential for normal neurodevelopmental processes.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of Studies and Patients:**
Seven independent clinical case reports or case series were identified, encompassing approximately 41 patients with TCF4 pathogenic variants. The largest series included 36 patients with Pitt-Hopkins syndrome (ASHG 2018 - Diagnostic flow-chart to improve the detection rate and to uncover the genetic heterogeneity in Pitt-Hopkins syndrome: Experience on 61 subjects), with additional single case reports and small cohort studies from India, Colombia, Germany, Japan, and the United States.

**Most Common Phenotypes:**
The clinical presentation is dominated by severe neurodevelopmental features:
- Severe to profound intellectual disability (present in all reported cases)
- Severe speech and language impairment
- Global developmental delay
- Postnatal microcephaly
- Characteristic facial dysmorphism (including café au lait spots, blue scleras, flared nostrils, large mouth, wide-spaced teeth)
- Breathing abnormalities and movement disorders
- Seizures (absence seizures documented in at least one case)
- Behavioral abnormalities and stereotypical movements
- Motor incoordination and hypotonia

Additionally, TCF4 variants have been identified in autism spectrum disorder (ASHG 2023 - Deciphering the genetic architecture of autism spectrum disorder using chromosomal microarray and whole exome sequencing in patient-parent trios in India) and cerebral palsy (ASHG 2017 - Debunking the cerebral palsy (CP)-birth asphyxia myth: Frequent genomic etiologies for CP identified by exome sequencing).

**Predominant Variant Type:**
Loss-of-function (LoF) variants predominate, including frameshift deletions (e.g., c.1191_1194delTCAT, p.I397Mfs*64 from Colombia case), splice site mutations (e.g., c.1486+3A>C, IVS16+3 A>C from Germany case), and intragenic deletions/duplications detected by MLPA. One missense variant (c.1738C>T, p.R580W) was reported in a Japanese patient with Pitt-Hopkins syndrome (ASHG 2017 - Whole genome sequencing of neurodevelopmental disorders in Japanese), suggesting that some missense variants may also be pathogenic. De novo inheritance was documented in multiple cases, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance.

**Cohort Membership:**
One case was identified from the DiscovEHR cohort (ASHG 2017 - Debunking the cerebral palsy (CP)-birth asphyxia myth), a large exome sequencing initiative from Geisinger and Regeneron Genetics Center. Most other cases appear to be from clinical diagnostic centers rather than large research cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL membership not explicitly documented).

**Strength of Clinical Evidence: STRONG**

The clinical evidence is strong, supported by: (1) multiple independent case reports and case series from geographically diverse centers; (2) consistent phenotypic presentation across patients, particularly the well-characterized Pitt-Hopkins syndrome phenotype; (3) de novo variants in unaffected parents, supporting pathogenicity; (4) functional validation in some cases (e.g., RNA studies demonstrating aberrantly spliced transcripts with stop codons); (5) detection by gold-standard methods (WES, WGS, direct sequencing, MLPA); and (6) expansion of the phenotypic spectrum to include autism and cerebral palsy, broadening the clinical relevance of TCF4 mutations.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms Studied:**
Mouse models have been the primary organism studied, with evidence from multiple transgenic and knockout approaches.

**Key Phenotypes from Model Organisms:**
The model organism evidence is primarily indirect, focusing on TCF4 expression and pathway regulation rather than direct TCF4 knockout studies:

- **CNV models (ASHG 2014 - Transcriptional consequences of 16p11.2 microdeletion/microduplication syndrome in mouse cortex):** A mouse model of 16p11.2 microdeletion (syntenic 7qf3 region) showed altered TCF4 expression alongside differential expression of genes involved in intellectual disability and autism spectrum disorder pathogenesis. The study identified enrichment for genes related to Joubert syndrome (p = 2.36e-4) and Bardet-Biedl syndrome (p = 0.046), suggesting TCF4 dysregulation contributes to neurodevelopmental pathology.

- **REST/NRSF transgenic models (ASHG 2012 - MIR137, a candidate gene for schizophrenia; ASHG 2010 - Genome-wide supported schizophrenia-associated genes):** Transgenic mice with REST/NRSF manipulation showed altered TCF4 expression and abnormal dendritic spine morphology, with TCF4 identified as one of 8 out of 10 GWAS-supported schizophrenia-associated genes in the interacting network.

- **Epigenetic models (ASHG 2014 - 5-hydroxymethylcytosine Dysregulation in Neurodegenerative Disorders):** Multiple neurodegenerative disease mouse models (FXTAS, SCA1, SCA7, SCA8, SCA17) showed altered 5-hydroxymethylcytosine distributions at TCF4 binding sites, affecting TCF4 binding affinity and neuronal pathways including Purkinje neuron differentiation.

- **Hirschsprung disease analysis (ASHG 2010 - A novel analysis of genomic rearrangements in multifactorial Hirschsprung disease):** TCF4 was identified as one of 12 genes with segregating mutations in Hirschsprung disease, showing non-Mendelian inheritance patterns.

**Consistency with Human Phenotype:**
The model organism evidence shows consistency with human neurodevelopmental phenotypes through pathway analysis and expression studies, though direct TCF4 loss-of-function knockout models are not explicitly described in the provided evidence. The findings support TCF4's role in neural development, dendritic morphology, and neuronal differentiation—all processes disrupted in human TCF4-related neurodevelopmental disorders.

**Strength of Model Organism Evidence: MODERATE**

The evidence is moderate because: (1) most studies examine TCF4 expression changes rather than direct loss-of-function knockouts; (2) the phenotypes are inferred from pathway analysis and gene expression changes rather than direct behavioral or neurological assessment; (3) no explicit lethality or Mendelian ratio data are provided for TCF4-specific knockouts; and (4) the evidence is primarily supportive of TCF4's role in neurodevelopmental pathways rather than directly demonstrating that TCF4 haploinsufficiency causes the observed human phenotypes. However, the consistency across multiple model systems and the identification of TCF4 in multiple neurodevelopmental pathways strengthen the evidence.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known Pathways and Molecular Function:**

TCF4 functions primarily as a transcriptional effector of the canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway. Key findings include:

- **Wnt/β-catenin signaling:** TCF4 physically interacts with β-catenin to form a transcriptional complex that activates target genes including c-Myc and Cyclin D1 (PMID: 28716029 - FOXP3 promotes tumor growth and metastasis by activating Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway and EMT in non-small cell lung cancer). This interaction is regulated by TNIK kinase, which phosphorylates components of the β-catenin/TCF4 complex to enable transcription (PMID: 36361804 - Structural Insight into TNIK Inhibition).

- **Blood-brain barrier integrity:** TCF4 is a key component of the Wnt3/β-catenin/TCF4 signaling pathway that regulates blood-brain barrier integrity and cognitive function (PMID: 40102948 - Inhibition of neutrophil extracellular traps alleviates blood-brain barrier disruption and cognitive dysfunction via Wnt3/β-catenin/TCF4 signaling in sepsis-associated encephalopathy). This pathway promotes VCL/β-catenin/VE-cadherin complex formation and increases Occludin expression.

- **Neural progenitor proliferation and differentiation:** TCF4 loss-of-function in patient-derived neural progenitor cells from Pitt-Hopkins Syndrome individuals reduces proliferation and neuronal differentiation capacity (PMID: 35501322 - Transcription Factor 4 loss-of-function is associated with deficits in progenitor proliferation and cortical neuron content). TCF4 mutations decrease Wnt signaling and SOX gene expression, leading to reduced progenitor proliferation. Phenotypes are rescued by TCF4 correction or Wnt pathway modulation in brain organoids.

- **Inflammatory regulation:** TCF4 acts as a negative regulator of IL-17C transcription in human keratinocytes, with TCF4 suppression promoting proinflammatory responses (PMID: 38470486 - Suppression of TCF4 promotes a ZC3H12A-mediated self-sustaining inflammatory feedback cycle involving IL-17RA/IL-17RE epidermal signaling).

- **Hepatic transporter regulation:** TCF4 acts as a transcriptional regulator of OATP1B1 expression in hepatocytes, with IL-6 downregulating the β-catenin/TCF4 complex (PMID: 39130625 - STING-mediated IL-6 Inhibits OATP1B1 Expression via the TCF4 Signaling Pathway in Cholestasis).

- **Erythroid development:** TCF4 overexpression increases erythroid colony-forming units twofold, while TCF4 knockdown dramatically decreases erythroid colonies (PMID: 30315825 - TCF4 promotes erythroid development).

**Expression Pattern:**
TCF4 is expressed in the developing and adult brain, with particular relevance to neural progenitor cells and cortical development. The gene is also expressed in other tissues including blood, liver, and skin, explaining the pleiotropic effects observed in TCF4-related disorders.

**Key Experimental Findings:**
- Patient-derived iPSC-derived neural progenitors from Pitt-Hopkins Syndrome children show reduced proliferation and neuronal differentiation when TCF4 is mutated (PMID: 35501322).
- Functional rescue of TCF4 mutations or Wnt pathway modulation restores neural progenitor function in brain organoids (PMID: 35501322).
- Class I HDAC inhibitors upregulate TCF4 expression and activate Wnt/β-catenin signaling in CAR-T cells, with multi-omics confirmation (PMID: 38578828 - Class I HDAC inhibitors enhance antitumor efficacy and persistence of CAR-T cells by activation of the Wnt pathway).
- An intronic trinucleotide repeat expansion within TCF4 associates with altered levels of multiple serum biomarkers and increased cataract risk in 454,799 UK Biobank participants (ASHG 2022 - Phenotypic effects of common coding copy-number variation), demonstrating that TCF4 variation affects multiple physiological systems.

**Strength of Biological Evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is strong because: (1) TCF4's role in the canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway is well-established across multiple cell types and tissues; (2) patient-derived cellular models directly demonstrate that TCF4 loss-of-function impairs neural progenitor proliferation and differentiation; (3) functional rescue experiments confirm that TCF4 correction restores normal cellular phenotypes; (4) multiple independent studies confirm TCF4's role in blood-brain barrier integrity, neural development, and inflammatory regulation; and (5) the molecular mechanisms linking TCF4 haploinsufficiency to neurodevelopmental deficits are increasingly well-characterized.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall Evidence Level: STRONG**

TCF4 is supported by strong evidence as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous (autosomal dominant) pathogenic variants. The evidence meets criteria for a well-established disease gene based on: (1) multiple independent clinical case reports and case series (n≈41 patients) with consistent phenotypes; (2) de novo variants in unaffected parents; (3) functional validation demonstrating loss-of-function mechanisms; (4) patient-derived cellular models showing impaired neural progenitor function; (5) well-characterized molecular pathways linking TCF4 to neurodevelopment; and (6) model organism evidence supporting TCF4's role in neurodevelopmental pathways.

**Key Strengths of the Evidence:**

1. **Clinical consistency:** The phenotypic presentation is remarkably consistent across patients, particularly for Pitt-Hopkins syndrome, with characteristic features including severe intellectual disability, speech impairment, microcephaly, and distinctive facial dysmorphism.

2. **Mechanistic understanding:** Patient-derived neural progenitor cells and brain organoids directly demonstrate that TCF4 loss-of-function impairs proliferation and neuronal differentiation, with rescue by TCF4 correction or Wnt pathway modulation.

3. **Molecular pathway clarity:** TCF4's role in the canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway is well-established, with clear mechanisms linking haploinsufficiency to impaired neural development.

4. **Expanding phenotypic spectrum:** Beyond Pitt-Hopkins syndrome, TCF4 variants have been identified in autism spectrum disorder and cerebral palsy, broadening the clinical relevance of this gene.

5. **Diverse detection methods:** Variants have been identified by multiple gold-standard approaches (WES, WGS, direct sequencing, MLPA), reducing ascertainment bias.

**Key Gaps and Limitations:**

1. **Limited large cohort data:** Most evidence comes from single case reports or small case series rather than large prospective cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD). Systematic screening of large neurodevelopmental cohorts would strengthen prevalence estimates.

2. **Incomplete genotype-phenotype correlation:** While Pitt-Hopkins syndrome is well-characterized, the relationship between specific TCF4 variants and phenotypic severity or spectrum (autism vs. cerebral palsy vs. PTHS) remains incompletely understood.

3. **Limited direct knockout models:** The provided evidence lacks explicit descriptions of TCF4 complete or conditional knockout mouse models with detailed behavioral and neurological phenotyping.

4. **Incomplete functional characterization of missense variants:** While loss-of-function variants are well-established as pathogenic, the functional consequences of missense variants (e.g., p.R580W) require further investigation.

5. **Incomplete understanding of modifier genes:** The role of genetic background and potential modifier genes in determining phenotypic severity is not addressed.

**Conclusion:**

TCF4 is a well-established neurodevelopmental disorder gene in which heterozygous loss-of-function variants cause Pitt-Hopkins syndrome and contribute to autism spectrum disorder and cerebral palsy. The evidence is supported by consistent clinical phenotypes across multiple independent case reports, de novo variants in unaffected parents, functional validation in patient-derived neural progenitor cells, and clear molecular mechanisms linking TCF4 haploinsufficiency to impaired neural progenitor proliferation and cortical development through disruption of Wnt/β-catenin signaling. Future work should focus on systematic screening of large neurodevelopmental cohorts, detailed genotype-phenotype correlation studies, and investigation of potential genetic modifiers that influence phenotypic severity.
