# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: PAPOLG AND NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

PAPOLG (Poly(A) Polymerase Gamma) is a nuclear poly(A) polymerase involved in mRNA polyadenylation and post-transcriptional gene regulation. The gene functions in alternative polyadenylation and mRNA stability control, with emerging roles in inflammatory response pathways and developmental processes.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Case Reports and Patient Numbers:**
Two independent clinical reports were identified, both describing the same patient case from a French center. The clinical evidence is based on 1 unique patient with a heterozygous de novo deletion.

**Phenotypes Observed:**
The patient presented with:
- Speech delay (reported in both studies)
- Autistic traits / autism spectrum features (both studies)
- Motor stereotypies / stereotyped behavior (both studies)
- Brain structure abnormalities (Levy et al. 2016)

**Variant Type:**
The pathogenic variant is a 427 kb heterozygous de novo deletion at 2p16.1 (chr2:60624940-61051867) encompassing PAPOLG and BCL11A. This is a contiguous gene deletion (loss-of-function variant), making it difficult to attribute phenotypes to PAPOLG alone versus BCL11A or other deleted genes.

**Cohort Source:**
The patient was identified through clinical diagnostic testing at a French center, not from established research cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL).

**Strength of Clinical Evidence: LIMITED**

The clinical evidence is limited by: (1) only one unique patient case; (2) a multi-gene deletion making it impossible to isolate PAPOLG's contribution; (3) lack of independent replication; and (4) no segregation data or family studies. While the phenotypes (developmental delay, autism, speech delay) are consistent with neurodevelopmental disorders, the evidence does not establish PAPOLG as a causative gene for these features.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms Studied:**
No model organism studies were identified in the evidence provided.

**Strength of Model Organism Evidence: NONE**

The absence of knockout or knockdown studies in mice, zebrafish, or other model organisms represents a significant gap. Such studies would be critical for establishing causality and understanding the neurodevelopmental role of PAPOLG.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known Pathways and Molecular Function:**

PAPOLG participates in multiple biological processes:

- **mRNA Polyadenylation and Stability:** PAPOLG stabilizes NF-κB mRNA through alternative polyadenylation in SARS-CoV-2-infected macrophages; knockdown of PAPOLG promoted NF-κB mRNA degradation and decreased inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6) (PMID: 40607946).

- **Immune and Inflammatory Response:** PAPOLG was significantly upregulated in SARS-CoV-2-infected THP-1 macrophages and contributes to inflammatory signaling (PMID: 40607946).

- **Oncogenic Pathways:** PAPOLG is amplified and overexpressed in follicular lymphoma and transformed follicular lymphoma, suggesting a proto-oncogenic role (PMID: 31410080). PAPOLG was also identified in a competitive endogenous RNA (ceRNA) regulatory axis (GSEC/miR-101-3p/SNX16/PAPOLG) associated with hepatocellular carcinoma prognosis (PMID: 35482720).

- **Metabolic Regulation:** PAPOLG genetic variants significantly affected plasma metabolic profiles in response to dietary intervention, particularly lipid and amino acid metabolites, TCA cycle intermediates, and acylcarnitines (P<0.001 for genotype and genotype × diet interaction) (PMID: 23964055).

- **Developmental Processes:** PAPOLG expression was significantly altered during oocyte maturation and competence acquisition in rabbits, affecting embryo viability (PMID: 35724452).

**Expression Pattern:**
PAPOLG is expressed in multiple tissues including immune cells (macrophages), cancer cells, and reproductive tissues. However, specific expression data in developing brain or neurodevelopmental tissues is not provided in the available evidence.

**Key Experimental Findings:**
- PAPOLG knockdown reduces inflammatory cytokine production (PMID: 40607946)
- PAPOLG overexpression enhances mRNA stability and cytokine expression (PMID: 40607946)
- PAPOLG is a target of multiple miRNAs involved in MAPK signaling and inflammatory pathways (PMID: 35997417)
- PAPOLG was identified in a diagnostic biomarker panel for epithelial ovarian cancer with 97.8% sensitivity and 99.6% specificity (PMID: 23551967)

**Strength of Biological Evidence: MODERATE**

The biological evidence demonstrates clear molecular functions for PAPOLG in mRNA stability and inflammatory pathways. However, the evidence is primarily derived from cancer biology, immunology, and metabolic studies rather than neurodevelopmental contexts. No direct evidence links PAPOLG dysfunction to neurodevelopmental processes, brain development, or neuronal function.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall Evidence Level: LIMITED**

**Key Strengths:**
1. The clinical case presents a de novo deletion with a clear neurodevelopmental phenotype (speech delay, autism, developmental delay)
2. PAPOLG is located within a defined microdeletion region (2p15-p16.1) associated with neurodevelopmental features
3. PAPOLG has established molecular functions in mRNA stability and gene regulation, which could theoretically impact developmental processes
4. The variant is heterozygous and de novo, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance

**Key Gaps and Limitations:**
1. **Single patient with multi-gene deletion:** The only clinical case involves a 427 kb deletion encompassing both PAPOLG and BCL11A, making it impossible to determine whether PAPOLG or BCL11A (or both) causes the phenotype. BCL11A is a known transcription factor with established roles in development.
2. **No independent replication:** Only one unique patient has been reported; no additional cases with isolated PAPOLG deletions or mutations have been identified.
3. **Absence of model organism data:** No knockout or knockdown studies in mice or other organisms to establish causality or mechanism.
4. **Limited neurodevelopmental evidence:** Biological studies focus on cancer, immunity, and metabolism rather than brain development or neuronal function.
5. **No functional studies of haploinsufficiency:** No evidence that heterozygous loss of PAPOLG alone produces neurodevelopmental phenotypes.

**Conclusion:**

While PAPOLG is located within a microdeletion region associated with neurodevelopmental disorder and has established roles in mRNA regulation, the current evidence is insufficient to establish PAPOLG as a causative gene for autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder. The single reported patient carries a multi-gene deletion, preventing attribution of phenotypes to PAPOLG specifically. Additional cases with isolated PAPOLG variants, functional studies in model organisms, and investigation of PAPOLG expression and function in developing brain tissue would be necessary to establish a causal relationship with neurodevelopmental phenotypes.
