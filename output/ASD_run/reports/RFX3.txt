# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: RFX3 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

RFX3 is a ciliogenic transcription factor that encodes a master regulator of central nervous system development and ciliogenesis. It functions as a DNA-binding transcription factor with a highly-conserved DNA binding domain that regulates genes involved in primary and motile cilia formation, neuronal development, and activity-dependent gene expression in the brain.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of Studies:** 10 independent case reports, case series, and cohort studies were identified.

**Total Patient Count:** Approximately 45 patients with RFX3 variants across all studies (17 patients in PMID: 33658631; 17 patients in ASHG 2019; 8 patients in ASHG 2017; 1 patient in PMID: 40200712; 1 patient in PMID: 37717291; 1 patient in PMID: 25844147; plus additional cases from functional studies).

**Most Common Phenotypes Observed:**
- Autism spectrum disorder (reported in 9/10 studies)
- Intellectual disability or neurodevelopmental delay (8/10 studies)
- Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) (5/10 studies)
- Behavioral difficulties or dysregulated behavior (4/10 studies)
- Sleep problems (3/10 studies)
- Hypotonia (2/10 studies)
- Infantile epileptic spasms syndrome (1/10 studies)
- Congenital heart disease (1/10 studies)

**Predominant Variant Type:** Loss-of-function (LoF) variants predominate, including de novo deletions, de novo point mutations, and inherited heterozygous mutations. Specific variant details are limited in most abstracts, but haploinsufficiency is the proposed mechanism (PMID: 40200712, ASHG 2019).

**Cohort Information:** No patients are explicitly stated to be from known diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL). Most cases appear to be identified through whole exome sequencing, GeneMatcher, or long-read genome sequencing in clinical settings.

**Diagnostic Reasoning:** Clinical diagnosis is supported by: (1) de novo or inherited deleterious variants in RFX3; (2) RFX3 strong expression in developing and adult human brain; (3) RFX binding motifs enriched in cis-regulatory regions of known ASD risk genes (PMID: 33658631); (4) RFX3 identified in CRISPR interference screens as a CHD gene disrupting neural progenitor cell biology (PMID: 40552535); (5) heterozygous LoF variants resulting in disrupted synaptic target gene expression and impaired neuronal activity-dependent responses (PMID: 40060598).

**Strength of Clinical Evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strong due to: multiple independent case series with consistent phenotypes across studies; identification of RFX3 variants in 17+ patients through rigorous genomic methods; consistent association with autism, intellectual disability, and ADHD; functional validation showing LoF mechanism; and enrichment of RFX binding sites near ASD risk genes.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms Studied:** Mouse (primary model) and Xenopus tropicalis (secondary model).

**Key Phenotypes from Full Knockouts:**

*Mouse (biallelic/full knockout):*
- Embryonic lethality with sub-Mendelian ratios (PMID: 15121860)
- Left-right asymmetry defects and situs inversus (PMID: 15121860)
- Hydrocephalus without aqueduct stenosis (PMID: 16930429)
- Corpus callosum agenesis (PMID: 22479201)
- Severe defects in cilia formation and motility (PMID: 15121860, PMID: 19671664)
- Pancreatic endocrine cell defects with reduced insulin production (PMID: 17229940, PMID: 20413507)
- Choroid plexus differentiation defects (PMID: 16930429)

*Xenopus (knockdown):*
- Impaired primary cilia formation in vitro (PMID: 40552535)
- Defects in motile cilia formation (PMID: 40552535)
- Heart development defects (PMID: 40552535)

**Consistency with Human Phenotype:** The model organism data shows strong consistency with human neurodevelopmental phenotypes. Mouse RFX3 knockout causes corpus callosum agenesis and hydrocephalus, which are structural brain abnormalities relevant to autism and intellectual disability. The cilia defects observed in both mouse and Xenopus align with the ciliary biology pathway implicated in autism and congenital heart disease (PMID: 40552535). However, full biallelic knockout causes embryonic lethality or severe structural defects, whereas human patients carry heterozygous variants with milder neurodevelopmental phenotypes, suggesting haploinsufficiency rather than complete loss-of-function.

**Strength of Model Organism Evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strong due to: consistent phenotypes across multiple mouse studies demonstrating critical roles in CNS development and ciliogenesis; demonstration of RFX3 function in conserved developmental pathways; and Xenopus studies confirming cilia and heart development roles. The main limitation is that full knockout models do not directly recapitulate the heterozygous human phenotype, though this is expected for haploinsufficient genes.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known Pathways and Molecular Function:**

RFX3 functions as a master regulator in multiple interconnected pathways:

1. **Ciliogenesis and Ciliary Biology:** RFX3 regulates expression of genes required for primary and motile cilia formation, including FOXJ1 and axonemal dynein genes (PMID: 19671664). It controls cilia growth, beating efficiency, and ciliary motility (PMID: 19671664).

2. **CNS Development:** RFX3 regulates genes involved in corpus callosum development through control of guidepost neuron distribution (PMID: 22479201) and is essential for ependymal cell differentiation (PMID: 16930429).

3. **Neuronal Activity-Dependent Gene Expression:** Heterozygous RFX3 loss disrupts synaptic target gene expression and impairs CREB-dependent responses to neuronal depolarization. RFX3 binding sites co-localize with CREB binding sites near activity-dependent genes, and RFX3 enhances CREB binding in human neurons (PMID: 40060598).

4. **Interneuron Development:** RFX3 controls human cortical interneuron progenitor specification; RFX3 deficiency causes precocious neuronal differentiation and disrupts interneuron development (PMID: 38827400).

5. **Pancreatic Endocrine Development:** RFX3 regulates beta-cell differentiation and function, controlling expression of glucokinase (Gck) and Glut-2 (PMID: 20413507, PMID: 40263183).

**Expression Pattern:**

RFX3 is strongly expressed in developing and adult human brain (PMID: 33658631). It is highly expressed in pancreatic endocrine cell populations (PMID: 40263183) and in tissues undergoing ciliogenesis. Spatial transcriptomics of embryonic mouse tissue shows RFX3 expression in craniofacial and brain development regions (PMID: 40196253).

**Key Experimental Findings:**

- RFX3 undergoes auto-fatty acylation at a conserved cysteine residue, which is required for homodimerization and promotion of ciliary gene expression (PMID: 30127002). Fatty acylation-deficient mutants fail to promote ciliogenesis.

- RFX binding motifs (X-box elements) are enriched in cis-regulatory regions upstream of genes dysregulated in brain tissue from individuals with idiopathic autism (PMID: 40060598).

- RFX3 knockout in iPSC-derived pancreatic islet organoids disrupts endocrine gene regulation and impairs beta cell function (PMID: 40263183).

- RFX3 cooperates with ATOH1 and RFX1 to promote differentiation of hair cells from iPSCs (PMID: 29740017), demonstrating functional interactions with other transcription factors.

- RFX3 is identified as one of 24 important genes for 9p deletion syndrome, present in 83% of affected individuals (PMID: 40196253), suggesting dosage sensitivity.

**Strength of Biological Evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strong due to: comprehensive characterization of RFX3 function across multiple developmental pathways; demonstration of molecular mechanisms (CREB interaction, fatty acylation, X-box binding); consistent findings across multiple cell culture systems (iPSC-derived neurons, pancreatic islets, interneurons); and enrichment of RFX binding motifs near ASD risk genes.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall Evidence Level: STRONG**

RFX3 meets criteria for a high-confidence neurodevelopmental disorder gene with autosomal dominant (heterozygous) pathogenic variants.

**Key Strengths:**

1. **Consistent Clinical Phenotype:** Multiple independent case series (PMID: 33658631, ASHG 2019, ASHG 2017) report 45+ patients with RFX3 variants presenting with a recognizable syndrome of autism, intellectual disability, ADHD, and behavioral difficulties.

2. **Mechanistic Clarity:** Heterozygous loss-of-function is the predominant mechanism, supported by functional studies showing that monoallelic RFX3 loss disrupts synaptic gene expression and activity-dependent neuronal responses (PMID: 40060598).

3. **Convergent Evidence:** Clinical, model organism, and biological evidence converge on RFX3's critical roles in CNS development, ciliogenesis, and neuronal function. The ciliary biology pathway is independently implicated in autism and congenital heart disease (PMID: 40552535).

4. **Pathway Enrichment:** RFX binding motifs are enriched in cis-regulatory regions of known ASD risk genes, suggesting RFX3 acts as a master regulator of autism-relevant transcriptional networks (PMID: 33658631).

5. **Dosage Sensitivity:** RFX3 is under marked haploinsufficient constraint in human populations (ASHG 2019), and is deleted in 80-83% of individuals with 9p minus syndrome (ASHG 2023, PMID: 40196253).

**Key Gaps or Limitations:**

1. **Limited Variant Specificity:** Most clinical reports do not provide detailed variant nomenclature or coordinates, limiting ability to assess genotype-phenotype correlations or identify recurrent mutations.

2. **Heterozygous Model Gap:** Full biallelic RFX3 knockout in mice causes embryonic lethality or severe structural defects, whereas human heterozygous variants cause milder neurodevelopmental phenotypes. Conditional or heterozygous mouse models would better recapitulate human disease.

3. **Incomplete Phenotypic Characterization:** Some case reports lack detailed developmental, behavioral, or neuroimaging data. Standardized phenotyping across cohorts would strengthen genotype-phenotype correlations.

4. **Limited Functional Validation:** While CRISPR and knockdown studies support LoF mechanism, patient-derived cellular models (iPSC-derived neurons) with specific RFX3 variants would provide stronger functional validation.

5. **Cohort Representation:** Patients do not appear to be from large diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD), limiting statistical power for gene discovery and phenotype estimation.

**Conclusion:**

RFX3 is a well-established neurodevelopmental disorder gene with strong evidence for autosomal dominant inheritance through heterozygous loss-of-function variants. The gene encodes a master regulator of ciliogenesis and CNS development with critical roles in neuronal activity-dependent gene expression, interneuron specification, and corpus callosum development. Heterozygous RFX3 variants cause a recognizable neurodevelopmental syndrome characterized by autism spectrum disorder, intellectual disability, ADHD, and behavioral difficulties, with additional features including sleep problems, hypotonia, and in some cases congenital heart disease. The convergence of clinical, model organism, and biological evidence, combined with enrichment of RFX binding sites near autism risk genes and marked haploinsufficient constraint, provides strong support for RFX3 as a high-confidence autism and neurodevelopmental disorder gene.
