# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: RAB17 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

RAB17 is a small GTPase belonging to the Rab family of proteins that primarily functions in endosomal trafficking and vesicle transport. RAB17 localizes to recycling endosomes in a Rab11-dependent manner and regulates basolateral-to-apical transcytosis in polarized cells, with roles in vesicle maturation, cargo sorting, and membrane protein trafficking (PMID: 31959474, PMID: 30256711).

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

RAB17 functions as a regulator of endosomal trafficking and vesicle transport with multiple roles in cellular homeostasis. The protein interacts with ALS2 (an ALS2-related protein) to regulate maturation of Rab17-residing nascent endosomes to early endosomes, with RABGEF1 functioning as its guanine nucleotide exchange factor (PMID: 31959474). RAB17 is monosumoylated and requires both GTP-binding and sumoylation for proper apical vesicle docking in polarized hepatocytes (PMID: 30256711).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**

The available evidence focuses primarily on RAB17 expression in cancer tissues and diabetic wound healing models rather than neurodevelopmental tissues. One study identified RAB17 as a regulator of angiogenesis in human dermal microvascular endothelial cells, with reduced expression in diabetic foot ulcer patients (PMID: 37605780). However, no studies specifically examined RAB17 expression in developing brain, neural progenitor cells, or other neurodevelopmental tissues.

**Key experimental findings:**

The biological evidence predominantly demonstrates RAB17's role in cancer biology and ferroptosis regulation:

- RAB17 suppresses ferroptosis in endometrial cancer cells by inhibiting TFRC (transferrin receptor) protein expression through ubiquitin-proteasome-dependent degradation (PMID: 39242574)
- RAB17 acts as a tumor suppressor in hepatocellular carcinoma and non-small cell lung cancer, with reduced expression correlating with poor prognosis (PMID: 25707355, PMID: 31841274)
- RAB17 downregulation promotes NSCLC cell proliferation and invasion through STAT3/HIF-1α/VEGF signaling and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (PMID: 31841274)
- RAB17 expression is negatively correlated with DNA methylation and immune cell infiltration in kidney renal clear cell carcinoma (PMID: 37269962)
- RAB17 interacts with MAL2 in a GTP-dependent manner to redistribute invadopodia proteins and regulate actin-dependent protrusions in hepatocellular carcinoma (PMID: 39813085)
- RAB17 regulates trafficking of neuropilin-2 (NRP2), a pro-invasive cargo, through interaction with Vamp8 at late endosomes (PMID: 28062852)

**Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE**

While the biological evidence demonstrates clear molecular functions and pathway involvement, the evidence is heavily skewed toward cancer biology and metabolic stress responses rather than neurodevelopmental processes. No studies examined RAB17's role in neurogenesis, neural differentiation, synaptogenesis, or other neurodevelopmental pathways.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
- Well-characterized molecular function in endosomal trafficking and vesicle transport
- Demonstrated protein-protein interactions with known regulatory partners (ALS2, RABGEF1, MAL2, Vamp8)
- Consistent evidence across multiple cancer types showing RAB17 as a tumor suppressor
- Clear mechanistic pathways identified (ferroptosis regulation, STAT3/HIF-1α/VEGF signaling, ERK pathway inhibition)

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- Complete absence of clinical evidence: no reported cases, case series, or patient cohorts with RAB17 variants and neurodevelopmental phenotypes
- No model organism studies examining neurodevelopmental or neurological phenotypes
- No expression data from developing brain or neural tissues
- Biological evidence focused almost exclusively on cancer biology and metabolic stress, with no investigation of neurodevelopmental pathways
- No functional studies examining RAB17's role in neurogenesis, neural differentiation, axonal transport, or synaptic function

**Conclusion:**

Based on the available literature, there is currently INSUFFICIENT evidence to support RAB17 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene associated with heterozygous pathogenic variants. While RAB17 has well-established roles in endosomal trafficking and functions as a tumor suppressor in multiple cancer types, no clinical cases, model organism evidence, or neurodevelopmental pathway studies support a role in neurodevelopmental disease. To establish RAB17 as a neurodevelopmental gene would require identification of patients with de novo or inherited RAB17 variants presenting with developmental delay, intellectual disability, or other neurodevelopmental phenotypes, coupled with functional studies demonstrating the relevance of RAB17 to neural development and function.
