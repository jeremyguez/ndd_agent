# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: CACNA1D AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

CACNA1D encodes the pore-forming α1 subunit of Cav1.3 L-type calcium channels, which are critical for neuronal excitability, synaptic transmission, and calcium-dependent gene transcription. The gene is involved in neuronal pacemaking, excitability-transcription coupling, and circadian rhythm regulation, with expression particularly important in the central and peripheral nervous systems.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of Independent Case Reports/Series:** Seven distinct clinical studies were identified, including three detailed case series with molecular characterization and four large-scale genomic studies.

**Total Patient Numbers:** Approximately 15-20 well-characterized individuals with pathogenic CACNA1D variants across detailed case reports, plus identification in large exome sequencing cohorts (>4,000 ASD probands in aggregate studies; 370 schizophrenia and 192 autism spectrum disorder patients in Japanese cohort).

**Most Common Phenotypes Observed:**
- Autism spectrum disorder (reported in 6 of 7 studies)
- Developmental delay / intellectual disability (reported in 5 of 7 studies)
- Seizures / epilepsy (reported in 3 of 7 studies)
- Primary aldosteronism (reported in 2 of 7 studies)
- Congenital tremor, hypotonia, and respiratory insufficiency (reported in 1 detailed series)
- Sensorineural deafness (reported in 1 series)
- Hyperinsulinemic hypoglycemia / hypoglycemia (reported in 2 of 7 studies)

**Predominant Variant Type:** Missense variants predominate (9 distinct de novo missense variants across 12 patients in PMID: 32583268; additional missense variants in PMID: 39246741, PMID: 37698939, and PMID: 38553610). Loss-of-function variants (nonsense and in-frame deletions) were identified in the Japanese psychiatric cohort (PMID: ASHG 2015), suggesting both gain-of-function and loss-of-function mechanisms may contribute to disease.

**Molecular Mechanism:** De novo missense variants demonstrate gain-of-function through activity-enhancing gating changes, characterized by:
- Shift of channel activation and inactivation to more hyperpolarized potentials
- Impaired inactivation and deactivation kinetics
- Enhanced Ca2+ influx demonstrated by patch-clamp electrophysiology (PMID: 32583268, PMID: 39246741)

**Cohort Affiliation:** No patients were explicitly identified as from known research cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL). Cases appear to be from individual clinical centers and large exome sequencing studies.

**Strength of Clinical Evidence: STRONG**

Rationale: Multiple independent case series with detailed electrophysiological validation of pathogenic mechanisms; consistent phenotypic pattern across studies; identification in large-scale exome sequencing studies (ASHG 2013, 2014) establishing CACNA1D as recurrently disrupted in ~3% of ASD probands; de novo inheritance pattern in all detailed cases supports autosomal dominant pathogenicity.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms Studied:** Mouse (5 studies) and zebrafish (1 study).

**Key Neurological Phenotypes from Genetic Models:**

*Mouse Models:*
- Full knockout of Cav1.3 is viable with no spontaneous neuropsychiatric or neurological disease symptoms, but acute pharmacological activation results in depressive-like behavior (PMID: 26842699)
- Heterozygous A749G knock-in mice (Cav1.3AG) reproduced human neurodevelopmental phenotype including developmental delay, social deficit, and hyperactivity without major neuroanatomical changes (PMID: 37698939)
- Conditional knockout in inner hair cells resulted in strongly enhanced channel inactivation, impaired sustained exocytosis, and hearing impairment (PMID: 39718549)
- Conditional knockout in superior olivary complex resulted in dramatic volume reduction (43-47%) of major nuclei, reduced cell number (46-56% decline), and abnormal auditory processing (PMID: 22678062)

*Zebrafish Model:*
- Heterozygous splice variant mutation resulted in 50% reduction of specific splice variants (haploinsufficiency) with hyperlocomotion and psychosis-like behavior (PMID: 33057948)
- Homozygous mutation showed gain-of-function with increased transcript levels above wild-type
- Sub-Mendelian ratios and perinatal lethality in homozygotes observed
- Antipsychotics (risperidone, haloperidol) reversed hyperactivity to wild-type levels, supporting psychiatric relevance (PMID: 33057948)

**Consistency with Human Phenotype:** The heterozygous A749G mouse model directly reproduced the human neurodevelopmental syndrome phenotype (developmental delay, social deficit, hyperactivity), providing strong validation. The zebrafish model demonstrated that both haploinsufficiency and gain-of-function variants can produce behavioral abnormalities responsive to antipsychotic medication, consistent with human psychiatric manifestations.

**Lethality and Mendelian Ratios:** Full knockout mice are viable, suggesting haploinsufficiency is not lethal. Zebrafish homozygotes show perinatal lethality with sub-Mendelian ratios, suggesting potential dosage sensitivity in some genetic backgrounds.

**Strength of Model Organism Evidence: STRONG**

Rationale: Heterozygous gain-of-function mouse model (A749G) directly reproduces human neurodevelopmental phenotype with validated gating changes; zebrafish model demonstrates behavioral abnormalities responsive to antipsychotics; multiple conditional knockout models clarify tissue-specific functions; consistency between human variants and model organism phenotypes supports pathogenic mechanism.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known Pathways and Molecular Function:**

CACNA1D encodes Cav1.3 L-type calcium channels involved in:
- Neuronal excitability and pacemaking
- Synaptic transmission and exocytosis (particularly in inner hair cells, requiring calcium-binding protein CaBP1/2 for sustained currents; PMID: 39718549)
- Calcium-dependent gene transcription and excitability-transcription coupling
- Circadian rhythm regulation (PMID: 40460120)
- Intracellular calcium signaling in adrenocortical cells (PMID: 32583268, PMID: 35841527)
- Macrophage inflammation via MAPK pathway (PMID: 41223220)

**Expression Pattern:** CACNA1D is expressed broadly in the central nervous system (dopamine neurons, medium spiny neurons, auditory brainstem nuclei) and peripheral tissues (adrenal cortex, inner ear, macrophages). Brain expression is particularly relevant to neurodevelopmental phenotypes.

**Key Experimental Findings:**

*Functional Studies:*
- Heterologous expression of pathogenic variants demonstrates enhanced Ca2+ influx through activity-enhancing gating changes (PMID: 32583268, PMID: 39246741)
- Native LTCC recordings in adrenal chromaffin cells from A749G mice confirmed predicted gating changes (PMID: 37698939)
- Evolutionary co-variation analysis successfully predicted pathogenicity of novel variants (PMID: 38553610)

*Pathway Studies:*
- CACNA1D identified as one of ten calcium channel genes associated with intellectual disability; gain-of-function variants associated with more severe phenotypes than loss-of-function (PMID: 33985586)
- CACNA1D is a major driver of aldosterone-producing adenomas through somatic mutations, with germline variants causing familial hyperaldosteronism (PMID: 35841527, PMID: 32507359, PMID: 31207362)
- GWAS studies identified CACNA1D as genome-wide significant locus for diastolic blood pressure in African Americans (p = 1.26×10⁻¹²; ASHG 2017) and resistant hypertension (ASHG 2018), suggesting broader cardiovascular relevance

*Psychiatric Relevance:*
- Japanese cohort screening identified rare disruptive variants (nonsense and in-frame deletions) in schizophrenia and autism spectrum disorder patients, suggesting dysregulation of cellular calcium homeostasis in psychiatric pathogenesis (ASHG 2015)

**Strength of Biological Evidence: STRONG**

Rationale: Well-characterized molecular function of Cav1.3 channels with clear mechanistic link between gain-of-function variants and increased neuronal excitability; multiple independent functional studies validate pathogenic mechanisms; pathway analysis demonstrates involvement in neurodevelopmental processes (excitability-transcription coupling, synaptic transmission); GWAS evidence supports broader biological relevance; consistent findings across cell types and tissues.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall Evidence Level: STRONG**

**Key Strengths of the Evidence:**

1. **Robust Clinical Evidence:** Multiple independent case series with detailed molecular characterization; consistent phenotypic pattern (autism, developmental delay, seizures) across studies; identification in large-scale exome sequencing studies establishing CACNA1D as recurrently disrupted in ~3% of ASD probands; de novo inheritance pattern in all detailed cases strongly supports autosomal dominant pathogenicity.

2. **Validated Molecular Mechanism:** Gain-of-function pathogenic mechanism clearly demonstrated through patch-clamp electrophysiology in multiple studies; activity-enhancing gating changes (hyperpolarized activation/inactivation, impaired deactivation) consistently associated with increased Ca2+ influx; mechanism validated in both heterologous expression systems and native tissue recordings.

3. **Direct Model Organism Validation:** Heterozygous A749G mouse model directly reproduces human neurodevelopmental phenotype (developmental delay, social deficit, hyperactivity) with validated gating changes; zebrafish model demonstrates behavioral abnormalities responsive to antipsychotic medication; consistency between human variants and model organism phenotypes strongly supports pathogenic mechanism.

4. **Biological Pathway Clarity:** Well-characterized molecular function of Cav1.3 channels with clear mechanistic link to neurodevelopmental processes (excitability-transcription coupling, synaptic transmission, neuronal pacemaking); GWAS evidence supports broader biological relevance to neuropsychiatric and cardiovascular phenotypes.

**Key Gaps or Limitations:**

1. **Limited Functional Diversity:** Identified pathogenic variants are predominantly missense with gain-of-function mechanism; loss-of-function variants identified in psychiatric cohorts but not well-characterized functionally, limiting understanding of whether both mechanisms contribute to disease.

2. **Incomplete Phenotypic Characterization:** Some case reports lack comprehensive neuropsychological testing or standardized diagnostic criteria; variable expressivity noted (e.g., primary aldosteronism present in some patients but not others), with limited genotype-phenotype correlation analysis.

3. **Absence from Major Research Cohorts:** No cases identified from well-established research cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL), limiting integration with standardized phenotyping and data sharing infrastructure.

4. **Limited Therapeutic Data:** While dihydropyridine LTCC inhibitors proposed as therapeutic option (PMID: 36592224), no clinical trial data or systematic treatment outcomes reported; zebrafish antipsychotic response suggests potential psychiatric benefit but requires human validation.

**Conclusion:**

CACNA1D is a well-established neurodevelopmental disorder gene with strong evidence for autosomal dominant pathogenicity through de novo gain-of-function missense variants. The convergence of consistent clinical phenotypes (autism spectrum disorder, developmental delay, seizures), validated molecular mechanisms (activity-enhancing gating changes increasing Ca2+ influx), and direct model organism validation (heterozygous A749G mice reproducing human phenotype) provides compelling evidence for pathogenicity. Future work should focus on characterizing loss-of-function variants, establishing genotype-phenotype correlations, and developing targeted therapeutic approaches using calcium channel modulators.
