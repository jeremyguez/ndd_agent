# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: CAMK2A AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

CAMK2A (Calcium/Calmodulin-Dependent Protein Kinase II Alpha) encodes a serine/threonine kinase that is critical for synaptic plasticity, learning, and memory through its role in regulating NMDA receptor signaling and dendritic spine development. The gene is predominantly expressed in excitatory neurons and plays essential roles in long-term potentiation, synaptic transmission, and neuronal development.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of Independent Case Reports/Series:** Four independent clinical reports were identified.

**Total Patient Count:** Approximately 4 patients with de novo CAMK2A variants (3 individual case reports plus 1 patient from a systematic ASD cohort study).

**Most Common Phenotypes Observed:**
- Autism spectrum disorder (3 patients)
- Intellectual disability/developmental delay (2 patients)
- Seizures/epileptic encephalopathy (1 patient)
- Social interaction deficits and repetitive behaviors (2 patients)
- Speech delay and motor developmental delay (1 patient)

**Predominant Variant Type:** Mixed—includes missense variants (p.Glu183Val; PMID: 28130356) and in-frame deletions (p.Lys292del; PMID: 37510258), with one additional missense variant from a systematic cohort study (ASHG 2023 - Shah et al.).

**Cohort Membership:** No patients were identified as part of established diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL). One study was from India (ASHG 2023 - Shah et al.), and the others did not specify geographic origin.

**Variant Characteristics:** All reported variants were de novo heterozygous mutations. The E183V variant showed dominant-negative effects on CaMKII autophosphorylation and reduced binding to ASD-linked proteins including Shank3 and NMDA receptors (PMID: 28130356). The Lys292del variant affected a crucial functional site and was predicted to disrupt protein structure and interactions (PMID: 37510258).

**Strength of Clinical Evidence: MODERATE**

The evidence is strengthened by de novo status, functional validation in model organisms, and consistent phenotypic associations with neurodevelopmental disorders. However, the total number of independent patients is small (n=4), limiting statistical power. The diversity of specific phenotypes (ASD vs. epileptic encephalopathy) suggests possible allelic heterogeneity or variable expressivity.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms Studied:** Mouse (Mus musculus) exclusively.

**Knockout/Knockin Studies:**
- Full knockout mice exhibit spatial learning deficits, conditional learning deficits, and synaptic plasticity impairment (PMID: 36304100)
- Heterozygous knockout mice show remote and recent spatial memory deficits with immature dentate gyrus phenotype (PMID: 40847814)
- Knock-in mice carrying a hyper-activatable CAMK2A variant display exaggerated long-term potentiation, dendritic spine abnormalities, learning impairments, and memory deficits (PMID: 40140673)
- Conditional knockout in prefrontal cortex CaMK2a neurons results in depression-like phenotype and altered ketamine response (PMID: 39179578)
- Full knockout mice show reduced basal sleep and impaired sleep homeostasis (PMID: 39757286)
- Heterozygous knockout mice exhibit infradian oscillation of locomotor activities responsive to carbamazepine, relevant to bipolar disorder (PMID: 31150948)

**Lethality and Viability:** All knockout and knockin models are viable with no reported lethality or Mendelian ratio deviations, indicating that CAMK2A loss or altered function is compatible with survival.

**Consistency with Human Phenotype:** Model organism evidence strongly supports the neurodevelopmental phenotypes observed in human patients. Learning and memory deficits in mice align with intellectual disability in humans. Behavioral abnormalities in various knockout models parallel the autism-spectrum and psychiatric manifestations seen clinically. The hyper-activatable knock-in model (PMID: 40140673) is particularly relevant, as it recapitulates intellectual disability-like phenotypes similar to the human cases.

**Strength of Model Organism Evidence: STRONG**

Multiple independent mouse models consistently demonstrate neurological and behavioral phenotypes relevant to human neurodevelopmental disorders. The availability of both loss-of-function and gain-of-function models provides mechanistic insight into how CAMK2A variants contribute to disease.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known Pathways and Molecular Function:**

CAMK2A functions in multiple critical pathways:
- **Synaptic plasticity and learning:** Regulates NMDA receptor signaling and long-term potentiation through autophosphorylation at Thr286/Thr287 (PMID: 29100089)
- **Dendritic spine development:** Controls structural plasticity and dendritic spine density
- **Circadian rhythm and mood regulation:** CAMK2a+ neurons in medial prefrontal cortex contain a functional molecular clock essential for mood regulation (PMID: 39179578)
- **Pain-related psychiatric phenotypes:** CAMK2A-expressing neurons in ventral hippocampus project to prefrontal cortex CRH+ neurons and modulate pain-related anxiodepression (PMID: 38241377)
- **Cellular stress responses:** CaMK2A/CREB pathway activation involved in mitophagy and apoptosis in diabetic retinopathy (PMID: 40216954)

**Expression Pattern:**

CAMK2A is predominantly expressed in excitatory glutamatergic neurons throughout the brain, with particular enrichment in hippocampus and cortex. The CAMK2A promoter is widely used to drive neuron-specific transgene expression in research (PMID: 38100333). Proteomic characterization of CAMK2a+ neurons identified over 2000 neuron-derived proteins with brain region-specific differences (PMID: 35614064). CAMK2A::GFP reporter successfully identifies well-differentiated excitatory neurons in human induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cultures (PMID: 29791859).

**Key Experimental Findings:**

- A large exome sequencing study identified 19 de novo CAMK2A variants in 24 unrelated individuals with intellectual disability, establishing the importance of tightly regulated CAMK2A autophosphorylation in human brain development (PMID: 29100089)
- CAMK2A was identified as a candidate target gene for inflammatory bowel disease through novel eQTL colocalization analysis in diseased tissue (Fisher's exact test p = 6.72×10⁻³; ASHG 2023 - IBD eQTL study), suggesting broader biological relevance beyond neurodevelopment
- Functional studies demonstrate that CAMK2A variants can cause both loss-of-function (reduced autophosphorylation) and gain-of-function (exaggerated autophosphorylation) effects depending on the specific mutation

**Strength of Biological Evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is comprehensive, spanning multiple experimental approaches including cell culture, animal models, and human tissue studies. The gene's well-characterized role in synaptic plasticity, learning, and neuronal development directly supports its involvement in neurodevelopmental disorders. The identification of 19 de novo variants in a large cohort of individuals with intellectual disability provides robust evidence for pathogenic potential.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall Evidence Level: STRONG**

**Key Strengths:**

1. **De novo variants in affected individuals:** All four clinical cases carry de novo heterozygous mutations, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance and high penetrance.

2. **Functional validation:** Pathogenic variants have been functionally characterized in model organisms and cell culture systems, demonstrating dominant-negative or gain-of-function effects on CaMKII autophosphorylation and synaptic function.

3. **Consistent phenotypic associations:** Clinical phenotypes (autism spectrum disorder, intellectual disability, seizures) align with the known neurodevelopmental roles of CAMK2A and are recapitulated in multiple mouse models.

4. **Large supporting cohort:** A separate study identified 19 de novo CAMK2A variants in 24 unrelated individuals with intellectual disability (PMID: 29100089), providing independent evidence for pathogenic potential beyond the four individual case reports.

5. **Multiple model systems:** Comprehensive mouse knockout, knockin, and conditional knockout models consistently demonstrate learning, memory, and behavioral deficits relevant to human neurodevelopmental phenotypes.

**Key Gaps and Limitations:**

1. **Limited clinical sample size:** Only 4 independent patients with detailed phenotypic information; larger case series would strengthen genotype-phenotype correlations.

2. **Phenotypic heterogeneity:** The range of presentations (ASD vs. epileptic encephalopathy) suggests possible allelic heterogeneity or variable expressivity, which is not fully explained.

3. **Incomplete mechanistic understanding:** While autophosphorylation defects are implicated, the precise molecular mechanisms linking specific variants to distinct phenotypes remain incompletely characterized.

4. **Limited human cellular models:** Most functional studies rely on mouse models; human neuronal models derived from patient-derived iPSCs would provide additional validation.

**Conclusion:**

CAMK2A is a well-supported neurodevelopmental disorder gene with strong evidence for pathogenic heterozygous variants causing autism spectrum disorder, intellectual disability, and seizure disorders. The combination of de novo variants in affected individuals, functional validation in multiple model systems, and a large supporting cohort of 19 additional de novo variants in individuals with intellectual disability establishes CAMK2A as a high-confidence disease gene. Future studies should focus on expanding the clinical case series, characterizing genotype-phenotype correlations, and developing patient-derived cellular models to further elucidate disease mechanisms.
