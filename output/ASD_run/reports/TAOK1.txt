# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: TAOK1 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

TAOK1 (Thousand and One Amino Acid protein Kinase 1) is a Ste20-related MAP3K (mitogen-activated protein kinase kinase kinase) that regulates MAPK signaling pathways and is essential for neuronal maturation, cortical development, and mitotic microtubule dynamics. The protein functions as a plasma membrane remodeling kinase and plays critical roles in neural migration, dendritic arbor development, and chromosome segregation during cell division.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent studies:** 7 case reports/case series identified

**Total patient count:** Approximately 63 patients with TAOK1 variants across all studies (50 patients in the largest series [PMID: 39737487], plus 1 patient each in [PMID: 37656623] and [PMID: 38443934], 4 patients in [PMID: 35091509], and 7 patients in [ASHG 2017 - McLaughlin et al.])

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Neurodevelopmental abnormalities/developmental delay (100% in largest series)
- Macrocephaly (83% in largest series)
- Hypotonia (58% in largest series)
- Autism spectrum disorder (reported across multiple studies)
- Intellectual disability/learning disabilities
- Feeding difficulties
- Behavioral abnormalities
- Joint hypermobility/laxity
- Facial dysmorphism
- Motor and language delays

Additional phenotypes include male genital anomalies, hypoglycemia, periventricular nodular heterotopia (first report of neuronal migration disorder in TAOK1-related disorder [PMID: 38443934]), and congenital heart disease.

**Predominant variant types:**
- Missense variants: 37 unique variants identified, predominantly clustered in the protein kinase domain (PMID: 39737487)
- Loss-of-function variants: Frameshifts, nonsense, truncating, and splice site variants reported across multiple studies
- Both de novo and inherited variants documented, with inherited variants showing incomplete penetrance and variable expressivity (PMID: 35091509)

**Cohort source:** Multiple international centers; not explicitly from known cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL), though cases identified through exome and genome sequencing in research and clinical settings

**Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strong due to: (1) large case series with 50 patients showing consistent phenotypic features; (2) multiple independent case reports and series from different centers confirming the association; (3) both de novo and inherited variants documented; (4) clear genotype-phenotype correlation with missense variants clustered in functionally critical kinase domain; (5) demonstration of incomplete penetrance and variable expressivity supporting autosomal dominant inheritance; (6) consistent phenotypic features across studies despite variant heterogeneity.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:**
- *Xenopus tropicalis* and *Xenopus laevis*
- Mouse

**Key phenotypes from knockdown/knockouts:**

*Xenopus* studies:
- TAOK1 knockdown impairs primary cilia formation in vitro and causes defects in motile cilia formation (PMID: 40552535)
- Heart development abnormalities observed with TAOK1 knockdown (PMID: 40552535)
- Spatiotemporal expression during early embryonic development, particularly in neural tube, brain, otic vesicles, and eye (PMID: 37011704)

Mouse studies:
- AAV-mediated TAOK1 knockdown is viable and does not cause lethality (PMID: 39964965, PMID: 40339965)
- No Mendelian ratio deviations or infertility reported
- Knockdown studies show functional effects on cardiac and muscle phenotypes rather than neurodevelopmental phenotypes

**Consistency with human phenotype:**
Moderate consistency. The *Xenopus* studies demonstrate TAOK1's role in early development, cilia formation, and neural tissue expression, which aligns with the neurodevelopmental phenotype in humans. However, the mouse knockdown studies focused on cardiac and muscle phenotypes rather than neurodevelopmental outcomes. A direct mouse model of neurodevelopmental disorder with TAOK1 haploinsufficiency was demonstrated in one study showing autistic-like behaviors via dorsal raphe nucleus dysfunction (PMID: 37656623), but detailed characterization of neurodevelopmental phenotypes in knockout mice is limited.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**

The evidence is moderate because: (1) expression studies confirm TAOK1 presence in developing neural tissues; (2) knockdown studies demonstrate functional consequences in early development and cilia formation; (3) mouse haploinsufficiency model shows autism-like behaviors; (4) however, comprehensive neurodevelopmental characterization in mouse models is limited; (5) no complete knockout lethality data provided; (6) most detailed phenotyping focuses on non-neurological systems.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

TAOK1 is a MAP3K that activates p38 MAPK and other downstream signaling cascades. Key pathways include:

- **MAPK signaling:** TAOK1 activates p38-MAPK pathway, which regulates multiple cellular processes including protein degradation, apoptosis, and cell cycle control (PMID: 37439183, PMID: 40653040)
- **Neuronal development:** TAOK1 regulates neural migration, neuronal maturation, and dendritic arbor growth; catalytically inactive mutants become trapped in membrane-bound state inducing abnormal membrane protrusions and impaired dendritic development (PMID: 36595571)
- **Cilia formation:** TAOK1 is essential for primary and motile cilia formation, linking ciliary biology to autism and congenital heart disease (PMID: 40552535)
- **Protein interactions:** TAOK1 phosphorylates multiple substrates including ETS2 (in innate immunity [PMID: 40983701]), USP7 (in DNA repair [PMID: 40106350]), and interacts with WWC1 (in cancer biology [PMID: 36158126])
- **Mitotic function:** TAOK1 maintains mitotic microtubule dynamics and chromosome segregation (ASHG 2017 - McLaughlin et al.)

**Expression pattern:**

TAOK1 shows spatiotemporal expression during early embryonic development with particular enrichment in:
- Neural tube and developing brain
- Otic vesicles and eye
- Branchial arches
- Expression present from pre-gastrula through gastrula stages and beyond (PMID: 37011704)

This expression pattern is consistent with the neurodevelopmental phenotype observed in patients.

**Key experimental findings:**

- **Neurodevelopmental function:** Altered TAOK1 expression levels in embryonic mouse brain affect neural migration in vivo and neuronal maturation in vitro (PMID: 33565190)
- **Membrane remodeling:** Disease-associated missense mutations result in catalytically inactive kinase trapped in membrane-bound state, inducing abnormal dendritic arbor growth (PMID: 36595571)
- **Haploinsufficiency:** De novo frameshifting mutation causes haploinsufficiency leading to autistic-like behaviors in mice via dorsal raphe nucleus dysfunction (PMID: 37656623)
- **Cilia and cardiac development:** CRISPR screen identified TAOK1 as CHD gene with ASD risk; role in motile cilia formation and heart development demonstrated in *Xenopus* (PMID: 40552535)

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strong because: (1) TAOK1's role in neural development, migration, and neuronal maturation is well-established; (2) expression pattern in developing neural tissues is documented; (3) molecular mechanisms linking mutations to phenotypes are characterized (membrane remodeling, dendritic arbor defects); (4) haploinsufficiency mechanism demonstrated in vivo; (5) multiple independent pathways and protein interactions identified; (6) functional consequences of disease-associated mutations experimentally validated.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths of the evidence:**

1. **Large, well-characterized patient cohort:** 50 patients in the largest series (PMID: 39737487) with consistent phenotypic features and 37 unique variants identified, providing robust clinical evidence
2. **Multiple independent confirmations:** Seven independent case reports/series from different centers consistently demonstrate TAOK1-related neurodevelopmental disorder
3. **Variant spectrum:** Both de novo and inherited variants documented; missense variants clustered in functionally critical kinase domain; loss-of-function variants also pathogenic
4. **Mechanistic understanding:** Clear molecular mechanisms linking mutations to phenotypes (haploinsufficiency, catalytic inactivation with membrane trapping, impaired neural migration)
5. **In vivo validation:** Mouse model demonstrates haploinsufficiency causes autism-like behaviors (PMID: 37656623); *Xenopus* studies confirm developmental role
6. **Consistent phenotype:** Core features (neurodevelopmental delay, macrocephaly, hypotonia, autism spectrum disorder) consistently observed across studies
7. **Functional evidence:** TAOK1's essential role in neural development, cilia formation, and dendritic arbor growth experimentally validated

**Key gaps or limitations:**

1. **Limited mouse neurodevelopmental characterization:** While haploinsufficiency model exists, comprehensive neurodevelopmental phenotyping in TAOK1 knockout mice is not extensively reported
2. **Incomplete penetrance and variable expressivity:** Inherited variants show variable expression, suggesting genetic or environmental modifiers not yet identified
3. **Neuronal migration disorder:** Only one case report of periventricular nodular heterotopia (PMID: 38443934); frequency and mechanisms of neuronal migration defects unclear
4. **Genotype-phenotype correlation:** While missense variants cluster in kinase domain, detailed correlation between specific variant types and phenotypic severity not fully characterized
5. **Functional studies limited to specific mutations:** Most functional studies examine a limited number of disease-associated mutations; broader mutational landscape not fully explored

**Conclusion:**

TAOK1 is a well-established neurodevelopmental disorder gene with strong evidence supporting autosomal dominant inheritance through heterozygous pathogenic variants. The convergence of clinical evidence (63+ patients with consistent phenotypes), functional evidence (demonstrated role in neural development and dendritic maturation), and mechanistic understanding (haploinsufficiency and catalytic inactivation mechanisms) provides compelling support for TAOK1 as a bona fide neurodevelopmental disorder gene. The identification of both de novo and inherited variants, combined with documented incomplete penetrance and variable expressivity, is consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance with variable expressivity.
