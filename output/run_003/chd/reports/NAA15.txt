# NAA15 Evidence Report: Congenital Heart Disease / Autosomal Dominant Neurodevelopmental Syndrome

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

NAA15 encodes the auxiliary (regulatory) subunit of the NatA N-terminal acetyltransferase complex, where it serves as the ribosome-anchoring and substrate-recruiting partner for the catalytic subunit NAA10. Together, NAA10 and NAA15 form the major co-translational N-terminal acetyltransferase in eukaryotes, modifying the majority of the human proteome and playing essential roles in protein stability, folding, and diverse developmental processes.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports/series:** Ten independent publications or conference reports were identified, spanning case series, cohort studies, and natural history studies.

**Total approximate number of patients:** Across all studies, approximately 247 individuals with pathogenic or likely pathogenic NAA15 variants have been reported, though some overlap between cohorts cannot be excluded. The largest individual series included 66 patients (PMID: 37130971), 64 patients (PMID: 39825710), 38 patients (PMID: 29656860), 30 patients (PMID: 31127942), and 27 patients (PMID: 39991982).

**Most common phenotypes observed:**

The core phenotypic spectrum of NAA15-related neurodevelopmental disorder includes:
- **Intellectual disability** (mild to moderate; present across virtually all series)
- **Delayed speech and language milestones**
- **Delayed motor milestones**
- **Autism spectrum disorder (ASD)**
- **Congenital cardiac anomalies** — including ventricular septal defect and, notably, **hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM)** (PMID: 33103328; PMID: 42146379)
- **Mild craniofacial dysmorphology**
- **Seizures**
- **Brain structural abnormalities** on MRI (average 2.8 abnormalities per patient, including cerebellar, brainstem, and myelination abnormalities) (PMID: 38978667)
- **Behavioral abnormalities** and reduced adaptive functioning (PMID: 39991982)
- **Visual abnormalities**, including cortical visual impairment and microphthalmia (PMID: 37130971)
- **Musculoskeletal abnormalities** (PMID: 39991982)

Cardiac involvement is a recurrent and clinically significant feature. Two patients with pediatric HCM were specifically attributed to NAA15 variants identified by exome sequencing (PMID: 33103328), and zebrafish modeling has further substantiated the cardiac phenotype (PMID: 42146379). Congenital cardiac anomalies (including septal defects) appear across multiple large series (PMID: 29656860; PMID: 37130971; PMID: 42091191).

**Predominant variant type:** Loss-of-function (LoF) variants — including frameshift, nonsense, and splice-site variants — predominate and are the best-established disease mechanism, consistent with haploinsufficiency. De novo or dominantly acting missense variants have also been reported and shown to impair NatA complex function (PMID: 31127942; PMID: 39825710). The seminal series identified 25 different LGD variants across 38 individuals (PMID: 29656860), and a subsequent large study compiled 64 distinct variants (PMID: 39825710). Specific well-characterized variants include c.822_823insTA (p.E275*), c.376_379delCAAAinsTCCTTACTACAGGT (p.Q126Sfs*9), c.1029_1038delAGAGTTAGTA (p.E344*), and c.692-5A>G (splice-site), all confirmed by functional RNA studies (PMID: 42091191).

**Cohort origin:** Patients were ascertained through multiple international centers across Europe, North America, and Asia. Several studies used genotype-first approaches via large-scale exome sequencing programs. A targeted resequencing study of 11,000 autism/ID probands and 4,000 controls identified NAA15 at locus-specific significance for LGD burden, with no disrupting events in unaffected siblings or controls (ASHG 2015 - Stessman et al.). No explicit UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL cohort designations were specified in the available abstracts, though the breadth of international centers involved makes participation in such programs likely.

**Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG**

Multiple large independent series, consistent core phenotype, functional validation of variants, and a natural history study all support a well-established gene-disease relationship for NAA15-related autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder with cardiac involvement.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Zebrafish (*Danio rerio*) and mouse (*Mus musculus*), as well as *Arabidopsis thaliana* (plant, less directly relevant to human disease).

**Key findings:**

- **Zebrafish (knockdown):** Morpholino-mediated knockdown of *naa15* produced defects in myotome morphogenesis, including U-shaped somite segmentation and abnormally long myofibers spanning adjacent somites (PMID: 30502388), demonstrating a role in musculoskeletal development consistent with human phenotypes.

- **Zebrafish (full knockout):** A zebrafish *naa15* knockout model recapitulated the cardiac phenotype with high fidelity, showing diminutive ventricles, low cardiac contractility, bradycardia, fewer and smaller cardiomyocytes, cardiomyocyte proliferation defects, and moderately disorganized myofibrils. Adult fish with reduced *naa15* dosage showed smaller ventricular chambers and mosaic myofibril disarray. Mechanistically, reduced mitochondrial content and function in the myocardium, with differential expression of mitochondrial respiratory complex I subunits, was identified as an underlying basis for the cardiac contractility defects (PMID: 42146379). This directly supports the human HCM phenotype.

- **Mouse (full knockout):** *Naa15* knockout mice showed sub-Mendelian ratios and increased early neonatal/perinatal lethality, likely related to congenital heart defects, as well as supernumerary thoracic ribs and vertebrae, piebaldism, and urogenital/renal abnormalities (ASHG 2017). Variable expressivity was noted. A related study examining the NatA pathway showed that *Naa10/Naa12* double knockout results in embryonic lethality, underscoring the essential nature of NatA complex function (PMID: 34355692).

- **Mouse (knockdown/cell culture):** siRNA knockdown of *Naa15* in mouse aortic endothelial cells increased tube formation, implicating NAA15 in angiogenesis regulation (PMID: 27755984).

- **Neurodevelopmental mouse models:** Loss of NAA15 in mouse models led to a substantial increase in neuronal count, aberrant brain development, and repetitive and anxious behaviors, consistent with the ASD/neurodevelopmental phenotype in humans (PMID: 39825710).

**Consistency with human phenotype:** High. The zebrafish cardiac knockout model directly recapitulates the human HCM/cardiac contractility phenotype. Mouse models show perinatal lethality, cardiac defects, and skeletal anomalies consistent with the human congenital anomaly spectrum. Neurodevelopmental mouse phenotypes align with the ASD and intellectual disability observed in patients.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:** NAA15 is the auxiliary subunit of the NatA complex, the predominant co-translational N-terminal acetyltransferase in eukaryotes. It anchors the complex to the ribosome and recruits substrates for acetylation by NAA10. N-terminal acetylation by NatA affects protein stability, folding, complex formation, and subcellular localization for a large fraction of the proteome. Haploinsufficiency of NAA15 leads to reduced NatA activity, as demonstrated by RNA analysis showing nonsense-mediated decay of LGD transcripts and functional yeast assays confirming deleterious effects of LGD variants (PMID: 29656860). Missense variants have been shown biochemically to impair NAA10-NAA15 complex formation and NatA enzymatic activity (PMID: 31127942). A NAA10 variant (p.Arg4Ser) that disrupts NAA10-NAA15 complex formation caused repolarization abnormalities and decreased contractile force in iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes and engineered heart tissues, directly linking NatA complex integrity to cardiac function (PMID: 40234403).

**Expression pattern:** NAA15 is broadly expressed, with particular relevance to:
- **Cardiac tissue:** Expression in cardiomyocytes; loss leads to mitochondrial dysfunction and contractility defects (PMID: 42146379).
- **Retinal endothelium:** NAA15 (also known as Tubedown/Tbdn) is expressed in retinal endothelial cells; knockdown induces hyperpermeability and fibrovascular lesions, with loss of expression observed in proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PMID: 26142315).
- **Neuronal tissue:** NAA15 is required for normal neurogenesis; its loss increases neuronal count and impairs axon and synapse formation (PMID: 39825710).
- **Endothelium:** Implicated in angiogenesis regulation via the miR-27b/NAA15 axis (PMID: 27755984).

**Key experimental findings:**
- NMD of LGD transcripts and yeast functional assays confirm haploinsufficiency as the primary disease mechanism (PMID: 29656860).
- RT-PCR and qPCR in patient-derived samples confirmed aberrant splicing and reduced NAA15 mRNA expression for splice-site variants (PMID: 42091191).
- Cellular models show that NAA15-deficient neurons have impaired axon and synapse formation (PMID: 39825710).
- Mitochondrial dysfunction is a mechanistic link between NAA15 loss and cardiac disease (PMID: 42146379).
- A large-scale gene-based meta-analysis across >1.2 million individuals identified ultra-rare predicted LoF variants in NAA15 as associated with type 2 diabetes risk, suggesting broader metabolic roles for NatA-mediated acetylation (PMID: 42238450).
- NAA15 interacts with ICER and regulates its protein stability through the N-end rule pathway (PMID: 35227662), indicating roles beyond simple NatA catalysis.

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths:**
1. **Robust clinical evidence:** Multiple large, independent international cohorts totaling approximately 247 patients consistently demonstrate a core phenotype of intellectual disability, ASD, and congenital cardiac anomalies associated with heterozygous LoF and missense variants in NAA15. A dedicated natural history study (PMID: 39991982) and neuroimaging study (PMID: 38978667) further characterize the syndrome.
2. **Cardiac phenotype specifically supported:** Pediatric HCM has been directly attributed to NAA15 variants (PMID: 33103328), and a zebrafish knockout model faithfully recapitulates cardiac contractility defects with a defined mitochondrial mechanism (PMID: 42146379), providing strong cross-species validation of the cardiac phenotype.
3. **Mechanistic coherence:** Haploinsufficiency is well-supported by NMD studies, yeast functional assays, and biochemical analyses of NatA complex integrity, providing a clear and consistent molecular mechanism.

**Key gaps or limitations:**
- The specific contribution of NAA15 to isolated congenital heart disease (as distinct from the broader neurodevelopmental syndrome) remains incompletely characterized; most cardiac cases are identified in the context of syndromic presentations.
- No congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH) cases were identified in the available evidence, and NAA15 cannot currently be considered a CDH gene based on the literature reviewed.
- Precise genotype-phenotype correlations (e.g., which variant types or positions preferentially cause HCM versus neurodevelopmental features) remain to be fully established.

**Conclusion:** NAA15 is a well-established autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene with strong, multi-layered evidence from clinical cohorts, model organisms, and molecular studies. Congenital cardiac anomalies — including hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and septal defects — are a recognized and mechanistically supported component of the NAA15-related syndrome, making NAA15 a credible congenital heart disease gene in the context of syndromic presentations. No evidence was identified linking NAA15 to congenital diaphragmatic hernia specifically.
