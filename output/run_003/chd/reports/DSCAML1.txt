# Evidence Report: DSCAML1 as a Congenital Heart Disease / Congenital Diaphragmatic Hernia Gene (Autosomal Dominant)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

DSCAML1 (Down Syndrome Cell Adhesion Molecule Like-1) is a human paralogue of DSCAM located on chromosome 11q23, encoding a large transmembrane cell adhesion protein with 10 immunoglobulin domains, six fibronectin-III domains, and an intracellular signaling domain (PMID: 11453658). Its primary known function is mediating homophilic cell-cell adhesion in the developing nervous system, with established roles in neuronal self-avoidance, synaptic regulation, radial migration, and GABAergic interneuron development.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports:** Three independent publications were identified (PMID: 33501714; PMID: 33256836; PMID: 42003794).

**Total approximate number of patients:** Approximately 5 patients in total across all studies — 3 patients reported in PMID: 33501714 and 1 patient each in PMID: 33256836 and PMID: 42003794 (with the latter two studies describing the same p.A2105T variant, likely representing the same or overlapping patient).

**Most common phenotypes observed:** All reported phenotypes are neurological and neurodevelopmental in nature. These include autism spectrum disorder (ASD), intellectual disability (ID), developmental delay, epilepsy, and spike-and-wave discharges. No cardiovascular, diaphragmatic, or structural congenital anomalies were reported in any patient.

**Predominant variant type:** Both loss-of-function (LoF) variants (p.R1685*, p.K2108Nfs*37) and missense variants (p.C729R, p.A2105T) are represented. Variants were identified through databases of ASD, ID/DD, and epilepsy cohorts, not through cardiac or CDH-focused studies.

**Known cohorts:** No patients were explicitly identified from recognized cardiac or CDH cohorts (e.g., UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL). Variants were ascertained from neurodevelopmental disorder databases.

**Strength of clinical evidence for CHD/CDH:** NONE. There is no clinical evidence linking DSCAML1 variants to congenital heart disease or congenital diaphragmatic hernia. All documented human phenotypes are restricted to neurodevelopmental and neuropsychiatric conditions.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*) and zebrafish (*Danio rerio*) have been used to model DSCAML1 deficiency, using both full knockout and knock-in strategies.

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**
- In mouse full knockouts, phenotypes are confined to the nervous system and retina: fasciculated rod bipolar cell dendrites, clumped AII amacrine cell bodies, precocious rod bipolar cell dendritic sprouting, and reduced mitochondrial complexity in retinal bipolar cells (PMID: 19945391; PMID: 37910517). Cortical development, interneuron migration, and forebrain morphogenesis were not significantly affected in constitutive knockouts (PMID: 33585465).
- In zebrafish full knockouts, phenotypes include impaired retinal patterning, oculomotor deficits resembling ocular motor apraxia (PMID: 31685652), dysregulation of the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) stress axis with increased CRH neuron number and elevated baseline cortisol (PMID: 36875755), and altered retinofugal connectivity (PMID: 32038180).
- A patient-specific knock-in mouse (p.A2105T) exhibited epileptic spike-and-wave discharges, dendritic retraction in cortical pyramidal neurons, and abnormal clustering of somatostatin-positive interneurons in the hippocampus (PMID: 42003794).
- All knockout and knock-in animals were viable and fertile; no lethality, Mendelian ratio deviations, cardiac defects, or diaphragmatic anomalies were reported in any model.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** Model organism phenotypes are broadly consistent with the human neurodevelopmental phenotype (epilepsy, synaptic dysfunction, interneuron abnormalities) but provide no evidence of cardiac or diaphragmatic involvement.

**Strength of model organism evidence for CHD/CDH:** NONE. No cardiac, vascular, or diaphragmatic phenotypes have been observed in any DSCAML1 animal model.

---

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:** DSCAML1 mediates homophilic cell adhesion independently of divalent cations and is involved in neuronal self-avoidance, dendritic arborization, axon growth, radial migration, and callosal projection (PMID: 11453658; PMID: 25451118). Its intracellular domain undergoes gamma-secretase-dependent cleavage and nuclear translocation via importin IPO5, where it regulates genes associated with neuronal differentiation, apoptosis, and synapse formation (PMID: 30745319). Interacting partners include Dyrk1a, STAT3, USP21, and SH2D2A — all with established neurodevelopmental relevance.

**Expression pattern:** DSCAML1 shows highly dynamic spatiotemporal expression in cortical layers and ganglionic eminence-derived neural populations of the embryonic mouse brain (PMID: 33585465). Expression has also been documented in the retina and hypothalamus. A goat GWAS identified DSCAML1 expression in testicular tissue at mitotic and meiotic stages (PMID: 32036056), but no cardiac or diaphragmatic expression data are reported in any study.

**Key experimental findings:** Functional studies of patient-derived variants demonstrated loss of mature N-glycosylation (p.C729R), proteasome-dependent degradation (p.K2108Nfs*37), and loss of synaptic regulatory function for all tested mutants (PMID: 33501714). The p.A2105T variant causes intracellular protein accumulation and loss of cell surface localization, with pharmacological rescue by 4-phenylbutyrate (4PBA) restoring surface expression and ameliorating epileptic phenotypes in knock-in mice (PMID: 42003794). Human DSCAML1 is functionally conserved with Drosophila Dscam in controlling dendrite development (PMID: 21645617).

**Strength of biological evidence for CHD/CDH:** NONE. All biological evidence pertains exclusively to nervous system function. No pathway, expression, or interaction data implicate DSCAML1 in cardiac morphogenesis, diaphragm development, or related processes.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level for DSCAML1 as a CHD/CDH gene (autosomal dominant): INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths of the available evidence (in its actual disease context):** There is moderate and growing evidence supporting DSCAML1 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene, with multiple patient-derived variants functionally validated in cell-based and animal models, and consistent phenotypes across mouse and zebrafish systems. The identification of both LoF and missense variants in patients with ASD, ID, and epilepsy, combined with mechanistic studies, provides a credible basis for its role in neuropsychiatric disease.

**Key gaps and limitations relevant to CHD/CDH:** There are no published patients with DSCAML1 variants and congenital heart disease or congenital diaphragmatic hernia. No animal model of DSCAML1 deficiency has demonstrated cardiac or diaphragmatic pathology. No biological or expression data link DSCAML1 to cardiac morphogenesis or diaphragm development. The inheritance pattern in reported cases is not clearly established as autosomal dominant, as most variants were identified in small case series without formal segregation analysis.

**Conclusion:** Based on the totality of available evidence, DSCAML1 cannot be supported as a gene for congenital heart disease or congenital diaphragmatic hernia. All clinical, model organism, and biological evidence consistently points to a role in neurodevelopmental disorders — specifically ASD, intellectual disability, and epilepsy — with no data, direct or indirect, implicating this gene in structural cardiac or diaphragmatic anomalies. Any assertion of DSCAML1 as a CHD/CDH gene would currently be unsupported by the published literature.
