# CHAMP1 Evidence Report: Congenital Heart Disease / Congenital Diaphragmatic Hernia (Autosomal Dominant)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

CHAMP1 (Chromosome Alignment-Maintaining Phosphoprotein 1) encodes a zinc-finger–containing phosphoprotein that functions primarily in kinetochore-microtubule attachment and chromosome segregation during mitosis, and has additional roles in heterochromatin assembly, DNA double-strand break repair via homologous recombination, and—as recently described—myoblast fusion through interaction with the transcription factor MyoD (PMID: 26340335; PMID: 41540007). Its best-established disease association is an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental syndrome caused by de novo heterozygous loss-of-function variants.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports/series:** Ten independent publications or case series were identified, spanning 2015–2025 and originating from Europe, North America, Asia, the Middle East, and Turkey.

**Total approximate patients:** Approximately 72 individuals across all studies (5 + 6 + 5 + 14 + 11 + 24 + 3 + 1 + 2 + 1).

**Most common phenotypes observed:** Across all studies, the consistent clinical features are intellectual disability, global developmental delay, severe speech/language impairment, hypotonia, and dysmorphic facial features. Additional features reported in larger series include autism spectrum disorder, ADHD, repetitive/sensory behaviors, microcephaly, seizures, and ophthalmologic and gastrointestinal issues (PMID: 34021018; PMID: 35271727). One long-term follow-up case described macrocephaly, thoracic hyperkyphosis, decreased pain sensation, and metabolic syndrome (PMID: 37628598). Two Turkish cases reported vesicoureteral reflux and thin corpus callosum (PMID: 42125344). **Notably, congenital heart disease (CHD) and congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH) are not reported as features in any of the identified publications.**

**Predominant variant type:** Overwhelmingly heterozygous de novo loss-of-function variants (nonsense and frameshift), with the majority predicted to truncate the C-terminal zinc-finger domains. One missense variant (p.Gly23Ser) was associated with epileptic encephalopathy (PMID: 36797464). One study identified that individuals with whole-gene deletions (13q34 deletions encompassing CHAMP1) had a milder phenotype than those with intragenic truncating mutations, raising the possibility of a dominant-negative or gain-of-function mechanism for coding mutations rather than simple haploinsufficiency (PMID: 37454340).

**Known cohorts:** The Simons Searchlight registry contributed 14 patients (PMID: 34021018). No patients from UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL cohorts were explicitly identified in the structured data provided.

**Strength of clinical evidence for CHD/CDH:** **NONE.** No clinical evidence links heterozygous CHAMP1 variants to congenital heart disease or congenital diaphragmatic hernia in any of the identified studies.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*) exclusively.

**Key phenotypes:**
- Homozygous (*Champ1*−/−) knockout mice on a pure C57BL/6J background exhibit perinatal lethality and are slightly smaller than wild-type littermates, with no gross anatomical brain defects but increased mitotic cells in the cerebral cortex and delayed neuronal differentiation in vitro (PMID: 36106092).
- Heterozygous (*Champ1*+/−) knockout mice are viable and display mild memory deficits, altered social interaction, and depression-like behaviors—broadly consistent with the human neurodevelopmental phenotype (PMID: 36106092).
- A knock-in model carrying a CRISPR/Cas9-induced 35 bp deletion in *Champ1* exon 2 showed delayed motor development and impaired memory and learning; females exhibited dystocia (ASHG 2023 – Carneiro et al.; ASHG 2022 – Carneiro et al.).

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The mouse models recapitulate neurodevelopmental and behavioral features (cognitive impairment, motor delay, social deficits) consistent with the human CHAMP1 syndrome. **No cardiac or diaphragmatic structural defects were reported in any mouse model.**

**Strength of model organism evidence for CHD/CDH:** **NONE.** Model organism studies provide no evidence supporting a role for CHAMP1 in cardiac or diaphragmatic development.

---

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:** CHAMP1 operates in at least three interconnected molecular contexts: (1) kinetochore-microtubule attachment and chromosome alignment during mitosis (PMID: 26340335); (2) heterochromatin assembly at centromeres and telomeres via a complex with POGZ, HP1α, and the methyltransferase SETDB1, which also stabilizes stalled replication forks and promotes homology-directed DNA repair (PMID: 39962076; PMID: 41481470); and (3) promotion of homologous recombination by competing with the Shieldin complex through direct binding to REV7/MAD2L2 via a 'WK' motif (PMID: 36044844; PMID: 28887307). Additionally, CHAMP1 regulates anti-apoptotic Mcl-1 expression and cell survival during mitotic arrest (PMID: 34107118), and has been identified as a noncanonical MyoD cofactor essential for myoblast fusion and muscle development through activation of the fusogen Myomaker (PMID: 41540007).

**Neurodevelopmental relevance:** Lymphoblastoid cells and fibroblasts from individuals with CHAMP1 premature termination codon mutations show defective heterochromatin clustering and impaired homologous recombination repair (PMID: 39962076; PMID: 39738383). Embryonic mouse brains from *Champ1*−/− animals show downregulation of genes related to neurotransmitter transport and neurodevelopmental disorders (PMID: 36106092). These findings provide a plausible mechanistic basis for the neurodevelopmental syndrome.

**Relevance to CHD/CDH:** No published experimental evidence links CHAMP1 function to cardiac morphogenesis, cardiomyocyte differentiation, neural crest cell migration to the heart, or diaphragm development. The recently described role in myoblast fusion (PMID: 41540007) involves skeletal muscle, not cardiac muscle or diaphragmatic mesenchyme, and has not been connected to structural birth defects.

**Strength of biological evidence for CHD/CDH:** **NONE.**

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level for CHAMP1 as a CHD/CDH gene (autosomal dominant): INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths of the available evidence (for CHAMP1 as a disease gene generally):** There is robust, multi-study clinical evidence establishing CHAMP1 as a well-characterized autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene, with >70 patients reported across 10 independent publications, consistent de novo heterozygous loss-of-function variants, functional validation in patient-derived cells, and mouse models recapitulating core phenotypic features.

**Key gaps and limitations relevant to CHD/CDH:** Across all clinical reports totaling approximately 72 patients, congenital heart disease and congenital diaphragmatic hernia are not described as features of CHAMP1-related disorder. No mouse model has demonstrated cardiac or diaphragmatic structural anomalies. No biological or pathway evidence connects CHAMP1 to heart or diaphragm development.

**Conclusion:** The available evidence does not support CHAMP1 as a gene for congenital heart disease or congenital diaphragmatic hernia. CHAMP1 is a well-established autosomal dominant intellectual disability/neurodevelopmental disorder gene, but neither clinical, model organism, nor biological data implicate it in cardiac or diaphragmatic morphogenesis. Any putative association with CHD or CDH would require prospective clinical documentation and independent experimental validation before it could be considered.
