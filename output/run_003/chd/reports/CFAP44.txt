# CFAP44 Evidence Report: Congenital Heart Disease / Congenital Diaphragmatic Hernia (Autosomal Dominant)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

CFAP44 (Cilia and Flagella Associated Protein 44) encodes an axonemal-associated structural protein that plays a critical role in the assembly and integrity of the sperm flagellum and motile cilia, functioning specifically within the inner dynein arm tether/tether head complex. Its primary established role is in male reproductive biology, where loss-of-function leads to multiple morphological abnormalities of the sperm flagella (MMAF) and male infertility.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent case reports/series:** None identified for congenital heart disease (CHD) or congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH).

**Total patients:** Zero patients with CHD or CDH attributable to CFAP44 variants were identified across any of the reviewed literature.

**Most common phenotypes observed:** All clinical evidence pertains exclusively to male infertility and MMAF (absent, short, coiled, bent, or irregular-caliber sperm flagella with impaired motility). Two independent case series were identified for this reproductive phenotype: one reporting a single patient with a homozygous frameshift mutation (PMID: 28552195) and one conference report describing two additional patients (ASHG 2018 - Li/Zhang). A separate study identified biallelic CFAP44 mutations in 5 of 27 MMAF patients (PMID: 29277146), and another identified a homozygous nonsense variant (c.4963C>T: p.Arg1655*) in one of 13 MMAF patients (PMID: 30904354). No cardiac or diaphragmatic phenotypes were reported in any patient.

**Predominant variant type:** Biallelic loss-of-function variants (frameshift, nonsense) and homozygous/compound heterozygous missense variants, all consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance. No heterozygous (autosomal dominant) pathogenic variants have been reported in any disease context.

**Known cohorts:** Patients are from Chinese multi-center MMAF cohorts. None are from CHD/CDH-specific cohorts such as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL.

**Strength of clinical evidence for CHD/CDH (autosomal dominant): NONE**
There is a complete absence of clinical evidence linking CFAP44 to CHD, CDH, or any cardiac/diaphragmatic phenotype. Furthermore, no heterozygous dominant pathogenic variants have been described for any phenotype.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*) — multiple independent full knockout lines; *Xenopus laevis* — knockdown; *Leishmania mexicana* — null mutant; *Trypanosoma* — studied in the context of flagellar defects (PMID: 29449551).

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:** In all mouse knockout studies, *Cfap44*-null males are viable and fertile females are unaffected, but males are infertile due to severe MMAF (PMID: 28552195; PMID: 29449551; PMID: 40070084; ASHG 2018 - Li/Zhang). A more detailed study found that *Cfap44*-null sperm also exhibit nuclear defects (abnormal DNA compaction, chromosomal architecture, ploidy abnormalities) and reduced embryo developmental potential after ICSI, though this was the least severe among four MMAF knockout lines tested (PMID: 40070084). No lethality, no Mendelian ratio deviations, no cardiac defects, no diaphragmatic defects, and no neurological phenotypes were reported in any model organism study. In *Xenopus*, CFAP44 was identified as an axonemal and DynAP-localizing RNA-associated protein in mucociliary epithelium (PMID: 32898505), but no structural cardiac or diaphragmatic phenotypes were described.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** Model organism data are highly consistent with the human MMAF/male infertility phenotype but provide no evidence relevant to CHD or CDH.

**Strength of model organism evidence for CHD/CDH (autosomal dominant): NONE**
No model organism study has reported cardiac, diaphragmatic, or dominant-effect phenotypes.

---

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:** CFAP44 encodes a structural component of the axonemal inner dynein arm tether/tether head (T/TH) complex. In *Leishmania*, CFAP44 null mutants lack the IDA f protein IC140 and the T/TH component LAX28 from axonemes, demonstrating its role in IDA f and T/TH complex assembly (PMID: 31932510). A separate study proposed that a spliced isoform of CFAP44 has proteolytic activity against β-tubulin and the bacterial tubulin homolog FtsZ, potentially affecting cell division and intracellular transport (PMID: 28887034), though this finding has not been independently replicated in the context of human disease. CFAP44 expression in sperm is positively correlated with sperm motility and normal morphology (PMID: 35470451), and its expression is reduced by environmental toxins including sodium fluoride (PMID: 31901658) and microcystin-LR (PMID: 37001189).

**Expression pattern:** CFAP44 is specifically or preferentially expressed in testis and sperm (PMID: 28552195; PMID: 30904354; ASHG 2018 - Li/Zhang). The spliced isoform is also expressed in brain and embryonic tissues in mice and humans (PMID: 28887034), but no neurodevelopmental or cardiac expression data are provided in any reviewed study. There is no published evidence of CFAP44 expression in cardiac tissue, the developing heart, or the diaphragm.

**Key experimental findings:** Real-time qPCR confirmed markedly reduced CFAP44 mRNA in sperm of affected patients (PMID: 29277146; PMID: 30904354). A suggestive but non-significant association between CFAP44 variants and opioid dependence was reported in a WES study (PMID: 39371181), but this did not survive Bonferroni correction and is of uncertain biological relevance.

**Strength of biological evidence for CHD/CDH (autosomal dominant): NONE**
All functional and expression data pertain to flagellar/ciliary biology in reproductive tissues. No mechanistic, pathway, or expression evidence links CFAP44 to cardiac or diaphragmatic development, and no haploinsufficiency mechanism has been proposed or demonstrated.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level for CFAP44 as a CHD/CDH gene with autosomal dominant pathogenic variants: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:** The evidence base for CFAP44 as a gene relevant to human disease is robust, but exclusively within the domain of autosomal recessive male infertility/MMAF, supported by multiple independent patient cohorts, consistent mouse knockout phenotypes, and functional validation in multiple organisms.

**Key gaps and limitations:** There are no reported patients with CHD or CDH attributed to CFAP44 variants in any published study or conference abstract reviewed. No heterozygous (autosomal dominant) pathogenic variants have been described for any phenotype. No cardiac or diaphragmatic expression, functional, or model organism data exist for this gene. The one non-reproductive association (opioid dependence, PMID: 39371181) was sub-threshold and not replicated.

**Conclusion:** Based on all available evidence, CFAP44 has no established or emerging role in congenital heart disease or congenital diaphragmatic hernia, and there is no support for an autosomal dominant disease mechanism. The gene's well-characterized function is restricted to axonemal integrity in motile cilia and sperm flagella, causing autosomal recessive male infertility. Any proposed association of CFAP44 with CHD/CDH in a heterozygous dominant context would require de novo clinical and experimental evidence before it could be considered.
