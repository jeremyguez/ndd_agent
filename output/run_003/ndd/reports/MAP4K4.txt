# MAP4K4 as a Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene: Literature Evidence Report

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

MAP4K4 (Mitogen-Activated Protein Kinase Kinase Kinase Kinase 4) is a serine/threonine kinase of the STE20 family that functions as an upstream regulator of multiple signaling cascades, including the JNK, Hippo (LATS1/2-YAP/TAZ), and RAS/ERK pathways. In early embryonic development, MAP4K4 acts as a negative regulator of RAS signaling and plays critical roles in cell migration, cytoskeletal organization, and tissue morphogenesis (PMID: 37126546).

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports:** Five independent reports or series were identified, spanning case series, conference presentations, and individual case reports.

**Total approximate number of patients:** Approximately 32 patients across all reports: 21 families (PMID: 37126546), 2 patients (PMID: 36469137), 1 patient (PMID: 38679877), 7 individuals from 4 families (ASHG 2018 – Bhoj et al.), and 1 patient with brain arteriovenous malformation (PMID: 30120215). The BAVM case involves potential oligogenic inheritance and should be interpreted with caution.

**Most common phenotypes observed:** The predominant phenotype across studies is a syndromic neurodevelopmental disorder. Shared features include:
- Neurodevelopmental delay / intellectual disability (present across all major series)
- Autism spectrum disorder (PMID: 36469137)
- Congenital heart defects (pulmonary artery stenosis, septal defects, valve insufficiency) (ASHG 2018 – Bhoj et al.; PMID: 38679877)
- Multiple congenital anomalies (PMID: 37126546)
- Craniofacial dysmorphism, including craniosynostosis (PMID: 38679877)
- RASopathy / Noonan-like syndrome features (ASHG 2018 – Bhoj et al.; PMID: 38679877)

**Predominant variant type:** Loss-of-function (LoF) variants — including frameshift, early truncating, and dominant-negative alleles — predominate across the largest series (PMID: 37126546; PMID: 36469137; ASHG 2018 – Bhoj et al.). Missense variants in conserved or functionally critical regions of the kinase domain have also been reported (PMID: 38679877; ASHG 2018 – Bhoj et al.). All reported pathogenic variants appear to act via haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative mechanisms, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. This is strongly supported by population-level constraint metrics: MAP4K4 has a pLI score of 1.0 and a missense Z-score of 4.01, indicating extreme intolerance to both loss-of-function and missense variation (ASHG 2018 – Bhoj et al.).

**Known cohort affiliation:** None of the patients are explicitly identified as belonging to established rare disease cohorts such as the Undiagnosed Diseases Network (UDN), GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL. The ASHG 2018 report originated from Children's Hospital of Philadelphia and GeneDx (United States), and the craniosynostosis case was from Korea (PMID: 38679877).

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**
The evidence is supported by a meaningful number of patients (~32 across five independent reports), de novo occurrence of variants in multiple cases, consistent phenotypic overlap, and functional validation in at least one large series. However, the largest cohort (PMID: 37126546, n=21) is published as a single study, individual variant-level data are not fully available from abstracts for most series, and no patients are drawn from large systematic rare disease cohorts. The evidence base is growing but not yet at the level of well-established dominant neurodevelopmental disorder genes.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Both zebrafish (*Danio rerio*) and mouse (*Mus musculus*) models have been employed, using full knockout, conditional knockout, and knockdown strategies.

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

- *Zebrafish (knockdown):* Reduction of Map4k4 activity in zebrafish recapitulated developmental defects consistent with the human phenotype, including neurodevelopmental differences and multiple congenital anomalies, directly validating the pathogenicity of human variants (PMID: 37126546).

- *Mouse (full knockout):* Homozygous Map4k4 knockout mice are embryonic lethal with neural tube malformations, and surviving heterozygotes show sub-Mendelian ratios, indicating strong developmental constraint (PMID: 37290629). Additionally, Map4k4 full knockout studies demonstrated stabilized focal adhesions and impaired cell migration (PMID: 25490267), consistent with a role in tissue morphogenesis.

- *Mouse (conditional knockout):* Tissue-specific deletions have been viable and informative. Conditional knockout in CD8 T cells demonstrated that Map4k4 loss is tolerated in adult animals under homeostatic conditions (PMID: 32220977). Retinal studies showed that Map4k4 is involved in photoreceptor neurite formation and neurogenesis-related factor balance (PMID: 37290629). Knockdown in a mouse model of chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy preserved intraepidermal nerve fiber density and rescued axon degeneration (PMID: 42097147), further supporting a neuronal role.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The zebrafish knockdown model directly mirrors the human neurodevelopmental and congenital anomaly phenotype (PMID: 37126546). Mouse full knockout embryonic lethality and neural tube defects are consistent with the severe developmental consequences of complete MAP4K4 loss, while the human heterozygous phenotype aligns with haploinsufficiency. Neuronal expression and function in photoreceptor development (PMID: 37290629) and axonal integrity (PMID: 42097147) further support neurodevelopmental relevance.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**
The zebrafish model provides direct, variant-specific functional validation of human alleles. Mouse full knockout data demonstrate embryonic lethality with neural tube defects, strongly supporting developmental essentiality. However, a heterozygous mouse model recapitulating the human dominant phenotype has not been described in the available literature, which is a notable gap.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
MAP4K4 is a multifunctional kinase operating at the intersection of several disease-relevant signaling networks:
- **RAS/ERK signaling:** MAP4K4 negatively regulates RAS signaling during early embryogenesis; loss of this function is proposed as the mechanistic basis for the RASopathy-like human phenotype (PMID: 37126546).
- **Hippo pathway:** MAP4K4 (with MAP4K6/7) acts downstream of the GTPase RAP2 to activate LATS1/2 and suppress YAP/TAZ in response to low ECM stiffness, placing it as a mechanosensitive kinase in the Hippo cascade (PMID: 30135582).
- **JNK/NF-κB signaling:** MAP4K4 activates JNK signaling and sustains NF-κB activity, relevant to both neurodevelopment and oncogenesis (PMID: 38084701).
- **Cytoskeletal regulation:** MAP4K4 phosphorylates moesin (an ERM family protein) to regulate focal adhesion dynamics, cell-cell adhesion, and collective cell migration (PMID: 37369604; PMID: 25490267).
- **Protein interactions:** MAP4K4 interacts with striatin 3/4 (STRN3/4) and is coupled to PP2A, modulating its growth-suppressive activities and enabling direct phosphorylation of VASP at S157 (PMID: 35941177).

**Expression pattern (neurodevelopmental relevance):**
MAP4K4 is expressed in neuronal isoforms and in blood, as demonstrated by transcriptome analysis in patients with ASD (PMID: 36469137). Mouse studies confirm expression in retinal photoreceptors and a role in neurogenesis-related factor balance (PMID: 37290629). Single-cell RNA sequencing data show predominant expression in retinal endothelial cells in the context of diabetic retinopathy (PMID: 40072537), suggesting broad vascular and neural expression. The gene's role in axonal integrity is supported by neuroprotection studies in sensory neurons (PMID: 42097147).

**Key experimental findings:**
- De novo frameshift variants in ASD patients were shown by transcriptome analysis to trigger nonsense-mediated decay and disrupt MAP4K4 mRNA stability, confirming a LoF mechanism at the RNA level (PMID: 36469137).
- Zebrafish functional assays directly validated that human disease-associated variants abrogate MAP4K4 protein function and impair RAS signaling regulation (PMID: 37126546).
- Population constraint metrics (pLI=1, missense Z=4.01) provide strong computational evidence for haploinsufficiency as a disease mechanism (ASHG 2018 – Bhoj et al.).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
MAP4K4 has well-characterized molecular functions in multiple signaling pathways relevant to neurodevelopment and congenital anomalies. Mechanistic studies have directly linked human disease variants to loss of RAS regulatory function. Expression in neuronal tissues and functional roles in axonal integrity, neurogenesis, and cytoskeletal dynamics are well-supported across independent experimental systems.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths:**
- Convergent evidence from five independent clinical reports totaling approximately 32 patients, with consistent neurodevelopmental and cardiac phenotypes.
- Direct functional validation of human variants in zebrafish, demonstrating loss of RAS regulatory function as the disease mechanism (PMID: 37126546).
- Extreme population-level constraint (pLI=1, missense Z=4.01) strongly supports haploinsufficiency as a pathogenic mechanism.
- De novo occurrence documented across multiple families and confirmed by trio sequencing.
- Robust biological plausibility through MAP4K4's established roles in RAS, Hippo, and JNK signaling pathways critical to embryonic neurodevelopment.
- Mouse full knockout embryonic lethality with neural tube defects corroborates developmental essentiality (PMID: 37290629).

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- The largest clinical series (n=21, PMID: 37126546) represents a single publication, and granular variant-level data are not available from the abstract; independent replication in large systematic cohorts (e.g., DDD, GEL, UDN) has not been documented.
- A heterozygous mouse model recapitulating the human dominant phenotype has not been reported, limiting direct in vivo modeling of the disease-relevant genotype.
- The BAVM case (PMID: 30120215) involves potential oligogenic inheritance and does not clearly support MAP4K4 as a standalone disease gene for that phenotype.
- Variant-level functional data (e.g., protein activity assays for individual missense variants) are limited outside of the zebrafish series.

**Conclusion:**
MAP4K4 has accumulating evidence supporting its role as an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene, with a consistent phenotypic spectrum encompassing syndromic neurodevelopmental delay, congenital heart defects, and RASopathy-like features. The combination of de novo variant identification in multiple independent families, direct functional validation in zebrafish, extreme population constraint, and strong biological plausibility in RAS/Hippo/JNK signaling pathways collectively support a causal role. Further replication in large unbiased cohorts and development of heterozygous animal models would substantially strengthen the evidence and facilitate formal gene-disease classification.

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. Abrogation of MAP4K4 protein function causes congenital anomalies in humans and zebrafish. (PMID: 37126546)
2. Integrated exome and transcriptome analysis prioritizes MAP4K4 de novo frameshift variants in autism spectrum disorder as a novel disease-gene association. (PMID: 36469137)
3. Syndromic craniosynostosis caused by a novel missense variant in MAP4K4: Expanding the genotype-phenotype relationship in RASopathies. (PMID: 38679877)
4. Perturbations of BMP/TGF-β and VEGF/VEGFR signalling pathways in non-syndromic sporadic brain arteriovenous malformations (BAVM). (PMID: 30120215)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. MAP4K4 is involved in the neuronal development of retinal photoreceptors. (PMID: 37290629)
2. Microtubules regulate focal adhesion dynamics through MAP4K4. (PMID: 25490267)
3. MAP4K4 negatively regulates CD8 T cell-mediated antitumor and antiviral immunity. (PMID: 32220977)
4. A human iPSC-derived sensory neuron platform for high-throughput discovery of neuroprotectants against chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy. (PMID: 42097147)

### Biological Evidence
1. RAP2 mediates mechanoresponses of the Hippo pathway (PMID: 30135582)
2. Histone lactylation-derived LINC01127 promotes the self-renewal of glioblastoma stem cells via the cis-regulating the MAP4K4 to activate JNK pathway (PMID: 38084701)
3. MAP4K4 regulates forces at cell-cell and cell-matrix adhesions to promote collective cell migration (PMID: 37369604)
4. Cooperation of Striatin 3 and MAP4K4 promotes growth and tissue invasion (PMID: 35941177)
5. MAP4K4 aggravates microvascular anomalies in diabetic retinopathy in a YTHDF2-dependent manner (PMID: 40072537)
