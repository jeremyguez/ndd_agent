# Literature Evidence Report: AP2S1 as a Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene (Autosomal Dominant / Heterozygous Variants)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

AP2S1 encodes the sigma subunit (AP2σ) of the heterotetrameric adaptor protein-2 (AP-2) complex, which also comprises AP2α, AP2β, and AP2μ subunits. The AP-2 complex is a central component of clathrin-mediated endocytosis, mediating the internalization of transmembrane receptors — most notably the calcium-sensing receptor (CaSR) — from the cell surface, and thereby playing a critical role in calcium homeostasis and intracellular signaling.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports and patients:**
Two independent case reports were identified in the structured evidence. Both describe single patients (total n = 2 patients across these reports) with heterozygous missense variants in AP2S1. These are supplemented by a larger cohort study (PMID: 26963950) identifying AP2S1 mutations in 22 patients across 19 of 133 FHH families, and a smaller series (PMID: 24731014) identifying AP2S1 Arg15 mutations in 5 of 39 CASR-mutation-negative FHH patients, bringing the broader published patient count to at least ~27 individuals with confirmed AP2S1-related disease across the literature.

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
The predominant phenotype associated with heterozygous AP2S1 pathogenic variants is familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia type 3 (FHH3), characterized by hypercalcemia, hypocalciuria, and variably elevated parathyroid hormone (PTH). Notably, both recent case reports (PMID: 39949382; PMID: 41501259) document neurodevelopmental features — specifically described as "developmental disorder" and "neurodevelopmental difficulties," respectively — in patients carrying the p.Arg15Leu variant. Low bone mineral density was also reported in both cases. The larger cohort study (PMID: 26963950) noted that FHH3 patients had more severe hypercalcemia and higher renal tubular calcium reabsorption compared to FHH type 1 (CASR mutations).

**Predominant variant type:**
All identified pathogenic variants are heterozygous missense mutations, with a strong mutational hotspot at codon Arg15 of AP2S1. Reported variants include p.Arg15Leu (c.44G>T), p.Arg15Cys, and p.Arg15His (PMID: 24731014; PMID: 26963950). These are loss-of-function in effect, impairing AP2σ interaction with CaSR and disrupting receptor internalization. No gain-of-function AP2S1 mutations causing autosomal dominant hypocalcemia (ADH3) have been identified in a dedicated search of 19 hypocalcemic patients (PMID: 24708097).

**Cohort affiliation:**
Neither of the two recent case reports specifies affiliation with recognized large-scale cohorts such as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL.

**Strength of clinical evidence for neurodevelopmental disorder specifically: LIMITED**
While the metabolic/endocrine phenotype (FHH3) is well-established across multiple studies and dozens of patients, the neurodevelopmental phenotype is reported in only two individual case reports (PMID: 39949382; PMID: 41501259), both carrying the same p.Arg15Leu variant. Systematic characterization of neurodevelopmental features across the broader FHH3 patient population is lacking, and it is not yet clear whether neurodevelopmental difficulties represent a consistent, penetrant feature of AP2S1 haploinsufficiency or are incidental findings in these two cases.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:**
AP2S1/Ap2s1 has been studied in mouse (Mus musculus) and zebrafish (Danio rerio).

**Key findings in mouse:**

- A CRISPR/Cas9 knock-in mouse carrying the orthologous p.Arg15Leu missense mutation (Ap2s1+/L15) recapitulated the human FHH3 metabolic phenotype, including marked hypercalcemia, hypermagnesemia, hypophosphatemia, and elevated alkaline phosphatase and FGF-23 (PMID: 33729479). Importantly, this study demonstrated impaired protein-protein interaction between AP2σ2 and CaSR, providing direct mechanistic validation. Cinacalcet treatment reduced plasma calcium and PTH in these mice, mirroring therapeutic responses observed in human patients. Homozygous Ap2s1L15/L15 mice died perinatally, confirming that complete loss of AP2S1 function is incompatible with survival.

- An independent ENU-induced loss-of-function mouse model (Ap2s1+/del17, carrying an in-frame 17-amino-acid deletion) showed that heterozygous mice were viable with entirely normal plasma biochemistry, including calcium, PTH, and vitamin D, and normal urinary calcium excretion (PMID: 29479578). Homozygous Ap2s1del17/del17 mice were embryonic lethal (dying between E3.5 and E9.5), indicating that complete AP2S1 loss is essential for early embryonic development. The discrepancy between this model and the Arg15Leu knock-in model suggests that the Arg15 missense allele exerts a dominant-negative or gain-of-toxic-function effect on CaSR endocytosis, rather than simple haploinsufficiency.

**Key findings in zebrafish:**

- Loss-of-function of ap2s1 in zebrafish impairs acoustically evoked habituation learning and biases escape behavior choice in a sensory modality-specific manner (PMID: 38550987; PMID: 36972301). These behavioral phenotypes are consistent with a role for AP2S1 in neuronal function and synaptic plasticity. Specifically, ap2s1 was identified as part of a distinct molecular module that drives habituation by antagonizing dopamine signaling (PMID: 36972301), implicating the AP-2 complex in neurotransmitter receptor recycling and behavioral plasticity.

- A forward genetic screen in zebrafish further implicated the CaSR–AP2 endocytosis axis in sensorimotor decision-making (PMID: 29681477), providing a functional link between CaSR internalization (the pathway disrupted by AP2S1 mutations) and neurological behavior.

**Consistency with human neurodevelopmental phenotype:**
The zebrafish data provide biologically plausible support for a neurodevelopmental/neurological role of AP2S1, consistent with the neurodevelopmental difficulties reported in the two human cases. However, the mouse knock-in model (PMID: 33729479) did not report neurological or behavioral phenotypes, and the ENU heterozygous model showed no overt phenotype at all (PMID: 29479578). Direct neurological characterization of the Ap2s1+/L15 mouse has not been reported.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**
The metabolic phenotype is strongly validated in mouse. Neurological/behavioral phenotypes are supported in zebrafish but have not been systematically characterized in mammalian models.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
AP2S1 encodes the σ subunit of the AP-2 complex, which is the master adaptor for clathrin-mediated endocytosis (CME). The AP-2 complex recognizes tyrosine-based and dileucine sorting motifs in the cytoplasmic tails of transmembrane cargo proteins, recruits clathrin, and drives the formation of clathrin-coated vesicles. The Arg15 residue of AP2σ is located within the cargo-recognition interface and is critical for binding to the internalization motif of CaSR (PMID: 24731014; PMID: 33729479). Loss-of-function mutations at Arg15 impair CaSR internalization, leading to sustained CaSR signaling at the cell surface and dysregulated calcium homeostasis.

Beyond CaSR, AP2S1 participates in the endocytosis of multiple receptor systems. It has been identified as a binding partner of GPN3 within the CME machinery regulating EGFR internalization (PMID: 39893205), as a novel interactor of the E3 ubiquitin ligase RNF41 with roles in leptin and LIF receptor signaling (PMID: 29560723), and as a host factor exploited by Zika virus for placental entry via CME (PMID: 40156834). AP2S1 expression is also upregulated during Listeria monocytogenes infection, and its knockout inhibits intracellular bacterial replication (PMID: 40847839), further underscoring its broad role in receptor-mediated membrane trafficking.

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**
AP2S1 mRNA expression has been assessed in parathyroid adenoma tissue, where it showed a variable and less pronounced response to extracellular calcium compared to CaSR and GNA11 (PMID: 32666007). The zebrafish studies (PMID: 38550987; PMID: 36972301) demonstrate that ap2s1 is functionally active in the nervous system, where it modulates habituation learning and escape behavior through dopamine signaling pathways. CME is a fundamental process in neurons for synaptic vesicle recycling and neurotransmitter receptor internalization, providing a mechanistic basis for neurodevelopmental involvement. However, systematic brain expression data for AP2S1 in humans or mammals are not reported in the available evidence.

**Key experimental findings:**
- Arg15 mutations (p.R15C, p.R15L, p.R15H) account for 13% of CASR-mutation-negative FHH cases (PMID: 24731014), establishing AP2S1 as a recurrent, clinically significant FHH3 gene.
- The p.Arg15Leu knock-in mouse directly demonstrated impaired AP2σ–CaSR interaction and pharmacological rescue with cinacalcet (PMID: 33729479).
- Zebrafish ap2s1 loss-of-function identifies a role in dopamine-dependent behavioral plasticity and habituation (PMID: 36972301).
- No gain-of-function AP2S1 mutations causing ADH3 were identified in a dedicated screen (PMID: 24708097), reinforcing that pathogenic variants act through loss-of-function/dominant-negative mechanisms.

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG** (for the endocrine/calcium phenotype mechanism); **MODERATE** (for neurodevelopmental mechanism specifically).

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level for AP2S1 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene: LIMITED**

**Key strengths:**
- AP2S1 is a well-established, recurrently mutated gene for FHH3, with strong mechanistic, animal model, and clinical validation of its role in calcium homeostasis.
- Neurodevelopmental features (developmental disorder, neurodevelopmental difficulties) are reported in two independent cases carrying the same p.Arg15Leu variant (PMID: 39949382; PMID: 41501259), suggesting this may be a genuine, if undercharacterized, feature of the condition.
- Zebrafish loss-of-function studies provide biologically plausible mechanistic support for a neurological role of AP2S1, demonstrating impaired habituation learning and dopamine-dependent behavioral plasticity (PMID: 38550987; PMID: 36972301).

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- The neurodevelopmental phenotype is documented in only two case reports (n = 2 patients), both with the same variant, and has not been systematically assessed across the larger published FHH3 cohort (n ≥ 22 patients; PMID: 26963950). It is unknown whether neurodevelopmental difficulties are a consistent feature of AP2S1 haploinsufficiency or represent coincidental findings.
- Mammalian (mouse) models carrying the Arg15Leu mutation have not been reported to exhibit neurological or behavioral phenotypes, and no dedicated neurological characterization of Ap2s1+/L15 mice has been published (PMID: 33729479).
- Brain-specific expression data for AP2S1 in humans are not available in the current evidence base, and the molecular mechanism linking AP2S1 dysfunction to neurodevelopmental outcomes in humans remains speculative.

**Conclusion:**
AP2S1 is a robustly validated gene for familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia type 3 (FHH3), caused by heterozygous loss-of-function missense mutations — predominantly at codon Arg15 — that impair CaSR internalization via clathrin-mediated endocytosis. Emerging clinical reports and zebrafish functional data raise the possibility that AP2S1 variants also contribute to neurodevelopmental phenotypes, consistent with the gene's role in neuronal receptor trafficking and dopamine signaling. However, the evidence specifically supporting AP2S1 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene remains limited, and systematic prospective studies characterizing neurodevelopmental outcomes in FHH3 patients are needed to determine whether this represents a true, penetrant feature of AP2S1 haploinsufficiency.
