# Evidence Report: THEGL as a Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene (Autosomal Dominant)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

THEGL (Theg Spermatid Protein-Like) is a gene with limited functional characterization in the published literature, with its closest paralog THEG being primarily associated with spermatid development and male fertility. The broader biological role of THEGL, particularly in neurodevelopmental contexts, remains poorly defined.

---

- **Independent case reports / case series found:** 0
- **Total approximate number of patients across studies:** 0
- **Most common phenotypes observed:** No clinical phenotypes have been reported in association with THEGL variants in the available evidence base.
- **Predominant variant type:** Not applicable; no pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants have been reported.
- **Cohort representation (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL):** No cases from any known neurodevelopmental disease cohort were identified.
- **Strength of clinical evidence: NONE**

No published case reports, case series, or cohort studies were identified linking heterozygous (autosomal dominant) pathogenic variants in THEGL to neurodevelopmental disorder or any other human disease phenotype.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

- **Known pathways and molecular function:** THEGL is a paralog of THEG (testis-expressed gene), which has been implicated in spermatogenesis. No well-characterized molecular pathway involving THEGL in neurodevelopment has been described in the available literature.
- **Expression pattern:** No neurodevelopmentally relevant expression data (e.g., from brain transcriptomic atlases, single-cell RNA sequencing of neural tissue, or developmental expression profiling) were identified in the provided evidence base.
- **Key experimental findings:** None identified.
- **Strength of biological evidence: NONE**

There is currently no experimental or bioinformatic evidence available in the provided data to support a neurodevelopmental role for THEGL at the molecular or cellular level.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

- **Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:** None can be identified from the current evidence base.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- There are no published clinical cases linking THEGL variants to human neurodevelopmental disease.
- No model organism functional studies exist to support a neurological role.
- No expression or pathway data have been provided to establish biological plausibility.
- The gene remains largely uncharacterized beyond its structural homology to THEG.

**Conclusion:** Based on the evidence reviewed, there is currently insufficient support to classify THEGL as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with autosomal dominant pathogenicity. All three lines of evidence — clinical, model organism, and biological — returned no findings. Before any disease association can be proposed, foundational work including expression profiling, functional studies, and the identification of patients with de novo or segregating heterozygous variants in well-characterized neurodevelopmental cohorts would be required.
