# Literature Evidence Report: ACTL6B as an Autosomal Dominant Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

ACTL6B (actin-like protein 6B; also known as BAF53b) encodes a neuron-specific subunit of the nBAF (neuronal Brg1/Brm-associated factor) SWI/SNF chromatin remodeling complex, which is selectively incorporated into the complex upon mitotic exit in post-mitotic neurons via a miR-9*/miR-124-mediated switch from its paralog ACTL6A (PMID: 19561591). ACTL6B is essential for activity-dependent dendritic morphogenesis and the regulation of gene expression programs underlying post-mitotic neural development and connectivity.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports/series:** At least ten independent reports or case series were identified, spanning peer-reviewed publications and conference abstracts from multiple countries and institutions.

**Total approximate number of patients:** The largest and most comprehensive study (PMID: 39275948) analyzed 105 individuals and delineated both autosomal recessive and autosomal dominant forms, reporting findings on 66 patients with confirmed ACTL6B variants. An earlier landmark study (PMID: 31031012) described 10 unrelated individuals carrying the recurrent de novo heterozygous missense variant c.1027G>A, p.Gly343Arg. Additional patients are reported in smaller case series and single case reports (PMID: 42109474; ASHG 2016 – Bell et al.; ASHG 2015 – VanGils et al.; PMID: 28867142). Across all sources, the total number of patients with heterozygous (autosomal dominant) ACTL6B variants conservatively exceeds 70–80 individuals.

**Most common phenotypes observed:** The dominant (heterozygous de novo) form is consistently associated with:
- Moderate-to-severe global developmental delay and intellectual disability
- Absent or severely impaired speech/language
- Autistic features
- Epilepsy/seizures (including drug-resistant forms)
- Hypotonia
- Structural brain anomalies, particularly hypoplastic corpus callosum and parenchymal volume loss/atrophy
- Dysmorphic facial features (e.g., wide mouth, bulbous nose, diastema)
- In at least one case, gastrointestinal dysmotility/pediatric chronic intestinal pseudo-obstruction (PMID: 42109474)

**Predominant variant type:** Both missense and loss-of-function (LoF) de novo heterozygous variants are reported in the dominant form. Notably, the recurrent de novo missense variant p.Gly343Arg was identified in 9 of 10 unrelated individuals in one study (PMID: 31031012), and functional data support a gain-of-function or dominant-negative mechanism for heterozygous missense variants rather than haploinsufficiency. The large delineation study (PMID: 39275948) confirmed that de novo monoallelic variants—both missense and LoF—cause moderate-to-severe presentations.

**Cohort representation:** Patients have been identified through multiple international clinical and research cohorts. One conference abstract explicitly identifies the RUMC (Radboud University Medical Center) cohort (ASHG 2017 – Lelieveld et al.). Additional patients derive from the Simons Simplex Collection (PMID: 28867142), Baylor College of Medicine/GeneDx clinical WES databases (ASHG 2019 – Lattier et al.), and multi-center international collaborations (PMID: 39275948; PMID: 31031012). French (PMID: 31036916; ASHG 2018 – Lecoquierre et al.) and Canadian (ASHG 2016 – Bell et al.) cohorts are also represented.

**Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG**
The evidence includes a large multi-center delineation study with >100 individuals, a recurrent de novo variant identified independently in 9 unrelated probands, trio-based sequencing confirmation across multiple studies, and consistent phenotypic profiles across independent international cohorts.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*) and *Drosophila melanogaster*.

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**
- *Actl6b* full knockout mice are viable and fertile but display a constellation of ASD-relevant behavioral phenotypes including social impairments, memory deficits, repetitive behaviors, and hyperactivity (PMID: 32312822). Structural brain abnormalities—specifically corpus callosum hypoplasia—were observed in both knockout mice and humans lacking ACTL6B, providing a direct structural correlate to the human phenotype.
- At the molecular level, ACTL6B loss in mice relieved repression of early response genes (including AP1 transcription factors *Fos*, *Fosl2*, *Fosb*, *Junb*) and increased chromatin accessibility at AP1 binding sites, identifying a specific mechanistic pathway (PMID: 32312822).
- In *Drosophila*, patient-derived ACTL6B mutations destabilized the protein in neurons and caused misrouting of dendrites to incorrect glomeruli in the olfactory system, demonstrating evolutionary conservation of ACTL6B's role in neural connectivity (PMID: 32312822).

**Consistency with human phenotype:** High. Corpus callosum hypoplasia, autistic-spectrum behaviors, and impaired neural connectivity observed in animal models directly mirror the core human phenotypic features of intellectual disability, autistic features, and structural brain anomalies.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**
Convergent findings across two species, with mechanistic insight into chromatin remodeling dysfunction and direct structural brain phenotypes matching the human condition.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:** ACTL6B is a dedicated subunit of the nBAF SWI/SNF chromatin remodeling complex in post-mitotic neurons. Its incorporation into the complex is regulated by a developmentally timed microRNA switch (miR-9*/miR-124) that replaces ACTL6A with ACTL6B upon neuronal differentiation (PMID: 19561591). The nBAF complex containing ACTL6B regulates chromatin accessibility and transcription of genes critical for dendritic morphogenesis, synaptic function, and neural connectivity.

**Expression pattern:** ACTL6B expression is highly restricted to neural lineages. It is expressed in mature olfactory neurons (PMID: 26633363), discriminates neural from non-neural retinal lineages during hiPSC differentiation (PMID: 29751772), and is expressed in a mutually exclusive pattern with ACTL6A in the developing cerebellum and kidney—with co-expression of both subunits representing an aberrant state in rhabdoid tumors (PMID: 31912158). High expression is also observed across neuroendocrine neoplasm subtypes (PMID: 40946270), consistent with its neural lineage specificity.

**Key experimental findings:**
- In human iPSC-derived neurons, ACTL6B knockout caused profound deficits in dendrite development that were rescued by genetic repair or exogenous ACTL6B expression. Heterozygous de novo missense mutations did not recapitulate the knockout phenotype, supporting a gain-of-function or dominant-negative mechanism for the dominant form, while biallelic mutations caused loss-of-function effects (PMID: 31031012).
- iPSC-derived neurons from patients carrying the ACTL6B p.(Val421_Cys425del) variant showed impaired protein stability, dysregulated nBAF target gene expression, and elevated electrophysiological activity on multielectrode arrays, directly modeling the epileptic encephalopathy phenotype (PMID: 33141462).
- Patient-derived iPSC cortical neural progenitor cells showed significantly reduced ACTL6B expression correlating with dysregulation of neuronal differentiation (ASHG 2016 – Bell et al.).
- Whole-transcriptome profiling in ACTL6B mutant neurons identified transcriptional changes in cytoskeletal genes *TPPP* and *FSCN1*, linking ACTL6B to dendritic cytoskeletal regulation (PMID: 31031012).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent human iPSC/neuronal models, mechanistic dissection of gain-of-function versus loss-of-function variant effects, well-characterized pathway membership, and consistent neural-restricted expression across multiple experimental systems.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths:**
1. *Clinical convergence at scale:* A large multi-center delineation study (PMID: 39275948) and a landmark recurrent-variant study (PMID: 31031012) together document >70 individuals with heterozygous de novo ACTL6B variants and a consistent, recognizable neurodevelopmental syndrome, with independent replication across at least ten studies from multiple countries.
2. *Mechanistic clarity:* Human iPSC neuronal models (PMID: 31031012; PMID: 33141462) and mouse/Drosophila knockouts (PMID: 32312822) provide convergent mechanistic evidence linking ACTL6B dysfunction to dendritic pathology, epileptic hyperexcitability, and chromatin dysregulation, with experimental distinction between dominant and recessive mechanisms.
3. *Biological plausibility:* ACTL6B's role as a neuron-specific SWI/SNF subunit with a developmentally regulated expression switch (PMID: 19561591) provides a compelling biological framework for its selective involvement in neurodevelopmental disease.

**Key gaps or limitations:**
- Specific variant-level functional data are available for only a subset of reported variants; the mechanistic basis (gain-of-function vs. dominant-negative vs. haploinsufficiency) for the full spectrum of heterozygous LoF variants remains incompletely resolved.
- Most conference abstracts lack full peer-reviewed publication, limiting detailed variant-level and phenotypic data for those cohorts.
- Genotype-phenotype correlations within the dominant form (e.g., missense vs. LoF) require further systematic characterization.

**Conclusion:** The evidence supporting ACTL6B as an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene is strong and multi-layered, encompassing large-scale clinical genetics, recurrent de novo variant identification, human iPSC neuronal modeling, and cross-species functional validation. Heterozygous de novo variants in ACTL6B—both missense and LoF—cause a recognizable syndrome characterized by moderate-to-severe intellectual disability, absent speech, autistic features, epilepsy, and structural brain anomalies, with a likely non-haploinsufficiency (gain-of-function or dominant-negative) mechanism for at least a subset of missense variants. ACTL6B meets criteria for definitive gene-disease association in the autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder context.
