# UPF1 as a Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene: Literature Evidence Report

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

UPF1 (Up-Frameshift Suppressor 1 Homolog) encodes an ATP-dependent RNA helicase that serves as the central effector of nonsense-mediated mRNA decay (NMD), a conserved post-transcriptional surveillance mechanism that degrades transcripts harboring premature termination codons and regulates the stability of a broad subset of normal cellular mRNAs. Beyond canonical NMD, UPF1 participates in additional RNA decay pathways including Staufen-mediated mRNA decay (SMD) and structure-mediated RNA decay, positioning it as a master regulator of transcriptome homeostasis (PMID: 23681777; PMID: 32017897).

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports and patients:**
Four independent reports were identified, collectively describing five patients with de novo heterozygous UPF1 variants and neurodevelopmental phenotypes. These include two peer-reviewed publications (PMID: 39993774; PMID: 39571789), one conference abstract (ASHG 2023 – Chung et al.), and one whole-genome sequencing cohort study (PMID: 28539120).

**Variants identified:**
- PMID: 39993774: c.1576A>C, p.(Ala526Thr) — missense, de novo, in a conserved helicase motif; classified pathogenic/likely pathogenic.
- PMID: 39571789: c.949_951del, p.(Asp317del) — in-frame deletion, de novo; and c.1984G>A, p.(Asp662Asn) — missense, de novo; both identified by trio exome sequencing with functional Drosophila validation.
- ASHG 2023 (Chung et al.): c.1846A>G, p.(Thr616Ala) — missense, de novo, classified Likely Pathogenic per ACMG guidelines by trio exome sequencing.
- PMID: 28539120: c.1576_1577delinsA — two-base indel, de novo, identified by whole-genome sequencing; classified as potentially causative but of uncertain significance.

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
Intellectual disability (all five patients), global developmental delay (motor and/or verbal), and autism spectrum disorder/autistic features were the most consistently reported phenotypes. Additional features included ADHD and behavioral disturbances (PMID: 39993774), dysmorphic facial features (frontal bossing, hypertelorism, high frontal hairline, thin upper lip) in two patients from the same study (PMID: 39571789), and a notably broad phenotypic spectrum in the ASHG 2023 case including infantile hypotonia, myoclonic epilepsy, bilateral high-frequency hearing loss, overgrowth (head circumference, height, and weight >2 SD), scoliosis, and coarse facial features — with overgrowth described as a previously unreported feature of this condition.

**Predominant variant type:**
Missense variants predominate (three of five variants), with one in-frame deletion and one two-base indel also reported. All identified variants are heterozygous and arose de novo, consistent with an autosomal dominant mechanism. Variants cluster within or near the helicase domain of UPF1, suggesting a dominant-negative or gain-of-function mechanism rather than simple haploinsufficiency, though this has not been formally established for all variants.

**Cohort affiliation:**
None of the reported patients are explicitly identified as belonging to established large-scale cohorts such as the Undiagnosed Diseases Network (UDN), GREGoR, Deciphering Developmental Disorders (DDD), Radboud University Medical Center (RUMC), or Genomics England (GEL).

**Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED**
While all five cases carry de novo heterozygous variants and share a core neurodevelopmental phenotype, the total patient number remains very small (n=5 across four reports), no large cohort studies have been published, and one variant (PMID: 28539120) was classified only as of uncertain significance. Functional validation in human cellular systems is largely absent. The evidence is emerging and directionally consistent but not yet sufficient to reach a MODERATE or STRONG designation by standard gene-disease curation frameworks.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:**
UPF1 has been studied in zebrafish (*Danio rerio*) and mouse (*Mus musculus*). No *Drosophila* knockout data were extracted in the model organism evidence set, though the clinical study (PMID: 39571789) employed Drosophila as a functional validation tool.

**Key phenotypes from knockouts/knockdowns:**

*Zebrafish:* Morpholino-mediated knockdown of *upf1* in zebrafish causes severe embryonic developmental defects, including disrupted early patterning and reduced embryonic viability, with phenotypes comparable to depletion of other core NMD factors (Upf2, Smg5, Smg6) (PMID: 19414594; PMID: 21227923). These studies collectively demonstrate that UPF1 is essential for embryonic development and survival in vertebrates. Additional zebrafish studies show that *upf1* knockdown disrupts regulation of APP family transcripts relevant to neurodegeneration (PMID: 38789273) and impairs the rapid turnover of cyclic gene transcripts during somitogenesis (PMID: 32246943).

*Mouse:* Conditional knockout of *Upf1* in B cells severely impedes early B cell development and VH-DHJH recombination, demonstrating cell-type-specific essential roles beyond canonical NMD (PMID: 38982067). A separate conditional knockout in T cells results in increased CD8+ T cell exhaustion and reduced anti-tumor immunity, with viable animals (PMID: 40749425), indicating that tissue-specific loss is tolerated in some lineages but functionally consequential. In the context of fragile X syndrome, hyperactivated NMD (reflected by altered UPF1 activity) during cortical, hippocampal, and cerebellar development in *Fmr1*-KO mice was associated with abnormal regulation of neuronal mRNAs critical for brain development (PMID: 34784943), providing indirect evidence linking dysregulated UPF1/NMD activity to neurodevelopmental brain pathology.

**Consistency with human phenotype:**
The zebrafish embryonic lethality upon complete *upf1* loss is consistent with the expectation that heterozygous hypomorphic or dominant-negative variants — rather than complete loss-of-function — underlie the human neurodevelopmental disorder. The mouse data demonstrating cell-type-specific roles in immune and potentially neural lineages, combined with the fragile X model implicating NMD dysregulation in cortical and hippocampal development, provide biological plausibility for neurodevelopmental consequences of UPF1 dysfunction. However, no model organism study has directly modeled the specific human missense variants in a neurological context.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**
The zebrafish data robustly establish that UPF1 is essential for vertebrate embryonic development, and mouse conditional knockouts demonstrate tissue-specific roles. Indirect evidence links NMD dysregulation to neurodevelopmental brain pathology. However, no study has directly recapitulated the human neurodevelopmental phenotype using patient-specific variants in a mammalian model, limiting the strength of this evidence tier.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
UPF1 is the central ATPase/helicase of the NMD pathway, acting downstream of translation termination to recognize aberrant stop codons and recruit the SMG kinase complex for phosphorylation, which in turn recruits endonucleases (SMG6) and decapping/deadenylation factors for mRNA degradation. Structural and biochemical studies have shown that UPF1's CH domain mediates mutually exclusive interactions with SMG6 and UPF2, with distinct NMD complexes dictated by UPF1's RNA-binding conformational state (PMID: 38709891). Beyond NMD, UPF1 participates in SMD through direct interaction with STAU1/STAU2, regulating cellular differentiation including myogenesis and adipogenesis (PMID: 23681777), and in structure-mediated RNA decay targeting mRNAs and circular RNAs with highly structured 3' UTRs (PMID: 32017897).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**
Rapid depletion experiments using degron-tagged endogenous UPF1 in human cell lines have defined the consolidated NMD-regulated human transcriptome (NMDRHT), revealing that alternative splicing is a major contributor of NMD-targeted mRNAs and identifying non-canonical NMD events (PMID: 40934927). This broad transcriptome-regulatory role is particularly relevant in neurons, which are known to express a high proportion of alternatively spliced transcripts and long 3' UTR mRNAs — both categories enriched among NMD substrates. The fragile X mouse model data (PMID: 34784943) further implicate NMD activity, mediated through UPF1, in the regulation of neuronal mRNAs during embryonic and early postnatal brain development.

**Post-translational regulation:**
UPF1 protein stability and activity are subject to multiple layers of post-translational regulation. TRIM34-mediated ubiquitination promotes UPF1 proteasomal degradation in hepatocellular carcinoma (PMID: 38704136), while PRMT4-mediated asymmetric di-methylation at R433 inhibits autophagic degradation of UPF1, modulating NMD activity and tumor immunogenicity (PMID: 40570371). The splicing factor SLU7 physically interacts with UPF1 and is required to maintain UPF1 protein levels; caspase-mediated SLU7 cleavage during liver injury leads to downstream UPF1 downregulation (PMID: 39105183). These findings collectively demonstrate that UPF1 protein levels are tightly regulated and that perturbations — including those caused by missense variants affecting helicase domain conformation — could have broad transcriptomic consequences.

**Key experimental findings relevant to neurodevelopment:**
The Drosophila functional data reported in PMID: 39571789 showed that overexpression of the patient-derived variants p.(Asp317del) and p.(Asp662Asn) caused more severe developmental defects than wild-type UPF1 overexpression, supporting a dominant-negative or gain-of-function mechanism. This is mechanistically important because it suggests the pathogenic variants do not simply reduce NMD activity through haploinsufficiency but may actively interfere with normal UPF1 function in a dominant manner.

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
UPF1's molecular function is exceptionally well characterized across multiple experimental systems. Its role in regulating the transcriptome — including neuronal mRNAs and alternatively spliced transcripts — provides strong biological plausibility for neurodevelopmental consequences of dominant pathogenic variants. The structural basis for variant pathogenicity (helicase domain clustering) and functional Drosophila data supporting a dominant mechanism further strengthen this tier.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: LIMITED to MODERATE (emerging)**

**Key strengths:**
1. All five reported patients carry *de novo* heterozygous variants, providing strong genetic evidence for an autosomal dominant mechanism with high prior probability of pathogenicity.
2. Variants cluster within the helicase domain of UPF1, a region with well-established structural and functional importance, and Drosophila functional data (PMID: 39571789) support a dominant-negative mechanism.
3. The biological role of UPF1 in regulating neuronal transcriptomes and brain development is well-supported by multiple independent experimental systems, providing strong mechanistic plausibility.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. The total number of reported patients remains very small (n=5), with no large cohort studies, no recurrent variants across unrelated individuals, and no systematic case series establishing a defined clinical syndrome with consistent genotype-phenotype correlations.
2. No mammalian model organism study has directly modeled patient-specific UPF1 variants in a neurological context, and no human neuronal cell culture data are available to demonstrate variant-specific NMD dysregulation.
3. Several variants remain classified as of uncertain significance or likely pathogenic rather than definitively pathogenic, and the precise molecular mechanism (dominant-negative vs. gain-of-function vs. haploinsufficiency) has not been formally established for most variants.

**Conclusion:**
UPF1 is an emerging neurodevelopmental disorder gene supported by a small but internally consistent body of clinical evidence comprising five de novo heterozygous variants in patients with intellectual disability, developmental delay, and autism spectrum features, underpinned by strong biological plausibility from UPF1's well-characterized role in transcriptome regulation during brain development. The evidence base is currently limited by small patient numbers and the absence of direct mammalian neurological models of patient variants, and the gene-disease relationship should be considered as having preliminary/limited clinical validity pending identification of additional cases and functional validation in relevant neuronal systems. Systematic inclusion of UPF1 in neurodevelopmental gene panels and international data sharing efforts will be critical to establishing definitive clinical validity.
