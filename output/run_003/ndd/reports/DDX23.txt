# Evidence Report: DDX23 as a Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene (Autosomal Dominant)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

DDX23 (DEAD-box helicase 23), also known as PRP28, is an ATP-dependent RNA helicase of the DEAD-box family that plays a central role in pre-mRNA splicing as a component of the U5 snRNP, where it facilitates the transition from the pre-B to B spliceosome complex by mediating release of U1 snRNP from the 5' splice site (PMID: 38743621). Beyond its core spliceosomal function, DDX23 participates in microRNA biogenesis, transcriptional regulation, and mRNA processing across multiple cellular contexts (PMID: 26121981; PMID: 34966670).

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports:** Three independent publications/presentations have reported DDX23 variants in patients with neurodevelopmental disorders: one peer-reviewed case series (PMID: 34050707), one peer-reviewed cohort study (PMID: 38712318), and one conference abstract (ASHG 2023 – Järvelä et al.).

**Total approximate number of patients:** Approximately 11 patients across all reports (9 from the primary case series, PMID: 34050707; 1 from the Finnish polymicrogyria cohort, PMID: 38712318; and 1 from the ASHG 2023 abstract). It is possible that the PMID: 38712318 patient and the ASHG 2023 patient represent the same individual, given the overlapping Finnish origin, phenotype, and authorship; if so, the total is approximately 10 unique patients.

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Global developmental delay / cognitive impairment (all reports)
- Tone abnormalities (PMID: 34050707)
- Facial dysmorphism (PMID: 34050707)
- Epilepsy / seizures (all reports)
- Autism spectrum disorder (PMID: 34050707)
- Bilateral perisylvian polymicrogyria / cortical malformation (PMID: 38712318; ASHG 2023)
- Dysarthria and oromotor dysfunction (PMID: 38712318; ASHG 2023)
- Motor dysfunction including hemiparesis/quadriparesis (PMID: 38712318; ASHG 2023)
- Multisystem anomalies (skeletal, renal, ocular, cardiac) in a subset (PMID: 34050707)

**Predominant variant type:** All reported pathogenic variants are de novo missense variants. In the primary case series, variants cluster within the RecA-like domain, proximal to or within the DEAD-box helicase motif, suggesting a dominant-negative or gain-of-function mechanism rather than haploinsufficiency (PMID: 34050707). No loss-of-function (truncating) variants have been reported in affected individuals to date.

**Known cohorts:** Patients were identified through GeneMatcher (international cohort, PMID: 34050707) and through a Finnish family cohort (PMID: 38712318; ASHG 2023). None of the patients are explicitly reported as belonging to named large-scale cohorts such as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL.

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**
The evidence is supported by nine unrelated individuals with overlapping phenotypes and de novo missense variants in a functionally critical domain (PMID: 34050707), with independent replication in a separate cohort identifying a novel cortical malformation phenotype (PMID: 38712318). However, specific variant notations are not available from the abstracts reviewed, the total patient number remains modest, and detailed genotype-phenotype correlations have not yet been fully established in the published literature.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** No dedicated vertebrate model organism studies (mouse, zebrafish, or Drosophila) specifically investigating the neurodevelopmental role of DDX23 were identified in the evidence provided. One invertebrate study examined the C. elegans ortholog DDX-23 in the context of dietary restriction and longevity (PMID: 39578298), demonstrating that DDX-23 functions in the germline to regulate stress responses and lifespan via miR-231 and the FOXO transcription factor DAF-16. While this confirms evolutionary conservation of DDX23 function, the phenotypes studied (longevity, stress resistance) are not directly relevant to neurodevelopment.

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:** No neurological knockout phenotypes in vertebrate model organisms were reported in the available evidence.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The C. elegans data confirm that DDX23 orthologs are functionally conserved across species and regulate RNA-mediated pathways, but do not directly model the human neurodevelopmental phenotype.

**Strength of model organism evidence: NONE**
No model organism data directly supporting a neurodevelopmental role for DDX23 were identified. This represents a significant gap in the current evidence base.

---

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
DDX23 is a multifunctional RNA helicase with well-characterized roles in at least three molecular processes:

1. *Pre-mRNA splicing:* DDX23/PRP28 is an essential component of the U5 snRNP and is required for the pre-B to B spliceosome transition. Its ATPase activity drives displacement of U1 snRNP from the 5' splice site, and its N-terminal RS-like domain mediates direct interaction with the SR protein SRSF1 and is required for spliceosome incorporation (PMID: 38743621). Disruption of this function by missense variants in the RecA-like/DEAD-box domain would be expected to impair global splicing fidelity, consistent with the broad, syndromic neurodevelopmental phenotype observed in patients.

2. *MicroRNA biogenesis:* DDX23 facilitates pri-miRNA to pre-miRNA processing by interacting with the Drosha microprocessor complex, with its helicase activity required for this function (PMID: 26121981). Dysregulation of miRNA biogenesis during neurodevelopment could plausibly contribute to the observed phenotypes.

3. *mRNA processing and alternative splicing:* DDX23 regulates alternative splicing of specific transcripts (e.g., FOXM1C in ovarian cancer, PMID: 34966670; PEDS1 in urothelial carcinoma, PMID: 41360761), indicating a broader role in mRNA maturation beyond core spliceosome function.

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**
Direct evidence for DDX23 expression in the developing human brain is not provided in the available studies. However, the glioma study (PMID: 26121981) demonstrates that DDX23 is expressed in brain tissue and is upregulated in glioma relative to normal brain, confirming CNS expression. The spliceosomal function of DDX23 is expected to be broadly required in highly transcriptionally active cells such as neurons and neural progenitors, where precise splicing regulation is critical for normal cortical development.

**Key experimental findings:**
- DDX23 directly interacts with SRSF1 via its N-terminal RS-like domain, and this interaction is essential for spliceosome assembly (PMID: 38743621). Missense variants in the RecA-like domain could disrupt helicase activity or protein-protein interactions critical for this process.
- In C. elegans, DDX-23 regulates miRNA-mediated suppression of a FOXO transcription factor, demonstrating conservation of its miRNA regulatory function across species (PMID: 39578298).
- DDX23 functions as an IRES trans-acting factor and interacts with viral RNA structures, further illustrating its capacity to engage diverse RNA substrates (PMID: 33255534).

**Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE**
DDX23 has well-established and experimentally validated roles in pre-mRNA splicing and miRNA biogenesis — processes known to be critical for neurodevelopment. The clustering of patient variants within the functionally essential RecA-like/DEAD-box domain provides a plausible mechanistic link between variant location and disrupted helicase/spliceosomal function. However, no studies have directly examined the consequences of patient-specific variants on DDX23 function in neural cells or during brain development, and no neurodevelopment-specific expression data were identified.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths:**
- Nine unrelated individuals with de novo missense variants and an overlapping syndromic neurodevelopmental phenotype were reported in a single peer-reviewed publication (PMID: 34050707), providing the strongest single line of clinical evidence.
- Independent replication from a separate Finnish cohort identifies DDX23 variants in patients with bilateral perisylvian polymicrogyria, a specific cortical malformation, broadening and reinforcing the gene-disease association (PMID: 38712318; ASHG 2023).
- All reported variants are de novo, and their clustering within the functionally critical RecA-like/DEAD-box domain is biologically coherent given DDX23's established role as a spliceosomal helicase (PMID: 38743621).
- The biological function of DDX23 in splicing and miRNA biogenesis provides a plausible mechanistic basis for a neurodevelopmental disorder.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- Specific variant notations (HGVS nomenclature) are not available from the reviewed abstracts, precluding formal assessment of variant pathogenicity classifications or population frequency data.
- No vertebrate model organism data exist to directly support the neurodevelopmental role of DDX23, representing a critical gap.
- No functional studies have been performed on patient-derived variants in neural or neurodevelopmental contexts.
- The total number of reported patients (~10–11) remains modest, and the phenotypic spectrum appears broad (ranging from syndromic NDD with multisystem anomalies to isolated cortical malformation), which may reflect either variable expressivity or the inclusion of phenotypically heterogeneous patients.

**Conclusion:**
DDX23 has moderate overall evidence supporting its role as an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene, driven primarily by the de novo occurrence of missense variants in a functionally critical domain across approximately 10–11 unrelated affected individuals with overlapping neurodevelopmental phenotypes. The biological plausibility is strong given DDX23's essential spliceosomal function, but the evidence base would be substantially strengthened by the publication of full variant-level data, functional validation of patient variants in neural models, and dedicated vertebrate model organism studies. At present, DDX23 should be considered a strong candidate gene for a syndromic neurodevelopmental disorder with cortical malformation features, warranting continued case ascertainment and functional investigation.
