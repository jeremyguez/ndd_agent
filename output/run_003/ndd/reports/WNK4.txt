# Evidence Report: WNK4 as a Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

WNK4 (With-No-Lysine Kinase 4) is a serine-threonine kinase expressed predominantly in the distal convoluted tubule (DCT) of the kidney, where it functions as a central regulator of the thiazide-sensitive NaCl cotransporter (NCC) through the SPAK/OSR1 signaling axis (PMID: 33491560). Its primary established role is in renal electrolyte homeostasis and blood pressure regulation; gain-of-function mutations cause familial hyperkalemic hypertension (FHHt/pseudohypoaldosteronism type II, PHA2), while loss of WNK4 markedly reduces NCC activity.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports:** 1 case series identified.

**Total patients:** Approximately 44 patients from a single large family (PMID: 37666233).

**Most common phenotypes observed:** The reported phenotype is familial hyperkalemic hypertension (FHHt), characterized by hyperkalemia, hypertension, and hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis. Notably, this family also exhibited proximal renal tubular acidosis and, in a subset of members, epileptic seizures and migraine. The epileptic seizures represent a potential neurological feature, though they were present only in a subset of the 44 individuals and occurred in the context of a broader metabolic syndrome rather than as an isolated neurodevelopmental phenotype.

**Predominant variant type:** A single heterozygous missense variant, p.Q565E in WNK4, segregating in an autosomal dominant pattern consistent with PHA2/FHHt.

**Known cohort affiliation:** No affiliation with recognized neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL) was identified.

**Strength of clinical evidence for neurodevelopmental disorder: NONE**

The available clinical evidence does not support WNK4 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene. The single identified study (PMID: 37666233) describes a renal/metabolic phenotype (FHHt) with incidental neurological features (seizures, migraine) in a subset of patients. There are no reports of intellectual disability, developmental delay, autism spectrum disorder, or other core neurodevelopmental phenotypes attributable to WNK4 variants. The seizures observed may be secondary to metabolic derangements (e.g., acidosis, electrolyte imbalance) rather than a primary neurodevelopmental effect of WNK4 dysfunction.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*), including full knockout (WNK4−/−) and conditional knockout models targeting the DCT.

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

All WNK4 knockout and conditional knockout mouse models reported are viable and fertile, with no documented lethality or Mendelian ratio deviations. The predominant phenotypes are renal and metabolic: WNK4−/− mice show markedly reduced NCC abundance, phosphorylation, and functional activity (PMID: 29384416; PMID: 28743496), impaired NCC activation in response to low potassium intake (PMID: 31736353), increased calcium wasting following furosemide treatment with decreased TRPV5 expression (PMID: 31496133), and attenuated LPS-induced macrophage activation and acute lung injury (PMID: 31786261). One conference abstract (ASHG 2017 - Statistical framework for biological interpretation and improvement of genetic association studies) reported that mice with non-functional WNK4 develop histologically confirmed focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS), a glomerular kidney disease.

**Neurological phenotypes:** No neurological, behavioral, or neurodevelopmental phenotypes have been reported in any WNK4 knockout or knockin mouse model across the studies reviewed. There is no evidence of seizures, abnormal brain morphology, cognitive impairment, or other CNS findings in these animals.

**Consistency with human neurodevelopmental phenotype:** The mouse models are not consistent with a neurodevelopmental disorder phenotype, as no such phenotypes have been observed. The models are highly consistent with the established renal/electrolyte role of WNK4.

**Strength of model organism evidence for neurodevelopmental disorder: NONE**

---

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:** WNK4 is a chloride-sensitive serine-threonine kinase that acts as the primary activator of the SPAK/OSR1-NCC signaling cascade in the DCT (PMID: 33394730; PMID: 33491560). It functions as an intracellular chloride sensor: low intracellular chloride (as occurs in hypokalemia) activates WNK4, which phosphorylates SPAK/OSR1, which in turn phosphorylates and activates NCC to increase renal NaCl reabsorption. WNK4 protein abundance is regulated by the CUL3-KLHL3 E3 ubiquitin ligase complex via neddylation and autophagy pathways (PMID: 37481568; PMID: 39205661). WNK4 also serves as a central node integrating vasopressin-cAMP-PKA signaling to cotransporter regulation (PMID: 38096266), and is activated upstream by NRBP1 pseudokinase (PMID: 40668933) and by the calcium-sensing receptor via PKC (PMID: 37530086). Outside the kidney, WNK4 regulates NKCC1 phosphorylation and NF-κB-mediated inflammatory signaling in alveolar macrophages (PMID: 31786261).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:** The available evidence consistently identifies WNK4 as predominantly expressed in the renal DCT. No evidence from the reviewed literature documents significant WNK4 expression in the brain, neurons, or other neurodevelopmental tissues. NKCC1, a related cotransporter regulated by WNK kinases, is known to be expressed in the nervous system, but no direct evidence links WNK4 specifically to neuronal function or brain development in the reviewed studies.

**Key experimental findings:** WNK4 is indispensable for OSR1/SPAK-NCC signaling in the DCT (PMID: 28743496), regulates mitochondrial bioenergetics in renal tubules (PMID: 38370657), and modulates macrophage inflammatory responses (PMID: 31786261). Network-based GWAS analysis identified WNK4 as a candidate gene for FSGS (ASHG 2017). None of these findings implicate WNK4 in neurodevelopmental biology.

**Strength of biological evidence for neurodevelopmental disorder: NONE**

The biological evidence robustly supports WNK4's role in renal physiology and electrolyte homeostasis but provides no mechanistic, expression-based, or pathway-level support for a role in neurodevelopment.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level for WNK4 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths of the evidence:**
- There is strong, multi-layered evidence (clinical, model organism, and biological) establishing WNK4 as a bona fide disease gene for familial hyperkalemic hypertension (FHHt/PHA2), with well-characterized gain-of-function missense variants acting in an autosomal dominant manner.
- The single clinical report (PMID: 37666233) documents epileptic seizures in a subset of a large FHHt family, which could prompt hypothesis generation regarding secondary neurological involvement in the context of metabolic derangement.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- There are no published case reports or series describing WNK4 variants in patients with primary neurodevelopmental disorders (intellectual disability, developmental delay, autism, or epileptic encephalopathy without metabolic context).
- No mouse model has demonstrated neurological or neurodevelopmental phenotypes following WNK4 manipulation.
- No biological evidence supports WNK4 expression or function in the brain or during neurodevelopment.
- The neurological features (seizures, migraine) observed in the single clinical study (PMID: 37666233) are most parsimoniously explained as secondary consequences of metabolic acidosis and electrolyte disturbance rather than primary neurodevelopmental pathology.

**Conclusion:** Based on the totality of available evidence, WNK4 is a well-established renal disease gene for familial hyperkalemic hypertension but cannot be classified as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene at this time. The evidence is insufficient across all three lines of inquiry—clinical, model organism, and biological—to support a primary neurodevelopmental role for heterozygous WNK4 variants. Reclassification would require identification of WNK4 variants in patients with primary neurodevelopmental phenotypes, ideally supported by neurological phenotypes in animal models and evidence of WNK4 expression or function in the developing nervous system.
