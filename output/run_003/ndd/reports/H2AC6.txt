# Evidence Report: H2AC6 as a Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

H2AC6 (Histone H2A type 1-E, also known as HIST1H2AE) encodes a replication-dependent core histone H2A protein that forms part of the nucleosome, the fundamental unit of chromatin packaging. Its primary known function is in chromatin structure, DNA packaging, and the regulation of gene expression through epigenetic mechanisms involving histone modification and nucleosome remodeling.

- **Most common phenotypes observed:** None reported in the context of neurodevelopmental disorders
- **Predominant variant type:** No pathogenic variants identified or reported
- **Known cohort representation (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL):** None identified
- **Strength of clinical evidence: NONE**

No clinical evidence was identified linking heterozygous pathogenic variants in H2AC6 to neurodevelopmental disorders or any other Mendelian disease phenotype. The available literature does not contain case reports, case series, or cohort studies describing patients with germline H2AC6 variants and neurodevelopmental or other clinical phenotypes.

- **Organisms studied:** None identified

- **Consistency with human phenotype:** Cannot be assessed

No model organism studies (mouse, zebrafish, Drosophila, or other) examining the neurodevelopmental or neurological consequences of H2AC6 loss or gain of function were identified in the available evidence base. One study (PMID: 40463856) utilized a rat model of intervertebral disc degeneration in which H2AC6 expression was modulated by baicalin treatment, but this does not constitute a genetic model organism study relevant to neurodevelopmental phenotypes.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

- **Known pathways and molecular function:** H2AC6 encodes a core histone H2A protein involved in nucleosome assembly and chromatin organization. The available literature implicates H2AC6 in several disease-relevant biological contexts, including PANoptosis (integrated pyroptosis, apoptosis, and necroptosis), mitochondrial metabolism, succinylation-related gene networks, and lactylation-related pathways. One study identified H2AC6 as a downstream target of miR-155-5p in hypertensive renal injury in glomerular endothelial cells (PMID: 34926670). Another identified H2AC6 as a core PANoptosis-related gene upregulated in degenerated intervertebral discs, with expression reduced by baicalin treatment in vitro and in vivo (PMID: 40463856). A Mendelian randomization study using GWAS, cis-eQTL, and cis-mQTL data found a causal association between H2AC6 expression and intervertebral disc degeneration risk (p_SMR < 0.05, p_HEIDI > 0.01) (PMID: 40988222). H2AC6 was also identified as an independent prognostic factor in colon adenocarcinoma in a model derived from 524 TCGA-COAD samples (PMID: 42176930), and as a potential biomarker in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma through cross-population transcriptomic analysis (PMID: 37641095). Additionally, H2AC6 was among the top 5 downregulated genes in serum extracellular vesicle RNA profiles during exercise in long COVID patients (q < 0.05; n=14) (PMID: 41588020).

- **Expression pattern (neurodevelopmental relevance):** No studies specifically examining H2AC6 expression in the developing brain, neural tissue, or neurodevelopmental contexts were identified. The available expression data are derived from renal, musculoskeletal, gastrointestinal, and peripheral blood/extracellular vesicle contexts.

- **Key experimental findings:** The biological evidence collectively suggests that H2AC6 is a transcriptionally regulated gene responsive to inflammatory and metabolic stimuli across multiple tissue types. However, none of these findings are directly relevant to neurodevelopmental biology or to the mechanism by which heterozygous loss-of-function or dominant-negative variants might cause a neurodevelopmental disorder.

- **Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED**

While H2AC6 has documented roles in disease-relevant biological processes, none of the available experimental evidence pertains to neurodevelopmental function, brain expression, or mechanisms relevant to autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disease.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

- **Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:** H2AC6 encodes a histone H2A protein, and histone genes as a class have established roles in chromatin regulation and neurodevelopment; several other histone H2A-encoding genes have been implicated in neurodevelopmental disorders (e.g., as part of the "chromatinopathy" spectrum). The gene is biologically plausible as a candidate based on gene family membership alone.

**Key gaps and limitations:** There is a complete absence of clinical evidence linking H2AC6 variants to any human disease, including neurodevelopmental disorders. No model organism genetic studies have been performed. The available biological evidence is restricted to non-neurological disease contexts (renal injury, disc degeneration, cancer, long COVID) and does not address brain expression, neurodevelopmental function, or the consequences of heterozygous loss of function.

**Conclusion:** Based on the currently available evidence, there is insufficient support to classify H2AC6 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with autosomal dominant pathogenic variants. While the gene's membership in the core histone H2A family provides biological plausibility, no clinical cases, model organism data, or neurodevelopmentally relevant experimental findings have been reported. Systematic evaluation in large neurodevelopmental cohorts and functional studies in neural model systems would be required before any disease association could be established.
