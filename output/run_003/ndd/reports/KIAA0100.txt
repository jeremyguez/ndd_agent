# KIAA0100 (BLTP2) — Literature Evidence Report for Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene Assessment

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

KIAA0100 (also known as BLTP2, Bridge-Like Lipid Transfer Protein 2) is a large, evolutionarily conserved protein encoded on chromosome 17q11.2 that functions primarily as a bridge-like lipid transfer protein, mediating bulk lipid transport between the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) and the plasma membrane (PM) at ER-PM contact sites (PMID: 39974967). It has additionally been implicated in cancer cell biology through interactions with microtubules and heat shock proteins, and has been identified as overexpressed in multiple tumor types.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
KIAA0100/BLTP2 functions as a bridge-like lipid transfer protein at ER-PM contact sites, where it mediates non-vesicular bulk lipid transport from the ER to the plasma membrane (PMID: 39974967). It is recruited to these contact sites through interactions with plasma membrane phosphoinositides, the adaptor proteins FAM102A and FAM102B, and N-BAR domain proteins at membrane-connected tubular structures (PMID: 40899996). The protein also tethers the ER to tubular endosomes that become continuous with the PM and to macropinosomes fusing with the PM; loss of BLTP2 results in accumulation of intracellular vacuoles connected to the PM, demonstrating its role in controlling PM dynamics (PMID: 39974967).

In cancer cell biology, KIAA0100 has been characterized as a microtubule-binding protein that interacts with the heat shock protein HSPA1A in MDA-MB-231 breast cancer cells; siRNA-mediated silencing reduced cell aggregation, invasion, and metastasis, and sensitized cells to anoikis (PMID: 29867023). CRISPR/Cas9-mediated knockdown in U937 acute monocytic leukemia cells inhibited proliferation and promoted apoptosis, suggesting an anti-apoptotic role (PMID: 29404013). Bioinformatic analysis predicts the protein contains a signal peptide, transmembrane region, and four FMP27 (fungal mitochondrial protein 27) domains (PMID: 29404013).

**Expression pattern:**
Northern blot analysis in U937 cells identified two distinct mRNA products (6,000–10,000 bp and 5,000–6,000 bp) and two protein products (~254 kDa and <250 kDa), suggesting alternative transcripts or post-translational processing (PMID: 29404037). KIAA0100 is overexpressed in breast carcinoma relative to normal epithelial tissue, with expression progressively increasing from ductal carcinoma in situ to invasive and metastatic breast carcinoma (PMID: 16289875). Recurrent copy-number amplification at 17q11.2 is associated with high KIAA0100 expression in breast tumors (PMID: 37145128). No brain-specific or neurodevelopmentally relevant expression data were identified in the extracted literature.

**Neurodevelopmental relevance:**
No experimental data specifically addressing KIAA0100 expression or function in the developing brain, neurons, or neural progenitor cells were identified. The gene's role in ER-PM lipid transfer is biologically plausible in a neuronal context — lipid homeostasis at the plasma membrane is critical for neuronal function and synaptogenesis — but this connection remains entirely speculative based on available evidence. A regulatory axis involving circ_0000520/miR-512-5p/KIAA0100 has been described in lung cancer (PMID: 35866672), and KIAA0100 was included in a prognostic model for lung squamous cell carcinoma (PMID: 39724502), but neither study has neurodevelopmental relevance.

- **Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED**

While the molecular function of KIAA0100 as a lipid transfer protein at ER-PM contacts is reasonably well characterized in non-neuronal cell types, there is no direct experimental evidence linking this function to neurodevelopment, brain expression, or any neurological phenotype.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

- **Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
- KIAA0100/BLTP2 has a defined and experimentally validated molecular function as a bridge-like lipid transfer protein at ER-PM contact sites, with multiple protein interaction partners identified (PMID: 39974967; PMID: 40899996).
- The gene is expressed and functional in human cell lines, with tools (monoclonal antibodies, CRISPR reagents) available for further study (PMID: 29404013; PMID: 29404037).

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- There are **no clinical reports** of human patients with neurodevelopmental disorders caused by germline KIAA0100 variants, heterozygous or otherwise.
- There are **no model organism studies** examining neurological or developmental phenotypes resulting from KIAA0100 loss or gain of function.
- There are **no brain expression data** or neurodevelopmentally relevant functional studies in the published literature.
- All existing human genetic data involve somatic copy-number amplifications in cancer, not constitutional variants causing Mendelian disease.
- The biological plausibility of a neurodevelopmental role, while not implausible given the gene's function in lipid homeostasis, is entirely inferential and unsupported by direct evidence.

**Conclusion:**
Based on a comprehensive review of available published literature, there is currently **insufficient evidence** to support KIAA0100 as a gene for a heterozygous autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder. The gene has a well-characterized role in ER-to-plasma membrane lipid transfer in non-neuronal contexts, but no clinical cases, model organism phenotypes, or brain-relevant functional data have been reported. Establishing KIAA0100 as a neurodevelopmental disease gene would require, at minimum, the identification of de novo or inherited heterozygous variants in affected individuals, supported by functional validation and/or model organism studies demonstrating neurological phenotypes.
