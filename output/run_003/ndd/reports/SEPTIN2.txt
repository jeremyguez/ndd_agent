# SEPTIN2 (SEPT2) — Literature Evidence Report for Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene Assessment

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

SEPTIN2 (SEPT2) encodes a GTP-binding cytoskeletal protein and founding member of the septin family, which forms hetero-oligomeric filamentous complexes (canonically SEPT2-SEPT6-SEPT7) that serve as scaffolds at membrane interfaces, regulate cell division, cytoskeletal organization, and compartmentalization of cellular domains. In neurons, SEPTIN2 has an emerging role in maintaining the structural and functional integrity of the axon initial segment (AIS), the critical zone for action potential generation and neuronal polarity.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports / case series:** One published case series was identified (PMID: 41408595).

**Total approximate number of patients:** Seven (7) unrelated individuals carrying heterozygous variants in SEPTIN2.

**Most common phenotypes observed:** All seven patients presented with cognitive impairment as the core clinical feature. Cellular and neuronal phenotypes associated with patient-derived variants included loss of Ankyrin G at the axon initial segment, aberrant MAP2 localization (a marker of somatodendritic compartment), axonal shortening, and electrophysiological hypoexcitability — collectively indicating disrupted neuronal polarity and reduced intrinsic excitability (PMID: 41408595).

**Predominant variant type:** Heterozygous missense variants. Specific nucleotide/amino acid notations were not available in the extracted abstract, but the mechanistic framing indicates these are dominant-negative alleles that preclude SEPTIN2 homodimerization and ablate the SEPTIN2–Ankyrin G interaction.

**Cohort affiliation:** The cohort of origin (e.g., UDN, DDD, GEL, GREGoR) was not specified in the available data.

**Mechanism:** A dominant-negative mechanism is predicted. Expression of mutant SEPTIN2 constructs in neurons recapitulated AIS defects, providing functional validation beyond simple variant identification (PMID: 41408595).

**Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED**
*Rationale:* A single publication reporting seven patients constitutes preliminary but not yet replicated clinical evidence. The functional neuronal validation substantially strengthens the case, but independent replication in additional cohorts and detailed variant-level data are needed to elevate this rating.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*) — two independent studies using knockdown approaches.

**Key phenotypes:**

- *Neural tube and ciliogenesis:* Knockdown of Septin2 in mouse embryos caused neural tube defects and impaired ciliogenesis, with failure to form the SEPT2-6-7 complex and disrupted GTP-binding activity. This phenotype was embryonic lethal in the knockdown context, underscoring the developmental essentiality of SEPT2 (PMID: 28432198). Notably, this study also linked folate-dependent methylation of SEPT2 to neural tube closure, providing a mechanistic bridge to a well-established human neurodevelopmental risk factor.

- *Spermatogenesis / fertility:* A separate mouse knockdown study demonstrated that loss of Sept2 in spermatogonia caused G1-S phase arrest, reduced sperm quality and motility, and impaired fertility, phenocopying hypoxia-induced reproductive defects. Animals were viable but infertile (PMID: 40487848). While this phenotype is not directly relevant to cognitive neurodevelopment, it confirms that SEPT2 loss-of-function produces significant developmental consequences in vivo.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The neural tube defect and ciliogenesis phenotype in mice (PMID: 28432198) is broadly consistent with a neurodevelopmental role, though the specific human phenotype (cognitive impairment with AIS dysfunction) has not been directly modeled in a dedicated neuronal knockout. No published constitutive neuronal knockout or conditional brain-specific knockout data were identified in the available evidence set.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**
*Rationale:* Knockdown (rather than true knockout) studies demonstrate clear developmental and neurological relevance (embryonic lethality with neural tube defects), but a dedicated neuronal or brain-specific conditional model directly recapitulating the human cognitive phenotype has not yet been reported.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
SEPTIN2 participates in multiple well-characterized molecular pathways:

- *Axon initial segment maintenance:* SEPTIN2 interacts with Ankyrin G at the AIS; disruption of this interaction by patient-derived missense variants leads to AIS disassembly and neuronal hypoexcitability (PMID: 41408595).
- *Cytoskeletal scaffolding:* SEPT2 co-localizes with and stabilizes actin bundles in mammalian cells; RNAi depletion accelerates actin turnover, demonstrating a functional interdependence between septin and actin networks (PMID: 15541171).
- *Ciliogenesis and GTP-dependent complex assembly:* Methylation of SEPT2 regulates its GTP-binding activity and formation of the SEPT2-6-7 heterotrimer, which is required for ciliogenesis and neural tube closure (PMID: 28432198).
- *Cell morphology and Rho GTPase signaling:* SEPT2 interacts with ARHGAP25 to modulate Rac1 activity and lamellipodia formation (PMID: 40205813). Separately, S-nitrosylation of SEPT2 at Cys111 disrupts its interaction with TIAM1, activating the TIAM1-RAC1-NF-κB axis in macrophages and promoting vascular inflammation (PMID: 38357802).
- *ER stress and immune regulation:* SEPT2 acts as a negative regulator of IFN-γ-independent macrophage autoactivation by balancing acetylation versus ubiquitination of HSP5 (BiP/GRP78), constraining unfolded protein response-driven inflammation (PMID: 37978190).
- *Cell cycle and meiosis:* SEPT2 is SUMOylated in mouse oocytes and is required for chromosome congression and the metaphase I/anaphase I transition; its disruption activates the spindle assembly checkpoint (PMID: 20372094).
- *Oncology context:* SEPT2 is overexpressed in glioblastoma, and its knockdown (with SEPT9) suppresses GBM proliferation via p53/p21 and MEK/ERK pathways (PMID: 29724999).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**
A GWAS-based transcriptomic association study identified SEPTIN2 predicted expression as significantly associated with addiction-related traits (family alcoholism, cannabis craving, alcohol/nicotine/cannabis dependence) aggregated across 10 brain tissues (PMID: 36407771), indicating that SEPTIN2 is expressed across multiple brain regions and that its expression level is functionally relevant to brain phenotypes. This provides indirect but supportive evidence for brain-enriched expression relevant to neurodevelopment.

**Key experimental findings:**
The most directly relevant biological finding is the demonstration that patient-derived SEPTIN2 missense variants, when expressed in neurons, disrupt the SEPTIN2–Ankyrin G interaction at the AIS, causing loss of AIS integrity, aberrant MAP2 localization, axonal shortening, and electrophysiological hypoexcitability — a coherent cellular mechanism linking variant to phenotype (PMID: 41408595). The broader literature establishes SEPT2 as a multifunctional scaffold with roles in cytoskeletal organization, GTPase regulation, and cell cycle control across diverse cell types.

**Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE**
*Rationale:* There is strong mechanistic plausibility for a neurodevelopmental role, with direct experimental evidence linking SEPT2 to AIS integrity in neurons. However, much of the broader biological literature pertains to non-neuronal contexts (macrophages, endothelial cells, oocytes, cancer cells), and dedicated characterization of SEPT2's neuronal biology remains limited to the single clinical study.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: LIMITED (trending toward MODERATE)**

**Key strengths:**
1. A single well-characterized case series of seven patients with heterozygous SEPTIN2 missense variants and a consistent phenotype of cognitive impairment, supported by compelling neuronal functional validation demonstrating AIS disruption via a dominant-negative mechanism (PMID: 41408595).
2. Convergent biological evidence from multiple independent lines — cytoskeletal scaffolding, AIS maintenance, ciliogenesis, and GTPase regulation — all pointing to SEPT2 as a critical regulator of neuronal structure and function.
3. Mouse knockdown data demonstrating embryonic lethality with neural tube defects, consistent with a neurodevelopmental role (PMID: 28432198), and brain-tissue expression associations with neuropsychiatric traits (PMID: 36407771).

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. Clinical evidence rests on a single publication with seven patients; independent replication in separate cohorts (e.g., DDD, UDN, GEL) has not yet been reported, and specific variant-level data (HGVS notation, segregation, de novo status) were not available in the extracted abstract.
2. No dedicated neuronal or brain-specific conditional knockout mouse model has been published that directly recapitulates the human cognitive phenotype; existing animal data rely on knockdown approaches in non-neuronal developmental contexts.
3. The cohort of origin is unspecified, limiting assessment of ascertainment bias, and the mechanism (dominant-negative vs. haploinsufficiency) requires further experimental delineation.

**Conclusion:**
SEPTIN2 is a biologically plausible and mechanistically coherent candidate gene for autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder, with its role in maintaining axon initial segment integrity providing a direct cellular explanation for cognitive impairment in affected individuals. The current evidence base — anchored by a single case series of seven patients with functional neuronal validation — is promising but requires independent clinical replication and dedicated in vivo neuronal modeling to achieve a definitive gene-disease classification. Prioritization for inclusion in neurodevelopmental gene panels and prospective case collection through international consortia is warranted.
