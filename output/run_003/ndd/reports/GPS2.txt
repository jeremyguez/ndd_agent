# Literature Evidence Report: GPS2 as a Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

GPS2 (G-Protein Pathway Suppressor 2) encodes a multifunctional transcriptional coregulator that serves as a core subunit of the NCoR/SMRT-HDAC3 corepressor complex and functions as an inhibitor of non-proteolytic K63-linked ubiquitination mediated by the E2 enzyme Ubc13. Beyond transcriptional corepression, GPS2 regulates protein stability, nuclear-mitochondrial communication, and diverse signaling pathways including PI3K/AKT, NF-κB, PPARα, and HIF-1α across multiple tissue types.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

- **Number of independent case reports/series:** 0. No clinical case reports or case series were identified in the evidence provided.
- **Total approximate number of patients:** 0 patients with GPS2-associated neurodevelopmental disorder have been reported in the structured evidence.
- **Most common phenotypes observed:** None documented in the clinical evidence base.
- **Predominant variant type:** Not applicable; no pathogenic variants in human patients have been reported.
- **Known cohort representation (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL):** None identified.
- **Strength of clinical evidence: NONE**

There is a complete absence of published clinical evidence linking heterozygous GPS2 variants to a neurodevelopmental disorder phenotype in humans. No case reports, cohort studies, or variant-level data were retrieved.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** All published model organism studies identified were conducted in mouse (*Mus musculus*), using tissue-specific conditional knockout strategies targeting hepatocytes, adipocytes, macrophages, B cells, and endothelial cells, as well as full knockout and xenograft models.

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

- *Metabolic/hepatic:* Hepatocyte-specific GPS2 loss alleviated diet-induced hepatic steatosis and reduced liver fibrosis via PPARα activation (PMID: 30975991). Adipocyte-specific loss caused adipocyte hypertrophy, white adipose tissue inflammation, mitochondrial dysfunction, and a pro-diabetic metabolic state via HIF1A activation (PMID: 30208320), and further disrupted the adipose secretome to promote fibrosis and insulin resistance (PMID: 36731652). Adipocyte GPS2 loss also impaired pancreatic beta cell insulin secretion and caused glucose intolerance through altered adipokine signaling (PMID: 32937117).
- *Immune:* Macrophage-specific loss sensitized cells to pro-inflammatory metabolic stress signals (PMID: 27270589). B cell-specific loss caused developmental defects at multiple stages of B cell differentiation with dysregulated TLR/BCR and AKT/FOXO1 signaling (PMID: 28039360).
- *Vascular/developmental:* Endothelial cell-specific GPS2 knockout (Gps2ECKO) resulted in **embryonic lethality at E16.5** due to defective vascularization and aberrant TNFR1-mediated endothelial cell death; lethality was rescued by concurrent TNFR1 deletion (PMID: 41507360). This is the only study demonstrating developmental lethality, and it is restricted to the endothelial compartment.
- *Oncology:* Full knockout and knockdown models demonstrated increased tumor burden and enhanced PI3K/AKT signaling in breast cancer and uveal melanoma xenograft models (PMID: 33490071; PMID: 35941228).
- *Hematopoiesis:* Global GPS2 deletion in mice caused impaired fetal liver erythropoiesis and severe anemia (PMID: 32384137).

**Neurological phenotypes:** No neurological, neurodevelopmental, or behavioral phenotypes were reported in any of the mouse knockout studies identified. No brain-specific conditional knockouts have been described in the available evidence.

**Mendelian ratio deviations:** Not reported in any study.

**Consistency with human neurodevelopmental phenotype:** The available model organism data do not support a neurodevelopmental phenotype. The embryonic lethality observed in the endothelial-specific knockout (PMID: 41507360) is tissue-restricted and mechanistically distinct from a heterozygous neurodevelopmental disorder model.

**Strength of model organism evidence (for neurodevelopmental relevance): NONE**

While the model organism evidence is substantial for metabolic, immune, and vascular biology, it provides no support for a neurodevelopmental disorder association.

---

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

GPS2 operates through several distinct molecular mechanisms:
1. As a core subunit of the NCoR/SMRT-HDAC3 corepressor complex, it regulates enhancer and gene activation in macrophages (PMID: 36610795) and mediates interactions between ANKRD26 and ETV6 relevant to thrombocytopenia (PMID: 39791724).
2. As an inhibitor of Ubc13-mediated K63 ubiquitination, GPS2 regulates mitochondria-associated translation and nuclear-mitochondrial communication (PMID: 39094987), B cell signaling (PMID: 28039360), and NF-κB pathway activity via HOIP stabilization (PMID: 41507360).
3. As a regulator of protein stability, GPS2 prevents proteasomal degradation of EKLF/KLF1 (PMID: 32384137), HIF-1α via RACK1 (PMID: 38942092), ATF4 via BTRC (PMID: 40693356), and HOIP via K48-ubiquitination inhibition (PMID: 41507360).
4. GPS2 undergoes liquid-liquid phase separation via its coiled-coil domain to modulate LATS1/YAP/SREBP1 signaling in colorectal cancer (PMID: 40715488).
5. GPS2::GRHL1/2/3 gene fusions have been identified as recurrent oncogenic drivers in trichogerminoma (PMID: 35049062).

**Expression pattern (brain/neurodevelopmental relevance):** No study in the provided evidence base examined GPS2 expression in the brain, central nervous system, or during neurodevelopment. There is no reported evidence of brain-enriched expression or neurodevelopmental transcriptional programs regulated by GPS2.

**Key experimental findings relevant to disease mechanisms:** The most developmentally relevant finding is the embryonic lethality of endothelial-specific GPS2 knockout due to vascular defects (PMID: 41507360), and the severe fetal anemia in global knockout mice (PMID: 32384137). These suggest GPS2 has essential roles in embryonic development, but in non-neuronal contexts.

**Strength of biological evidence (for neurodevelopmental relevance): NONE**

The biological evidence is extensive for metabolic, immune, hematopoietic, and oncological functions, but contains no data supporting a role in neuronal development, synaptic function, or brain-relevant transcriptional programs.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths of the available evidence:**
- GPS2 has well-characterized and diverse molecular functions as a transcriptional coregulator and ubiquitination inhibitor, demonstrating broad biological importance.
- Conditional knockout mouse models across multiple tissues confirm essential, non-redundant roles in development and homeostasis, including embryonic lethality in the endothelial-specific model (PMID: 41507360).
- The gene's role in regulating protein stability and nuclear-mitochondrial communication (PMID: 39094987) represents mechanisms that, in principle, could be relevant to neuronal biology.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- There are **zero published clinical cases** of GPS2-associated neurodevelopmental disorder in humans, representing a fundamental evidentiary gap.
- No model organism study has examined neurological or neurodevelopmental phenotypes following GPS2 loss, and no brain-specific conditional knockout has been reported.
- No expression data supporting brain-enriched or neurodevelopmentally relevant GPS2 activity was identified in the provided evidence.
- No heterozygous (haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative) models have been described, making inference about autosomal dominant pathogenicity speculative.

**Conclusion:** Based on the totality of available evidence, there is currently **insufficient support** to classify GPS2 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous autosomal dominant pathogenic variants. While GPS2 is a biologically important and multifunctional gene with demonstrated roles in embryonic development, metabolism, and immunity, the complete absence of human clinical cases, neurological phenotypes in animal models, and neurodevelopmental expression data means that any such association remains entirely unestablished. Future investigation should prioritize brain-specific conditional knockout studies, examination of GPS2 expression in neural tissues, and systematic variant analysis in neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts.
