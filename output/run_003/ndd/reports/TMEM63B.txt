# TMEM63B Evidence Report: Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene (Autosomal Dominant)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

TMEM63B encodes a transmembrane protein that functions as a mechanosensitive and osmosensitive cation channel, forming monomeric structures with a highly restricted pore capable of responding to membrane stretch, osmotic stress, and changes in membrane curvature (PMID: 37543036). Beyond ion channel activity, TMEM63B has been shown to act as a phospholipid scramblase and participates in diverse physiological processes including osmosensation, hearing, surfactant secretion, and neuronal migration (PMID: 39424995; PMID: 38127458).

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent case reports/series:** Three independent lines of clinical evidence have been reported. The primary and most informative study is a multi-center international case series (PMID: 37421948). A second source is a multi-consortia genomic screen presented at ASHG 2019 (ASHG 2019 – PgmNr 521, Cacheiro et al.). A third is an Italian prospective pediatric WES study (PMID: 38041506), which identified a single uncharacterized TMEM63B variant of uncertain significance.

**Total approximate number of patients:** At least 17 unrelated individuals with confirmed pathogenic TMEM63B variants are described in detail (PMID: 37421948), with additional unspecified cases from the ASHG 2019 consortia screen. The Italian study (PMID: 38041506) contributes one additional candidate case.

**Most common phenotypes observed:** Across the 17 individuals in the primary series (PMID: 37421948), the core phenotype comprises:
- Severe early-onset developmental and epileptic encephalopathy (DEE)
- Profound intellectual disability
- Severe motor impairment
- Cortical visual impairment
- Progressive neurodegenerative brain changes on neuroimaging
- Hematological abnormalities including macrocytosis, hemolysis, and in some cases requirement for blood transfusions

This combination of neurological and hematological features appears to be a distinctive clinicopathological signature of TMEM63B-related disease.

**Predominant variant type:** All confirmed pathogenic variants are heterozygous missense or in-frame variants; no protein-truncating (loss-of-function) variants have been reported as causative. The most recurrent variant is p.Val44Met, identified in 7 of 17 individuals. Other confirmed variants include p.Arg433His, p.Thr481Asn, p.Gly580Ser, p.Arg660Thr, and p.Phe697Leu, all affecting conserved residues in or near transmembrane domains. The variant spectrum and functional data are consistent with a dominant gain-of-function mechanism rather than haploinsufficiency.

**Cohort origin:** Patients were ascertained from multiple international centers (PMID: 37421948). The ASHG 2019 report (Cacheiro et al.) drew cases from Genomics England (GEL) and other independent disease sequencing consortia including the Deciphering Developmental Disorders (DDD) study. No cases from UDN, GREGoR, or RUMC are specifically documented in the available evidence.

**De novo status:** De novo occurrence was confirmed in 16 of 17 individuals for whom parental DNA was available (PMID: 37421948), providing strong genetic evidence for pathogenicity.

**Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG**
The combination of 17 unrelated individuals with de novo heterozygous variants, a consistent and recognizable phenotype, a recurrent variant (p.Val44Met in 7/17), and supporting functional data constitutes strong clinical evidence.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** All model organism studies have been conducted in mouse (*Mus musculus*), with additional in vivo evidence from Drosophila melanogaster (ectopic expression experiments reported in PMID: 37421948).

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

- *Full knockout (IMPC viability screen):* TMEM63B full knockout mice were classified as developmentally lethal with sub-Mendelian survival, indicating an essential role during embryogenesis (ASHG 2019 – Cacheiro et al.). This is notable given that heterozygous human patients survive, suggesting the human disease mechanism is gain-of-function rather than simple haploinsufficiency.
- *Full knockout – hearing:* Tmem63b knockout mice develop progressive hearing loss due to necroptosis of outer hair cells, impaired hypo-osmolarity-induced Ca²⁺ influx, and failure of regulatory volume decrease (PMID: 32375046).
- *Full knockout – thirst/osmosensation:* Tmem63b knockout mice exhibit profound deficits in thirst behavior and impaired neuronal responses to hypertonic stimulation in subfornical organ neurons, with SFO-specific conditional deletion recapitulating the full knockout phenotype (PMID: 40107268).
- *Conditional knockout – intestinal:* Intestinal stem cell-specific deletion caused decelerated motility, impaired digestion, reduced stem cell proliferation, and exacerbated colitis (PMID: 39513891).
- *Conditional knockout – urinary tract:* Urothelial- and sensory neuron-specific deletion produced no voiding phenotype, even under chemical stress (PMID: 41348674).
- *Drosophila:* Ectopic expression of disease-associated variants p.Val44Met and p.Gly580Cys caused early death in flies, providing in vivo evidence of dominant toxicity (PMID: 37421948).

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The embryonic lethality of full knockout mice is not directly recapitulated in heterozygous human patients, which is consistent with a dominant gain-of-function disease mechanism in humans rather than loss-of-function. The neurological and movement phenotypes partially modeled in IMPC knockout mice align with the intellectual disability and motor impairment seen in patients (ASHG 2019 – Cacheiro et al.). The Drosophila lethality data directly support the pathogenicity of specific human disease variants. The hearing loss phenotype in knockout mice has not been prominently reported as a feature of the human syndrome, suggesting tissue-specific differences in channel function.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**
The embryonic lethality of full knockouts, partial neurological modeling, and Drosophila gain-of-function lethality provide meaningful support, but the mechanistic disconnect between knockout lethality and dominant human disease limits direct translational inference. No knock-in mouse models carrying human disease variants have been reported.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
TMEM63B is a mechanosensitive and osmosensitive cation channel with several well-characterized molecular functions:

1. *Ion channel activity:* Cryo-EM structural studies established that TMEM63B functions as a monomeric high-threshold mechanosensitive ion channel with small conductance, distinct from the homodimeric architecture of its plant OSCA homologs (PMID: 37543036). Brain-specific alternative splicing of exon 4 generates a short isoform with enhanced surface expression and stronger osmosensitive responses, regulated by an RXR-type ER retention signal in the long isoform (PMID: 36496074).

2. *Phospholipid scramblase activity:* TMEM63B functions as a membrane structure-responsive lipid scramblase at the plasma membrane and lysosomes, translocating phospholipids bidirectionally in response to membrane curvature and thickness changes. The disease-associated p.Val44Met substitution confers constitutive scramblase activity, disrupting plasma membrane phospholipid asymmetry (PMID: 39424995). TMEM63B also forms a heterodimer with the thiamine transporter SLC19A2, and disease-related mutants induce constitutive phospholipid scrambling (PMID: 39217145).

3. *Neuronal migration:* TMEM63B mediates mechanosensory Ca²⁺ influx in response to plasma membrane tension during neuronal migration, with its blockade inhibiting somal translocation in three-dimensional environments (PMID: 39707024). This finding is directly relevant to the neurodevelopmental phenotype in patients.

4. *Gain-of-function disease mechanism:* In transfected Neuro2a cells, six disease-associated variants produced inward leak cation currents under isotonic conditions and impaired responses to hypo-osmotic challenge, as well as disrupted Ca²⁺ transients, consistent with constitutive channel activation (PMID: 37421948).

**Expression pattern:**
TMEM63B is expressed in the brain, with a brain-specific short isoform generated by alternative splicing (PMID: 36496074). In the kidney and urinary tract, TMEM63B protein is detected in proximal tubule epithelial cells, intercalated cells of the collecting duct, thick ascending limb epithelial cells, and throughout the urothelium (PMID: 38724885). Expression has also been documented in outer hair cells of the cochlea (PMID: 32375046), subfornical organ neurons (PMID: 40107268), intestinal stem cells (PMID: 39513891), and alveolar type 2 epithelial cells (PMID: 38127458). The existence of a brain-enriched isoform with enhanced surface expression is particularly relevant to the neurological phenotype.

**Key experimental findings supporting pathogenicity:**
- Disease variants produce constitutive inward cation leak currents in neuronal cell lines (PMID: 37421948)
- p.Val44Met confers constitutive phospholipid scramblase activity, disrupting membrane asymmetry (PMID: 39424995)
- TMEM63B activates Ca²⁺ influx during neuronal migration; its disruption impairs this process (PMID: 39707024)
- Ectopic expression of disease variants in Drosophila causes early lethality (PMID: 37421948)
- The SLC19A2 interaction and constitutive scrambling by disease mutants may explain the hematological phenotype (PMID: 39217145), as SLC19A2 is a thiamine transporter expressed in erythrocytes

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent experimental approaches—structural biology, electrophysiology, lipid biochemistry, cell biology, and in vivo functional studies—converge on a coherent gain-of-function mechanism. The brain-specific isoform, the role of TMEM63B in neuronal migration, and the constitutive channel/scramblase activity of disease variants provide a mechanistically plausible explanation for the human phenotype.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths of the evidence:**
- A well-powered primary case series of 17 unrelated individuals with de novo heterozygous missense variants, a consistent and recognizable phenotype (DEE + progressive neurodegeneration + hematological abnormalities), and a recurrent hotspot variant (p.Val44Met in 7/17) provides robust clinical evidence (PMID: 37421948).
- Functional studies directly demonstrate that disease-associated variants produce constitutive gain-of-function effects—inward cation leak currents in neuronal cells, constitutive phospholipid scramblase activity, and in vivo lethality in Drosophila—providing a mechanistically coherent explanation for dominant pathogenicity (PMID: 37421948; PMID: 39424995).
- Convergent biological evidence from structural biology, neuronal migration studies, and protein interaction screens supports the plausibility of TMEM63B dysfunction causing neurodevelopmental disease (PMID: 37543036; PMID: 39707024; PMID: 39217145).

**Key gaps or limitations:**
- No knock-in mouse models carrying human disease variants have been reported; existing knockout mouse models do not directly model the dominant gain-of-function human disease mechanism.
- The hematological phenotype (macrocytosis, hemolysis) is unusual for a channelopathy and its mechanistic basis, while partially addressed by the SLC19A2 interaction data (PMID: 39217145), requires further investigation.
- The Italian WES study (PMID: 38041506) identified only a single variant of uncertain significance without functional validation, contributing minimally to the overall evidence base.
- Long-term natural history data and genotype-phenotype correlations across the full variant spectrum remain incompletely characterized.

**Conclusion:**
TMEM63B is a well-supported neurodevelopmental disorder gene in which heterozygous de novo missense variants cause a clinically distinctive syndrome of severe early-onset developmental and epileptic encephalopathy with progressive neurodegeneration and hematological abnormalities, consistent with a dominant gain-of-function mechanism. The convergence of a large de novo case series, direct functional evidence of constitutive channel and scramblase activity, and biologically plausible roles for TMEM63B in neuronal migration and brain-specific isoform expression collectively justify classification of TMEM63B as a definitive autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene. Further work to develop knock-in animal models and to elucidate the basis of the hematological phenotype would strengthen the mechanistic understanding of this condition.
