# ABI2 as a Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene: Literature Evidence Report

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

ABI2 (Abl Interactor 2) encodes a scaffold/adaptor protein that is a core component of the WAVE Regulatory Complex (WRC), where it plays a critical role in regulating actin cytoskeleton dynamics, cell migration, and dendritic spine morphogenesis through interactions with Abl tyrosine kinases and downstream effectors including RAC1 and the WAVE/SCAR actin-nucleation promoting factors. In the context of human neurodevelopmental disease, heterozygous missense variants in ABI2 have recently been implicated in a syndromic neurodevelopmental disorder; the gene is distinct from the plant protein ABI2 (ABA INSENSITIVE 2), which is an unrelated Arabidopsis phosphatase sharing only the gene symbol.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent case reports/series:** Two independent publications have reported ABI2 variants in patients with neurodevelopmental phenotypes.

**Total approximate number of patients:** Approximately 8–9 patients across both studies, though precise numbers are not fully specified in the second report.

**Most common phenotypes observed:** The primary clinical series (PMID: 40475134) described 8 patients with a consistent and recognizable phenotypic constellation including: severe neurodevelopmental delay, moderate-to-severe motor delay, absent or significantly delayed expressive language, intellectual disability, seizures/epilepsy, autistic traits, macrocephaly, thinning or hypoplasia of the corpus callosum, and white matter signal abnormalities on neuroimaging. A second, earlier study (PMID: 28397838) identified an ABI2 loss-of-function variant in consanguineous families presenting with non-syndromic intellectual disability, suggesting that biallelic loss of ABI2 may also contribute to a recessive form of intellectual disability.

**Predominant variant type:** The dominant neurodevelopmental disorder is associated with heterozygous missense variants, most notably the recurrent de novo variant p.Tyr491Cys, which was identified in six unrelated individuals and maps to the highly conserved SH3 domain of ABI2 (PMID: 40475134). Additional heterozygous missense variants were identified in the remaining patients. The recessive intellectual disability report (PMID: 28397838) described a loss-of-function variant in a homozygous state in consanguineous families, representing a potentially distinct genetic mechanism.

**Cohort affiliation:** The patients in the primary series were identified through international collaboration across multiple centers using exome sequencing and data-sharing platforms; they are not explicitly attributed to named cohorts such as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL, though the international collaborative design is consistent with approaches used by such programs.

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**
The recurrence of the identical de novo missense variant (p.Tyr491Cys) in six unrelated individuals, combined with de novo occurrence confirmed by parental testing, a consistent and recognizable phenotype, and location in a functionally critical protein domain, provides meaningful clinical evidence. However, the total patient number remains small (n=8), the study represents a single primary publication, and detailed variant-level data for all patients beyond p.Tyr491Cys are not fully specified in the available evidence. The recessive report (PMID: 28397838) provides supporting but limited additional evidence, as ABI2 was listed among 26 candidate genes without deep phenotypic characterization.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*) and *Xenopus laevis* have been studied in the context of ABI2/ortholog function.

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

In mice, homozygous full knockout of *Abi2* (PMID: 15572692) produced viable animals with a range of neurologically relevant phenotypes: defective cell migration in the neocortex and hippocampus, abnormal dendritic spine morphology and density, and severe deficits in both short-term and long-term memory. Additional findings included defective secondary lens fiber orientation and migration, impaired adherens junction formation, and downregulation of the WAVE actin-nucleation promoting factor. A separate genome-wide knockout mouse database analysis (PMID: 36456625) identified ABI2 as a candidate ciliopathy gene based on ocular abnormalities, renal or reproductive trait abnormalities, and protein interactions with known ciliopathy proteins, though this finding is of uncertain relevance to the human neurodevelopmental phenotype.

In *Xenopus laevis*, overexpression of THRP (the ABI2 ortholog) halted growth during early neurulation in most animals, with few survivors developing into grossly malformed tadpoles, indicating that dysregulation of ABI2 ortholog expression is not compatible with normal early embryogenesis (PMID: 21095221).

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The mouse knockout data are highly consistent with the human neurodevelopmental phenotype. Defective neuronal migration in the neocortex and hippocampus, abnormal dendritic spine morphogenesis, and severe memory deficits in *Abi2*-null mice directly parallel the intellectual disability, structural brain abnormalities (corpus callosum hypoplasia, white matter changes), and neurodevelopmental delay observed in human patients. The mechanistic link—loss of WAVE complex activity leading to impaired actin dynamics—provides a plausible biological explanation for both the animal and human phenotypes.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**
The mouse knockout study (PMID: 15572692) provides directly relevant neurological phenotypes with strong face validity for the human disorder. However, the available data reflect homozygous null models rather than heterozygous or dominant-negative models that would more precisely mirror the proposed autosomal dominant mechanism in humans. The *Xenopus* data support developmental essentiality but are based on overexpression rather than loss-of-function. No heterozygous mouse data are reported in the available evidence.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:** ABI2 (Abl Interactor 2) functions as a scaffold protein within the WAVE Regulatory Complex (WRC), which is a key activator of the ARP2/3 complex responsible for branched actin filament nucleation. ABI2 mediates interactions between Abl kinases, RAC1 GTPase, and WAVE proteins, thereby transducing extracellular signals into cytoskeletal remodeling. In cancer cell biology, ABI2 has been shown to be phosphorylated by PIM1 kinase at Ser183, enhancing WRC formation and promoting cell protrusion and motility (PMID: 37042842). ABI2 also interacts with RAC1 and suppresses PI3K/Akt signaling in breast cancer cells, functioning as a tumor suppressor (PMID: 38937761). In nasopharyngeal carcinoma, ABI2 suppresses epithelial-mesenchymal transition, and its downregulation by EBV-miR-BART13-3p promotes tumor invasion via c-JUN/SLUG signaling (PMID: 31907338).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:** The mouse knockout study (PMID: 15572692) demonstrated that ABI2 localizes to adherens junctions and dendritic spines in the brain, and its loss leads to downregulation of WAVE actin-nucleation promoting factors, impaired dendritic spine morphogenesis, and defective neuronal migration in the neocortex and hippocampus. These findings strongly implicate ABI2 in neurodevelopmental processes including cortical development and synaptic connectivity. A GWAS-level observation in the Old Order Amish identified intronic ABI2 SNPs contributing to diastolic blood pressure variation (ASHG 2010 - Montasser et al.), though this finding is of uncertain relevance to the neurodevelopmental phenotype.

**Key experimental findings relevant to neurodevelopmental disease:** The SH3 domain of ABI2, in which the recurrent pathogenic variant p.Tyr491Cys resides, is critical for protein-protein interactions within the WRC. Disruption of this domain would be predicted to impair WRC assembly and downstream actin dynamics, consistent with the observed neuronal migration and dendritic spine defects in mouse knockouts. The cell culture data demonstrating ABI2's role in regulating RAC1-dependent actin dynamics and cell motility (PMID: 37042842; PMID: 38937761) further support the mechanistic plausibility of SH3 domain missense variants causing dominant-negative or gain-of-function disruption of WRC activity.

*Note: Several biological evidence entries in the source data refer to the plant protein ABI2 (ABA INSENSITIVE 2) in Arabidopsis (PMIDs: 19407143, 11208021, 38280208, 35508659, 40811817), which is an entirely unrelated protein sharing only the gene symbol. These findings are not relevant to the human ABI2 gene and have been excluded from this summary.*

**Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE**
The mechanistic framework linking ABI2 to actin cytoskeletal dynamics, dendritic spine morphogenesis, and neuronal migration is well-supported by experimental data in mammalian systems. The localization of the recurrent pathogenic variant to the functionally critical SH3 domain is biologically coherent. However, direct functional characterization of the specific disease-associated variants (e.g., p.Tyr491Cys) in relevant neuronal cell models or patient-derived cells has not been reported in the available evidence, representing a gap in the mechanistic validation.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths:**
- The recurrence of the identical de novo missense variant p.Tyr491Cys in six unrelated individuals, confirmed as de novo where parental testing was available, is a particularly compelling observation that substantially reduces the probability of coincidental association (PMID: 40475134).
- The mouse knockout model (PMID: 15572692) demonstrates neurological phenotypes—defective cortical and hippocampal neuronal migration, abnormal dendritic spine morphology, and severe memory deficits—that are directly consistent with the human clinical presentation of intellectual disability, structural brain abnormalities, and neurodevelopmental delay.
- The biological mechanism is coherent: ABI2's role as a WRC scaffold protein regulating actin dynamics provides a plausible molecular explanation for both the neuronal migration defects and the synaptic/cognitive phenotypes observed in humans and mice.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- The total number of reported patients remains small (n=8 in the primary series), and the evidence base rests substantially on a single primary publication (PMID: 40475134).
- No functional studies directly characterizing the effect of the disease-associated missense variants (particularly p.Tyr491Cys) on ABI2 protein function, WRC assembly, or neuronal biology have been reported in the available evidence.
- The available animal models are homozygous null knockouts; heterozygous mouse models that would more accurately reflect the proposed autosomal dominant mechanism in humans have not been described, leaving uncertainty about whether haploinsufficiency or a dominant-negative mechanism underlies the human disorder.
- The relationship between the dominant missense-associated syndrome and the recessive loss-of-function intellectual disability reported in consanguineous families (PMID: 28397838) remains unresolved.

**Conclusion:** ABI2 is an emerging candidate gene for an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by intellectual disability, epilepsy, corpus callosum hypoplasia, and white matter abnormalities, supported primarily by the recurrence of the de novo p.Tyr491Cys variant in six unrelated individuals and a consistent clinical phenotype across eight patients (PMID: 40475134). The mouse knockout model provides strong biological plausibility through directly relevant neurological phenotypes, and the mechanistic role of ABI2 in WRC-mediated actin dynamics and dendritic spine morphogenesis is well-established. Functional validation of the specific disease-associated variants and expansion of the patient cohort through additional case identification and data sharing will be essential to elevate the evidence to a STRONG classification.

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. Missense ABI2 variants linked to a neurodevelopmental disorder with intellectual disability, epilepsy, hypoplasia of the corpus callosum, and white matter abnormalities (PMID: 40475134)
2. Mapping autosomal recessive intellectual disability: combined microarray and exome sequencing identifies 26 novel candidate genes in 192 consanguineous families (PMID: 28397838)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. ABI2-deficient mice exhibit defective cell migration, aberrant dendritic spine morphogenesis, and deficits in learning and memory (PMID: 15572692)
2. Analysis of genome-wide knockout mouse database identifies candidate ciliopathy genes (PMID: 36456625)
3. The thyroid hormone responsive protein (THRP) has a critical role in the embryogenesis of Xenopus laevis (PMID: 21095221)

### Biological Evidence
1. PIM1 phosphorylates ABI2 to enhance actin dynamics and promote tumor invasion (PMID: 37042842)
2. Inhibition of ABI2 ubiquitination-dependent degradation suppresses TNBC cell growth via down-regulating PI3K/Akt signaling pathway (PMID: 38937761)
3. Downregulation of ABI2 expression by EBV-miR-BART13-3p induces epithelial-mesenchymal transition of nasopharyngeal carcinoma cells through upregulation of c-JUN/SLUG signaling (PMID: 31907338)
4. Regulators of PP2C phosphatase activity function as abscisic acid sensors (PMID: 19407143)
5. The ABI1 and ABI2 protein phosphatases 2C act in a negative feedback regulatory loop of the abscisic acid signalling pathway (PMID: 11208021)
6. ABI INSENSITIVE 2 promotes flowering by inhibiting OST1/ABI5-dependent FLOWERING LOCUS C transcription in Arabidopsis (PMID: 38280208)
7. Phytocytokine signalling reopens stomata in plant immunity and water loss (PMID: 35508659)
8. Ethylene antagonizes ABA responses in stomatal movement and seed germination by upregulating ABI1 and ABI2 at both the transcript and protein levels (PMID: 40811817)
