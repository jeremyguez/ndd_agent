# RAP1B as a Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene: Literature Evidence Report

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

RAP1B (RAS-related protein Rap-1b) is a small GTPase of the RAS superfamily that functions as a molecular switch cycling between inactive GDP-bound and active GTP-bound states, regulating downstream signaling cascades including MEK/ERK, PI3K/Akt, and NF-κB pathways. Its primary known functions include integrin activation, cytoskeletal remodeling, cell adhesion and migration, and platelet activation, with emerging roles in immune cell trafficking, vascular biology, and neurodevelopment.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports and patients:**
Four independent publications reporting human RAP1B variants in the context of syndromic disease were identified. Across these studies, a total of approximately six patients with germline heterozygous RAP1B variants are described. The first report identified two unrelated patients with de novo missense variants (PMID: 32627184), a second report described a third patient with a novel de novo gain-of-function variant (PMID: 35451551), and a fourth report added two further patients carrying variants at previously implicated residues (PMID: 37850357). An additional single patient with a Kabuki syndrome-like phenotype harboring a de novo dominant RAP1B mutation was reported in the context of functional studies (PMID: 26280580). A separate report of a somatic (non-germline) de novo G12E variant causing neonatal thrombocytopenia and combined immunodeficiency further corroborates the pathogenicity of gain-of-function variants at this residue (PMID: 39225097).

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
The core phenotypic spectrum across all germline cases includes:
- Syndromic thrombocytopenia (present in all reported patients)
- Neurodevelopmental delay / learning difficulties
- Microcephaly
- Dysmorphic features
- Congenital malformations (cardiovascular, genitourinary, skeletal)
- Behavioral features

**Predominant variant type:**
All reported pathogenic variants are heterozygous missense variants, consistent with a gain-of-function (GoF) mechanism. Recurrently affected residues include Gly12 (p.Gly12Val [PMID: 32627184], p.Gly12Glu [PMID: 37850357], somatic p.Gly12Glu [PMID: 39225097]) and Gly60 (p.Gly60Arg [PMID: 32627184; PMID: 37850357]). These positions are analogous to activating hotspot mutations well characterized in RAS oncoproteins, strongly supporting a GoF mechanism through impaired GTPase activity and constitutive RAP1B activation.

**Cohort affiliation:**
None of the reported patients are explicitly identified as belonging to named research cohorts such as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL based on the available abstracts.

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**
The evidence is based on a small but growing series of independently reported patients (n ≈ 6 germline cases across four publications) with consistent phenotypes, recurrent de novo variants at functionally critical residues, and supporting functional data. The total patient number remains limited, and detailed clinical descriptions are not uniformly available across all reports, precluding a STRONG rating at this time.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:**
RAP1B has been extensively studied in mouse (*Mus musculus*) models using both full germline knockouts and tissue-specific conditional knockouts, as well as in zebrafish (*Danio rerio*) via morpholino-based knockdown.

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**
- *Platelet/hematologic:* Full Rap1b knockout mice exhibit a significant bleeding defect due to impaired platelet aggregation, reduced integrin αIIbβ3 activation, and protection from arterial thrombosis (PMID: 15696195), directly recapitulating the thrombocytopenia/platelet dysfunction observed in human patients.
- *Immune:* Rap1b knockout mice show defects in B cell development, homing, and T cell-dependent humoral immunity (PMID: 18714009), consistent with the immunodeficiency observed in the somatic GoF patient (PMID: 39225097).
- *Neurological:* Forebrain-specific conditional Rap1b knockout mice demonstrate impaired retrieval of learned fear with decreased c-Fos expression in the prelimbic cortex, while fear acquisition and innate fear responses remain intact (PMID: 32944221). This provides direct evidence for a role of RAP1B in higher cognitive and memory functions, consistent with the neurodevelopmental and learning difficulties reported in human patients.
- *Vascular/endothelial:* Endothelial-specific Rap1b knockout restricts tumor growth and alters immune cell trafficking (PMID: 36403190), and combined Rap1a/Rap1b endothelial knockout causes hypertension and impaired NO-dependent vasorelaxation (PMID: 34222255).
- *Zebrafish:* Rap1b knockdown in zebrafish recapitulates aberrant MEK/ERK signaling, disruption of F-actin polymerization, and cell intercalation defects, with phenotypes rescued by MEK inhibitor administration (PMID: 26280580), directly linking RAP1B dysfunction to the signaling pathway implicated in the human Kabuki syndrome-like phenotype.

**Lethality and Mendelian ratio deviations:**
Full Rap1b knockout mice are viable and fertile, indicating that complete loss of function is compatible with survival. No Mendelian ratio deviations were reported across studies.

**Consistency with human phenotype:**
The mouse and zebrafish data are highly consistent with the human phenotype. Thrombocytopenia/platelet dysfunction, immune defects, and neurocognitive impairment are all recapitulated in model organisms. The zebrafish model specifically supports the MEK/ERK pathway dysregulation proposed as the mechanism underlying the Kabuki syndrome-like and neurodevelopmental features in human patients (PMID: 26280580).

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent laboratories have generated and characterized Rap1b knockout and conditional knockout models across two species, with convergent findings in hematologic, immune, neurological, and vascular phenotypes that are directly relevant to the human disease spectrum.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
RAP1B is a small GTPase that functions as a central signaling hub downstream of multiple receptors. Key pathways include:
- **MEK/ERK signaling:** RAP1B activates the RAF-MEK-ERK cascade, relevant to cell proliferation, differentiation, and neurodevelopment. Aberrant MEK/ERK signaling was demonstrated in zebrafish Rap1b knockdown and in patient cell lines (PMID: 26280580).
- **Integrin activation:** RAP1B promotes talin recruitment and inside-out integrin activation, critical for platelet function and immune cell adhesion (PMID: 39225097).
- **PI3K/Akt signaling:** RAP1B negatively regulates Akt activation in neutrophils (PMID: 25092872), and FN1-RAP1B activates Akt/CREB signaling in cancer contexts (PMID: 40638546).
- **NF-κB and inflammatory signaling:** RAP1B modulates NF-κB transcriptional activity in multiple cell types (PMID: 40083059; PMID: 38342927).
- **Cytoskeletal remodeling:** RAP1B regulates F-actin polymerization, cell intercalation, and cytoskeletal dynamics via downstream effectors including RAC1 and CDC42 (PMID: 26280580; PMID: 41423555; PMID: 37515145).
- **cAMP/PKA/Epac axis:** RAP1B is regulated by cAMP through both PKA-dependent phosphorylation (inhibitory) and Epac-dependent activation, integrating GPCR signaling with downstream effector pathways (PMID: 36897926; PMID: 39256999).

**Gain-of-function mechanism at disease-associated residues:**
Functional studies demonstrate that variants at Gly12 and Gly60 — positions homologous to oncogenic RAS hotspots — impair intrinsic GTPase activity, locking RAP1B in the active GTP-bound state. This results in increased talin recruitment, enhanced integrin activation, altered platelet activation, and modified T cell proliferation and migration (PMID: 39225097). The somatic G12E variant causing neonatal thrombocytopenia and combined immunodeficiency, fully reversed by hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, provides particularly compelling functional validation of the GoF mechanism (PMID: 39225097).

**Neurodevelopmental relevance:**
The forebrain-specific conditional knockout mouse study (PMID: 32944221) establishes RAP1B expression and function in the brain, specifically in the prelimbic cortex, with a role in fear memory retrieval. The MEK/ERK pathway, dysregulated by RAP1B GoF variants, is a well-established pathway in neurodevelopmental disorders including RASopathies (Noonan syndrome, Costello syndrome, cardio-facio-cutaneous syndrome), providing strong biological plausibility for the neurodevelopmental features observed in RAP1B patients.

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
The molecular function of RAP1B is well characterized across multiple cell types and signaling contexts. The specific GoF mechanism of disease-associated variants has been directly validated in functional assays (PMID: 39225097), and the relevant downstream pathways (MEK/ERK, integrin activation, cytoskeletal remodeling) are biologically coherent with the observed human phenotypes.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths:**
1. *Recurrent de novo variants at functionally critical residues:* Multiple independent patients carry de novo missense variants at Gly12 and Gly60, positions analogous to well-characterized activating hotspots in RAS oncoproteins, with a consistent GoF mechanism validated by functional studies (PMID: 39225097; PMID: 32627184; PMID: 37850357).
2. *Phenotypic consistency across independent reports:* The combination of syndromic thrombocytopenia, neurodevelopmental delay, microcephaly, dysmorphic features, and congenital malformations is reproducibly observed across four independent publications spanning at least six patients.
3. *Strong model organism and biological support:* Mouse knockout models directly recapitulate hematologic, immune, and neurocognitive phenotypes (PMID: 15696195; PMID: 32944221), and zebrafish studies validate the MEK/ERK pathway mechanism (PMID: 26280580), providing robust cross-species biological plausibility.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. *Small total patient number:* Approximately six germline cases have been reported to date, limiting statistical power and phenotypic characterization. Larger cohort studies or systematic reanalysis of exome/genome databases are needed.
2. *Absence of loss-of-function variants:* All reported pathogenic variants are GoF missense variants; no LoF (truncating) variants have been reported in association with the neurodevelopmental syndrome, and the GoF mechanism has not been formally demonstrated for all reported variants (e.g., the Gly60Arg variant in PMID: 37850357 is described as disrupting GTPase activity but detailed functional data are not uniformly available).
3. *Limited neurodevelopmental-specific functional data:* While the forebrain-specific mouse knockout (PMID: 32944221) and zebrafish studies (PMID: 26280580) support neurological relevance, direct functional studies in human neuronal or neurodevelopmental model systems (e.g., iPSC-derived neurons) are lacking.

**Conclusion:**
RAP1B represents an emerging autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene, with a growing body of clinical, model organism, and functional evidence supporting pathogenicity of heterozygous gain-of-function missense variants at conserved GTPase-regulatory residues. The phenotypic spectrum — encompassing syndromic thrombocytopenia, neurodevelopmental delay, microcephaly, and congenital malformations — is consistent across independent reports and biologically coherent with RAP1B's role in MEK/ERK signaling and cytoskeletal regulation. Further case ascertainment, systematic functional characterization of individual variants, and neurodevelopmental-specific mechanistic studies are needed to elevate the overall evidence to STRONG and fully delineate the clinical spectrum of this condition.
