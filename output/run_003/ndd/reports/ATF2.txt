# ATF2 as a Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene: Literature Evidence Report

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

ATF2 (Activating Transcription Factor 2) is a member of the ATF/CREB family of basic leucine zipper (bZIP) transcription factors that is activated by stress-responsive kinases, particularly p38 MAPK and JNK, through phosphorylation of its N-terminal transactivation domain. ATF2 regulates diverse cellular processes including stress response, apoptosis, inflammation, cell proliferation, and differentiation, and has been implicated in both neuroprotective and neuroinflammatory signaling cascades in the central nervous system.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** All model organism studies identified were conducted in mouse (*Mus musculus*), using a range of genetic approaches including conditional knockout, full knockout, knock-in, and knockdown strategies.

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

Neurological phenotypes from direct ATF2 loss-of-function models are not well characterized in the available literature. The most directly relevant neurological findings come from indirect or secondary models. In a study of A53T-alpha-synuclein overexpressing mice, Atf2 transcript levels were progressively and genotype-dependently reduced in the postsynaptic dopaminergic response, and long-term depression was absent in corticostriatal slices from aged transgenic animals (ASHG 2010 – Gispert Sanchez et al.); however, this reflects ATF2 as a downstream readout rather than a primary loss-of-function model. In a kainic acid-induced epilepsy model, ATF2 knockdown in hippocampal microglia diminished M1 polarization, enhanced M2 polarization, and relieved neuroinflammation, with ATF2 shown to negatively regulate TSC1 transcription (PMID: 40595935). In a trigeminal neuropathic pain model, downstream reduction of ATF2 activation via TRPC4 knockdown attenuated pain behaviors (PMID: 40202077). These findings position ATF2 as a modulator of neuroinflammatory and pain signaling rather than a primary neurodevelopmental regulator.

Outside the nervous system, conditional double knockout of ATF2 and ATF7 in intestinal epithelium produced no major homeostatic phenotype but caused severe impairment of regenerative responses after injury (PMID: 31958521). A knock-in model expressing transcriptionally inactive ATF2 in melanocytes strongly suppressed melanoma development (1/21 mice vs. 7/21 controls) (PMID: 21203491). A full knockout model demonstrated that ATF2 loss promotes tumor invasiveness in colorectal cancer xenografts (PMID: 35838828).

**Lethality and Mendelian ratio deviations:** No lethality or Mendelian ratio deviations were reported in any of the mouse models described. Conditional and full knockout animals were viable.

**Consistency with human neurodevelopmental phenotype:** The available mouse data do not demonstrate a neurodevelopmental phenotype (e.g., structural brain abnormalities, intellectual disability, seizures, or behavioral deficits) attributable to primary ATF2 loss of function. The neurological findings are largely secondary or context-dependent (neuroinflammation, pain signaling, dopaminergic modulation in a synucleinopathy model).

- **Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED**

While mouse studies confirm ATF2's role in stress-responsive and neuroinflammatory signaling, there is no direct evidence from knockout or knock-in models of a primary neurodevelopmental phenotype relevant to autosomal dominant human disease.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

ATF2 functions at the intersection of multiple stress-responsive signaling cascades. It is phosphorylated and activated by p38 MAPK and JNK in response to inflammatory stimuli, oxidative stress, and DNA damage. Several key pathway roles have been characterized:

- **Neuroinflammation:** ATF2 is upregulated, phosphorylated, and translocates to the nucleus in microglia during LPS-induced neuroinflammation, where it drives pro-inflammatory gene expression via the TRAF6-p38/JNK-ATF2 axis (PMID: 30763583). Conversely, O-GlcNAcylation of ATF2 at Ser44 by OGT inhibits its phosphorylation and nuclear translocation, suppressing NLRP3 inflammasome activation and microglial pyroptosis in sepsis-associated encephalopathy (PMID: 40411666). These findings establish ATF2 as a bidirectionally regulated node in microglial inflammatory signaling.

- **BDNF regulation:** ATF2 directly binds to Bdnf promoters in cultured rat neurons and in vivo, competing with CREB and other AP1 family members to regulate stimulus-dependent Bdnf transcription (PMID: 39947922). This is the most direct evidence linking ATF2 to a neurodevelopmentally relevant molecular function, as BDNF-TrkB signaling is critical for neuronal survival, synaptic plasticity, and neurodevelopment.

- **Oxidative stress and ferroptosis:** ATF2 is a component of a repressive epigenetic complex (with ZNF354A, KAP1/TRIM28, and SETDB1) that silences the Lipid Oxygen Radical Defense (LORD) pathway under basal conditions; p38/JNK-dependent phosphorylation of ATF2 disassembles this complex and activates NRF2 target genes in response to lipid peroxide accumulation (PMID: 41372219).

- **Apoptosis:** ATF2 transcriptionally activates BAP1, which in turn stabilizes P53 and promotes neuronal apoptosis in a subarachnoid hemorrhage model (PMID: 38965653). ATF2 also transcriptionally activates TLR4 in cardiomyocytes, a function suppressed by MG53-mediated ubiquitination (PMID: 40107350).

- **Macrophage/immune function:** ATF2 regulates monocyte-to-macrophage differentiation and M1 polarization, with overexpression enhancing phagocytosis and control of intracellular Mycobacterium tuberculosis (PMID: 37403209).

- **Post-translational regulation:** ATF2 activity is regulated by multiple post-translational modifications beyond phosphorylation, including O-GlcNAcylation (PMID: 40411666) and lysine demyristoylation by SIRT6 (PMID: 40810740), which control its subcellular localization and transcriptional output.

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**

ATF2 is expressed in the brain, including in neurons and microglia, and its expression is modulated by neurological insults (upregulated in hippocampal tissues of kainic acid-induced epilepsy mice [PMID: 40595935] and in brains of doxorubicin-treated mice [PMID: 38248237]). Its direct regulation of Bdnf transcription in neurons (PMID: 39947922) provides a mechanistic link to neurodevelopmental processes. However, no studies specifically characterize ATF2 expression during embryonic brain development or in the context of neurodevelopmental disorder pathogenesis.

- **Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE**

There is solid mechanistic evidence for ATF2's role in neuronal gene regulation (particularly Bdnf) and neuroinflammatory signaling, supported by multiple independent experimental systems. However, the biological evidence does not specifically address haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative mechanisms relevant to autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disease, and the neurodevelopmental relevance remains inferential rather than directly demonstrated.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

- **Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
- ATF2 has a well-characterized role in stress-responsive transcription in the CNS, including direct regulation of Bdnf in neurons (PMID: 39947922) and modulation of microglial inflammatory signaling (PMID: 30763583; PMID: 40411666).
- Multiple independent experimental systems (cell culture, mouse models, biochemical assays) confirm ATF2's biological activity in neural cell types.
- Mouse knockout and knockdown models are viable, providing a platform for future neurodevelopmental phenotyping.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- There is a complete absence of clinical evidence: no patients with ATF2 variants and neurodevelopmental phenotypes have been reported in the literature surveyed.
- No mouse model has demonstrated a primary neurodevelopmental phenotype (structural, behavioral, or cognitive) attributable to ATF2 loss of function, and no haploinsufficiency data are available.
- The biological evidence, while supportive of a neurological role, does not specifically address the mechanisms (haploinsufficiency, dominant-negative, gain-of-function) relevant to autosomal dominant disease.

**Conclusion:**

Based on the available evidence, ATF2 cannot currently be classified as an established neurodevelopmental disorder gene with autosomal dominant pathogenic variants. While the biological literature supports a plausible role for ATF2 in neuronal gene regulation and neuroinflammatory signaling—most notably through direct regulation of Bdnf transcription (PMID: 39947922)—the complete absence of clinical case reports and the lack of a neurodevelopmental phenotype in animal models means the evidence is insufficient to support gene-disease validity at this time. Future studies should prioritize systematic phenotyping of Atf2 heterozygous mice and active surveillance for ATF2 variants in neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts.
