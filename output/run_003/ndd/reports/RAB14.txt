# RAB14 Evidence Report: Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene Assessment

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

RAB14 is a member of the RAS oncogene family of small GTPases that localizes primarily to the trans-Golgi network (TGN), early endosomes, and recycling endosomes, where it regulates intracellular vesicular trafficking, endosomal sorting, and membrane receptor transport. Its known functions span a broad range of cellular processes including receptor recycling, lipophagy, mitophagy, cytokinesis, and the trafficking of lipids, growth factor receptors, and adhesion molecules between intracellular compartments.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*), zebrafish (*Danio rerio*), and *Xenopus* oocytes have been used to study RAB14 function.

**Key phenotypic findings:**

The most developmentally informative study demonstrated that dominant-negative overexpression of Rab14-GDP in mouse embryos impaired FGFR transport, disrupted FGF signaling, caused defective basement membrane assembly by the primitive endoderm lineage, and resulted in failure to develop epiblast and primitive endoderm lineages — phenocopying both KIF16B knockout and FGFR2 knockout embryonic lethality at the peri-implantation stage (PMID: 21238925). This finding establishes that loss of Rab14 function is embryonic lethal in mice, though it should be noted this was achieved via dominant-negative overexpression rather than a conventional knockout, and no Mendelian ratio data were reported.

In zebrafish, RAB14 knockdown increased erythroid cell numbers and beta-haemoglobin expression, identifying RAB14 as a physiological inhibitor of erythropoiesis regulated by miR-144 and miR-451 (PMID: 25312678). No neurological or neurodevelopmental phenotypes were reported in this model.

In a mouse model of female infertility, Rab14 overexpression in granulosa cells enhanced lipophagy, disrupted lipid homeostasis, induced granulosa cell apoptosis, and impaired follicle development, resulting in decreased litter size (PMID: 41553083). This study implicates Rab14 gain-of-function in reproductive biology but does not address neurodevelopment.

In cathepsin B/L double-knockout mice, Rab14 protein levels were more than 10-fold elevated in brain lysosomes, and Rab14 specifically accumulated in swollen axons associated with early neurodegeneration (PMID: 17765022). While this finding is notable for its neurological context, it reflects Rab14 as a secondary accumulation in a lysosomal storage model rather than a primary Rab14 loss-of-function phenotype.

In Rab11FIP1 knockout mice, Rab14 was mislocalized in colonocytes and associated with impaired mucosal integrity (PMID: 35819177), again reflecting Rab14 as a secondary participant rather than a primary driver.

**Consistency with human neurodevelopmental phenotype:** The model organism data do not specifically support a neurodevelopmental disorder phenotype. The embryonic lethality observed with dominant-negative Rab14 in mice is relevant to early development but is not specific to the nervous system. No neurological, cognitive, or structural brain phenotypes have been reported in Rab14 loss-of-function models.

**Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED**

While embryonic lethality with dominant-negative Rab14 demonstrates developmental essentiality, the absence of dedicated Rab14 knockout studies with neurological phenotyping, the lack of Mendelian ratio data, and the absence of any nervous system-specific findings substantially limit the relevance of this evidence to a neurodevelopmental disorder hypothesis.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

RAB14 participates in multiple intracellular trafficking pathways. It regulates endosomal budding at Golgi-endosome contact sites through the RhoBTB3-SHIP164-Vps26B complex (PMID: 38565878), controls cytokinesis by recruiting Rab11-endosomes to intracellular bridge microtubules via interaction with the MACF2/CAMSAP3 complex (PMID: 33566684), and promotes Parkin-dependent mitophagy through TGN-derived vesicle contact sites with mitochondria (PMID: 40737294). RAB14 also interacts directly with PKCι to regulate claudin-2 trafficking and epithelial barrier function (PMID: 25694446), and facilitates fatty acid trafficking from lipid droplets to mitochondria via interaction with CPT1A (PMID: 29721083).

In the context of lipid metabolism, RAB14 regulates ceramide transport from the ER to the Golgi apparatus for sphingomyelin synthesis, a process exploited by classical swine fever virus for particle assembly (PMID: 37255314). In skeletal muscle, RAB14 (along with Rab8 and Rab10) contributes to long-chain fatty acid uptake via SLC27A4/FATP4 (PMID: 32868338).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**

No studies specifically characterizing RAB14 expression in the developing human or mouse brain were identified. The observation that Rab14 accumulates in swollen axons in cathepsin B/L-deficient mice (PMID: 17765022) suggests that RAB14 is present in neurons and may participate in axonal vesicular trafficking, but this does not constitute evidence of a primary neurodevelopmental role. No transcriptomic or proteomic studies specifically addressing RAB14 expression during brain development were captured in the available evidence.

**Key experimental findings relevant to disease mechanism:**

RAB14 has been implicated in oncology contexts — it is overexpressed in non-small cell lung cancer (65/115 cases) and activates YAP signaling (PMID: 32982313), and is overexpressed in ovarian cancer where it promotes Wnt/β-catenin signaling (PMID: 27718127). While these findings demonstrate that RAB14 dysregulation has functional consequences in proliferating cells, they do not directly inform a neurodevelopmental disease mechanism.

The role of RAB14 in cytokinesis (PMID: 33566684) is potentially relevant to neurodevelopment, as cytokinesis failure could affect neural progenitor proliferation, but this connection has not been experimentally demonstrated.

**Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED**

RAB14 has well-characterized roles in vesicular trafficking, lipid transport, mitophagy, and cytokinesis, and is expressed in neurons based on indirect evidence. However, no studies have specifically linked RAB14 biological function to neurodevelopmental processes, brain morphogenesis, synaptic biology, or neuronal differentiation. The biological evidence, while mechanistically rich in other contexts, does not specifically support a neurodevelopmental disorder mechanism.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
- RAB14 has a well-established and essential role in intracellular vesicular trafficking, a pathway broadly important for neurodevelopment.
- Dominant-negative Rab14 expression causes embryonic lethality in mice by disrupting FGFR signaling and early lineage specification (PMID: 21238925), demonstrating developmental essentiality.
- RAB14 participates in cytokinesis regulation (PMID: 33566684) and is detectable in neuronal compartments (PMID: 17765022), providing a biological basis for potential neurological relevance.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- There is a complete absence of clinical evidence: no patients with RAB14 variants and neurodevelopmental phenotypes have been reported in any published study or major disease cohort.
- No dedicated Rab14 knockout mouse or zebrafish model with neurological phenotyping has been published; the embryonic lethality data derive from dominant-negative overexpression rather than heterozygous loss-of-function, which is the relevant genetic model for autosomal dominant disease.
- No studies characterize RAB14 expression specifically in the developing brain, and no functional studies address synaptic, neuronal, or neurodevelopmental biology.

**Conclusion:**

At present, the evidence is insufficient to support RAB14 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous autosomal dominant pathogenic variants. While RAB14 encodes a biologically important trafficking GTPase with demonstrated developmental roles in mouse embryogenesis, the complete absence of human clinical data, the lack of neurologically focused model organism studies, and the absence of brain-specific expression or functional data represent critical gaps that preclude any meaningful gene-disease association at this time. Systematic phenotyping of heterozygous Rab14 knockout mice and targeted patient cohort screening would be necessary first steps to evaluate this hypothesis.
