# Evidence Report: REM1 as a Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene (Autosomal Dominant)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

REM1 (Rad and Gem-like GTP-binding protein 1) is a member of the RGK subfamily of the Ras GTPase superfamily, characterized by atypical nucleotide-binding motifs and extended N- and C-terminal domains (PMID: 17107948). Its primary known function in mammals is as a potent inhibitor of voltage-gated L-type calcium channels (VLCCs), with strong cardiac expression and regulation by adrenergic signaling pathways (PMID: 22076634).

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

- **Independent case reports / case series found:** 0
- **Total approximate number of patients:** 0
- **Most common phenotypes observed:** None reported in the literature retrieved
- **Predominant variant type:** No clinical variant data available
- **Known cohort representation (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL):** None identified
- **Strength of clinical evidence: NONE**

No clinical evidence was identified linking heterozygous pathogenic variants in REM1 to neurodevelopmental disorders or any other human Mendelian disease. There are no published case reports, case series, or cohort studies describing patients with REM1 variants and associated phenotypes in the retrieved literature.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

- **Organisms studied:** The available model organism evidence is restricted entirely to yeast systems — specifically *Schizosaccharomyces pombe* (fission yeast) and *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* (budding yeast). No vertebrate or mammalian model organism studies (e.g., mouse knockout, zebrafish morpholino) were identified.
- **Key phenotypes from knockouts:** In *S. pombe*, loss of Rem1 (a meiosis-specific B-type cyclin, functionally unrelated to the mammalian REM1 GTPase) results in impaired meiotic recombination and defective premeiotic DNA synthesis (PMID: 16024772). In *S. cerevisiae*, rem1 mutations (alleles of the DNA repair gene RAD3) cause hyper-recombination, elevated spontaneous mutation rates, and synthetic lethality with recombination-repair pathway defects (PMID: 2840336). These yeast "REM1" genes are not orthologous to human REM1 and represent distinct biological entities sharing only a naming convention.
- **Consistency with human neurodevelopmental phenotype:** Not applicable. The yeast Rem1 proteins studied are functionally unrelated to the mammalian REM1 RGK GTPase, and no neurological or developmental phenotypes were reported in any model system.
- **Strength of model organism evidence: NONE** (with respect to the human REM1 gene and neurodevelopmental disease)

---

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

- **Known pathways and molecular function:** Mammalian REM1 is classified within the RGK protein family (Rad, Gem, Rem1, Rem2), a branch of the Ras superfamily (PMID: 17107948). RGK proteins, including REM1, function as inhibitors of voltage-gated calcium channels. REM1 has also been shown to interact with the tumor suppressor RASSF1A, linking it to Hippo pathway regulation through blockade of YAP1 nuclear localization (PMID: 33051258). Downstream of α1-adrenergic signaling, protein kinase D1 phosphorylates REM1 at Ser18, relieving its inhibition of L-type calcium channels in cardiomyocytes (PMID: 22076634).
- **Expression pattern:** REM1 is described as strongly and specifically expressed in cardiac tissue in mammals (PMID: 22076634). Expression has also been examined in rat fracture callus and healing tissue (PMID: 31830958). No brain-specific or neurodevelopmentally relevant expression data were identified in the retrieved evidence. An Arabidopsis protein designated REM1.3 (a remorin scaffold protein) was identified in a phosphoproteomic screen for cell wall stress signaling (PMID: 26811356), but this plant protein is entirely unrelated to mammalian REM1.
- **Key experimental findings:** The most functionally characterized role of mammalian REM1 is in cardiac calcium channel regulation. Its interaction with RASSF1A and potential involvement in Hippo/YAP1 signaling represents an additional molecular connection, though this has not been explored in a neurodevelopmental context. No functional studies in neurons, brain tissue, or neurodevelopmental models were identified.
- **Strength of biological evidence (for neurodevelopmental relevance): LIMITED**

While REM1 has defined molecular functions in calcium channel regulation and GTPase signaling, there is no direct biological evidence supporting a role in neurodevelopment. The related family member REM2 has documented roles in neuronal development and synaptogenesis, but no equivalent data exist for REM1 in the retrieved literature.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

- **Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
- REM1 belongs to a well-characterized GTPase family (RGK proteins) with defined roles in calcium channel regulation, a pathway with established relevance to neurological function.
- Interaction with RASSF1A and the Hippo pathway (PMID: 33051258) provides a molecular framework for potential broader signaling roles.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- There is a complete absence of clinical evidence: no patients with REM1 variants and neurodevelopmental phenotypes have been reported.
- No vertebrate model organism studies (mouse, zebrafish, or other) examining neurological or developmental phenotypes of REM1 loss or gain of function were identified.
- Brain or neurodevelopmental expression data for mammalian REM1 are entirely lacking in the retrieved evidence base.
- Much of the retrieved literature pertains to yeast "Rem1" proteins that are functionally and evolutionarily unrelated to human REM1, or to plant REM1.3, introducing significant noise into the evidence landscape.

**Conclusion:** Based on the available evidence, there is currently insufficient support to classify REM1 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with autosomal dominant pathogenic variants. No clinical cases, no relevant vertebrate model organism data, and no neurodevelopmental expression or functional data have been identified. While REM1's membership in the RGK GTPase family and its role in calcium channel regulation are biologically plausible entry points for future investigation, the gene cannot be considered a candidate neurodevelopmental disease gene on the basis of current published evidence alone.
