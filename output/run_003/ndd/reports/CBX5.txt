# CBX5 Evidence Report: Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene Assessment

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

CBX5 encodes Heterochromatin Protein 1 alpha (HP1α), a highly conserved chromodomain-containing protein that binds H3K9me2/3 marks deposited by histone methyltransferases such as SUV39H1/H2, thereby promoting heterochromatin formation, transcriptional repression, and genome stability (PMID: 32979540). CBX5/HP1α plays fundamental roles in cell cycle regulation, DNA damage response, gene silencing, and maintenance of chromatin architecture across diverse cell types and developmental contexts.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*), including full knockout and knockdown models.

**Key phenotypes from knockout/knockdown studies:**

Mouse studies of CBX5/HP1α have focused primarily on non-neurological phenotypes. Full knockout of HP1γ (the closely related paralog CBX3, not CBX5) demonstrated a dramatic reduction in primordial germ cells (PGCs) due to impaired cell cycle progression, with reduced BrdU incorporation and accumulation of PGCs in G1 phase associated with increased p21/CDKN1A expression; notably, animals were viable and no neurological phenotypes were reported (PMID: 21778144). A separate full knockout study of HP1α (CBX5) in mouse embryonic fibroblasts (MEFs) demonstrated mitotic chromosomal instability, including chromatin bridges and micronuclei formation at anaphase/telophase, consistent with a role in maintaining genome integrity during cell division (PMID: 31130069). Knockdown studies in mouse models of AML identified Cbx5 as a critical component of leukemia stem cell (LSC) maintenance, with Cbx5 knockdown impairing LSC self-renewal (PMID: 19200802). In a pulmonary hypertension mouse model, Cbx5 knockdown in pulmonary artery smooth muscle cells suppressed proliferation, invasion, and migration while stimulating apoptosis under hypoxic conditions (PMID: 39151099). Cbx5 was also identified as a direct target of miR-206 during myogenesis in C2C12 mouse muscle cells (PMID: 32129700).

**Consistency with human neurodevelopmental phenotype:** Poor. No mouse knockout or knockdown study reported neurological, behavioral, or neurodevelopmental phenotypes. Lethality and Mendelian ratio deviations were not reported for CBX5 full knockouts. The available model organism data do not support a neurodevelopmental disease hypothesis.

**Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED** (for neurodevelopmental relevance specifically; evidence exists for other biological roles but none are neurological in nature)

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

CBX5 functions as a core component of the HP1 complex, reading H3K9me2/3 chromatin marks to establish and maintain constitutive heterochromatin and transcriptional repression (PMID: 32979540). It partners with the histone methyltransferase G9a to deposit H3K9me marks and silence target genes such as PPARGC1A, a mechanism demonstrated in the context of lung fibroblast activation and fibrosis (PMID: 31095524). CBX5 also regulates E2F1 transcriptional activity and downstream anti-apoptotic gene expression (e.g., BIRC5/survivin), as shown in EGFR inhibitor resistance in lung adenocarcinoma (PMID: 36652476). Post-translational regulation by Aurora kinase B-mediated phosphorylation modulates HP1α function at mitosis (PMID: 31130069). CBX5 mRNA is directly regulated by RNA-binding proteins RBMX and RBMXL1 in AML cells, placing it downstream of RNA processing pathways relevant to leukemogenesis (PMID: 34458856).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**

One study reported that a subset of multiple sclerosis patients showed reduced CBX5 expression in monocytes, correlating with progressive disease and transcriptomic alterations including impaired U snRNA maturation and altered RNA Pol II pause-release; inactivation of Cbx5 in mice caused hypersensitivity to experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis, revealing an unexpected role for CBX5 in regulating Integrator complex activity (PMID: 39029934). While this finding touches on neuroinflammation, it does not constitute evidence for a primary neurodevelopmental role. No studies specifically characterize CBX5 expression in the developing human brain, neural progenitor cells, or neurons. CBX5 expression has been documented in fibroblasts, lung tissue, hematopoietic cells, prostate cancer, glioma, and breast cancer contexts, but dedicated neurodevelopmental expression profiling is absent from the retrieved evidence.

**Key experimental findings:**

- CBX5 is overexpressed in prostate cancer and promotes proliferation, invasion, and migration; knockdown suppresses tumorigenesis in xenograft models (PMID: 37014569).
- CBX5 is downregulated in metastatic breast cancer cells and patient tissues, suggesting context-dependent oncogenic versus tumor-suppressive roles (PMID: 26791953).
- In glioma, the lncRNA LINC02381 activates CBX5 transcription via CEBPβ, promoting proliferation and inhibiting apoptosis (PMID: 34274429).
- A GWAS of serum endotoxemia in 11,296 individuals identified genome-wide significant associations (741 SNPs) at a locus near CBX5, with the associated genetic risk score linked to venous thromboembolism in 195,170 FinnGen participants; however, CBX5 was identified only as the nearest gene and no functional neurodevelopmental link was established (PMID: 34668383).
- Single-cell RNA sequencing identified CBX5 enrichment in pathogenic fibroblast populations in IPF lungs (PMID: 39642371).

**Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED** (for neurodevelopmental disorder relevance; the gene has well-characterized molecular functions in chromatin biology, but no specific neurodevelopmental pathway evidence was identified)

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths of the evidence:**
- CBX5 encodes a well-characterized epigenetic regulator (HP1α) with fundamental roles in heterochromatin maintenance, cell cycle control, and transcriptional repression — biological functions broadly relevant to neurodevelopment.
- Mouse knockout studies confirm that loss of CBX5 causes chromosomal instability and cell cycle defects (PMID: 31130069), which are mechanisms implicated in other established neurodevelopmental disorder genes.
- The gene's role in regulating Integrator complex activity and RNA Pol II dynamics (PMID: 39029934) touches on transcriptional regulatory mechanisms relevant to brain development.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- There is a complete absence of clinical evidence: no patients with germline CBX5 variants and neurodevelopmental phenotypes have been reported in any published study, cohort, or database represented in this evidence set.
- No model organism study has reported neurological, behavioral, or neurodevelopmental phenotypes following CBX5 loss; existing knockout data focus on germ cell biology, chromosomal instability, and hematopoiesis.
- No neurodevelopmental expression data (e.g., brain-specific expression, neural progenitor function) were identified for CBX5, and no functional studies in neuronal cell types were retrieved.

**Conclusion:**

Based on the available evidence, there is currently **insufficient support** to classify CBX5 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene associated with heterozygous autosomal dominant pathogenic variants. While CBX5 encodes a biologically important chromatin regulator with plausible mechanistic relevance to neurodevelopment, the complete absence of clinical cases, the lack of neurological phenotypes in animal models, and the absence of neurodevelopmental expression or functional data represent critical evidentiary gaps. Systematic sequencing of neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts and targeted functional studies in neural cell types would be required before any disease association could be established.
