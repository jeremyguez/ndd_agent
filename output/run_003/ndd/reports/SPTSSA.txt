# Literature Evidence Report: SPTSSA as a Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene (Autosomal Dominant / Heterozygous Variants)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

SPTSSA (serine palmitoyltransferase small subunit A) encodes an activating small subunit of serine palmitoyltransferase (SPT), the rate-limiting enzyme of de novo sphingolipid biosynthesis. SPTSSA stimulates SPT catalytic activity, participates in acyl-CoA substrate coordination, and is subject to negative feedback regulation by ORMDL proteins, placing it at a critical control point in sphingolipid homeostasis (PMID: 33558762).

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports / total patients:**
Two independent publications describe patients with pathogenic SPTSSA variants. The foundational report (PMID: 36718090) described three children harboring either the missense variant p.Thr51Ile or a frameshift variant, establishing the condition designated Spastic Paraplegia 90 (SPG90). A subsequent case report (PMID: 40533086) identified a fourth patient carrying the recurrent p.Thr51Ile variant, detected by exome sequencing. In total, approximately four patients have been reported across these two publications.

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
The core phenotype is a complex hereditary spastic paraplegia. Consistent features across patients include progressive spastic paraplegia (with inability to walk in at least one patient), global developmental delay, axial hypotonia, and extremity spasticity. Variable features include dystonia, sensorineural hearing loss, and language/cognitive dysfunction (PMID: 36718090). The fourth patient extended the phenotypic spectrum to include distal renal tubular acidosis, nephrolithiasis, neurogenic bladder, recurrent urinary tract infections, and primary polydipsia, suggesting multisystem involvement beyond the nervous system (PMID: 40533086).

**Predominant variant type:**
Both missense (p.Thr51Ile, recurrent across three of four patients) and frameshift variants have been reported. The recurrence of p.Thr51Ile in three unrelated individuals strengthens its pathogenicity. Inheritance is heterozygous (autosomal dominant) in reported cases.

**Known cohort affiliation:**
No explicit affiliation with named diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL) was documented in the available structured data.

**Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED**
Four patients across two publications represent a small but growing case series. The recurrence of the same variant (p.Thr51Ile) in three unrelated patients and the consistent core phenotype support pathogenicity, but the overall patient number remains very small.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:**
No formal knockout or knock-in mouse studies were identified in the structured evidence. However, Drosophila melanogaster was used as a functional model system (ASHG 2022 - SPTSSA variants alter sphingolipid synthesis and cause a complex form of hereditary spastic paraplegia).

**Key findings from model organisms:**
In Drosophila, overexpression of human SPT produced excessive sphingolipid synthesis, severe motor defects, and shortened lifespan. Co-expression of human ORMDL3 rescued these phenotypes in flies expressing wild-type SPT but failed to rescue flies expressing the SPTSSA p.Thr51Ile variant, directly demonstrating that this variant abrogates ORMDL-mediated negative regulation of SPT in vivo. Additionally, a genome-wide CRISPR/Cas9 screen in HeLa cells showed that Ahr knockout mice exhibited reduced ceramide content and decreased myelin thickness in sciatic nerve, consistent with SPTSSA's role in sphingolipid-dependent myelination (PMID: 32029474).

**Consistency with human phenotype:**
The Drosophila motor defects are broadly consistent with the spastic paraplegia and motor disturbance observed in human patients. The failure of ORMDL3 rescue specifically for the p.Thr51Ile variant mirrors the proposed gain-of-function/dysregulation mechanism in patients.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**
The Drosophila data provide mechanistically informative in vivo support, but the absence of mammalian (rodent) knockout or knock-in studies is a notable gap. The fly model does not fully recapitulate the neurodevelopmental complexity of the human disorder.

---

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
SPTSSA is an integral component of the heteromeric SPT complex (alongside SPTLC1 and SPTLC2), where it stimulates enzymatic activity and contributes to acyl-CoA substrate selectivity. Cryo-EM structural analysis demonstrated that SPTSSA Thr51 resides in close physical proximity to ORMDL3 within the assembled SPT-ORMDL3 complex, providing a structural rationale for why the p.Thr51Ile substitution disrupts ORMDL-mediated feedback inhibition (PMID: 33558762). Functional studies confirmed that both the p.Thr51Ile missense and a frameshift variant impair ORMDL regulation, resulting in unrestrained SPT activity and excessive sphingolipid accumulation, as measured in patient serum and fibroblasts (PMID: 36718090; ASHG 2022).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**
SPTSSA expression is regulated by the aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR), which binds the SPTSSA promoter and upregulates its expression to stimulate de novo sphingolipid synthesis; AHR knockout mice showed reduced ceramide and myelin thickness in peripheral nerve, implicating SPTSSA in myelination (PMID: 32029474). Rhinovirus infection in mice and human bronchial epithelial cells downregulated Sptssa expression in lung epithelial cells, linking SPTSSA to sphingolipid homeostasis in epithelial tissues (PMID: 34851798). Additional bioinformatics analyses place SPTSSA within the broader serine palmitoyltransferase complex gene network (PMID: 31512789). Several oncology-focused studies report SPTSSA overexpression in gastric cancer (PMID: 40498284), glioblastoma (PMID: 36062185), and head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (PMID: 42051093), confirming broad tissue expression, though these findings are not directly relevant to the neurodevelopmental phenotype.

**Key experimental findings supporting pathogenicity:**
The mechanistic chain is well-supported: (1) structural data place Thr51 at the SPTSSA–ORMDL3 interface (PMID: 33558762); (2) patient-derived fibroblasts and serum show elevated sphingolipids (PMID: 36718090); (3) Drosophila in vivo experiments confirm that the p.Thr51Ile variant specifically abrogates ORMDL3 rescue of SPT overexpression phenotypes (ASHG 2022). Together, these data establish a coherent gain-of-dysregulation mechanism.

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
The convergence of structural, biochemical, patient-derived cellular, and in vivo Drosophila data provides robust mechanistic support for the pathogenicity of SPTSSA variants through a defined molecular mechanism.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths:**
- A clearly defined and mechanistically coherent disease mechanism (loss of ORMDL-mediated SPT regulation → excessive sphingolipid synthesis) supported by structural (PMID: 33558762), biochemical (PMID: 36718090), and in vivo Drosophila data (ASHG 2022).
- Recurrence of the p.Thr51Ile variant in three of four unrelated patients strengthens the genotype–phenotype correlation.
- Consistent core phenotype (complex hereditary spastic paraplegia with developmental delay) across independent reports from two publications (PMID: 36718090; PMID: 40533086).

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- The total number of reported patients remains very small (n ≈ 4), limiting phenotypic characterization and statistical confidence in variant pathogenicity.
- No mammalian (mouse) knockout or knock-in model data are available, which would substantially strengthen the evidence base.
- The autosomal dominant inheritance mechanism (gain-of-dysregulation rather than haploinsufficiency) is plausible but has not been formally demonstrated through allele-specific functional assays in human neurons or mammalian systems.

**Conclusion:**
SPTSSA is a well-characterized sphingolipid biosynthesis gene whose heterozygous pathogenic variants cause SPG90, a complex hereditary spastic paraplegia with developmental delay and variable multisystem features. The biological and mechanistic evidence is strong, anchored by structural data and in vivo Drosophila experiments, but clinical evidence is currently limited to approximately four patients. As additional cases are identified and mammalian model data become available, the overall evidence level is expected to advance from moderate toward strong.
