# Literature Evidence Report: PDE2A as a Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene

**Prepared for clinical genetics review | Inheritance mode assessed: Autosomal recessive (biallelic)**

> **Important prefatory note:** All clinical cases identified in this review carry **biallelic (homozygous or compound heterozygous)** pathogenic variants in PDE2A. No convincing evidence was identified supporting an **autosomal dominant (heterozygous)** disease mechanism in humans. This report summarizes the available evidence in full, but the requested framing of heterozygous pathogenicity is not supported by the current literature. This distinction is highlighted throughout.

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

PDE2A (Phosphodiesterase 2A; MIM *602658*; chromosomal locus 11q13.4) encodes a dual-substrate cyclic nucleotide phosphodiesterase that hydrolyzes both cAMP and cGMP, and is uniquely stimulated by cGMP binding to its GAF-B regulatory domain, creating a positive feedback loop that amplifies cAMP degradation. PDE2A is highly expressed in the brain, heart, liver, and vascular endothelium, where it plays a central role in compartmentalized cyclic nucleotide signaling downstream of G protein-coupled receptors, with particular importance for neuronal function, cardiac development, and hematopoiesis.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports and patients:**
Four independent publications or conference reports describe patients with PDE2A-related disease. Across these, approximately **16 patients** are reported in total (3 patients in PMID: 32467598; 1 patient in PMID: 29392776; 6 patients in PMID: 37317634; 6 patients in ASHG 2022 – Fatima et al., which likely overlaps substantially with PMID: 37317634 given the same Pakistani cohort, founder variant, and patient count). A broad review (PMID: 36003298) synthesized 203 patients across five related genes including PDE2A but did not provide PDE2A-specific variant-level data. Conservatively, approximately **10–16 unique patients** with confirmed biallelic PDE2A variants have been reported.

**Most common phenotypes:**
Across all reports, the core phenotypic spectrum of biallelic PDE2A disease includes:
- **Paroxysmal dyskinesia** (childhood-onset, often refractory to treatment; present in the majority of reported patients)
- **Cognitive impairment / intellectual disability / developmental delay** (universal across all cohorts)
- **Speech impairment**
- **Seizures / epilepsy** (including drug-refractory seizures and interictal EEG abnormalities; one infant sibling presented with epilepsy at 4 months without dyskinesia, suggesting age-dependent phenotypic expression)
- **Abnormal mitochondrial morphology** in patient-derived fibroblasts (reported in PMID: 32467598 and PMID: 37317634)
- **Choreodystonia** (variable; PMID: 37317634)
- **Progressive psychomotor deterioration** (PMID: 37317634)

One patient reported in PMID: 29392776 presented with infantile-onset chorea as the predominant movement disorder, broadening the phenotypic spectrum to include hereditary chorea.

**Predominant variant type:**
Both loss-of-function (LoF) and missense variants have been reported. LoF variants include a homozygous nonsense variant (c.1180C>T; p.Gln394*) and a splice-site variant producing an out-of-frame transcript lacking exon 22 (c.1922+5G>A), both reported in PMID: 32467598. Missense variants include p.Pro149Leu (PMID: 32467598), p.Asp480Gly in the GAF-B domain with severely reduced enzymatic activity confirmed functionally (PMID: 29392776), and p.Phe505Ser (or p.Phe497Ser in an alternate transcript numbering) identified as a founder variant in three consanguineous Pakistani families (PMID: 37317634; ASHG 2022 – Fatima et al.). Functional studies confirm that missense variants cause loss of enzymatic function, supporting a unified LoF mechanism across variant types.

**Cohort origin:**
Patients originate from France (PMID: 32467598; PMID: 29392776) and Pakistan (PMID: 37317634; ASHG 2022 – Fatima et al.). None of the reported patients are identified as belonging to established research cohorts such as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL.

**Inheritance:**
All reported patients carry **biallelic variants** (homozygous or compound heterozygous). No dominant (heterozygous) pathogenic variants have been reported in affected individuals. Heterozygous carriers within families are reported as unaffected.

**Strength of clinical evidence (for autosomal recessive disease): MODERATE**
The evidence is supported by multiple independent reports from geographically distinct populations, functional validation of variants, and consistent phenotypic overlap. However, the total number of reported patients remains small (~10–16), no patients are from large systematic cohorts, and the literature base is limited to a handful of publications. For the specifically requested **autosomal dominant** mechanism, the clinical evidence is **NONE**.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:**
PDE2A has been studied in mouse (*Mus musculus*), rat (*Rattus norvegicus*), and zebrafish (*Danio rerio*).

**Key findings from knockout and knockdown models:**

*Homozygous knockout (mouse):* Complete loss of PDE2A is **embryonic lethal** in mice, confirmed independently by two studies (PMID: 32326334; PMID: 29409032) and corroborated by a third reporting sub-Mendelian ratios of homozygous pups (PMID: 38154608). At E14.5, PDE2A-null embryos exhibit severely reduced liver size, loss of hepatic niche integrity, compromised hematopoiesis, and apoptosis in hepatoblasts and endothelial/stromal compartments, with increased intracellular cAMP and upregulation of the pro-apoptotic transcriptional repressor ICER (PMID: 32326334). Separately, PDE2A-null embryos develop severe congenital cardiac defects including absence of atrial trabeculation, interventricular septum defects, hypertrabeculation, myocardial wall thinning, and occasional overriding aorta, with mechanistic evidence pointing to dysregulated cAMP/ICER/Tbx signaling in cardiac progenitors (PMID: 29409032). The embryonic lethality of homozygous knockouts in mice is consistent with the severe, multi-system disease observed in human patients with biallelic variants.

*Heterozygous knockout (mouse):* Pde2a+/- mice are viable and fertile, with no gross histological abnormalities (PMID: 39604498). However, they display **sex-dependent socio-cognitive deficits**: males show severe deficits throughout life, while females show milder adult-onset cognitive impairment (PMID: 38154608). Male Pde2a+/- mice also exhibit microglia activation, elevated glutathione, increased GluR1 externalization in hippocampal CA1, and reduced mGluR-dependent long-term depression. Conversely, a separate study of Pde2a+/- mice reported **enhanced** spatial and object recognition memory, increased density of dendritic mushroom and thin spines in hippocampal CA3 and dentate gyrus, and a significantly longer lifespan compared to wild-type littermates (PMID: 39604498). These apparently contradictory findings in heterozygous mice may reflect differences in genetic background, behavioral paradigms, or the specific cognitive domains assessed, and importantly, **neither study reports a phenotype in heterozygous mice that recapitulates the severe human neurodevelopmental syndrome**, which is observed exclusively in biallelic cases.

*Knockdown models:* Pharmacological and genetic knockdown of PDE2A in rodent models has demonstrated roles in acute lung injury (PMID: 21571906), Fragile X Syndrome pathophysiology (PMID: 30137253), and cAMP dynamics in cardiomyocytes (PMID: 37296663). In the context of Parkinson's disease, PDE2A mediates cGMP-dependent downregulation of hypersensitive cAMP/PKA responses in striatal neurons, directly relevant to the dyskinesia phenotype observed in human patients (PMID: 40412662).

*Zebrafish:* A de novo PDE2A variant was identified in a patient with congenital vertebral anomalies, with zebrafish models planned but not yet reported (ASHG 2013 – Exome sequencing in congenital vertebral anomalies). This single abstract does not constitute robust evidence.

**Consistency with human phenotype:**
The embryonic lethality of homozygous mouse knockouts is broadly consistent with the severity of biallelic human disease. The neurological and cognitive phenotypes in heterozygous mice provide mechanistic plausibility for PDE2A's role in brain function, but do not model the human syndrome, which requires biallelic loss. The striatal cAMP/PKA dysregulation observed in PDE2A-deficient mice (PMID: 40412662) provides a compelling mechanistic link to the paroxysmal dyskinesia phenotype in human patients.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**
The homozygous lethality and cardiac/hepatic developmental defects in mice strongly support PDE2A's essential developmental role. Heterozygous mouse phenotypes provide neurobiological plausibility but do not model the human disease. Evidence for a dominant-negative or haploinsufficiency mechanism causing neurodevelopmental disease in heterozygous animals is absent.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Pathways and molecular function:**
PDE2A is a dual-specificity phosphodiesterase that degrades both cAMP and cGMP. Its defining biochemical feature is allosteric stimulation by cGMP binding to the GAF-B domain, which increases cAMP hydrolysis up to tenfold, creating a cross-talk mechanism between the two second messenger systems. PDE2A thereby acts as a critical regulator of compartmentalized cyclic nucleotide signaling downstream of G protein-coupled receptors, including dopamine receptors (D1/D2), beta-adrenergic receptors, and nitric oxide/cGMP pathways. Loss of PDE2A leads to elevated intracellular cAMP, upregulation of ICER (an inducible cAMP early repressor), and downstream effects on transcription factors including Tbx family members and anti-apoptotic genes such as Bcl2 (PMID: 29409032; PMID: 32326334). In the striatum, PDE2A activation by cGMP moderates hypersensitive D1 receptor-driven cAMP/PKA responses, directly linking PDE2A function to the regulation of basal ganglia circuitry relevant to movement disorders (PMID: 40412662).

**Expression pattern:**
PDE2A is broadly expressed, with particularly high levels in the brain (cerebellum, neocortex, hippocampus, striatum, amygdala), heart, liver, kidney, lung, and vascular endothelium. Within the brain, PDE2A mRNA expression increases across development and is regionally altered in psychiatric disease: reduced in the amygdala in schizophrenia, MDD, and bipolar disorder; reduced in frontal cortex in schizophrenia; and reduced in hippocampus and striatum in bipolar disorder (PMID: 32119913). In the vasculature, PDE2A protein is selectively expressed in venous and capillary endothelial cells but not arterial endothelial cells (PMID: 10375378). The high and developmentally regulated expression of PDE2A in brain regions critical for cognition and movement strongly supports its neurodevelopmental relevance.

**Key experimental findings:**
- Functional studies confirm that the missense variant p.Asp480Gly (GAF-B domain) causes severely decreased PDE2A enzymatic activity, supporting a loss-of-function mechanism for missense variants (PMID: 29392776).
- Patient-derived fibroblasts from individuals with biallelic PDE2A variants show abnormal mitochondrial network morphology, providing a cellular biomarker of disease (PMID: 32467598; PMID: 37317634).
- PDE2A overexpression in a heart failure model restored cAMP compartmentation and reduced arrhythmia, demonstrating the functional importance of PDE2A in regulating subcellular cAMP microdomains (PMID: 38776406).
- PDE2A inhibition enhanced post-stroke axonal sprouting and functional connectivity in mice, highlighting its role in neuronal plasticity (PMID: 36163142).
- Increased PDE2A activity in Fmr1-KO mice (Fragile X model) contributes to reduced cAMP/cGMP and synaptic dysfunction; pharmacological inhibition rescued multiple disease phenotypes (PMID: 30137253).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
PDE2A has a well-characterized molecular function, robust brain expression data, and multiple lines of experimental evidence linking its loss to dysregulated cyclic nucleotide signaling, synaptic dysfunction, and movement disorder-relevant striatal pathophysiology. The functional validation of patient variants and the mitochondrial morphology findings in patient fibroblasts further strengthen the biological case.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level for PDE2A as an autosomal recessive neurodevelopmental disorder gene: MODERATE**

**Overall evidence level for PDE2A as an autosomal dominant (heterozygous) neurodevelopmental disorder gene: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
- Multiple independent case reports from geographically distinct populations (France, Pakistan) with consistent core phenotypes (paroxysmal dyskinesia, intellectual disability, developmental delay, seizures).
- Functional validation of variants through enzymatic activity assays (PMID: 29392776), transcript analysis (PMID: 32467598), and patient fibroblast studies (PMID: 32467598; PMID: 37317634).
- Strong biological plausibility: PDE2A is highly expressed in brain regions relevant to the human phenotype, and its role in striatal cAMP/PKA regulation directly links to the paroxysmal dyskinesia phenotype (PMID: 40412662).
- Consistent embryonic lethality in homozygous mouse knockouts supports the severity of biallelic human disease.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- The total number of reported patients remains small (~10–16), limiting phenotypic characterization and genotype-phenotype correlations.
- No patients have been identified through large systematic cohorts (UDN, DDD, GEL, GREGoR), which would substantially strengthen the evidence base.
- **No heterozygous (autosomal dominant) pathogenic variants have been reported in affected individuals.** Heterozygous carriers in reported families are unaffected, and heterozygous mice show either subtle cognitive enhancement or mild sex-dependent deficits rather than a severe neurodevelopmental syndrome.
- The apparent discrepancy between two heterozygous mouse studies (cognitive deficits in PMID: 38154608 vs. cognitive enhancement in PMID: 39604498) is unresolved and complicates interpretation of haploinsufficiency effects.
- The ASHG 2022 abstract (Fatima et al.) likely overlaps with PMID: 37317634, meaning the Pakistani cohort may not represent fully independent replication.

**Conclusion:**
PDE2A is a well-supported autosomal recessive neurodevelopmental disorder gene, with biallelic loss-of-function and damaging missense variants causing a syndrome of childhood-onset paroxysmal dyskinesia, intellectual disability, developmental delay, and seizures, supported by functional variant validation and consistent model organism data. However, the evidence specifically for an **autosomal dominant** disease mechanism is currently **absent**: all reported patients carry biallelic variants, heterozygous carriers are unaffected, and heterozygous mouse models do not recapitulate the human syndrome. Any clinical interpretation of heterozygous PDE2A variants as pathogenic should be approached with caution and would require substantial additional evidence, including de novo occurrence in affected individuals, segregation data, and functional studies demonstrating dominant-negative or gain-of-function effects.

3. A homozygous founder variant in PDE2A causes paroxysmal dyskinesia with intellectual disability. (PMID: 37317634)
4. Motor, epileptic, and developmental phenotypes in genetic disorders affecting G protein coupled receptors-cAMP signaling. (PMID: 36003298)

### Biological Evidence
1. Phosphodiesterases PDE2A and PDE10A both change mRNA expression in the human brain with age, but only PDE2A changes in a region-specific manner with psychiatric disease (PMID: 32119913)

4. PDE2A Inhibition Enhances Axonal Sprouting, Functional Connectivity, and Recovery after Stroke (PMID: 36163142)
