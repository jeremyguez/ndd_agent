# Evidence Report: ARID4A as a Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene (Autosomal Dominant / Heterozygous Mechanism)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

ARID4A (AT-Rich Interaction Domain 4A; also known as RBBP1) encodes a chromatin remodeling protein that functions as a transcriptional coregulator, participating in SIN3-histone deacetylase complexes, epigenetic regulation of genomic imprinting, and—more recently recognized—post-transcriptional mRNA stabilization via direct RNA binding. It interacts with the retinoblastoma protein (RB) and androgen receptor (AR) pathways and plays roles in cell cycle control, hematopoiesis, and suppression of tumor progression.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports:** Three independent lines of clinical evidence have been identified.

**Total approximate number of patients:** Approximately 7–8 individuals with ARID4A variants in the context of neurodevelopmental or neuropsychiatric phenotypes, plus one patient with a larger chromosomal deletion encompassing ARID4A.

- The largest and most directly relevant series is an ASHG 2017 abstract (Monaghan et al., ASHG 2017) reporting 6 unrelated individuals identified through trio-based clinical exome sequencing of 11,649 probands at GeneDx and collaborating institutions. All 6 carried de novo ARID4A variants (1 frameshift, 5 missense; one missense possibly paternally mosaic).
- A single case report described a de novo 14q23.1–q23.3 microdeletion encompassing ARID4A and multiple other genes in a patient with multiple congenital anomalies, intellectual disability, neuroblastoma, congenital heart defect, epilepsy, and hypoplasia of the corpus callosum (PMID: 24665034). ARID4A haploinsufficiency was discussed as a potential contributor to the neurodevelopmental phenotype, though the deletion is multigenic and causality cannot be attributed to ARID4A alone.
- A study from China identified a heterozygous missense variant (c.1231C>G, p.His411Asp) by whole-exome sequencing in Han Chinese families with schizophrenia-affected siblings (PMID: 35365808). While schizophrenia is a neuropsychiatric rather than classic neurodevelopmental disorder, this finding extends the potential phenotypic spectrum of ARID4A variation to brain-related conditions.

**Most common phenotypes observed (from the Monaghan et al. series):** Developmental delay (6/6), behavioral abnormalities (6/6), autism spectrum disorder (4/6), tone abnormalities (4/6), developmental regression (3/6), intellectual disability, and brain structural anomalies on MRI including thin corpus callosum, absent septum pellucidum, holoprosencephaly, and pontocerebellar atrophy (2/4 tested). Facial dysmorphism was also noted.

**Predominant variant type:** Both loss-of-function (1 frameshift) and missense variants (5 cases) were identified in the primary neurodevelopmental series. All were de novo (or possibly mosaic in one case). The missense variants were supported by damaging in silico predictions and evolutionary conservation of affected residues. The schizophrenia-associated variant was also a heterozygous missense with functional support.

**Cohort origin:** The 6 neurodevelopmental cases were identified through GeneDx clinical exome sequencing and collaborating institutions (USA); they do not appear to derive from named research cohorts such as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL.

**Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED**

The primary evidence rests on a single conference abstract (not peer-reviewed full publication) describing 6 cases, supplemented by one multigenic deletion case and one neuropsychiatric family study. The absence of a peer-reviewed case series with detailed clinical and molecular characterization, the small total patient number, and the conference-abstract format of the key report substantially limit the strength of clinical evidence at this time. Nonetheless, the de novo occurrence across 6 unrelated individuals from a large exome cohort is a meaningful signal.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*); in vitro cell line models (human prostate cancer and breast cancer cell lines).

**Key findings from mouse knockouts:**

- Homozygous *Arid4a* knockout mice (*Arid4a*−/−) are viable but develop progressive hematopoietic failure, progressing from ineffective hematopoiesis to a chronic myelomonocytic leukemia (CMML)-like myelodysplastic/myeloproliferative disorder, with 5/42 mice transforming to acute myeloid leukemia (AML). Compound *Arid4a*−/−*Arid4b*+/− mice showed dramatically accelerated AML transformation (10/12 mice), with associated dysregulation of Hox genes and marked increases in histone trimethylation of H3K4, H3K9, and H4K20 in bone marrow (PMID: 18728284).
- *Arid4a*−/−*Arid4b*+/− male mice exhibit progressive infertility, hypogonadism, seminal vesicle agenesis, spermatogenic arrest, and blood-testis barrier disruption, phenocopying Sertoli cell-specific AR and RB knockouts (PMID: 23487765).
- In a TDP-43 knock-in mouse model of ALS-FTD, downregulation of *Arid4a* was associated with an improved behavioral phenotype, identifying it as a candidate suppressor of neurodegeneration (PMID: 29556029). This is an indirect and associative finding but provides a link between ARID4A expression levels and neurological outcomes in a mammalian model.

**Neurological phenotype consistency with human disease:** The mouse knockout models do not recapitulate a primary neurodevelopmental phenotype directly analogous to the human syndrome described in the Monaghan et al. series. The hematopoietic and fertility phenotypes are prominent in homozygous or compound heterozygous animals, whereas the human cases involve heterozygous de novo variants. The ALS-FTD model association is indirect. No dedicated heterozygous (*Arid4a*+/−) neurodevelopmental mouse model has been reported in the available evidence.

**Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED**

While robust mouse data exist for hematopoietic and reproductive phenotypes under homozygous or compound loss conditions, direct neurological or neurodevelopmental phenotyping of heterozygous *Arid4a* mice has not been reported. The indirect ALS-FTD association (PMID: 29556029) provides a tentative neurological link but is insufficient to strongly support the human neurodevelopmental phenotype.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

ARID4A is a multifunctional chromatin and RNA regulatory protein. It participates in SIN3-histone deacetylase (HDAC) chromatin remodeling complexes, where it interacts with the metastasis suppressor BRMS1 to regulate transcriptional repression of target genes including osteopontin and EGFR (PMID: 18211900). It functions as a transcriptional coactivator for the androgen receptor (AR) and retinoblastoma protein (RB) in Sertoli cells (PMID: 23487765). In the context of genomic imprinting, ARID4A (RBBP1) interacts with ARID4B at the Snrpn promoter and regulates epigenetic marks (H4K20me3, H3K9me3, DNA methylation) at the Prader-Willi/Angelman syndrome (PWS/AS) imprinting control region; combined deficiency of Arid4a and Arid4b suppressed an Angelman syndrome imprinting defect in mice (PMID: 17043311). More recently, ARID4A has been characterized as an RNA-binding protein that stabilizes metastasis-suppressing transcripts (MTSS1, TIMP2, Rb1, PTEN) via binding conserved structural elements in their 3'UTRs, with its ARID domain required for this function (PMID: 40066676).

**Neurodevelopmental and brain relevance:**

The involvement of ARID4A in PWS/AS imprinting regulation (PMID: 17043311) is directly relevant to neurodevelopmental biology, as PWS and AS are paradigmatic neurodevelopmental imprinting disorders. The structural characterization of the PWWP-ARID tandem domain supramodule and the demonstration that the schizophrenia-associated p.His411Asp variant disrupts dsDNA binding and alters cell cycle progression (PMID: 35365808) provide mechanistic plausibility for neuropsychiatric effects of ARID4A variants. ARID4A expression is regulated by reactive oxygen species (ROS) via miR-302 in human fibroblasts, linking it to oxidative stress-responsive cell cycle control (PMID: 22732186), a pathway relevant to neurodevelopment. ARID4A is also regulated by miR-30d in prostate cancer (PMID: 29797600) and miR-376c in gastric carcinoma (PMID: 28486502), indicating broad post-transcriptional regulatory integration.

**Key experimental findings supporting pathogenicity:**

- The p.His411Asp schizophrenia variant caused a conformational change in the ARID domain, reduced dsDNA-binding affinity, decreased G0/G1 arrest, and promoted cell proliferation in HEK293T cells (PMID: 35365808), demonstrating that single heterozygous missense variants can produce measurable functional consequences.
- Loss of ARID4A in hematopoietic cells causes epigenetic dysregulation (altered H3K4, H3K9, H4K20 trimethylation) and transcriptional changes in Hox genes and FoxP3 (PMID: 18728284), consistent with a broad chromatin regulatory role.
- ARID4A's role in stabilizing RB1 and PTEN mRNAs (PMID: 40066676) is notable given the established roles of these tumor suppressors in neurodevelopment and brain growth.

**Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE**

There is substantial mechanistic evidence for ARID4A's roles in chromatin remodeling, epigenetic regulation, cell cycle control, and mRNA stability. Its involvement in PWS/AS imprinting and the functional impact of a disease-associated missense variant on DNA binding and cell cycle provide biologically plausible mechanisms for neurodevelopmental pathogenicity. However, direct experimental evidence linking ARID4A function specifically to neuronal development, brain morphogenesis, or synaptic biology is currently limited.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: LIMITED**

**Key strengths:**
- Six unrelated individuals with de novo ARID4A variants (including one LoF frameshift) sharing a consistent neurodevelopmental phenotype (developmental delay, autism, intellectual disability, brain anomalies) were identified from a large clinical exome cohort of 11,649 probands (ASHG 2017, Monaghan et al.), providing a meaningful statistical signal for a rare gene.
- Functional studies of a disease-associated missense variant demonstrate measurable molecular consequences (altered DNA binding, cell cycle dysregulation) (PMID: 35365808), supporting biological plausibility.
- ARID4A's established role in PWS/AS imprinting regulation (PMID: 17043311) provides a direct mechanistic link to neurodevelopmental epigenetic pathways.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- The primary neurodevelopmental evidence derives from a conference abstract (ASHG 2017) that has not, to the knowledge reflected in this evidence base, been published as a peer-reviewed full paper, limiting critical appraisal of variant details, segregation data, and phenotypic characterization.
- No dedicated heterozygous mouse model with neurodevelopmental phenotyping has been reported, leaving the haploinsufficiency mechanism in the brain unvalidated in vivo.
- The total number of reported patients with ARID4A-associated neurodevelopmental disorder remains very small (approximately 6–7 individuals), and independent replication in additional cohorts or published case series is lacking.

**Conclusion:** ARID4A shows a promising but currently limited evidence base as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with an autosomal dominant (haploinsufficiency) mechanism. The convergence of de novo variant occurrence in a consistent neurodevelopmental phenotype, functional impact of missense variants on DNA binding and cell cycle, and the gene's established role in epigenetic regulation of neurodevelopmentally relevant imprinting loci collectively support further investigation. Independent replication through peer-reviewed publication of additional cases, dedicated heterozygous mouse neurodevelopmental studies, and neuronal functional assays are needed to elevate the evidence to a MODERATE or STRONG classification.

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. Multiple congenital anomalies-intellectual disability (MCA-ID) and neuroblastoma in a patient harboring a de novo 14q23.1q23.3 deletion. (PMID: 24665034)
2. A novel heterozygous missense variant of the ARID4A gene identified in Han Chinese families with schizophrenia-diagnosed siblings that interferes with DNA-binding activity. (PMID: 35365808)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. Identification of chromatin remodeling genes Arid4a and Arid4b as leukemia suppressor genes. (PMID: 18728284)
2. ARID4A and ARID4B regulate male fertility, a functional link to the AR and RB pathways. (PMID: 23487765)
3. TDP-43 gains function due to perturbed autoregulation in a Tardbp knock-in mouse model of ALS-FTD. (PMID: 29556029)
4. Downregulation of ARID4A and ARID4B promote tumor progression and directly regulated by microRNA-30d in patient with prostate cancer. (PMID: 29797600)

### Biological Evidence
1. Alterations of BRMS1-ARID4A interaction modify gene expression but still suppress metastasis in human breast cancer cells. (PMID: 18211900)
2. Deficiency of Rbbp1/Arid4a and Rbbp1l1/Arid4b alters epigenetic modifications and suppresses an imprinting defect in the PWS/AS domain. (PMID: 17043311)
3. Arid4a Suppresses Breast Tumor Metastasis by Enhancing MTSS1 Expression via mRNA Stability. (PMID: 40066676)
4. Reactive oxygen species mediate microRNA-302 regulation of AT-rich interacting domain 4a and C-C motif ligand 5 expression during transitions between quiescence and proliferation. (PMID: 22732186)
5. miR-376c promotes carcinogenesis and serves as a plasma marker for gastric carcinoma. (PMID: 28486502)
