# Evidence Report: HLA-F as a Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene (Autosomal Dominant)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

HLA-F (Human Leukocyte Antigen F) is a non-classical MHC class Ib molecule encoded within the major histocompatibility complex on chromosome 6p21.3. Its primary known functions include immune modulation through interactions with inhibitory receptors (ILT2/LILRB1 and ILT4/LILRB2) and KIR receptors, regulation of immune tolerance at the maternal-fetal interface, and participation in NK cell biology and alloimmune responses.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
HLA-F functions as a non-classical MHC class Ib immune checkpoint molecule. It can exist as open conformers (peptide-free) or peptide-bound forms, and interacts with inhibitory receptors ILT2 and ILT4 (PMID: 12213342), as well as activating KIR receptors. HLA-F expression is regulated by noncoding SNPs in its upstream region and in the antisense lncRNA HLA-F-AS1, with confirmed effects on mRNA and protein levels in PBMCs (PMID: 37166140). At the protein level, HLA-F localizes to the endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus under basal conditions and traffics to the cell surface upon cellular activation.

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**
Available expression data focus on reproductive tissues and immune cells. HLA-F is expressed in mid-secretory endometrium, fluctuating across the menstrual cycle with peak levels during the implantation window (PMID: 35234898), and in placental decidua, where it is reduced in preeclampsia (PMID: 37854606). HLA-F mRNA has been reported in most cell types broadly (PMID: 37166140), and is significantly upregulated in glioblastoma tissue compared to non-tumor brain controls (PMID: 35181585); however, this represents a cancer context rather than normal neurodevelopmental expression. No evidence was identified for HLA-F expression in developing brain tissue, neurons, or neural progenitor cells in a physiologically relevant neurodevelopmental context.

**Key experimental findings:**
Functional studies in trophoblast cell lines (Jar cells) demonstrate that HLA-F overexpression promotes cell proliferation, invasion, and migration, while knockdown has the opposite effect (PMID: 37854606). Mechanistically, HLA-F was shown to bind the PKM gene promoter (via ChIP-seq) and upregulate PKM2-dependent glycolysis, with PKM2 silencing abolishing HLA-F-mediated proliferation (PMID: 40251569). In NK-92MI cells, HLA-F overexpression increased secretion of immunoregulatory cytokines CSF1 and CCL22 and promoted adaptive NKG2C+ NK cell transformation (PMID: 37854606). Genetic polymorphisms in HLA-F regulatory regions are associated with alloimmunisation susceptibility in sickle cell disease (PMID: 37686397) and with nasopharyngeal carcinoma risk (GWAS: p = 7.36×10⁻¹¹ and 3.33×10⁻¹⁰; ASHG 2010 - Su et al.). The antisense transcript HLA-F-AS1 has been separately implicated in colorectal cancer progression via a ceRNA mechanism (PMID: 31863778), though this is distinct from HLA-F protein function itself.

None of these experimental findings relate to neuronal function, synaptic biology, brain development, or pathways conventionally associated with neurodevelopmental disorders (e.g., chromatin remodeling, transcriptional regulation in neurons, axon guidance, or mTOR/PI3K signaling in the CNS).

- **Strength of biological evidence (for neurodevelopmental relevance): NONE**

While the biological evidence for HLA-F's role in immune tolerance and reproductive biology is substantive, there is no biological evidence supporting a role in neurodevelopment.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

- **Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths of the evidence:**
HLA-F has well-characterized molecular functions in immune modulation, with documented receptor interactions (PMID: 12213342), genotype-expression relationships (PMID: 37166140), and functional roles in trophoblast biology (PMID: 37854606; PMID: 40251569). GWAS data link HLA-F variants to disease susceptibility in specific immune contexts (PMID: 37686397; ASHG 2010 - Su et al.), demonstrating that genetic variation at this locus can have phenotypic consequences.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
There are no clinical cases, case series, or patient cohort data linking HLA-F variants to neurodevelopmental disorders. No model organism studies have examined neurodevelopmental or neurological phenotypes. No biological evidence supports expression or function of HLA-F in the developing nervous system. The gene's established biology is centered on immune tolerance, reproductive immunology, and cancer immune evasion — domains entirely distinct from neurodevelopment.

**Conclusion:**
The current body of evidence provides no support for HLA-F as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with autosomal dominant pathogenic variants. All available evidence pertains to immune and reproductive biology, with no clinical, model organism, or mechanistic data linking HLA-F to brain development or neurodevelopmental phenotypes. This gene-disease hypothesis should be considered unsupported at this time, and any putative association would require de novo evidence from patient cohort studies, functional neurobiological experiments, and/or model organism phenotyping before further evaluation is warranted.
