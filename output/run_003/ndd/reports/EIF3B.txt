# EIF3B – Literature Evidence Report for Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene Candidacy

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

EIF3B (Eukaryotic Translation Initiation Factor 3 Subunit B) encodes a core structural subunit of the eukaryotic initiation factor 3 (eIF3) complex, the largest of the translation initiation factors, which plays a central role in ribosome assembly, mRNA recruitment, and the initiation of cap-dependent protein synthesis. EIF3B is highly conserved across species and is considered a scaffolding subunit of the eIF3 complex, making it essential for global translational regulation during development.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports / case series:**
Two citations are available in the structured evidence, both referencing the same primary publication describing a novel neurodevelopmental syndrome associated with EIF3B loss-of-function variants (PMID: 41033306; PMID: 41679311). These two PMIDs appear to represent the same study or closely related publications (possibly a preprint and its peer-reviewed version), and should be treated as a single case series rather than two independent reports.

**Total approximate number of patients:**
14 individuals with EIF3B loss-of-function variants are described across this case series (PMID: 41033306).

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
The cardinal features reported across affected individuals include:
- Cardiac defects (structural cardiovascular anomalies)
- Craniofacial dysmorphisms
- Mild developmental delays
- Behavioral abnormalities

This phenotypic constellation — cardiovascular, craniofacial, and neurodevelopmental involvement — defines a novel autosomal dominant syndrome. The cardiac phenotype is further supported by the observation that EIF3B maps within the minimally overlapping chromosomal region implicated in cardiac anomalies associated with 7p22.3 microdeletions (PMID: 41033306).

**Predominant variant type:**
All reported variants are loss-of-function (LoF) in nature, occurring de novo in affected individuals. EIF3B is noted to be highly constrained against loss-of-function variation in population databases, strengthening the pathogenic interpretation of these variants.

**Known cohort affiliation:**
Patients were identified through international matchmaking efforts across multiple centers. No specific named cohorts (e.g., UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL) are explicitly mentioned in the available evidence.

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**
A cohort of 14 individuals with a consistent phenotype and de novo LoF variants in a constrained gene represents meaningful clinical evidence. However, the evidence currently derives from a single publication/case series, specific variant-level data (e.g., HGVS notations, segregation details) are not provided in the available abstracts, and independent replication in a separate cohort has not yet been documented in the extracted literature.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:**
Zebrafish (*Danio rerio*) have been used as the primary model organism to investigate EIF3B function in vivo (PMID: 41033306).

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**
Full knockout of the zebrafish ortholog *eif3ba* resulted in:
- Thin heart tubes (consistent with the cardiac defects observed in human patients)
- Absence of craniofacial cartilage (consistent with craniofacial dysmorphisms in humans)
- Embryonic lethality
- Broad developmental abnormalities

**Consistency with human phenotype:**
The zebrafish model demonstrates strong phenotypic concordance with the human syndrome. The cardiac and craniofacial abnormalities observed in *eif3ba*-deficient zebrafish directly mirror the two most prominent organ systems affected in human patients. Embryonic lethality in the full knockout is consistent with the essential developmental role of EIF3B and is not unexpected for a complete loss of a core translation initiation factor; heterozygous human patients with partial loss of function would be expected to survive with a milder phenotype, which is indeed observed.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**
The zebrafish data provide direct functional validation of EIF3B's role in cardiac and craniofacial development, with phenotypes that closely recapitulate the human disorder. Limitations include the use of a single organism (zebrafish only), absence of reported sample sizes or statistical metrics, and the fact that neurological/behavioral phenotypes — prominent in human patients — were not specifically described in the zebrafish model. Mouse knockout data are not available in the current evidence base.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
EIF3B functions as a core subunit of the eIF3 complex and participates in canonical cap-dependent translation initiation. Beyond this canonical role, several studies have identified non-canonical functions of EIF3B relevant to cellular signaling and post-translational regulation:

- EIF3B is recruited by the m6A reader YTHDF2 to facilitate cap-independent translation of specific mRNAs (e.g., ETV5) by recognizing m6A modifications in their 5'-UTR, linking EIF3B to epitranscriptomic regulation of translation (PMID: 38247171).
- EIF3B physically interacts with MAP2K2 and stabilizes it by inhibiting VHL-mediated ubiquitination, thereby activating ERK/MAPK signaling (PMID: 40691141).
- EIF3B interacts with METTL3 to form a complex that promotes EGFR/AKT signaling in cervical cancer (PMID: 40576144).
- EIF3B acts as a downstream effector of the FOXA1/PUS1 axis, with PUS1 protecting EIF3B from proteasomal degradation (PMID: 39247811).
- EIF3B stabilizes PCNA by counteracting SYVN1-mediated ubiquitination, implicating it in DNA replication fidelity and cell cycle progression (PMID: 38687509).
- EIF3B mediates selective sorting of miR-100-5p into exosomes, revealing a non-canonical role in miRNA packaging (PMID: 40681642).
- eIF3b depletion inhibits G1-S cell cycle transition by altering protein levels of cyclin A, cyclin E, Rb, and p27Kip1, and disrupts integrin α5-mediated cytoskeletal organization (PMID: 23575475).
- eIF3b knockdown in chronic myelogenous leukemia cell lines reduced cell viability by ~50% and increased apoptosis ~8-fold, with C3G identified as a downstream transcriptional target (PMID: 32236758).
- Elevated eIF3B downstream of the miR-23a/27a/24-2 cluster downregulates MHC-I expression, contributing to immune evasion in non-small cell lung cancer (PMID: 39736629).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**
The available biological evidence is predominantly derived from cancer biology studies. Direct data on EIF3B expression in the developing brain or nervous system are not represented in the extracted evidence. However, the gene's role as a core translation initiation factor implies broad and essential expression during embryogenesis, and its high constraint against LoF in population databases (noted in the clinical evidence) is consistent with critical expression during neurodevelopment.

**Key experimental findings relevant to the neurodevelopmental syndrome:**
The most directly relevant biological evidence is the zebrafish functional data (discussed above) and the observation that EIF3B resides within the 7p22.3 microdeletion region associated with cardiac anomalies, providing genomic context for its developmental role (PMID: 41033306). The broader cancer literature establishes that EIF3B is not merely a passive translation factor but actively participates in protein stability, cell cycle regulation, and signaling pathway modulation — functions that are plausibly relevant to neurodevelopmental processes.

**Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED**
While EIF3B has a well-established molecular function as a translation initiation factor and an emerging set of non-canonical roles, the available biological evidence in the context of neurodevelopment specifically is limited. The majority of mechanistic studies are conducted in cancer cell lines and are not directly informative about the pathomechanism underlying the neurodevelopmental syndrome. No brain-specific expression studies, neuronal differentiation assays, or patient-derived cell line experiments are represented in the current evidence base.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths:**
- A cohort of 14 individuals with a consistent and distinctive phenotypic triad (cardiac defects, craniofacial dysmorphisms, neurodevelopmental delay/behavioral abnormalities) and de novo loss-of-function variants in a highly constrained gene provides a credible clinical signal (PMID: 41033306).
- Zebrafish functional data directly recapitulate the cardiac and craniofacial components of the human phenotype, providing in vivo mechanistic support (PMID: 41033306).
- The autosomal dominant inheritance pattern, de novo occurrence of variants, and high population-level constraint of EIF3B collectively strengthen the pathogenic interpretation.
- Genomic context (EIF3B within the 7p22.3 cardiac anomaly microdeletion region) provides additional independent support.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- All clinical evidence derives from a single publication/case series; independent replication in a separate cohort is not yet documented.
- Specific variant-level data (HGVS notations, functional impact of individual variants) are not available in the extracted evidence, limiting variant-level interpretation.
- Biological evidence directly relevant to neurodevelopment (brain expression, neuronal function, patient-derived cell studies) is absent; existing mechanistic data are largely from cancer models.
- Neurological/behavioral phenotypes observed in human patients are not recapitulated or investigated in the zebrafish model, leaving the neurodevelopmental mechanism unexplained.
- Mouse knockout data, which would provide a more translationally relevant mammalian model, are not available.

**Conclusion:**
EIF3B represents a credible candidate gene for a novel autosomal dominant cardiovascular, craniofacial, and neurodevelopmental syndrome, supported by a cohort of 14 individuals with de novo loss-of-function variants and concordant zebrafish model phenotypes (PMID: 41033306). The evidence is currently at a MODERATE level, primarily constrained by the absence of independent clinical replication and limited neurodevelopment-specific mechanistic data. Further studies — including additional patient identification through matchmaking registries, detailed variant-level functional assays, and neurobiological characterization in mammalian models — would be required to elevate the evidence to a STRONG classification and support definitive gene-disease curation.
