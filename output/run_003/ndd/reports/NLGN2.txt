# NLGN2 Literature Evidence Report: Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene Assessment

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

NLGN2 (Neuroligin-2) encodes a postsynaptic cell adhesion protein that is the only member of the neuroligin family to function exclusively at GABAergic inhibitory synapses, where it forms transsynaptic bridges with presynaptic neurexins to regulate inhibitory synapse formation and function (PMID: 33443230). Through its interactions with scaffolding proteins such as gephyrin and its role in organizing postsynaptic GABA receptor complexes, NLGN2 is a central orchestrator of inhibitory synaptic transmission in the brain.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent case reports/series:** Four to five independent clinical reports were identified, though two (PMID: 32405903 and ASHG 2019 – Shillington et al.) likely describe the same patient, reducing the number of truly independent cases to approximately four.

**Total approximate number of patients:** Approximately 4–5 patients with de novo heterozygous NLGN2 variants have been reported in the literature.

**Most common phenotypes observed:** Across the reported cases, the most consistently observed phenotypes include autism spectrum disorder (ASD), intellectual disability/developmental delay, anxiety, hyperphagia, and language impairment. Additional features reported in individual cases include obsessive-compulsive behaviors, macrocephaly, obesity, catatonia, developmental regression, and seizures (febrile and atypical absence). The co-occurrence of hyperphagia, anxiety, and ASD across multiple independent cases is a notable phenotypic signature.

**Predominant variant type:** Both loss-of-function (LoF) and missense variants have been reported. The most clearly characterized variant is a de novo heterozygous nonsense variant (c.441C>A, p.Tyr147Ter) identified by exome sequencing in a single patient with severe anxiety, ASD, intellectual disability, hyperphagia, and obesity (PMID: 27865048). The remaining reported cases carry de novo missense variants, though specific nucleotide/amino acid notations were not provided in the available abstracts (PMID: 32405903; PMID: 37506563; ASHG 2019 – Shillington et al.). One case (ASHG 2017 – McLaughlin et al.) carried a de novo NLGN2 missense variant alongside a de novo TAOK1 variant, complicating interpretation of NLGN2's independent contribution in that individual.

**Cohort affiliation:** None of the reported patients were explicitly identified as belonging to established diagnostic cohorts such as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL based on the available information.

**Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED**
The clinical evidence is limited by the very small number of reported patients (approximately 4–5 total), the absence of specific variant notations in several reports, and the potential overlap between case reports. However, the recurrence of de novo variants, the consistency of core phenotypic features across independent cases, and the identification of NLGN2 as a high-confidence NDD gene in a large-scale genomic study (PMID: 35468861; see Biological Evidence) collectively support its emerging clinical relevance.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** All model organism studies identified were conducted in mice (*Mus musculus*).

**Key phenotypes from knockouts and knock-ins:**
The mouse evidence is extensive and highly consistent across multiple independent laboratories and genetic models:

- *Full knockout (KO) mice* are viable but show sub-Mendelian birth ratios and reduced breeding efficiency (PMID: 22820233), suggesting partial prenatal lethality or reduced fitness. Core behavioral phenotypes include pronounced anxiety-like behavior (PMID: 26142252; PMID: 33960026), reduced exploratory activity, impaired motor performance, developmental milestone delays, and reduced ultrasonic vocalizations in pups (PMID: 22820233). Sleep architecture is profoundly disrupted, with increased wakefulness, reduced NREM and REM sleep, abnormal hypersynchronized EEG events, and altered slow-wave dynamics (PMID: 30231918; PMID: 38570872).

- *Conditional KO in medial prefrontal cortex* produced impaired anxiety behavior, fear memory, and social interaction, along with severely impaired experience-dependent gene expression in the mPFC (PMID: 25824299), demonstrating that NLGN2 loss in a specific cortical region is sufficient to produce cognitive and social deficits.

- *Knock-in mice carrying the R215H missense mutation* (a variant identified in human schizophrenia patients) showed near-complete loss of NLGN2 protein in vivo, reduced GABAergic transmission, anxiety-like behavior, impaired prepulse inhibition, and cognitive deficits (PMID: 29859117; PMID: 29230184), indicating that at least some missense variants act through a loss-of-function mechanism.

- Circuit-level studies demonstrated that NLGN2 deletion impairs inhibitory drive onto projection neurons in the basal amygdala, leading to exacerbated neuronal activation under anxiogenic conditions (PMID: 26142252), and disrupts theta and beta oscillatory synchrony in the vHPC-mPFC-BLA circuit (PMID: 33960026).

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The mouse phenotypes show strong convergence with the human clinical features. Anxiety, cognitive/intellectual impairment, abnormal social communication (reduced vocalizations), developmental delays, and seizure-related EEG abnormalities in mice parallel the anxiety, ASD, intellectual disability, language impairment, and seizures reported in human patients. Hyperphagia and obesity, prominent in human cases, have not been explicitly reported in mouse models, representing a potential gap.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent laboratories using full KO, conditional KO, and knock-in models consistently demonstrate neurological, behavioral, and electrophysiological phenotypes relevant to the human neurodevelopmental disorder phenotype.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:** NLGN2 is a type I transmembrane protein that functions as a postsynaptic organizer exclusively at GABAergic inhibitory synapses. It forms transsynaptic complexes with presynaptic α-neurexins and recruits postsynaptic scaffolding proteins, most critically gephyrin, to organize GABA-A receptor clustering (PMID: 33443230). Functional rescue experiments in neuroligin-null hippocampal neurons confirmed that NLGN2 selectively restores inhibitory synaptic transmission, with its extracellular domain conferring inhibitory synapse specificity and its cytoplasmic gephyrin-binding motif being essential for function (PMID: 39747663). NLGN2 also interacts with MDGA1, which modulates its function at inhibitory synapses; loss of MDGA1 ameliorates the synaptic and behavioral deficits caused by NLGN2 deletion, identifying cytosolic gephyrin aggregation as a potential downstream therapeutic target (PMID: 39284869). Additionally, NLGN2 physically associates with the serotonin transporter (SERT) in midbrain raphe neurons; NLGN2 loss reduces SERT expression and function and alters serotonergic neurotransmission (PMID: 26793096), providing a mechanistic link to anxiety and mood-related phenotypes.

**Expression pattern:** NLGN2 is expressed broadly in the brain, with documented roles in the amygdala, hippocampus, medial prefrontal cortex, and midbrain raphe nuclei. NLGN2 mRNA is significantly down-regulated in peripheral blood of both ASD and ADHD patients compared to controls (n=1,390; PMID: 37285744), suggesting that expression changes in NLGN2 may be detectable peripherally and reflect its role in neurodevelopmental pathology.

**Key experimental findings:**
- iPSC-derived cortical interneurons from schizophrenia patients showed reduced NLGN2 and gephyrin levels; NLGN2 overexpression rescued synaptic puncta deficits, while NLGN2 knockdown in healthy neurons recapitulated the deficit (PMID: 31780643), providing direct human cellular evidence for NLGN2's role in inhibitory synapse maintenance.
- A rare missense variant NLGN2:p.R309Q, identified by targeted sequencing in schizophrenia patients and absent in 1,517 controls, showed decreased protein expression in HEK293 cells, confirmed in an independent replication cohort (ASHG 2018 – Cheng et al.), supporting a loss-of-function mechanism for missense variants.
- Large-scale integrative analysis of de novo SNVs and CNVs in 41,165 NDD individuals identified NLGN2 as one of 11 high-confidence candidate NDD genes with >90% true-positive probability and FDR <5% for enrichment of de novo deleterious variants (PMID: 35468861), providing strong statistical genomic support.

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
The biological evidence is robust, spanning molecular function, protein interactions, human iPSC models, expression studies in patient cohorts, and large-scale genomic analyses, all consistently implicating NLGN2 in inhibitory synaptic function and neurodevelopmental pathology.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths:**
- The biological and model organism evidence for NLGN2 is strong and highly consistent: multiple independent mouse models across different laboratories demonstrate neurological, behavioral, and electrophysiological phenotypes directly relevant to the human disorder, and the molecular function of NLGN2 at inhibitory synapses is well-characterized.
- Large-scale genomic analysis (PMID: 35468861) provides statistical support for NLGN2 as a high-confidence NDD gene based on enrichment of de novo deleterious variants across tens of thousands of NDD cases.
- The small number of reported human cases show a consistent phenotypic core (ASD, intellectual disability, anxiety, hyperphagia) and all carry de novo heterozygous variants, consistent with an autosomal dominant mechanism.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- The clinical evidence base is very small (approximately 4–5 patients), with several reports lacking specific variant notations, limiting genotype-phenotype correlation and variant-level pathogenicity assessment.
- No patients have been reported from large, well-characterized diagnostic cohorts (e.g., DDD, GEL, UDN), and no case series with more than one patient has been published.
- The relationship between heterozygous human variants and the predominantly homozygous mouse KO phenotypes requires careful interpretation; haploinsufficiency in humans has not been formally demonstrated at the cellular or molecular level for most reported variants.

**Conclusion:** NLGN2 has strong biological plausibility and robust model organism support as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene, with its exclusive role at GABAergic inhibitory synapses providing a compelling mechanistic framework for the observed human phenotypes of ASD, intellectual disability, and anxiety. The clinical evidence, while currently limited in scale, is internally consistent and supported by large-scale genomic enrichment data. As additional patients are identified and variant-level functional studies are performed, NLGN2 is likely to achieve strong clinical gene-disease validity for an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder.
