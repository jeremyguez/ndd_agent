# SLC18A3 Evidence Report: Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene Assessment (Autosomal Dominant)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

SLC18A3 encodes the vesicular acetylcholine transporter (VAChT), a member of the Major Facilitator Superfamily (MFS)/Drug:H+ Antiporter-1 (DHA1) family, which actively transports acetylcholine from the neuronal cytoplasm into synaptic vesicles for calcium-dependent exocytotic release at cholinergic synapses (PMID: 23506877). It is the sole transporter responsible for vesicular ACh loading and is therefore indispensable for presynaptic cholinergic neurotransmission throughout the central and peripheral nervous systems.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

- **Number of independent case reports/series:** Zero independent clinical case reports or case series were identified in the structured clinical evidence provided.
- **Total approximate number of patients:** No patients with heterozygous (autosomal dominant) pathogenic variants in SLC18A3 were reported in the clinical evidence set.
- **Most common phenotypes observed:** Not applicable from the clinical evidence provided. However, contextual biological evidence (PMID: 28188302) documents two brothers with a **homozygous** missense variant (c.1078G>C; p.Gly360Arg) presenting with severe congenital myasthenic syndrome (CMS) featuring hypotonia, arthrogryposis, and respiratory failure — a recessive, not dominant, presentation.
- **Predominant variant type:** The only functionally characterized human variant identified across all evidence lines is a homozygous missense loss-of-function variant (PMID: 28188302); no heterozygous dominant variants were documented.
- **Known cohort representation:** No patients from recognized neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, Genomics England GEL) were identified.
- **Strength of clinical evidence: NONE**

There is a complete absence of clinical evidence supporting heterozygous (autosomal dominant) pathogenic variants in SLC18A3 as a cause of neurodevelopmental disorder. The only human genetic evidence available pertains to biallelic (autosomal recessive) loss-of-function causing a presynaptic congenital myasthenic syndrome.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

- **Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*) — full knockout model.
- **Key phenotypes:** Homozygous VAChT/SLC18A3 knockout mice display an **embryonic lethal phenotype** consistent with fetal akinesia deformation sequence (FADS), as referenced in the context of human FADS presentations (PMID: 31059209). This lethality reflects the essential role of cholinergic neuromuscular transmission for fetal movement and survival.
- **Consistency with human phenotype:** The mouse knockout phenotype (embryonic lethality, fetal akinesia) is broadly consistent with the severe end of the human recessive phenotypic spectrum (arthrogryposis, respiratory failure, hypotonia) documented in biallelic SLC18A3 cases (PMID: 28188302). However, the mouse data pertain exclusively to **homozygous** knockouts; no heterozygous mouse phenotype data were reported, which is a critical gap for assessing autosomal dominant disease relevance.
- **Mendelian ratio deviations:** Homozygous lethality is documented; heterozygous carrier viability and phenotype are not described in the available evidence.
- **Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED**

While the homozygous mouse knockout provides compelling support for SLC18A3's essential role in neuromuscular function, the absence of any reported heterozygous phenotype in mice means the model organism data do not support a haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative mechanism relevant to autosomal dominant disease.

---

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

- **Molecular function and pathways:** VAChT/SLC18A3 is the sole vesicular transporter for acetylcholine, functioning as an H+/ACh antiporter that concentrates ACh into synaptic vesicles prior to release (PMID: 23506877). Downstream, ACh activates muscarinic and nicotinic receptors, engaging pathways including PKA/CREB signaling (PMID: 36225635). Loss of VAChT function abolishes presynaptic cholinergic output, directly impairing neuromuscular junction transmission and central cholinergic circuits.

- **Expression pattern:** SLC18A3 expression is well-established in cholinergic neurons of the central and peripheral nervous systems. Spatial transcriptomic profiling of human lumbar sympathetic chain ganglia identified a discrete SLC18A3-expressing cholinergic neuronal population distinct from noradrenergic neurons (PMID: 40667173). In the rat hypothalamus, SLC18A3 marks a specific kisspeptin neuron subcluster in the arcuate nucleus across pubertal stages (PMID: 35063020). Human iPSC-derived motor neuron studies confirm SLC18A3 expression as part of the cholinergic synapse gene program during human motor neuron differentiation (PMID: 37624150). Non-neuronal expression has also been documented in rat testicular germ cells and Sertoli cells (PMID: 21482687) and in peripheral blood mononuclear cells in the context of obstructive sleep apnea (PMID: 24732660), though the functional significance of these non-neuronal expression sites is unclear.

- **Key experimental findings:** The most directly disease-relevant experimental finding is the functional characterization of the p.Gly360Arg missense variant in PC12A123.7 neuronal-like cells, which demonstrated that despite normal mRNA levels, mutant VAChT protein was undetectable, indicating post-translational degradation and complete loss of function (PMID: 28188302). This establishes a clear molecular mechanism for recessive disease. Downregulation of SLC18A3 by neurotoxic exposures (arsenic in iPSC-derived motor neurons [PMID: 37624150]; cylindrospermopsin in SH-SY5Y cells [PMID: 38091671]) further underscores its role as a sensitive marker of cholinergic integrity during neuronal differentiation. A candidate gene GWAS of the acetylcholine pathway found no significant associations between common SLC18A3 variants and heart rate variability (PMID: 25384021), providing no support for common variant effects on autonomic function.

- **Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE**

The biological evidence robustly establishes SLC18A3's essential and non-redundant role in cholinergic neurotransmission, with clear neurodevelopmental expression and a validated loss-of-function mechanism in cell culture. However, all functional evidence pertains to biallelic or complete loss-of-function contexts; there is no experimental evidence for dominant-negative effects, gain-of-function, or haploinsufficiency as a disease mechanism.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

- **Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
- SLC18A3 has a well-defined, non-redundant molecular function in cholinergic neurotransmission (PMID: 23506877), and biallelic loss-of-function is established as causing severe presynaptic congenital myasthenic syndrome in humans (PMID: 28188302).
- Homozygous mouse knockout produces embryonic lethality consistent with the severe human recessive phenotype (PMID: 31059209), validating the gene's essential role in vivo.
- Expression in human motor neurons, autonomic ganglia, and hypothalamic neurons confirms strong neurodevelopmental relevance (PMID: 40667173; PMID: 37624150).

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- There is **no clinical evidence** of heterozygous (autosomal dominant) pathogenic variants causing neurodevelopmental disease in humans. The established human disease mechanism is autosomal recessive.
- There are **no model organism data** describing a phenotype in heterozygous SLC18A3 knockout animals, leaving haploinsufficiency entirely uncharacterized.
- There is **no functional experimental evidence** for dominant-negative or gain-of-function mechanisms that would support an autosomal dominant disease model.
- No patients from major neurodevelopmental disorder discovery cohorts have been reported with heterozygous SLC18A3 variants.

**Conclusion:** The current body of evidence does not support SLC18A3 as an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene. The gene's established disease association is autosomal recessive presynaptic congenital myasthenic syndrome, supported by human genetics and functional cell-based data (PMID: 28188302), with corroborating mouse knockout lethality (PMID: 31059209). To substantiate a dominant disease mechanism, future work would require identification of multiple unrelated individuals with de novo or segregating heterozygous variants and a consistent neurodevelopmental phenotype, ideally supported by functional studies demonstrating haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative effects on VAChT activity.
