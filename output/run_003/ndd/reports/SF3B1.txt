# SF3B1 as a Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene: Literature Evidence Report

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

SF3B1 (Splicing Factor 3b Subunit 1) encodes a core component of the U2 small nuclear ribonucleoprotein (snRNP) complex of the spliceosome, where it plays an essential role in branch point recognition and 3′ splice site selection during pre-mRNA splicing. It is the most frequently mutated splicing factor gene across human cancers, particularly myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS), chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL), and uveal melanoma, where somatic gain-of-function hotspot mutations drive aberrant cryptic splice site usage.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent case reports/series:** One published case series was identified in the structured evidence.

**Total approximate number of patients:** 26 patients are described in a single study reporting de novo constitutional heterozygous variants in SF3B1 associated with neurodevelopmental disorders (PMID: 41577671).

**Most common phenotypes observed:** The predominant clinical features across the 26 patients include global developmental delay, neurological abnormalities, and facial dysmorphic traits. A subset of patients with de novo missense variants exhibited a more severe and syndromic phenotype additionally encompassing heart anomalies and gastrointestinal anomalies.

**Predominant variant type:** Both predicted loss-of-function (LoF) variants (nonsense, frameshift, and splice-site) and de novo missense variants were identified. Notably, the missense variants were reported to be largely distinct from the recurrent somatic cancer-associated hotspot mutations, suggesting a separate pathogenic mechanism in the neurodevelopmental context. Functional complementation assays demonstrated that the missense variants alter splicing activity in a manner distinct from simple loss-of-function, raising the possibility of a dominant-negative or gain-of-function mechanism for at least a subset of cases. Genome-wide RNA splicing analysis confirmed splicing dysregulation in affected individuals.

**Cohort affiliation:** The study does not specify affiliation with named research cohorts such as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL based on the available abstract information.

**Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED**
Rationale: While the study (PMID: 41577671) provides a meaningful initial series of 26 patients with de novo variants, functional data, and phenotypic characterization, this represents a single publication with a relatively small cohort and no independent replication series identified in the current evidence set. The absence of specific variant-level notations in the available abstract further limits granular assessment. The evidence is promising but requires independent corroboration to be upgraded.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Zebrafish (*Danio rerio*) and mouse (*Mus musculus*) have both been used to model SF3B1 dysfunction.

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

The model organism literature is dominated by hematopoietic and oncological phenotypes rather than neurological ones. In zebrafish, full knockout of *sf3b1* produces a macrocytic anemia-like phenotype with a block in erythroid maturation at the late progenitor stage, neutropenia, and a failure to generate definitive hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs) from hemogenic endothelium (PMID: 27260753). A second zebrafish study confirmed macrocytic anemia and demonstrated G0/G1 cell-cycle arrest in erythroid progenitors mediated through upregulation of TGF-β pathway genes (PMID: 31300417). A third zebrafish study showed that HSPC formation in *sf3b1* homozygous mutants is dependent on STAT3 activation, and that SF3B1 heterozygosity confers heightened sensitivity to STAT3 inhibition (PMID: 36516770). In mouse models, studies have focused on conditional knockout in pancreatic cancer contexts (PMID: 36001976), knock-in of the oncogenic K700E allele in hematologic and pancreatic malignancy models (PMID: 37823551; PMID: 36798339; PMID: 38521065), and knockdown in endometrial cancer xenografts (PMID: 33040078).

**Consistency with human neurodevelopmental phenotype:** The available model organism studies do not directly address neurological or neurodevelopmental phenotypes. No published knockout or knockdown studies in the current evidence set report brain morphology defects, behavioral abnormalities, or neurodevelopmental endpoints in zebrafish or mouse models. The zebrafish full knockout data confirm that *sf3b1* is essential for normal development and differentiation, consistent with a haploinsufficiency or dominant mechanism being plausible, but the specific tissue relevance to the human neurodevelopmental phenotype is not established by these models.

**Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED**
Rationale: While multiple well-characterized zebrafish and mouse models exist, none of the identified studies examined neurological or neurodevelopmental outcomes. The existing data confirm biological essentiality of SF3B1 in vertebrate development but do not provide direct phenotypic support for the neurodevelopmental disorder association reported in humans.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:** SF3B1 is a central component of the SF3b complex within the U2 snRNP, functioning in branch point adenosine recognition and 3′ splice site selection. CDK11 physically associates with SF3B1 and phosphorylates threonine residues at its N-terminus during spliceosome activation, a step required for the transition from the precatalytic B complex to the activated Bact complex; inhibition of this phosphorylation causes widespread intron retention (PMID: 36104565). Oncogenic SF3B1 mutations (e.g., K700E, R625X) cause use of cryptic 3′ splice sites located 13–17 bp downstream of the branch point that are sterically occluded in the wild-type context, a mechanism conserved across CLL, uveal melanoma, and breast cancer (ASHG 2014 - DeBoever et al.). The G-patch protein GPATCH8 has been identified as a trans-acting factor required for aberrant mutant SF3B1 splicing activity, functionally opposing SUGP1 and interacting with RNA helicase DHX15; silencing GPATCH8 corrected approximately one-third of mutant SF3B1-dependent splicing defects (PMID: 38688280). In hematologic contexts, mutant SF3B1 causes mis-splicing of ABCB7 contributing to ring sideroblast formation (PMID: 36028755) and mis-splicing of COASY leading to CoA/succinyl-CoA depletion and impaired erythropoiesis (PMID: 36857430).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:** The biological evidence set does not include studies specifically examining SF3B1 expression in the developing brain or nervous system. SF3B1 is broadly expressed and, as a core spliceosomal component, is expected to be active in all proliferating and differentiating cell types, including neural progenitors. However, direct experimental evidence for brain-enriched expression or neurodevelopment-specific splicing targets of SF3B1 is not present in the current evidence set.

**Key experimental findings relevant to neurodevelopmental mechanism:** The clinical study (PMID: 41577671) reports functional complementation assays and genome-wide RNA splicing analysis confirming that constitutional de novo variants—particularly missense variants distinct from cancer hotspots—alter splicing activity in a manner not fully explained by haploinsufficiency. This is conceptually consistent with the broader biological literature showing that SF3B1 mutations can act through aberrant splice site selection rather than simple loss of function (ASHG 2014 - DeBoever et al.; ASHG 2013 - Bowcock et al.). The observation that missense variants in the neurodevelopmental cohort are largely distinct from cancer-associated hotspot mutations suggests that different structural perturbations of SF3B1 may produce distinct splicing dysregulation profiles with tissue- or developmental stage-specific consequences.

**Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE**
Rationale: The molecular function of SF3B1 in splicing is exceptionally well characterized, and the mechanistic framework for how mutations alter splice site selection is robust. However, the biological evidence directly linking SF3B1 dysfunction to neurodevelopmental processes—specific neural splicing targets, brain expression data, or neuronal cell culture experiments—is absent from the current evidence set. The general mechanistic plausibility is high, but neurodevelopment-specific biological support remains to be established.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: LIMITED**

**Key strengths:**
- A single well-characterized case series (PMID: 41577671) provides the first systematic clinical evidence linking constitutional de novo SF3B1 variants to a neurodevelopmental disorder in 26 patients, supported by functional splicing assays and genome-wide RNA analysis.
- The de novo occurrence of variants, combined with functional evidence of altered splicing activity distinct from loss-of-function, provides a mechanistically coherent basis for pathogenicity.
- SF3B1's role as an essential, broadly expressed splicing regulator is exceptionally well established in the broader literature, lending strong biological plausibility to the association.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- The clinical evidence rests on a single publication with 26 patients and no independent replication cohort identified in the current evidence set; specific variant-level data were not available from the abstract.
- No model organism studies have examined neurological or neurodevelopmental phenotypes, leaving a significant gap in phenotypic cross-species validation.
- Neurodevelopment-specific biological evidence—including brain expression data, neural splicing targets, or neuronal functional assays—is entirely absent from the current evidence set.

**Conclusion:** SF3B1 is a biologically compelling candidate neurodevelopmental disorder gene based on an initial case series of 26 patients with de novo constitutional variants and supporting functional splicing data (PMID: 41577671), but the overall evidence base is currently limited by the absence of independent clinical replication, lack of neurologically relevant model organism data, and no neurodevelopment-specific biological characterization. Ascertainment of additional patients through international cohort collaboration and targeted neurological phenotyping in vertebrate models would be the highest-priority next steps to strengthen the gene-disease association. At present, SF3B1 should be considered a candidate neurodevelopmental disorder gene warranting active surveillance rather than a fully established disease gene.

8. Splicing factor SF3B1 promotes endometrial cancer progression via regulating KSR2 RNA maturation. (PMID: 33040078)
