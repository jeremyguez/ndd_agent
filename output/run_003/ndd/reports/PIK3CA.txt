# PIK3CA as a Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene: Literature Evidence Report

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

PIK3CA encodes the p110α catalytic subunit of phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K), a lipid kinase that phosphorylates phosphatidylinositol-4,5-bisphosphate to generate PIP3, thereby activating the PI3K–AKT–mTOR signaling axis. This pathway is a central regulator of cell growth, proliferation, survival, and differentiation, and is critically important during embryonic and brain development.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent case reports/series:** Ten independent clinical reports or case series were identified, spanning peer-reviewed publications and ASHG conference abstracts.

**Total approximate number of patients:** Approximately 266 patients across all studies, though there is potential for partial overlap between cohorts studying overlapping PROS phenotypes.

**Most common phenotypes observed:** The clinical spectrum associated with somatic mosaic PIK3CA gain-of-function mutations is broad and collectively termed the PIK3CA-Related Overgrowth Spectrum (PROS). Neurodevelopmental phenotypes are prominently represented and include:
- **Megalencephaly**, hemimegalencephaly, and brain overgrowth (PMID: 22729224; PMID: 27631024)
- **Malformations of cortical development (MCD)**, including focal cortical dysplasia and polymicrogyria (ASHG 2017 – Griffin et al.; ASHG 2018 – Garcia et al.; ASHG 2018 – Ruggeri et al.)
- **Drug-resistant / intractable epilepsy** associated with cortical dysplasia (ASHG 2017 – Griffin et al.; ASHG 2018 – Garcia et al.; ASHG 2018 – Ruggeri et al.)
- **Intellectual disability and autism** in the context of focal MCD (ASHG 2018 – Ruggeri et al.)
- **Polydactyly and hydrocephalus** as part of the MCAP/MPPH syndromes (PMID: 22729224)

Non-neurological phenotypes co-occurring in many patients include vascular malformations (capillary, venous, lymphatic), congenital lipomatous overgrowth, CLOVES syndrome, hemihypertrophy, scoliosis, and fibrolipomatous hamartoma of nerve (PMID: 29899452; PMID: 38880808; ASHG 2018 – Blackburn et al.; ASHG 2018 – Cheng et al.; ASHG 2019 – Bennett et al.).

**Predominant variant type:** All reported pathogenic variants are **missense** mutations conferring gain-of-function activity. Recurrent hotspot variants include p.H1047R, p.H1047L, p.E542K, and p.E545K, with additional non-hotspot variants (e.g., p.C420R, p.G106V, p.Q546K, p.F937S) also reported. The majority are **somatic mosaic** (postzygotic), with variant allele frequencies ranging from as low as 0.1% to 26% in affected tissues. A subset of patients (10 of 72 in one large cohort) carry **constitutional (germline) PIK3CA mutations** (PMID: 27631024). Fifteen mostly postzygotic mutations were identified in a megalencephaly cohort (PMID: 22729224). Loss-of-function variants are not implicated in this phenotypic spectrum.

**Known cohorts:** No patients were explicitly identified as belonging to established research cohorts such as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL. One study was conducted at Hospital das Clínicas, USP, Brazil (ASHG 2018 – Garcia et al.).

**Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent groups across different countries have identified recurrent somatic mosaic PIK3CA missense mutations in patients with overlapping neurodevelopmental and overgrowth phenotypes. Mutations were confirmed by orthogonal methods including exome sequencing, Sanger sequencing, digital droplet PCR, and molecular inversion probe sequencing. The consistency of genotype–phenotype correlations across studies, the recurrence of specific hotspot variants, and the demonstration of absent or very low allele frequencies in blood versus enrichment in affected tissue all strongly support pathogenicity.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:** PIK3CA encodes the catalytic subunit of class IA PI3K, which operates upstream of AKT and mTORC1/mTORC2. Gain-of-function mutations constitutively activate this pathway, driving increased cell proliferation, survival, and altered differentiation. Several mechanistic studies confirm pathway-level consequences:

- High-throughput functional assays demonstrated that PIK3CA variants above 5% population mutation frequency are significantly more oncogenic than wild-type in cell growth and transformation assays, with variant-specific activation of both PI3K signaling and the MEK1/2–MAPK module (PMID: 26627007).
- In lymphatic endothelial cells, expression of PIK3CA H1047L caused significantly greater lymphangiogenic sprouting (cumulative sprout length 2,666 µm vs. 1,381 µm in controls, p=0.029) with over-activation of both mTORC1 and mTORC2 targets; dual mTORC1/mTORC2 inhibition rescued this hyperproliferative phenotype (ASHG 2022 – Battig et al.). This is directly relevant to the vascular malformation phenotypes seen clinically.
- In the context of facial infiltrating lipomatosis, PIK3CA mutations were shown to transcriptionally repress PPT1 via PI3K–AKT–c-JUN signaling, leading to enhanced palmitoylation of the transcriptional coactivator P300, altered chromatin accessibility, and upregulation of adipogenic gene expression — establishing a novel epigenetic mechanism linking PIK3CA mutation to tissue overgrowth (PMID: 41610323).
- PIK3CA H1047R expression in transgenic mice drives clonal expansion in normal esophageal epithelium via PI3K–mTOR, with metabolic state modulating mutant cell fitness (PMID: 39169259), supporting the concept that somatic PIK3CA mutations confer a cell-autonomous proliferative advantage in normal tissues.
- A CRISPRi genome-wide screen in PIK3CA E545K-mutant head and neck cancer cells identified co-dependency on the neddylation pathway, suggesting mutation-specific synthetic lethal interactions (PMID: 40154000).
- BEX2 was shown to physically interact with PIK3CA and impair its interaction with the p85 regulatory subunit, thereby suppressing PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling and promoting autophagy, illustrating endogenous regulatory mechanisms controlling PIK3CA activity (PMID: 41398291).

**Neurodevelopmental relevance:** The PI3K–AKT–mTOR pathway is a well-established regulator of neuronal migration, cortical lamination, and brain size. The clinical observation that somatic PIK3CA mutations cause hemimegalencephaly, focal cortical dysplasia, and polymicrogyria is mechanistically consistent with mTOR pathway hyperactivation in neural progenitor cells. Somatic mutations detected in surgically resected brain tissue at allele frequencies of 0.3–25.6% (ASHG 2018 – Ruggeri et al.; ASHG 2018 – Garcia et al.) confirm that even low-level mosaicism in neural tissue is sufficient to produce severe cortical malformations and epilepsy.

**Expression pattern:** Direct brain expression data were not provided in the structured biological evidence. However, the clinical phenotypes (brain overgrowth, cortical dysplasia) and the detection of mutations in resected brain tissue implicate PIK3CA activity in neural progenitor and neuronal cell populations.

**Additional association:** A large-scale multi-ancestry GWAS meta-analysis (49,049 PD cases, 18,785 proxy cases, 2,458,063 controls) nominated PIK3CA as a putative risk gene near a novel genome-wide significant locus for Parkinson's disease using summary-based Mendelian randomization with multi-omic data (ASHG 2023 – Kim et al.), suggesting a potential broader role in neurological disease beyond developmental malformations, though this association requires further validation.

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent experimental systems — including cell culture, transgenic mouse models, patient-derived tissue, and genome-wide functional screens — consistently demonstrate that PIK3CA gain-of-function mutations activate PI3K–AKT–mTOR signaling, confer proliferative advantage, and drive tissue overgrowth phenotypes. The mechanistic link between PIK3CA activation and mTOR-dependent brain overgrowth is well-supported.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths:**
1. **Recurrent, functionally validated variants:** Hotspot missense mutations (p.H1047R, p.H1047L, p.E542K, p.E545K) are identified repeatedly across independent cohorts totaling over 250 patients, confirmed by multiple orthogonal molecular methods.
2. **Mechanistic coherence:** Gain-of-function PIK3CA mutations activate the PI3K–AKT–mTOR pathway, which is a well-established driver of brain overgrowth, cortical malformation, and epilepsy; cell-based models directly recapitulate the hyperproliferative phenotype and demonstrate rescue by mTOR inhibition.
3. **Therapeutic translation:** The identification of PIK3CA mutations in PROS has already led to clinical trials of PI3K inhibitors (e.g., alpelisib/BYL719), with published evidence of clinical benefit in 19 patients with CLOVES and related conditions (PMID: 29899452) and 16 patients with capillary-venous malformations (PMID: 38880808), further validating the causal role of these mutations.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. **Somatic mosaicism complicates classification:** The vast majority of pathogenic PIK3CA variants causing neurodevelopmental phenotypes are somatic mosaic rather than constitutional heterozygous germline variants, meaning standard autosomal dominant inheritance models do not fully apply; allele frequencies in blood are often very low or undetectable, requiring tissue-specific testing.
2. **No model organism data provided:** The absence of structured model organism evidence in this report is a gap, though the broader literature supports the mechanistic model.
3. **Phenotypic heterogeneity:** The wide spectrum of PROS phenotypes — ranging from isolated lymphatic malformations to severe brain malformations — makes genotype–phenotype prediction challenging, and the determinants of tissue distribution and clinical severity (beyond variant identity) remain incompletely understood.

**Conclusion:** PIK3CA is a well-established disease gene for a spectrum of neurodevelopmental and overgrowth disorders caused by somatic mosaic gain-of-function missense mutations that constitutively activate the PI3K–AKT–mTOR pathway. The evidence base is strong, with consistent findings across multiple independent clinical cohorts, mechanistically coherent biological data, and emerging therapeutic validation. The primary caveat for clinical interpretation is that pathogenic variants are predominantly somatic mosaic rather than germline heterozygous, necessitating sensitive tissue-based molecular testing for diagnosis.
