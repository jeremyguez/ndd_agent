# Literature Evidence Report: PPP2R5C as a Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

PPP2R5C encodes the B56γ regulatory subunit of Protein Phosphatase 2A (PP2A), a major serine/threonine phosphatase complex that controls diverse cellular processes including cell cycle progression, signal transduction, and development. As a member of the B56 family, B56γ directs substrate specificity and subcellular localization of the PP2A holoenzyme, and pathogenic variants in related B56 subunit genes (PPP2R5B, PPP2R5D) have established precedent for PP2A regulatory subunit dysfunction causing human neurodevelopmental disease within the Houge-Janssens syndrome (HJS) spectrum.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports:** Four independent publications or conference reports describe PPP2R5C-associated neurodevelopmental disease: a large international case series (PMID: 39978342), a focused two-patient report (PMID: 39696819), an early overgrowth cohort study (PMID: 25972378), and a conference abstract from ASHG 2019 (ASHG 2019 – Brimble et al.). An additional single case was reported in the DDD-Africa cohort (ASHG 2023 – Mubungu et al.).

**Total approximate number of patients:** Approximately 38 individuals across all reports (26 in PMID: 39978342; 2 in PMID: 39696819; 2 in PMID: 25972378; 7 in ASHG 2019 – Brimble et al.; 1 in ASHG 2023 – Mubungu et al.), with some overlap possible between the earlier conference abstract and the 2025 case series.

**Most common phenotypes observed:** The core phenotypic spectrum across studies is highly consistent and includes:
- Neurodevelopmental delay / intellectual disability (present in virtually all cases; notably described as milder than other HJS subtypes in PMID: 39978342)
- Hypotonia (congenital or early-onset)
- Macrocephaly or above-average head circumference
- Epilepsy / seizures
- Behavioral problems
- Mildly dysmorphic facial features
- Overgrowth (increased height and head circumference, particularly noted in PMID: 25972378)

**Predominant variant type:** Heterozygous de novo missense variants are the predominant pathogenic variant class. Nineteen unique missense variants were catalogued in the largest series (PMID: 39978342), with five recurrent variants identified across unrelated individuals. Notably, p.Glu153Lys was reported in two unrelated individuals (PMID: 39696819), and p.Glu122Lys was observed in three unrelated individuals (ASHG 2019 – Brimble et al.), providing strong recurrence evidence. Loss-of-function (LoF) variants were also identified but were inherited from non-symptomatic parents in some cases, suggesting that LoF alone is insufficient for disease and that the pathogenic mechanism for missense variants is likely dominant-negative rather than haploinsufficiency (PMID: 39978342).

**Known cohorts represented:** The DDD (Deciphering Developmental Disorders) cohort contributed cases in both PMID: 25972378 and ASHG 2023 – Mubungu et al. The 2025 case series (PMID: 39978342) represents an international multi-center collaboration. Cases were also identified via Matchmaker Exchange (ASHG 2019 – Brimble et al.).

**Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG**
The evidence is supported by a substantial number of patients (≥38), multiple independent groups across different countries and cohorts, recurrent de novo variants in unrelated individuals, a consistent and recognizable phenotypic spectrum, and absence of pathogenic missense variants from population databases.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*) is the primary model organism with direct relevance to PPP2R5C function.

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**
- *PPP2R5C single knockout mice are viable and phenotypically normal*, indicating that complete loss of B56γ alone is tolerated in mice (PMID: 35415460). This is consistent with the human observation that LoF variants in PPP2R5C are found in non-symptomatic carrier parents, further supporting a dominant-negative rather than haploinsufficiency mechanism for the human missense variants.
- *PPP2R5C/PPP2R5D double knockout mice* arrest fetal development at approximately embryonic day E12 and display a single cardiac outflow vessel (failure of outflow tract septation), demonstrating a strong genetic interaction between B56γ and B56δ specifically required for heart development (PMID: 35415460). This sub-Mendelian lethality in double knockouts underscores the functional redundancy within the B56 subfamily and the essential developmental role of these subunits collectively.
- Several studies examined mice with knockout of *LincR-PPP2R5C*, a lncRNA that regulates PPP2R5C expression in immune cells. These models demonstrated roles for PPP2R5C/PP2A in Th2 differentiation, airway inflammation, and macrophage biology (PMIDs: 38262392, 39018689, 39155740, 39287443, 39729722), but these findings are primarily relevant to immune function rather than neurodevelopment.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The mouse knockout data provide partial support. The viability of single PPP2R5C knockouts is consistent with the human genetic data suggesting that simple haploinsufficiency is not the disease mechanism. However, direct neurological or neurodevelopmental phenotypes have not been reported in PPP2R5C single knockout mice, representing a gap in the model organism evidence. The cardiac phenotype in double knockouts demonstrates the gene's essential developmental role but does not directly model the human neurodevelopmental syndrome.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**
The mouse data confirm essential developmental functions of PPP2R5C in combination with PPP2R5D and are consistent with the proposed dominant-negative mechanism, but direct neurological phenotypes in single-knockout or knock-in models recapitulating human missense variants have not yet been reported.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
PPP2R5C encodes the B56γ subunit, which confers substrate specificity to the PP2A heterotrimer (comprising scaffolding A, catalytic C, and regulatory B subunits). Pathogenic missense variants cluster in two functionally critical regions: those affecting substrate binding, those affecting C-subunit (catalytic subunit) binding, and those affecting both (PMID: 39978342). Functional studies in HEK293 cells demonstrated that disease-associated variants significantly impair co-precipitation of endogenous PP2A catalytic (C) and structural (A) subunits with GFP-tagged B56γ, and reduce PP2A catalytic activity on phospho-peptide substrates (ASHG 2019 – Brimble et al.). The recurrent variant p.Glu122Lys, affecting a residue that directly contacts the catalytic subunit, showed the most severe binding and activity defects. Similarly, p.Glu153Lys affects a highly conserved acidic loop that directly interacts with the PP2A catalytic subunit (PMID: 39696819). A dominant-negative mechanism on substrate dephosphorylation is proposed, whereby mutant B56γ competes with wild-type subunit for holoenzyme assembly while failing to support normal catalytic activity (PMID: 39978342).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**
PPP2R5C is expressed in the developing nervous system. The transcription factor NRL (neural retina leucine-zipper) directly binds the PPP2R5C promoter and regulates its expression during photoreceptor development, identifying PPP2R5C as a transcriptional target during retinogenesis (PMID: 21078119). Broader brain expression is implied by the neurodevelopmental phenotype in patients, though dedicated brain expression profiling data were not highlighted in the reviewed literature.

**Additional experimental findings:**
- In cancer biology, PPP2R5C loss activates an ERK/IER3/PP2A-B56γ positive feedback loop in lung adenocarcinoma (PMID: 26986830), and PPP2R5C overexpression is an adverse prognostic factor in cytogenetically normal AML (PMID: 32158086), collectively demonstrating that PPP2R5C regulates proliferative signaling pathways (MAPK, PI3K/AKT) relevant to cell growth—consistent with the overgrowth phenotype observed in patients.
- siRNA knockdown of PPP2R5C in T-cell leukemia lines altered expression of genes in TCR, Wnt, calcium, MAPK, and p53 signaling pathways (PMID: 23941244), and knockdown in CML lines induced apoptosis in imatinib-resistant cells (PMID: 24004697), further implicating PPP2R5C in broad signaling regulation.
- The lncRNA LincR-PPP2R5C regulates PPP2R5C expression via RNA-DNA triplex formation at the PPP2R5C promoter, modulating PP2A activity in immune cells (PMID: 38262392), demonstrating that PPP2R5C expression is subject to sophisticated non-coding RNA regulation.

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent functional studies directly demonstrate that disease-associated missense variants impair PP2A holoenzyme assembly and catalytic activity. The dominant-negative mechanism is well-supported by both biochemical data and the genetic observation that LoF variants are non-pathogenic in carrier parents. The gene's role in growth-regulatory signaling pathways is consistent with the overgrowth and macrocephaly phenotypes observed in patients.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths:**
1. *Robust clinical evidence:* Approximately 38 patients across at least four independent reports from multiple countries and cohorts, with a consistent and recognizable neurodevelopmental phenotype. Recurrent de novo missense variants in unrelated individuals (e.g., p.Glu122Lys in three individuals, p.Glu153Lys in two individuals) provide particularly compelling evidence of pathogenicity.
2. *Mechanistic clarity:* Functional studies directly demonstrate that pathogenic missense variants impair PP2A holoenzyme assembly and reduce catalytic activity, with a well-supported dominant-negative mechanism that explains why LoF variants are non-pathogenic in carrier parents.
3. *Disease context:* PPP2R5C-related disorder fits within the established Houge-Janssens syndrome spectrum caused by variants in related PP2A B56 subunit genes (PPP2R5B, PPP2R5D), providing strong biological plausibility and a well-characterized disease framework.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. *Model organism neurodevelopmental phenotypes:* No published mouse model directly recapitulates the human neurodevelopmental syndrome; single-knockout mice are phenotypically normal, and knock-in models carrying human disease-associated missense variants have not yet been reported.
2. *Brain expression data:* Detailed spatiotemporal expression profiling of PPP2R5C in the developing human or mouse brain is lacking in the reviewed literature, which would strengthen the biological plausibility of the neurological phenotype.
3. *Potential overlap between cohorts:* Some patients reported in the ASHG 2019 abstract (Brimble et al.) may be included in the larger 2025 case series (PMID: 39978342), meaning the true independent patient count may be somewhat lower than the aggregate total.

**Conclusion:**
PPP2R5C is a well-supported neurodevelopmental disorder gene in which heterozygous de novo missense variants cause a recognizable syndrome characterized by intellectual disability, hypotonia, macrocephaly, epilepsy, and overgrowth, classified within the Houge-Janssens syndrome spectrum. The evidence base is strengthened by the convergence of consistent clinical phenotypes across approximately 38 patients from multiple independent international cohorts, direct functional demonstration of dominant-negative impairment of PP2A activity by disease-associated variants, and strong biological plausibility from the established roles of PP2A B56 subunits in neurodevelopment and growth regulation. The primary remaining gap is the absence of a mouse model directly recapitulating the human missense variant phenotype, which would further solidify the gene-disease relationship.

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. Pathogenic de novo variants in PPP2R5C cause a neurodevelopmental disorder within the Houge-Janssens syndrome spectrum (PMID: 39978342)
2. A recurrent variant in PPP2R5C identified in individuals with macrocephaly, intellectual disability, and seizures (PMID: 39696819)
3. Mutations in the PP2A regulatory subunit B family genes PPP2R5B, PPP2R5C and PPP2R5D cause human overgrowth (PMID: 25972378)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. Growth arrest of PPP2R5C and PPP2R5D double knockout mice indicates a genetic interaction and conserved function for these PP2A B subunits (PMID: 35415460)
2. LincR-PPP2R5C Promotes Th2 Cell Differentiation Through PPP2R5C/PP2A by Forming an RNA-DNA Triplex in Allergic Asthma (PMID: 38262392)
3. LincR-PPP2R5C regulates IL-1β ubiquitination in macrophages and promotes airway inflammation and emphysema in a murine model of COPD (PMID: 39018689)
4. LincR-PPP2R5C Deficiency Alleviates Airway Remodeling by Inhibiting Epithelial-Mesenchymal Transition Through the PP2A/TGF-β1 Signaling Pathway in Chronic Experimental Allergic Asthma (PMID: 39155740)
5. LincR-PPP2R5C deficiency enhancing the fungicidal activity of neutrophils in pulmonary cryptococcosis is linked to the upregulation of IL-4 (PMID: 39287443)
6. LincR-PPP2R5C regulates the PP2A signaling pathway in the macrophage-myofibroblast transition in a mouse model of epidural fibrosis (PMID: 39729722)

### Biological Evidence
1. Neural retina leucine-zipper regulates the expression of Ppp2r5c, the regulatory subunit of protein phosphatase 2A, in photoreceptor development. (PMID: 21078119)
2. Activation of ERK/IER3/PP2A-B56γ-positive feedback loop in lung adenocarcinoma by allelic deletion of B56γ gene. (PMID: 26986830)
3. Prognostic Impact of PPP2R5C Gene Expression in Adult Acute Myeloid Leukemia Patients with Normal Cytogenetics. (PMID: 32158086)
4. Differential gene expression profiles of PPP2R5C-siRNA-treated malignant T cells. (PMID: 23941244)
5. Proliferation inhibition and apoptosis induction of imatinib-resistant chronic myeloid leukemia cells via PPP2R5C down-regulation. (PMID: 24004697)
