# SUMO2 as a Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene: Literature Evidence Report

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

SUMO2 (Small Ubiquitin-like Modifier 2) encodes one of the primary members of the SUMO protein family, which functions as a post-translational modifier that is covalently conjugated to lysine residues on target proteins to regulate their stability, localization, activity, and protein–protein interactions. SUMO2 is the predominantly expressed SUMO paralog in mammals and plays essential, non-redundant roles in embryonic development, DNA damage repair, synaptic plasticity, and stress responses across multiple organ systems.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent case reports / case series:** 1 (conference abstract only)

**Total approximate number of patients:** 2 (a proband and his mother)

**Most common phenotypes observed:** The single reported case involves a maternally inherited heterozygous variant in SUMO2 (c.2T>A; p.Met1?) identified by whole exome sequencing in a proband and his mother, both presenting with skeletal abnormalities including tibial agenesis, syndactyly, and clubfoot (ASHG 2022 – Maternally Inherited novel SUMO2 variant associated with a complex phenotype). Notably, the reported phenotype is primarily skeletal/limb rather than strictly neurodevelopmental, though the abstract references animal data supporting a broader developmental role.

**Predominant variant type:** A single loss-of-function variant affecting the initiator methionine (p.Met1?), predicted to abolish translation initiation, classified as likely pathogenic by ACMG criteria per the reporting authors.

**Are patients from known cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL)?** Not specified in the available abstract; the report was presented at ASHG 2022 but has not been published in a peer-reviewed journal based on the evidence provided.

**Strength of clinical evidence: NONE to LIMITED**
The clinical evidence is extremely limited. There is a single unpublished conference abstract describing two affected individuals from one family. No peer-reviewed case series, no independent replication, and no formal genotype–phenotype correlation study exists. The phenotype described (skeletal dysplasia) does not clearly map to a neurodevelopmental disorder, and the heterozygous autosomal dominant mechanism remains unestablished in human patients.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*), including full knockout, conditional knockout, knockdown, and knock-in models.

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

- **Embryonic lethality:** Homozygous *Sumo2* knockout (*Sumo2−/−*) results in severe developmental delay and embryonic lethality at approximately embryonic day 10.5 (E10.5), demonstrating that SUMO2 is essential and non-redundant during mammalian embryogenesis (PMID: 24891386). In contrast, *Sumo3−/−* mice are viable with no overt phenotype, confirming the unique requirement for SUMO2. Compound *Sumo2+/−;Sumo3−/−* mice are extremely rare at birth (only 2 of an expected larger number recovered across 35 litters) and are considerably smaller than littermates, indicating strong haploinsufficiency in the context of reduced SUMO3 (PMID: 24891386).

- **Neurological phenotypes:** Conditional neuron-specific knockout of *Sumo2* in mice produces marked impairments in episodic and fear memory, as well as deficits in the maintenance of hippocampal long-term potentiation (LTP), without constitutive changes in gene expression or neuronal morphology (PMID: 32910521). This implicates SUMO2 specifically in dynamic synaptic plasticity processes. Complementarily, genetic overexpression of human SUMO2 in an APP transgenic mouse model of Alzheimer's disease prevented LTP impairments and cognitive deficits without altering amyloid pathology (PMID: 40047257), further supporting a dose-sensitive role for SUMO2 in neuronal function.

- **Developmental/organogenesis phenotypes:** SUMO2 genetically interacts with the SUMO protease SENP2 to regulate placentation and organogenesis, including formation of the atrioventricular cushion and myocardium; reduction of SUMO2 in SENP2-deficient embryos alleviates placental defects and rescues cardiac structures (PMID: 31625212). SUMO2 modification of SATB2, mediated by ZFP451, is required for embryonic stem cell differentiation, with a SUMO2–SATB2 fusion protein rescuing impaired differentiation in relevant knockout ESCs (PMID: 34244292).

- **Other organ-specific phenotypes:** SUMO2 knockdown in cardiomyocytes exacerbated DNA damage following ischemia-reperfusion injury, while overexpression was protective (PMID: 35864967). SUMO2-KO mice showed altered GPX4 stability and disrupted ferroptosis resistance in the bone marrow niche (PMID: 39653239).

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The mouse data strongly support a critical role for SUMO2 in embryonic development and neurological function. The sub-Mendelian recovery of *Sumo2+/−;Sumo3−/−* compound mice suggests that even partial reduction of SUMO2 dosage can be deleterious in vivo. The conditional knockout neurological phenotypes (memory impairment, LTP deficits) are broadly consistent with a potential neurodevelopmental or neurocognitive disorder in humans, though direct phenotypic correspondence to the single reported human case (skeletal abnormalities) is not established.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent mouse studies using different genetic strategies (full knockout, conditional knockout, knockdown, knock-in) consistently demonstrate that SUMO2 is essential for embryonic viability and plays a non-redundant, dose-sensitive role in neurological function and synaptic plasticity.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
SUMO2 participates in a broad range of cellular processes through post-translational SUMOylation of target proteins. Key documented functions include:

- **Genome stability and DNA repair:** SUMO2 SUMOylation of DHX9 at lysine 120 is required for R-loop resolution and genome stability; fusion of SUMO2 to a non-SUMOylatable DHX9 mutant restored protein interactions and rescued cell survival defects (PMID: 39019926). SUMO2 modification of nuclear γ-actin supports DNA damage repair in cardiomyocytes (PMID: 35864967).

- **Proteostasis and neurodegeneration:** SUMO2 modification compartmentalizes TDP-43 in PML nuclear bodies and limits its aggregation under proteotoxic stress, with a SUMOylation–ubiquitylation cascade protecting against insoluble TDP-43 inclusions relevant to ALS/FTD (PMID: 40246979). This is directly relevant to neurodegenerative mechanisms.

- **Synaptic plasticity and cognition:** As noted above, SUMO2 is required for dynamic SUMOylation processes associated with synaptic plasticity and memory consolidation (PMID: 32910521; PMID: 40047257).

- **Oncology and metabolism:** SUMO2 has been identified as a selective dependency in synovial sarcoma (PMID: 40804185), regulates ferroptosis resistance via GPX4 SUMOylation (PMID: 39653239), promotes pulmonary fibrosis through Smad4 SUMOylation (PMID: 40473311), and drives lymphatic metastasis in bladder cancer through hnRNPA2B1 SUMOylation (PMID: 37991737). While these findings are not directly neurodevelopmental, they underscore the breadth and essentiality of SUMO2-mediated regulation.

- **Embryonic stem cell differentiation:** SUMO2-mediated SUMOylation of SATB2 by ZFP451 is a driver of ESC differentiation, with direct implications for early developmental gene regulation (PMID: 34244292).

**Expression pattern (brain/neurodevelopmental relevance):**
SUMO2 is described as the predominantly expressed SUMO isoform in mammals (PMID: 24891386). The conditional knockout neurological studies confirm expression and functional requirement in hippocampal neurons (PMID: 32910521). The ASHG 2022 abstract notes that SUMO2 knockdown alters expression of pluripotency factors CDX2, OCT4, and NANOG, supporting a role in early developmental transcriptional regulation. Specific quantitative brain expression data across developmental stages are not provided in the available evidence.

**Key experimental findings:**
The most neurodevelopmentally relevant biological finding is the demonstration that SUMO2 prevents TDP-43 aggregation via PML body compartmentalization (PMID: 40246979), directly linking SUMO2 to a mechanism of neuronal proteostasis. The role of SUMO2 in synaptic LTP maintenance (PMID: 32910521) and its protective effect against Alzheimer's-related synaptic dysfunction (PMID: 40047257) further establish neurobiological plausibility.

**Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE**
There is substantial mechanistic evidence for SUMO2 function in neuronal processes, genome stability, and developmental regulation. However, direct evidence specifically linking SUMO2 haploinsufficiency to a defined neurodevelopmental disorder mechanism in human cells or patient-derived material is absent. The biological evidence is broad and largely derived from non-neuronal or cancer cell systems, with neuronal relevance inferred from mouse models.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: LIMITED**

**Key strengths of the evidence:**
- Mouse knockout studies provide compelling evidence that SUMO2 is essential for embryonic development and that even partial dosage reduction (in the context of *Sumo3* loss) is severely deleterious, supporting biological plausibility for haploinsufficiency (PMID: 24891386).
- Conditional neuronal knockout and overexpression studies directly demonstrate a dose-sensitive role for SUMO2 in hippocampal synaptic plasticity, memory, and neuroprotection (PMID: 32910521; PMID: 40047257), providing strong mechanistic support for a neurocognitive phenotype.
- The breadth of SUMO2's molecular functions—spanning genome stability, proteostasis, and transcriptional regulation—provides multiple plausible pathomechanisms relevant to neurodevelopmental disorders.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- Clinical evidence is essentially absent: only a single unpublished conference abstract describes two individuals from one family with a SUMO2 variant, and the reported phenotype (skeletal dysplasia) does not clearly constitute a neurodevelopmental disorder.
- No independent replication of human SUMO2 variants in neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts has been reported, and no peer-reviewed case series exists.
- The autosomal dominant (heterozygous) mechanism is not established; the mouse full knockout data reflect homozygous lethality, and the relevance of heterozygous loss-of-function in humans remains speculative.
- Quantitative brain expression data across human developmental stages and direct functional studies in human neurons or patient-derived iPSCs are lacking.

**Conclusion:**
SUMO2 is a biologically compelling candidate gene for neurodevelopmental disease, supported by strong mouse model evidence of embryonic essentiality and neuronal dose-sensitivity, and by mechanistic studies implicating SUMO2 in synaptic plasticity and neuronal proteostasis. However, the current human clinical evidence is insufficient to establish SUMO2 as a confirmed neurodevelopmental disorder gene, with only a single unpublished family report available and no peer-reviewed replication. Systematic screening of SUMO2 in large neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts (e.g., DDD, GEL, UDN) and functional validation of candidate variants in human neuronal models are critical next steps to determine whether heterozygous SUMO2 variants cause disease in humans.

### Model Organism Evidence
1. SUMO2 is essential while SUMO3 is dispensable for mouse embryonic development. (PMID: 24891386)
2. Small ubiquitin-like modifier 2 (SUMO2) is critical for memory processes in mice. (PMID: 32910521)
3. Genetic and pharmacologic enhancement of SUMO2 conjugation prevents and reverses cognitive impairment and synaptotoxicity in a preclinical model of Alzheimer's disease. (PMID: 40047257)
4. The requirement of SUMO2/3 for SENP2 mediated extraembryonic and embryonic development. (PMID: 31625212)
5. ZFP451-mediated SUMOylation of SATB2 drives embryonic stem cell differentiation. (PMID: 34244292)
6. SUMOylation of Nuclear γ-Actin by SUMO2 supports DNA Damage Repair against Myocardial Ischemia-Reperfusion Injury. (PMID: 35864967)
7. Bone marrow stromal cells protect myeloma cells from ferroptosis through GPX4 deSUMOylation. (PMID: 39653239)

### Biological Evidence
1. DHX9 SUMOylation is required for the suppression of R-loop-associated genome instability (PMID: 39019926)
2. Induced proximity to PML protects TDP-43 from aggregation via SUMO-ubiquitin networks (PMID: 40246979)
3. Targeting SUMO2 reverses aberrant epigenetic rewiring driven by SS18::SSX fusion oncoproteins and impairs sarcomagenesis (PMID: 40804185)
4. Rbms1 promotes pulmonary fibrosis by stabilising Sumo2 mRNA to facilitate Smad4-SUMOylation and fibroblast activation (PMID: 40473311)
5. SUMOylation-Driven mRNA Circularization Enhances Translation and Promotes Lymphatic Metastasis of Bladder Cancer (PMID: 37991737)
