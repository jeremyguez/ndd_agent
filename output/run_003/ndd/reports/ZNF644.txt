# ZNF644 Literature Evidence Report: Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene Assessment

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

ZNF644 (Zinc Finger Protein 644) is a multi-zinc-finger transcription factor that functions as a core chromatin-targeting subunit of the G9a/GLP (EHMT2/EHMT1) histone H3K9 methyltransferase complex, directing epigenetic gene repression at specific genomic loci (PMID: 25789554). Its primary known role is to recruit the G9a/GLP complex to chromatin via sequence-specific DNA binding, thereby mediating H3K9 dimethylation and transcriptional silencing of progenitor genes during neurogenesis and other developmental contexts (PMID: 27546533).

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

- **Number of independent case reports / case series:** Zero. No clinical case reports, case series, or patient cohort studies specifically describing ZNF644-associated neurodevelopmental disorder were identified in the structured evidence provided.
- **Total approximate number of patients:** No patients with confirmed ZNF644-associated neurodevelopmental diagnoses were reported. One high-throughput functional genomics study (PMID: 37961498) identified ZNF644 5'UTR variants in individuals with autism from patient-derived cell lines, but this does not constitute a clinical case series with phenotypic characterization.
- **Most common phenotypes observed:** Not formally established in a clinical cohort. The only human phenotype with case-level genetic evidence is high-grade myopia, where rare coding variants (c.725C>T, c.821A>T) were identified in a US cohort of 131 cases versus 672 controls (PMID: 22539872). Neurodevelopmental phenotypes (autism) are implicated indirectly through 5'UTR functional studies (PMID: 37961498) but lack clinical case-level documentation.
- **Predominant variant type:** Missense variants and 5'UTR regulatory variants have been implicated; no formal classification of pathogenic LoF versus missense variants in a neurodevelopmental context is available from the current evidence.
- **Known cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL):** No patients from these cohorts were identified in the available evidence.
- **Strength of clinical evidence: NONE**

No peer-reviewed clinical evidence directly establishing ZNF644 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene through patient case reports or cohort studies was identified. The autism-related 5'UTR functional data (PMID: 37961498) is hypothesis-generating but does not constitute clinical evidence.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

- **Organisms studied:** Zebrafish (*Danio rerio*) and mouse (*Mus musculus*).

- **Zebrafish (PMID: 27546533):** Morpholino-based knockdown of *znf644a* and *znf644b* paralogs in zebrafish demonstrated disrupted retinal differentiation, with failure of proliferating progenitor cells to transition toward a differentiated state. The two paralogs showed complementary, non-overlapping roles in retinal differentiation programs, and dysregulation of G9a/H3K9me2-mediated gene silencing during neurogenesis was documented. This is directly relevant to neurodevelopmental function, as the retina is a CNS tissue, and the phenotype reflects a fundamental defect in neuronal progenitor cell fate transitions.

- **Mouse (PMID: 30834109):** TALEN-generated knock-in mice carrying either the disease-associated missense mutation *Zfp644* S673G or a protein-truncating allele (*Zfp644* Δ8) were viable and exhibited significant increases in vitreous chamber depth and altered lens diameter consistent with high myopia, while retinal physiological function (ERG) was preserved. These models confirm causality for ocular morphological changes but do not directly model neurological or neurodevelopmental phenotypes. Lethality was not observed, and Mendelian ratios were not reported as abnormal.

- **Consistency with human phenotype:** The zebrafish neurogenesis phenotype is consistent with a potential neurodevelopmental role, and the mouse ocular phenotype is consistent with the human high myopia association. However, no mouse model has been reported to exhibit overt neurological or behavioral phenotypes relevant to autism or intellectual disability.

- **Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**

Model organism studies provide meaningful mechanistic support for ZNF644's role in neuronal progenitor differentiation (zebrafish) and confirm variant pathogenicity for ocular phenotypes (mouse), but direct modeling of neurodevelopmental disorder phenotypes (e.g., behavioral, cognitive, or structural brain abnormalities) has not been reported.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

- **Known pathways and molecular function:** ZNF644 is a core subunit of the G9a/GLP histone H3K9 methyltransferase complex. It physically interacts with the transcriptional activation domain of G9a (EHMT2) and uses its zinc finger motifs to recognize consensus DNA sequences, thereby targeting the complex to specific chromatin loci to mediate H3K9 dimethylation and transcriptional repression (PMID: 25789554). This interaction was established by unbiased affinity purification and mass spectrometry. A review of the G9a/GLP complex in neurodevelopment further frames ZNF644 as a context-dependent, tissue- and temporally-specific regulator of G9a/GLP activity during early neurodevelopment (PMID: 28596979).

- **Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:** ZNF644 is expressed in neuronal progenitor cells and plays a role in the transition from proliferation to differentiation during neurogenesis, as demonstrated in zebrafish retinal development (PMID: 27546533). The G9a/GLP complex, of which ZNF644 is a subunit, is well-established as a critical epigenetic regulator of neurodevelopment; pathogenic variants in *EHMT1* (GLP) cause Kleefstra syndrome, a recognized neurodevelopmental disorder. ZNF644 5'UTR variants were shown to alter translational activity specifically in neurons in vivo, and patient-derived cell lines from individuals with autism confirmed altered ZNF644 protein production (PMID: 37961498), providing a direct link between ZNF644 dysregulation and neurodevelopmental disease at the molecular level.

- **Additional biological findings:** In porcine immature Sertoli cells, miR-362 directly targets the ZNF644 3'UTR, and ZNF644 knockdown arrests cell cycle progression and enhances apoptosis, indicating a broader role in cell survival and proliferation beyond the nervous system (PMID: 30021720). Transcriptomic studies have identified ZNF644 as differentially expressed in CD4 T cells of rheumatoid arthritis patients (PMID: 25880754) and as upregulated in peripheral blood of PTSD patients with high intrusion symptoms, with downregulation following successful treatment (PMID: 31039430). A breast cancer radiosensitivity study identified high ZNF644 expression as associated with improved outcomes following radiotherapy in a TCGA cohort of 976 samples (PMID: 34504527). These findings suggest broad expression across tissues but do not directly inform neurodevelopmental disease mechanisms.

- **Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE**

The molecular function of ZNF644 as a chromatin-targeting subunit of the G9a/GLP complex is well-characterized, and its role in neuronal progenitor differentiation is supported by both biochemical and model organism data. The functional demonstration that 5'UTR variants reduce ZNF644 protein production in neurons and in autism patient-derived cells (PMID: 37961498) is a notable mechanistic link. However, direct experimental evidence connecting ZNF644 loss-of-function to specific neurodevelopmental disorder endophenotypes (e.g., synaptic dysfunction, cortical development defects) remains limited.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

- **Overall evidence level: LIMITED**

**Key strengths:**
1. ZNF644 has a well-defined molecular function as a core subunit of the G9a/GLP epigenetic complex, a pathway with established relevance to neurodevelopmental disorders (e.g., Kleefstra syndrome via *EHMT1*), providing strong biological plausibility (PMID: 25789554; PMID: 28596979).
2. Zebrafish knockdown studies demonstrate a direct role for ZNF644 orthologs in neuronal progenitor differentiation (PMID: 27546533), and mouse knock-in models confirm that human disease-associated variants cause measurable phenotypes in vivo (PMID: 30834109).
3. High-throughput functional genomics identified ZNF644 5'UTR variants that reduce protein production in neurons and in autism patient-derived cell lines, providing a mechanistic bridge to neurodevelopmental disease (PMID: 37961498).

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. There is a complete absence of clinical case-level evidence: no patients with ZNF644-associated neurodevelopmental disorder have been reported in the peer-reviewed literature, and no data from major rare disease cohorts (UDN, DDD, GEL, GREGoR) are available.
2. No mouse model has been reported to exhibit neurological, behavioral, or cognitive phenotypes, and heterozygous loss-of-function has not been specifically modeled in the context of neurodevelopment.
3. The autism association from PMID: 37961498 is based on 5'UTR functional assays rather than clinical phenotyping, and the high myopia association (PMID: 22539872) involves a different organ system; neither constitutes robust clinical genetic evidence for a neurodevelopmental disorder.

**Conclusion:** ZNF644 is a biologically plausible neurodevelopmental disorder candidate gene based on its role as a chromatin-targeting subunit of the G9a/GLP complex and its demonstrated function in neuronal progenitor differentiation in zebrafish. However, the current evidence is insufficient to classify ZNF644 as an established neurodevelopmental disorder gene under heterozygous autosomal dominant inheritance, as no clinical case series or cohort data exist to support this designation. Systematic sequencing of ZNF644 in large neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts and generation of heterozygous mouse models with behavioral phenotyping represent critical next steps to advance this gene toward clinical validity.
