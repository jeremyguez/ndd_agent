# MAP2K7 Literature Evidence Report: Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene Assessment

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

MAP2K7 (Mitogen-Activated Protein Kinase Kinase 7), also known as MKK7, encodes a dual-specificity kinase that functions as a direct activator of JNK (c-Jun N-terminal kinase) within the MAPK signaling cascade, playing critical roles in stress response, apoptosis, cell proliferation, and neuronal development. It is the primary upstream activator of JNK in response to cytokines and environmental stressors, and has been implicated in neurodevelopmental processes relevant to psychiatric disease.

This represents the most critical gap in the overall evidence base.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*) and zebrafish (*Danio rerio*).

**Key findings:**

*Lethality:* Homozygous knockout of map2k7 in zebrafish is embryonic lethal, establishing that MAP2K7 is essential for early development (PMID: 37367226). In mice, homozygous Map2k7 knockout has previously been reported as embryonic lethal (referenced indirectly through the heterozygous studies), consistent with a critical developmental role.

*Neurological and cognitive phenotypes:* Heterozygous Map2k7+/- mice (haploinsufficient model) display a robust and reproducible neurocognitive phenotype. In a five-choice serial reaction time task (5-CSRTT), Map2k7+/- mice showed impaired attentional function, increased missed responses, a pronounced vigilance decrement over time, and elevated intra-individual reaction time variability — a pattern interpreted as consistent with schizophrenia-like cognitive deficits (PMID: 27774567). These attentional impairments were partially ameliorated by minocycline treatment, suggesting a neuroinflammatory or neurodevelopmental mechanism. Complementary neuroimaging studies in Map2k7+/- mice demonstrated schizophrenia-relevant brain metabolic and connectivity alterations, including hippocampal and mesolimbic hypermetabolism and prefrontal hypometabolism, alongside attenuated d-amphetamine behavioral responses implicating monoaminergic dysregulation (PMID: 31219577).

*Infertility:* Deletion of a testis-specific promoter region of Map2k7 in mice resulted in impaired sperm motility and an altered spermatid transcriptome, suggesting a reproductive phenotype associated with loss of tissue-specific Map2k7 expression (PMID: 35069705). This finding is of uncertain relevance to neurodevelopmental disease but indicates pleiotropic roles of MAP2K7.

*Immune/viability phenotypes:* Heterozygous map2k7+/- zebrafish showed significantly higher lethality upon viral challenge (Spring Viremia of Carp Virus), more pronounced tissue damage in immune organs, and greater viral burden compared to wild-type controls, demonstrating haploinsufficiency for antiviral immune function (PMID: 37367226).

*Oncological phenotypes:* Conditional knockout of Map2k7 in mouse mammary epithelial cells promoted HER2-driven breast cancer with accelerated tumor onset (PMID: 36656864), and Map2k7 loss in ER+ breast cancer models increased metastatic burden in vivo (PMID: 40826108). These findings are relevant to tumor suppressor function but are less directly informative for neurodevelopmental disease.

**Consistency with human neurodevelopmental phenotype:** The heterozygous mouse models show consistent, replicated neurocognitive and neuroimaging phenotypes relevant to schizophrenia and attentional disorders. The embryonic lethality of homozygous knockouts is consistent with a gene that is dosage-sensitive and essential for development. However, no animal model has been specifically designed to recapitulate a human neurodevelopmental syndrome caused by heterozygous loss-of-function variants.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**

The neurological phenotypes in heterozygous mice are replicated across independent studies and are mechanistically plausible, but the absence of structural brain malformations, epilepsy, or intellectual disability phenotypes in the animal models limits direct translation to a classic neurodevelopmental disorder presentation.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Pathways and molecular function:**
MAP2K7 is a core component of the MAP3K→MAP2K7→JNK→c-Jun/ATF2 signaling axis, activated by upstream kinases including MAP3K11 (MLK3) in response to cytokines, oxidative stress, and developmental signals (PMID: 35234473). It is the predominant direct activator of JNK isoforms and regulates downstream transcription factors including c-Jun, ATF2, and AP-1. MAP2K7 also participates in non-canonical functions: in zebrafish, it stabilizes the antiviral transcription factor IRF7 via K63-linked polyubiquitination and promotes proteasomal degradation of viral proteins (PMID: 37367226). In gastric cancer, MAP2K7 is regulated by methylation at K296 by EEF1AKMT3, which modulates its phosphorylation, ubiquitination, and stability, with downstream effects on TP53 (PMID: 35753528).

**Neurodevelopmental and brain expression relevance:**
Map2k7+/- mice exhibit altered brain metabolic activity and connectivity patterns on neuroimaging, with hippocampal hypermetabolism and prefrontal hypometabolism — patterns that mirror endophenotypes observed in schizophrenia (PMID: 31219577). The JNK pathway, of which MAP2K7 is a central activator, is well-established in neuronal apoptosis, axonal growth, and synaptic plasticity. The behavioral and neuroimaging phenotypes in haploinsufficient mice implicate MAP2K7 in monoaminergic neurotransmitter system regulation, particularly dopaminergic circuits relevant to attentional and executive function.

**Key experimental findings:**
- MAP2K7 is transcriptionally repressed by KLF4; loss of KLF4 via DNA methylation in pediatric T-ALL leads to aberrant MAP2K7/JNK/ATF2 activation and leukemia-initiating cell expansion, validating MAP2K7 as a functionally important signaling node (PMID: 27872496).
- Pharmacological inhibition of MAP2K7 with 5Z-7-oxozeaenol induces apoptosis in T-ALL cell lines and patient-derived xenograft models, confirming that MAP2K7 kinase activity is required for leukemic cell survival (PMID: 34504651).
- CRISPR/Cas9 genome-wide screens identified MAP2K7 loss as a driver of resistance to endocrine therapy and CDK4/6 inhibition in ER+ breast cancer, with MAP2K7 knockout reducing JNK-mediated stress responses and senescence entry (PMID: 40826108).
- MAP2K7 expression is significantly elevated in higher-grade colorectal cancer specimens (n=55), suggesting context-dependent oncogenic or tumor-suppressive roles (PMID: 41515982).
- Exercise-induced cardioprotection involves BAT-derived miRNAs (miR-125b-5p, miR-128-3p, miR-30d-5p) that cooperatively suppress MAP2K7 and the proapoptotic MAPK pathway in cardiomyocytes (PMID: 35387487), and miR-128-3p directly targets MAP2K7 in tongue squamous cell carcinoma (PMID: 39476205), indicating MAP2K7 is a biologically validated miRNA target across multiple tissues.

**Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE**

The molecular function of MAP2K7 within the JNK pathway is well-characterized, and there is convergent evidence for its role in neuronal and developmental contexts. However, the majority of functional studies have been conducted in cancer cell lines or non-neuronal systems, and direct mechanistic studies in human neurons or brain tissue are lacking in the retrieved evidence.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: LIMITED**

**Key strengths:**
1. Replicated neurocognitive and neuroimaging phenotypes in heterozygous Map2k7+/- mice across at least two independent studies (PMID: 27774567; PMID: 31219577) provide convergent model organism support for haploinsufficiency causing brain-relevant dysfunction.
2. Homozygous lethality in zebrafish (PMID: 37367226) and mice confirms that MAP2K7 is a dosage-sensitive, developmentally essential gene, consistent with the expectation that heterozygous loss-of-function variants could be pathogenic.
3. MAP2K7's central role in the JNK signaling pathway — which is well-established in neuronal survival, axonal development, and synaptic function — provides strong biological plausibility for neurodevelopmental relevance.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. **No human clinical evidence exists** in the retrieved literature: there are no published case reports, case series, or cohort studies describing patients with heterozygous MAP2K7 variants and a neurodevelopmental phenotype. This is the most significant limitation and prevents any clinical gene-disease association from being established at this time.
2. The animal model phenotypes, while neurologically relevant, are primarily behavioral and metabolic (attentional deficits, neuroimaging changes) rather than structural (e.g., brain malformations, microcephaly), making it difficult to define a specific human clinical syndrome.
3. Functional studies are predominantly conducted in cancer or immune cell contexts, with limited direct mechanistic data from human neuronal models.

**Conclusion:**
MAP2K7 is a biologically plausible neurodevelopmental candidate gene supported by replicated haploinsufficiency phenotypes in mouse models, including schizophrenia-relevant cognitive and neuroimaging endophenotypes, and by its essential role in JNK-mediated neuronal signaling. However, in the absence of any published human clinical cases linking heterozygous MAP2K7 variants to a neurodevelopmental disorder, the current evidence is insufficient to establish a definitive gene-disease relationship. Systematic sequencing of neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts and prospective case ascertainment are urgently needed to determine whether MAP2K7 haploinsufficiency causes a recognizable human syndrome.
