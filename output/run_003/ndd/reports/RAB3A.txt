# RAB3A Evidence Report: Autosomal Dominant Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

RAB3A encodes a small GTPase of the RAB family that is highly expressed in neurons, where it associates with synaptic vesicles and plays a central role in regulated neurotransmitter release, synaptic vesicle docking, and synaptic plasticity. It cycles between GDP-bound (inactive) and GTP-bound (active) states and interacts with key presynaptic effectors including RIM1, Rabphilin-3A, and the motor adaptor MADD to coordinate vesicle trafficking and exocytosis at the synapse.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent case reports/series:** Two independent publications have reported RAB3A as a disease gene for autosomal dominant cerebellar ataxia.

**Total approximate number of patients:** At least 18 individuals from 10 unrelated families are documented across the primary clinical series (PMID: 40166812), with additional families reported in a second independent study (PMID: 41492970).

**Most common phenotypes observed:** The predominant phenotype is adult-onset progressive cerebellar ataxia, often accompanied by cerebellar atrophy with vermis accentuation and cerebellar cortical degeneration on neuroimaging or neuropathology. Additional features include mild gait spasticity, tremor, and variable neurodevelopmental features. A subset of families with loss-of-function (LoF) variants or unique de novo missense variants presented with a broader neurodevelopmental phenotype rather than isolated ataxia (PMID: 40166812).

**Predominant variant type:** Missense variants predominate. The recurrent variant c.247C>T, p.Arg83Trp was identified in 9 of 10 families in the largest series (PMID: 40166812), including at least one confirmed de novo occurrence, and was independently identified in two unrelated families in the second study (PMID: 41492970). The p.Tyr91Cys missense variant was identified in one additional family. Three families harbored LoF variants associated with a neurodevelopmental phenotype, and three further families carried unique de novo missense variants also associated with neurodevelopmental features (PMID: 40166812).

**Known cohorts:** Patients were ascertained through multiple international centers, including the 100,000 Genomes Project (Genomics England/GEL cohort), which provided computational genetic association data supporting the gene-disease link (PMID: 40166812).

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE–STRONG**
The recurrence of p.Arg83Trp across 9 unrelated families, including a confirmed de novo occurrence, combined with independent replication in a second publication (PMID: 41492970), linkage data in a large dominant ataxia family, and GEL cohort-level association substantially strengthens the clinical case. The total patient count remains modest (~18+ individuals), and some variant-phenotype relationships (particularly for LoF variants and the neurodevelopmental subgroup) require further delineation.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** The mouse (*Mus musculus*) has been the primary model organism, with multiple independent knockout studies. Zebrafish (*Danio rerio*) data are available but are indirect (examining RAB3A as a marker in the context of C9orf72 loss-of-function) (PMID: 34172817).

**Key phenotypes from mouse knockouts:**

- *Single Rab3a knockout mice* are viable and fertile with normal Mendelian ratios, demonstrating that complete loss of Rab3A alone is not lethal in mice (PMID: 15269275). However, single knockouts display meaningful neurological phenotypes: a 26–27% reduction in docked synaptic vesicles and evoked neurotransmitter release at the neuromuscular junction, with over 50% reduction in evoked release at low extracellular calcium concentrations (PMID: 17640821). Behavioral studies reveal impaired spatial reversal learning, mild spatial working memory deficits, and increased exploratory/locomotor activity (PMID: 15078563), as well as deficits in cued fear conditioning (PMID: 16734774).

- *Quadruple Rab3 (A/B/C/D) knockout mice* die shortly after birth due to respiratory failure, with approximately 30% reduction in evoked neurotransmitter release in hippocampal neurons and near-complete ablation of dense-core vesicle (DCV) exocytosis — a deficit rescued by re-expression of RAB3A alone (PMID: 15269275; PMID: 31679900). Triple knockouts lacking Rab3A show sub-Mendelian survival, indicating a dose-dependent contribution of RAB3A to viability (PMID: 15269275).

- Rabphilin-3A protein levels are reduced in Rab3-deficient mice, consistent with RAB3A being required for synaptic enrichment of this effector (PMID: 15269275; PMID: 39412498).

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The mouse data are broadly consistent with the human phenotype. Loss of Rab3A impairs synaptic vesicle release and synaptic function, and behavioral phenotypes include cognitive and motor-related deficits. The absence of overt cerebellar ataxia in single Rab3a knockout mice may reflect species differences, compensation by paralogues (RAB3B/C/D), or the fact that the human disease is caused by dominant partial loss-of-function rather than complete biallelic loss. The Drosophila rescue experiments directly support the partial loss-of-function mechanism: both p.Arg83Trp and p.Tyr91Cys failed to rescue a Rab3 loss-of-function synaptic phenotype in flies, while overexpression in a wild-type background caused no phenotype, arguing against dominant negativity (PMID: 40166812).

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent mouse knockout studies consistently demonstrate that Rab3A is essential for normal synaptic vesicle release and synaptic function, with neurological and behavioral phenotypes. Drosophila functional rescue experiments directly interrogate the pathogenic variants identified in patients.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:** RAB3A is a central regulator of the synaptic vesicle cycle. In its GTP-bound state, it interacts with presynaptic effectors including RIM1 (a scaffolding protein at the active zone) and Rabphilin-3A to promote vesicle docking and priming for calcium-triggered exocytosis. GTP hydrolysis, facilitated by RAB3-GAP, is required for vesicle release and recycling. RAB3A also participates in anterograde axonal transport of synaptic vesicle precursors via the RAB3A–MADD–KIF1A/KIF1Bβ motor complex (PMID: 38512027). The RAB3–RIM pathway is specifically essential for dense-core vesicle exocytosis and neuropeptide/neurotrophin release (PMID: 31679900).

**Expression pattern:** RAB3A is highly and selectively expressed in neurons, particularly enriched at presynaptic terminals throughout the brain, making it directly relevant to neurodevelopmental and neurodegenerative disease contexts.

**Key experimental findings:**

- The recurrent pathogenic variant p.Arg83Trp lies in the switch II region of RAB3A, which is critical for effector binding. Mutant RAB3A R83W fails to bind RIM1 and Rabphilin-3A and shows diffuse cytoplasmic mislocalization resembling the GDP-bound state, consistent with a loss-of-function mechanism (PMID: 41492970).

- Functional assays in the primary clinical study demonstrated that variants in the GAP-binding domain reduce GAP-dependent GTP hydrolysis, and co-immunoprecipitation/mass spectrometry revealed variant-specific disruption of interactions with known RAB3A regulatory and effector proteins (PMID: 40166812).

- Hyperphosphorylation of RAB3A by pathogenic LRRK2 (p.R1441H) in iPSC-derived human neurons disrupts the RAB3A–MADD interaction and impairs anterograde axonal transport of synaptic vesicle precursors, demonstrating that perturbation of RAB3A function by upstream disease mechanisms causes neuronal dysfunction (PMID: 38512027).

- Alpha-synuclein interacts with GTP-bound RAB3A and inhibits its GTPase activity; pathological phospho-Ser129 alpha-synuclein enhances this interaction, linking RAB3A dysfunction to Parkinson's disease pathology (PMID: 35809645).

- Gene-gene interaction analyses across 15,502 participants in eight multi-ethnic studies identified significant interactions among MADD, PTPRN/PTPRN2, and RAB3A/RAB3C in association with type 2 diabetes (p = 1.9×10⁻⁸ to 3.1×10⁻⁵), consistent with RAB3A's role in insulin-containing dense-core vesicle exocytosis in pancreatic beta cells (ASHG 2016 – Identification of interactions in the MADD PTPRN RAB3 complex with type 2 diabetes in multiple populations).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
The molecular function of RAB3A is well characterized, its brain-enriched expression is established, and multiple independent experimental systems directly demonstrate that the specific pathogenic variants identified in patients disrupt RAB3A's effector interactions and GTPase activity through a partial loss-of-function mechanism.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE–STRONG**

**Key strengths:**
1. *Recurrent variant with independent replication:* The p.Arg83Trp variant has been identified in 9 of 10 families in the largest series (including a confirmed de novo) and independently in two unrelated families in a second publication (PMID: 40166812; PMID: 41492970), providing strong evidence for pathogenicity of this specific allele.
2. *Mechanistic coherence:* Functional studies across multiple platforms (biochemical GTPase assays, co-immunoprecipitation, structural analysis, Drosophila rescue experiments) consistently support a partial loss-of-function mechanism, with the variants disrupting effector binding and GTP hydrolysis at a structurally critical residue (PMID: 40166812; PMID: 41492970).
3. *Robust model organism support:* Multiple independent mouse knockout studies demonstrate that Rab3A is essential for normal synaptic vesicle release and neurological function, and Drosophila experiments directly test the patient variants (PMID: 15269275; PMID: 17640821; PMID: 40166812).

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. The total number of reported patients remains modest (~18+ individuals), and the full clinical spectrum — particularly for LoF variants and the neurodevelopmental subgroup — is not yet well defined.
2. Mouse single knockouts do not recapitulate cerebellar ataxia, limiting direct phenotypic modeling of the human disease; a knock-in mouse model expressing p.Arg83Trp has not yet been reported.
3. The mechanistic basis for why partial loss-of-function of a ubiquitously expressed presynaptic protein preferentially causes cerebellar degeneration in humans remains to be fully explained.

**Conclusion:** RAB3A is a well-characterized presynaptic GTPase with strong biological plausibility as a neurological disease gene. The identification of a recurrent, partially penetrant dominant variant (p.Arg83Trp) in multiple unrelated families with cerebellar ataxia, supported by independent replication, de novo occurrence, GEL cohort-level association, and convergent functional evidence for partial loss-of-function, establishes RAB3A as a credible autosomal dominant cerebellar ataxia gene. Further case ascertainment and knock-in animal models are needed to fully characterize the genotype-phenotype spectrum and confirm the pathogenic mechanism in vivo.

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. Heterozygous RAB3A variants cause cerebellar ataxia by a partial loss-of-function mechanism (PMID: 40166812)
2. An R83W mutation in Rab3A causes autosomal-dominant cerebellar ataxia (PMID: 41492970)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. Reduced C9orf72 function leads to defective synaptic vesicle release and neuromuscular dysfunction in zebrafish. (PMID: 34172817)
2. A complete genetic analysis of neuronal Rab3 function. (PMID: 15269275)
3. Rab3a deletion reduces vesicle docking and transmitter release at the mouse diaphragm synapse. (PMID: 17640821)
4. Mice deficient for the synaptic vesicle protein Rab3a show impaired spatial reversal learning and increased explorative activity but none of the behavioral changes shown by mice deficient for the Rab3a regulator Gdi1. (PMID: 15078563)
5. Biochemical, molecular and behavioral phenotypes of Rab3A mutations in the mouse. (PMID: 16734774)
6. The RAB3-RIM Pathway Is Essential for the Release of Neuromodulators. (PMID: 31679900)
7. Rabphilin-3A negatively regulates neuropeptide release, through its SNAP25 interaction. (PMID: 39412498)

### Biological Evidence
1. RAB3 phosphorylation by pathogenic LRRK2 impairs trafficking of synaptic vesicle precursors (PMID: 38512027)
2. Molecular and functional interactions of alpha-synuclein with Rab3a (PMID: 35809645)
