# Evidence Report: GFPT1 as a Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene with Heterozygous (Autosomal Dominant) Pathogenic Variants

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

GFPT1 (Glutamine-Fructose-6-Phosphate Transaminase 1) encodes the rate-limiting enzyme of the hexosamine biosynthetic pathway (HBP), catalyzing the conversion of glutamine and fructose-6-phosphate to glucosamine-6-phosphate, ultimately producing UDP-GlcNAc for N- and O-linked glycosylation of proteins (PMID: 37107691). GFPT1 is particularly critical in skeletal muscle, where its muscle-specific isoform regulates neuromuscular junction (NMJ) integrity and glycosylation of key synaptic proteins.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports/series:** Three independent clinical publications were identified.

**Total approximate number of patients:** The largest study reported biallelic mutations across 13 unrelated families (exact patient count not specified in the abstract) (PMID: 21310273); a Brazilian case series described 5 patients (PMID: 41323224); and one additional single case report was identified (PMID: 38235042). Including the ASHG 2014 conference report (ASHG 2014 - Congenital Myasthenia Syndrome), the total documented patient count is at minimum approximately 20 individuals across independent reports.

**Most common phenotypes observed:** All reported patients present with features consistent with **autosomal recessive congenital myasthenic syndrome (CMS)**, including proximal (and in some cases distal) muscle weakness, fatigable weakness, decremental response on repetitive nerve stimulation (RNS), and myopathic EMG findings. Additional features include elevated muscle enzymes, myopathic echogenicity on muscle ultrasound, and dramatic clinical response to acetylcholinesterase inhibitors (e.g., pyridostigmine). One patient in the Brazilian series had neonatal-onset epilepsy and cognitive impairment (PMID: 41323224), and one case exhibited the unusual finding of agenesis of the left pectoralis major muscle (PMID: 38235042).

**Predominant variant type:** The reported variants are heterogeneous and include missense variants (e.g., c.41G>A, p.Arg14Gln, homozygous; PMID: 41323224), loss-of-function splice-site variants (e.g., c.7+2T>G; PMID: 38235042), 3'UTR variants associated with reduced expression (c.*22C>A; PMID: 38235042), a homozygous frameshift insertion (c.865-866insG; ASHG 2014), and 18 distinct biallelic mutations across 13 families (PMID: 21310273). **All reported pathogenic variants are biallelic (homozygous or compound heterozygous), consistent exclusively with autosomal recessive inheritance.**

**Cohort affiliation:** None of the reported patients are identified as belonging to established research cohorts such as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL.

**Critical note regarding the query:** The available clinical evidence provides **no support** for heterozygous (autosomal dominant) pathogenic variants in GFPT1 causing disease. Every reported patient carries biallelic variants, and the established disease mechanism is autosomal recessive. No de novo heterozygous variants or dominant-negative mechanisms have been reported in the clinical literature surveyed.

**Strength of clinical evidence for autosomal RECESSIVE CMS:** STRONG
**Strength of clinical evidence for autosomal DOMINANT / heterozygous disease:** NONE

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Multiple model organisms have been employed, including mouse (*Mus musculus*) and zebrafish (*Danio rerio*).

**Key findings:**

- **Mouse (conditional knockout, muscle-specific):** Muscle-specific Gfpt1 knockout mice develop fatigable muscle weakness, progressive myopathy, tubular aggregates, and abnormal NMJ morphology with both pre- and postsynaptic changes, closely recapitulating the human CMS phenotype (PMID: 29905857). A knock-in mouse model with a frameshift in the muscle-specific exon 9 showed reduced UDP-HexNAc and protein O-GlcNAcylation, poor exercise performance, and abnormal NMJ structures in aged animals, with maladaptive ER stress and apoptosis in Gfpt1-silenced myotubes (PMID: 38903011). A further conditional knockout model demonstrated reduced glycosylation and expression of the acetylcholine receptor delta subunit (AChRδ) at the NMJ, providing a direct molecular mechanism for impaired neuromuscular transmission (PMID: 39456185). Importantly, galactose treatment rescued NMJ transmission deficits in Gfpt1-deficient mice via the Leloir pathway, establishing a potential therapeutic avenue (PMID: 40879313).

- **Zebrafish (morpholino knockdown):** Gfpt1 knockdown in zebrafish produced altered muscle fiber morphology and impaired NMJ development, directly supporting the findings in human patients (PMID: 21310273). A separate zebrafish full knockout model demonstrated defective melanocyte regeneration, indicating broader roles for GFPT1 in cell differentiation (PMID: 17542649). A gain-of-function zebrafish model showed hepatic steatosis and developmental arrest, highlighting the importance of HBP flux regulation (PMID: 28582574).

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The mouse and zebrafish models are highly consistent with the human autosomal recessive CMS phenotype, reproducing NMJ dysfunction, muscle weakness, and fatigability. All knockout/knockdown models are viable (no embryonic lethality reported), and no Mendelian ratio deviations are noted, consistent with recessive inheritance requiring biallelic loss. **No model organism study demonstrates a haploinsufficiency or dominant phenotype from heterozygous loss of Gfpt1.**

**Strength of model organism evidence for NMJ/muscle disease (biallelic loss):** STRONG
**Strength of model organism evidence for heterozygous dominant disease:** NONE

---

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:** GFPT1 is the committed, rate-limiting step of the HBP, producing UDP-GlcNAc, the obligate substrate for N-linked glycosylation, O-GlcNAcylation, and sialylation (PMID: 37107691). Its activity is regulated by mTOR, AMPK, and stress-responsive transcription factors. In muscle, GFPT1 is essential for proper glycosylation of NMJ-associated proteins, most critically the AChR delta subunit, whose hypo-glycosylation leads to reduced expression and impaired neuromuscular transmission (PMID: 39456185). GFPT1 also interacts with OGT to regulate O-GlcNAcylation of downstream targets (PMID: 41355137).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:** The available biological evidence focuses predominantly on skeletal muscle expression and function. A muscle-specific alternative splicing isoform of GFPT1 (including exon 9) is well characterized and is the isoform most relevant to NMJ disease (PMID: 37744035). Evidence for significant GFPT1 expression or function in the central nervous system or during neurodevelopment per se is not provided in the surveyed literature. The rare cases of epilepsy and cognitive impairment reported in one patient (PMID: 41323224) have not been mechanistically linked to CNS-specific GFPT1 dysfunction. GFPT1 has been identified as a candidate risk gene for primary open-angle glaucoma by TWAS/Mendelian randomization (OR=1.34, 95% CI 1.13–1.59; PMID: 40459497), suggesting possible roles beyond skeletal muscle, but this is a statistical association without functional validation in neural tissue.

**Key experimental findings:** Loss of GFPT1 in muscle reduces UDP-HexNAc pools, impairs O-GlcNAcylation and sialylation, causes hypo-glycosylation of AChRδ, triggers ER stress and maladaptive UPR, and ultimately disrupts NMJ structure and function (PMID: 38903011, PMID: 39456185). The transcription factor Tisp40 directly activates GFPT1 transcription via a UPRE in its promoter in cardiomyocytes, demonstrating tissue-specific transcriptional regulation (PMID: 37291168). CRISPR screens identified GFPT1 as critical for T cell expansion, indicating immunometabolic roles (PMID: 39151020).

**Strength of biological evidence for NMJ/muscle disease mechanism:** STRONG
**Strength of biological evidence supporting a neurodevelopmental or dominant disease mechanism:** LIMITED to NONE

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level for GFPT1 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous (autosomal dominant) pathogenic variants:** INSUFFICIENT

**Key strengths of the evidence:**
- There is robust, multi-layered evidence (clinical, model organism, and mechanistic) establishing GFPT1 as a well-validated gene for **autosomal recessive congenital myasthenic syndrome**, with consistent findings across at least 13 unrelated families and multiple independent model systems (PMID: 21310273, PMID: 29905857, PMID: 39456185).
- The molecular mechanism is well characterized: biallelic loss of GFPT1 reduces HBP flux, impairs glycosylation of NMJ proteins (particularly AChRδ), and disrupts neuromuscular transmission.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- **No clinical, model organism, or biological evidence supports heterozygous (autosomal dominant) pathogenic variants in GFPT1 as a disease mechanism.** All reported patients carry biallelic variants, and heterozygous carriers within families have not been reported as affected.
- There is no published evidence of de novo heterozygous GFPT1 variants in neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts, no haploinsufficiency phenotype in mouse heterozygotes, and no dominant-negative mechanism described.
- The occasional CNS features (epilepsy, cognitive impairment) reported in one patient (PMID: 41323224) remain unexplained mechanistically and may represent phenotypic expansion of recessive disease rather than a distinct dominant neurodevelopmental syndrome.

**Conclusion:** GFPT1 is a robustly validated gene for autosomal recessive congenital myasthenic syndrome, with strong convergent evidence from clinical genetics, animal models, and molecular biology. However, the current body of evidence provides **no support** for GFPT1 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene acting through heterozygous (autosomal dominant) pathogenic variants; this proposed disease mechanism is not substantiated by any of the three lines of evidence reviewed. Any clinical evaluation of heterozygous GFPT1 variants in a neurodevelopmental context should be interpreted with extreme caution and would require substantial additional evidence before a causal relationship could be established.
