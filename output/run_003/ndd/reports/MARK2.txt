# MARK2 as a Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene: Literature Evidence Report

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

MARK2 (Microtubule Affinity-Regulating Kinase 2), also known as Par-1b or EMK1, is a serine/threonine kinase that phosphorylates microtubule-associated proteins (MAPs) to regulate microtubule dynamics, neuronal polarity, and cell migration. It plays a central role in establishing cell polarity, dendritic vesicle trafficking, and multiple signaling pathways including WNT/β-catenin, Hippo/YAP, and PI3K/AKT, with particularly important functions during brain development and neuronal differentiation.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports/series:** Four independent clinical publications were identified, supplemented by two large-scale genomic studies.

**Total approximate number of patients:** At least 33 individually described patients with MARK2 variants, with substantially larger numbers implicated through population-level cohort analyses. The largest single case series reported 31 patients (PMID: 39419027), with one additional de novo case reported independently (PMID: 40542891).

**Most common phenotypes observed:** Across all clinical reports, the core phenotype is a neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by:
- Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) — present in virtually all reported cases
- Global developmental delay / intellectual disability
- Distinctive facial features
- Epilepsy (reported in at least one case; PMID: 40542891)

**Predominant variant type:** Loss-of-function (LoF) variants predominate strongly. In the largest case series (PMID: 39419027), 81% of variants were LoF (frameshift, nonsense, or splice-site), with missense variants comprising the remaining ~19%. A subsequent study (PMID: 41918168) confirmed that truncating variants cause complete loss of function, while most missense variants act as hypomorphic (partial loss-of-function) alleles in functional assays, with at least one missense variant (p.Lys224Glu) not confirmed as pathogenic in a Drosophila model. A de novo frameshift variant (c.645_646insA, p.Ala216Serfs*12) was independently confirmed in a single patient with ASD, epilepsy, and developmental delay (PMID: 40542891). One case of a de novo retrotransposon insertion disrupting MARK2 was identified by whole-genome sequencing in an ASD proband (ASHG 2013 - De novo and inherited retrotransposon insertions associated with autism revealed by whole genome sequencing).

**Large-scale cohort evidence:** MARK2 reached exome-wide significance (P < 2.5 × 10⁻⁶) for ASD association in an analysis of 42,607 autism cases from the SPARK cohort, identifying it as a new moderate-risk gene based on both de novo and inherited rare coding variants (PMID: 35982159). A cross-ancestry study further confirmed genome-wide significant association (FDR < 0.05) with ASD across Latin American and European cohorts, highlighting MARK2 as a highly constrained gene with consistent signal across populations (PMID: 41912808). Patients are not specifically reported from UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL cohorts in the available evidence.

**Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG**
The combination of a well-characterized case series of 31 patients, an independent single-case report with de novo confirmation, exome-wide significant association in a cohort exceeding 42,000 ASD cases, and cross-ancestry replication provides strong clinical evidence for MARK2 as an ASD/neurodevelopmental disorder gene.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*) — multiple independent full knockout and conditional knockout lines; *Xenopus laevis* (morpholino knockdown); *Drosophila melanogaster* (functional variant assessment, referenced in PMID: 41918168); *C. elegans* (PAR-1/MARK ortholog studies, PMID: 41381452).

**Key phenotypes from mouse knockouts:**

The most directly disease-relevant mouse study (PMID: 39436150) demonstrated that full MARK2 knockout mice exhibit:
- Defective social behavior
- Impaired learning and memory
- Heightened seizure susceptibility and neuronal hyperexcitability in hippocampal slices
- Altered dendritic spine morphology
- Decreased excitatory synaptic transmission
- Transcriptional dysregulation of genes governing synaptic transmission and ion homeostasis

An earlier independent knockout study (PMID: 17196307) confirmed impaired spatial learning and memory in MARK2-null mice using the radial water maze, with poor long-term retention, while locomotion and baseline anxiety were normal. A third study (PMID: 30654821) revealed that MARK2 knockout leads to a primed microglial phenotype with increased density, hypertrophic morphology, enhanced phagocytosis during early brain development, and exaggerated neuroinflammatory responses to injury — findings consistent with neurodevelopmental vulnerability.

Importantly, full MARK2 knockout mice are viable and fertile across all reported studies, which is consistent with an autosomal dominant haploinsufficiency mechanism in humans (heterozygous patients are affected, while homozygous null mice survive). No Mendelian ratio deviations or embryonic lethality were reported.

Additional mouse studies document metabolic phenotypes (lean body composition, insulin hypersensitivity; PMID: 17372192, PMID: 22206009), circadian period shortening in neuronal-specific conditional knockouts (PMID: 41812650), and neuroprotection under proteotoxic stress (PMID: 41231952), further supporting broad neurobiological roles.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The mouse knockout phenotype is highly consistent with the human neurodevelopmental phenotype, recapitulating ASD-relevant features including defective social behavior, learning and memory impairment, and seizure susceptibility. The absence of lethality in homozygous null mice supports the plausibility of heterozygous pathogenic variants causing disease in humans.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent mouse knockout lines from different laboratories consistently demonstrate neurological phenotypes directly relevant to the human disorder, with no lethality confounds.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
MARK2 is a multifunctional kinase with well-characterized roles in several signaling pathways relevant to neurodevelopment:

- **WNT/β-catenin signaling:** iPSC and mouse studies demonstrated that MARK2 loss downregulates WNT/β-catenin signaling, causing anomalous neural rosette polarity and imbalanced proliferation/differentiation of neural progenitor cells; lithium treatment (a WNT activator) partially rescued these deficits (PMID: 39419027).
- **Neuronal polarity and cortical migration:** In utero electroporation experiments showed that MARK2 reduction stalls multipolar neurons at the intermediate zone border during cortical radial migration by stabilizing microtubules, with antagonistic interactions with the migration factor doublecortin (DCX) (PMID: 19036994).
- **Dendritic vesicle trafficking:** MARK2 phosphorylates KIF13A at a 14-3-3 binding site, preventing axonal missorting of transferrin receptor-containing vesicles and maintaining polarized dendritic transport (PMID: 38709923).
- **Hippo/YAP signaling:** MARK2 and MARK3 are catalytic codependencies for YAP/TAZ function, directly phosphorylating NF2 and YAP/TAZ to modulate Hippo pathway activity (PMID: 39058094) — a pathway with emerging relevance to brain development.
- **Microtubule regulation:** MARK2 phosphorylates MAPs to destabilize microtubules, and its loss leads to microtubule hyperstabilization, directly impacting neuronal morphology and migration.

**Expression pattern:** While direct brain expression data are not explicitly quantified in the provided evidence, the convergence of multiple independent studies demonstrating neurological phenotypes in MARK2 knockout mice, iPSC-derived neural models, and in utero electroporation experiments in rodent cortex strongly implies robust expression in developing and mature neurons. Neuronal-specific conditional knockout mice show circadian phenotypes (PMID: 41812650), confirming neuronal expression.

**Key experimental findings:**
- Proband-derived iPSCs and CRISPR-engineered isogenic lines recapitulated neural developmental deficits, providing direct human cellular evidence (PMID: 39419027).
- Drosophila functional assays discriminated pathogenic from non-pathogenic missense variants, with truncating variants showing reduced protein levels and most missense variants showing partial loss of function (PMID: 41918168).
- MARK2 loss in mice leads to transcriptional changes in synaptic transmission and ion homeostasis genes (PMID: 39436150), providing a molecular mechanism for seizure susceptibility and synaptic dysfunction.

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
Multiple orthogonal experimental systems — iPSC-derived human neurons, mouse cortical electroporation, Drosophila functional assays, and biochemical pathway studies — converge on a loss-of-function mechanism affecting neuronal polarity, migration, synaptic function, and WNT signaling.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths:**
1. **Clinical convergence at scale:** MARK2 has reached exome-wide significance for ASD in a cohort of >42,000 cases (PMID: 35982159) and has been replicated across ancestries (PMID: 41912808), with a well-characterized case series of 31 patients (PMID: 39419027) and independent case reports confirming de novo LoF variants.
2. **Mechanistic coherence:** The predominance of LoF variants in patients, combined with functional evidence from iPSC models, Drosophila assays, and mouse knockouts, consistently supports a haploinsufficiency mechanism. The viability of homozygous null mice is compatible with autosomal dominant disease in humans.
3. **Phenotypic consistency across species:** Mouse knockouts recapitulate the core human phenotype (social behavior deficits, learning/memory impairment, seizure susceptibility), and iPSC studies demonstrate human-relevant neural developmental defects.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- The number of patients with detailed clinical characterization remains relatively modest (n=31 in the largest series), and phenotypic data on intellectual disability severity, adaptive functioning, and dysmorphic features are incompletely described across studies.
- The pathogenicity of missense variants requires case-by-case functional assessment; at least one reported variant (p.Lys224Glu) was not confirmed pathogenic in the Drosophila model (PMID: 41918168), indicating that not all missense variants in MARK2 are disease-causing.
- No patients are specifically reported from well-established diagnostic cohorts (UDN, DDD, GEL), and genotype-phenotype correlations remain to be fully established.

**Conclusion:** MARK2 is a well-supported autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene, with strong and convergent evidence from large-scale genomic studies, individual case series, functional iPSC and animal models, and mechanistic pathway studies. Heterozygous loss-of-function variants in MARK2 cause a neurodevelopmental syndrome characterized primarily by autism spectrum disorder, developmental delay, and in some cases epilepsy. The evidence base is sufficient to support clinical reporting of pathogenic LoF variants, while careful functional assessment remains warranted for missense variants of uncertain significance.

### Clinical Evidence
1. MARK2 variants cause autism spectrum disorder via the downregulation of WNT/β-catenin signaling pathway. (PMID: 39419027)
2. Identification of a de Novo MARK2 gene variant in a patient with autism spectrum disorder, epilepsy, and neurodevelopmental delay. (PMID: 40542891)
3. Loss-of-function variants in MARK2 cause neurodevelopmental disorder. (PMID: 41918168)
4. Integrating de novo and inherited variants in 42,607 autism cases identifies mutations in new moderate-risk genes. (PMID: 35982159)
5. Deleterious coding variation associated with autism is shared across ancestries. (PMID: 41912808)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. Neuronal connectivity, behavioral, and transcriptional alterations associated with the loss of MARK2. (PMID: 39436150)

3. Loss of Par1b/MARK2 primes microglia during brain development and enhances their sensitivity to injury. (PMID: 30654821)
4. Loss of the Par-1b/MARK2 polarity kinase leads to increased metabolic rate, decreased adiposity, and insulin hypersensitivity in vivo. (PMID: 17372192)
5. Regulation of glucose homeostasis by KSR1 and MARK2. (PMID: 22206009)
6. Discovery of MARK2 as a physiological kinase for PER2 in the mammalian clock. (PMID: 41812650)
