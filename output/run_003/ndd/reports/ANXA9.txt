# ANXA9 as a Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene: Literature Evidence Report

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

ANXA9 (Annexin A9) is a member of the annexin superfamily of calcium- and phospholipid-binding proteins, characterized by their ability to interact with membrane phospholipids and regulate intracellular signaling, cytoskeletal organization, and membrane trafficking. Its primary known functions include modulation of cell adhesion, migration, and proliferation, with emerging roles in oncogenic signaling pathways including Wnt, TGF-β, AKT/mTOR/STAT3, and p53/Bcl-2 axes across multiple cancer types.

- **Predominant variant type:** No pathogenic variants reported
- **Known cohort representation (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL):** None identified
- **Strength of clinical evidence: NONE**

No clinical evidence was identified linking heterozygous (or any) pathogenic variants in ANXA9 to neurodevelopmental disorders in humans. The structured clinical evidence dataset returned no entries. There are no published case reports, case series, or cohort studies describing individuals with ANXA9 variants and neurodevelopmental phenotypes such as intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, epilepsy, or structural brain anomalies.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

- **Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*)
- **Knockout type:** Conditional knockout (Sertoli cell-specific androgen receptor knockout; SCARKO model)
- **Key phenotypes:** The sole model organism study identified (PMID: 21152390) examined Sertoli cell-specific androgen receptor conditional knockout mice and found that *Anxa9* transcript levels were significantly altered in Sertoli cells of SCARKO mice compared to controls. *Anxa9* was identified among a set of genes related to cell adhesion and cytoskeletal dynamics showing marked differential expression. The broader phenotypic context of this model included defective Sertoli cell barrier formation, delayed barrier development, disturbances in Sertoli cell nuclear maturation, and aberrant positioning of cytoskeletal elements. Importantly, *Anxa9* was not the primary gene targeted in this model; its dysregulation was a downstream transcriptional consequence of androgen receptor loss.
- **Neurological phenotypes:** None reported. No dedicated *Anxa9* knockout or knockdown mouse model was identified, and no neurological, behavioral, or brain structural phenotypes were described.
- **Lethality / Mendelian ratio deviations:** Not reported for *Anxa9* specifically.
- **Consistency with human neurodevelopmental phenotype:** Not applicable; no relevant human phenotype has been established, and the model organism data do not address neurodevelopmental biology.
- **Strength of model organism evidence: NONE**

There is no dedicated *Anxa9* animal model, and the single study referencing *Anxa9* in mice does so only as a transcriptional readout in a reproductive biology context, with no neurodevelopmental relevance.

---

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

- **Known pathways and molecular function:** ANXA9 has been implicated in multiple oncogenic signaling cascades. In breast cancer, ANXA9 facilitates S100A4 secretion and activates the AKT/mTOR/STAT3 pathway, while also modulating p53/Bcl-2-mediated apoptosis; self-phosphorylation at Ser2 and Thr69 mediates cytokine excretion (IL-6, IL-8, CCL2, CCL5) and promotes angiogenesis (PMID: 38609357). In colorectal cancer (CRC), ANXA9 promotes proliferation via the Wnt signaling pathway (PMID: 37349657) and is transcriptionally regulated by the zinc finger protein ZMYM2, with its overexpression driving oxaliplatin resistance (PMID: 40427072). In gastric cancer, ANXA9 mediates cell migration and growth through TGF-β/SMAD2/3 signaling and promotes epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (PMID: 34587407). ANXA9 is also a direct transcriptional target of ERβ in breast cancer cells, placing it downstream of estrogen receptor signaling (PMID: 35872983).
- **Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:** No studies were identified demonstrating ANXA9 expression in the developing or adult brain, in neural progenitor cells, or in any neurodevelopmental context. All expression data pertain to epithelial cancers (breast, colorectal, gastric, ovarian, head and neck). In HPV-negative head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, ANXA9 is notably *downregulated* in 42% of tumor samples compared to normal epithelia (PMID: 30744186), contrasting with its upregulation in other cancer types and suggesting context-dependent regulation.
- **Key experimental findings:** Functional studies consistently demonstrate that ANXA9 knockdown (siRNA or shRNA) reduces cancer cell proliferation, migration, invasion, and in vivo tumor growth across multiple cancer types (PMID: 37294149; PMID: 29393380; PMID: 40427072). miR-186-5p directly targets ANXA9 and its overexpression phenocopies ANXA9 knockdown in breast cancer cells (PMID: 37841425). miR-105-1 similarly targets ANXA9 to modulate cisplatin resistance in ovarian carcinoma (PMID: 33680718). None of these findings have neurodevelopmental relevance.
- **Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED**

While there is a substantial body of biological evidence characterizing ANXA9 function in cancer biology, none of this evidence pertains to neurodevelopmental processes, brain expression, synaptic function, neuronal differentiation, or related mechanisms. The biological evidence is therefore rated LIMITED specifically in the context of neurodevelopmental disease relevance, despite being moderately robust for oncology.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

- **Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:** ANXA9 has documented roles in cell adhesion, cytoskeletal dynamics, and intracellular signaling (PMID: 21152390; PMID: 38609357), which are processes broadly relevant to neurodevelopment. The gene is biologically active and experimentally tractable.

**Key gaps and limitations:** There are no clinical reports of individuals with ANXA9 variants and neurodevelopmental phenotypes. No dedicated animal model of *Anxa9* loss or gain of function exists, and no neurological phenotypes have been described in any model system. All biological evidence is restricted to cancer biology in epithelial tissues, with no data on brain expression, neuronal function, or developmental neurobiology. The single model organism study referencing *Anxa9* does so only as a secondary transcriptional readout in a reproductive biology context (PMID: 21152390).

**Conclusion:** Based on the totality of available evidence, there is currently **no credible support** for ANXA9 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous autosomal dominant pathogenic variants. The gene's known biology is centered on epithelial cancer progression and does not intersect with established neurodevelopmental mechanisms. Establishing ANXA9 as a candidate neurodevelopmental disease gene would require, at minimum, identification of affected individuals with de novo or segregating heterozygous variants and compatible phenotypes, supported by neurologically relevant functional or animal model data.
