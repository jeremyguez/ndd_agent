# KLF7 Evidence Report: Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene Assessment

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

KLF7 (Krüppel-like Factor 7) is a member of the Krüppel-like family of zinc finger transcription factors, characterized by three C-terminal zinc finger DNA-binding domains and an N-terminal F-box motif involved in protein-protein interactions and ubiquitin-mediated proteolysis. Its primary known function is the regulation of neuronal morphogenesis, axon guidance, and neurogenesis during embryonic and postnatal nervous system development, with additional roles in sensory neuron differentiation, hippocampal development, and circadian rhythm regulation in the brain.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports:** Four independent case reports or case series were identified in the literature.

**Total approximate number of patients:** Approximately 9 patients across all studies, though there is likely overlap between the ASHG 2016 abstract (Shinde et al.) and the published report (PMID: 29251763), which together describe 3–4 unrelated individuals with de novo missense variants. An additional patient with a de novo 2q33.3-q34 chromosomal deletion encompassing KLF7 was reported (PMID: 25899208), and one further patient with a 2q33 deletion encompassing both CTLA4 and KLF7 was described in a French cohort (PMID: 39578275).

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
The phenotypic spectrum across patients is notably consistent and includes:
- Intellectual disability / developmental delay (present in virtually all patients)
- Hypotonia and/or abnormal muscle tone (hypo- or hypertonia, sometimes mixed)
- Feeding and swallowing difficulties (neonatal and beyond)
- Psychiatric features including autism spectrum disorder, anxiety, and attention deficit disorder
- Motor delay and neuromuscular symptoms (motor dyspraxia, coordination difficulties)
- Elevated creatine kinase (noted in at least one patient)

The chromosomal deletion cases additionally showed microcephaly, dysmorphic facial features, autistic traits, and Rett-like features (PMID: 25899208), though these may reflect contributions from other genes within the deleted interval.

**Predominant variant type:** The strongest evidence for KLF7 as a disease gene comes from de novo heterozygous missense variants. Three specific variants were characterized in the ASHG 2016 report (Shinde et al.): p.T137M (c.410C>T), p.P139S (c.415C>T), and p.D264N (c.790G>A). Two of these (p.T137M and p.P139S) fall within the F-box motif, and one (p.D264N) falls within the zinc finger DNA-binding domain — both functionally critical regions. All were predicted damaging by in silico tools and identified by diagnostic exome sequencing. The chromosomal deletion cases represent copy number variants (deletions) consistent with haploinsufficiency as an additional mechanism (PMID: 25899208; PMID: 39578275).

**Cohort affiliation:** None of the patients are explicitly identified as belonging to established research cohorts such as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL. The French deletion case (PMID: 39578275) was identified through a clinical immunology/genetics program in France.

**Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED to MODERATE**
The evidence is based on a small number of patients (fewer than 10 total), with the primary missense variant series reported only in an ASHG abstract (Shinde et al., 2016) rather than a peer-reviewed publication with full clinical details. The published peer-reviewed literature (PMID: 29251763) provides supporting context but limited granular variant-level data in its abstract. The chromosomal deletion cases (PMID: 25899208; PMID: 39578275) are confounded by the presence of multiple genes within the deleted regions, making it difficult to attribute the neurodevelopmental phenotype exclusively to KLF7. Nonetheless, the de novo occurrence of variants in multiple unrelated individuals with a shared, recognizable phenotypic spectrum is a meaningful signal. Overall, the clinical evidence is rated **LIMITED to MODERATE**, pending full peer-reviewed publication of the missense variant series.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*), using both germline full knockout and tissue-specific conditional knockout models.

**Key phenotypes from knockout studies:**

The most foundational study demonstrated that germline Klf7 knockout mice die perinatally and exhibit severe deficits in neurite outgrowth and axonal misprojection in the olfactory system, visual system, cerebral cortex, and hippocampus, with downregulation of cell cycle regulators p21 and p27 in the olfactory epithelium (PMID: 15964824). This perinatal lethality in homozygous null mice is consistent with an essential role in nervous system development, though it also means that heterozygous mice must be examined to model the human haploinsufficiency condition.

A conditional knockout of KLF7 in hippocampal progenitors (Emx1-Cre;Klf7F/F) produced viable mice with significant hippocampal shrinkage, disrupted neurogenesis, impaired neuronal differentiation and migration, and behavioral phenotypes including anxiety- and depression-like behaviors and memory impairments (PMID: 40762575). Transcriptomic profiling identified Draxin, a neural chemorepellent, as a key downstream transcriptional target of KLF7, and overexpression of Draxin rescued dentate gyrus granule cell migration defects, establishing a mechanistic pathway.

A separate full knockout study demonstrated autism-like behavior in Klf7-deficient mice, mediated through downregulation of the Clock gene in the brain and consequent disruption of circadian rhythm — a pathway increasingly implicated in neurodevelopmental disorders (PMID: 36207723).

Additional studies confirmed KLF7's role in sensory neuron development: Klf7 cooperates with Brn3a to control TrkA expression in trigeminal ganglia, and double mutant (Brn3a-/-;Klf7-/-) mice lose all Trk+ nociceptive sensory neurons (PMID: 17011544). Transcriptomic analysis of Klf7-null olfactory sensory neurons revealed altered expression of genes governing axonal growth, cytoskeletal dynamics, cell adhesion, and synaptogenesis, including reduced OMP and L1 adhesion molecule expression (PMID: 17123745). KLF7 also regulates neurogenic marker BLBP/FABP7 in neural stem cells and influences neuronal and cardiomyocytic differentiation of embryonic stem cells (PMID: 20580711).

Non-neurological conditional knockout phenotypes (cardiac hypertrophy, adipose inflammation) were also reported but are less relevant to the neurodevelopmental disease context (PMID: 36810848; PMID: 37342904).

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The mouse knockout data are highly consistent with the human phenotype. Deficits in hippocampal development, neuronal migration, axon guidance, and neurite outgrowth in mice parallel the intellectual disability, developmental delay, hypotonia, and neuromuscular features observed in human patients. The autism-like behavior in Klf7 knockout mice (PMID: 36207723) directly mirrors the autism spectrum disorder and psychiatric features reported in human cases.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent mouse studies using both full and conditional knockout strategies consistently demonstrate severe, reproducible neurological phenotypes that closely recapitulate the human clinical presentation. The mechanistic depth (Draxin pathway, Clock gene regulation, TrkA signaling) further strengthens this evidence tier.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
KLF7 functions as a transcriptional activator and repressor through its three C-terminal zinc finger domains, which bind GC-rich promoter elements. Its N-terminal F-box motif mediates protein-protein interactions relevant to ubiquitin-mediated proteolysis. Key transcriptional targets in the nervous system include: p21/p27 (cell cycle regulation in neuronal progenitors), TrkA and TrkB (neurotrophin receptors critical for sensory neuron survival), NGF and GAP43 (axonal growth and regeneration), Draxin (neural chemorepellent governing hippocampal neuronal migration), Clock (circadian rhythm master regulator), and MKNK2 (a kinase involved in microglial polarization after ischemic injury) (PMID: 40762575; PMID: 17011544; PMID: 27826105; PMID: 36207723; PMID: 40923147).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**
KLF7 is expressed broadly in post-mitotic neurons throughout the developing nervous system, including the olfactory epithelium, hippocampus, cerebral cortex, trigeminal ganglia, and dorsal root ganglia. Its expression is highest during embryonic neurogenesis and early postnatal development, consistent with a role in neuronal differentiation and circuit formation. The ASHG 2016 report (Shinde et al.) explicitly notes KLF7's implication in neurogenesis including axon guidance in the hippocampus, olfactory bulbs, and cortex — regions directly relevant to the cognitive and behavioral phenotypes observed in patients.

**Key experimental findings:**
- KLF7 overexpression in Schwann cells via AAV2 promoted DRG neurite outgrowth and sciatic nerve regeneration, with upregulation of NGF, GAP43, TrkA, and TrkB, confirming KLF7 as a transcriptional activator of neurotrophin signaling (PMID: 27826105).
- Conditional hippocampal KLF7 knockout identified Draxin as a direct transcriptional target, with rescue experiments confirming functional relevance to neuronal migration (PMID: 40762575).
- The two patient variants in the F-box motif (p.T137M, p.P139S) and one in the zinc finger domain (p.D264N) are predicted to disrupt protein-protein interactions and DNA binding, respectively, providing a plausible dominant-negative or haploinsufficiency mechanism (ASHG 2016 - Shinde et al.).
- KLF7 represses MKNK2 transcription via direct promoter binding, linking KLF7 to neuroinflammatory regulation in the context of ischemic injury (PMID: 40923147).

Additional biological roles in adipogenesis, cardiac metabolism, hepatocellular carcinoma, and CAR-T cell biology were identified but are not directly relevant to the neurodevelopmental disease context.

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
The molecular function of KLF7 as a transcription factor governing neuronal differentiation, axon guidance, and neurotrophin signaling is well-established across multiple independent experimental systems. Direct transcriptional targets relevant to neurodevelopment have been validated by ChIP, luciferase assays, and rescue experiments. The location of patient variants in functionally critical protein domains provides a mechanistically coherent link between genotype and phenotype.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths:**
1. *De novo* occurrence of heterozygous missense variants in multiple unrelated individuals with a shared, recognizable neurodevelopmental phenotype (intellectual disability, hypotonia, feeding difficulties, psychiatric features) provides meaningful clinical signal, consistent with an autosomal dominant mechanism.
2. Mouse knockout models robustly and reproducibly recapitulate the human neurological phenotype across multiple independent studies, including autism-like behavior, hippocampal developmental defects, and axonal misprojection — directly paralleling the human clinical features.
3. KLF7's molecular function as a transcriptional regulator of neurotrophin signaling, neuronal migration, and axon guidance is well-characterized, providing strong biological plausibility for its role in neurodevelopmental disease.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. The primary clinical series of missense variants (Shinde et al.) remains unpublished in a peer-reviewed journal, limiting independent scrutiny of variant curation, phenotypic characterization, and segregation data.
2. The total number of reported patients is small (fewer than 10), and the chromosomal deletion cases are confounded by multi-gene deletions, making it difficult to attribute phenotypes exclusively to KLF7 haploinsufficiency.
3. Functional validation of the specific patient variants (p.T137M, p.P139S, p.D264N) in cellular or animal models has not been reported, leaving the pathogenic mechanism (haploinsufficiency vs. dominant-negative) unresolved.

**Conclusion:**
KLF7 is a biologically compelling candidate gene for an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by intellectual disability, hypotonia, feeding difficulties, and psychiatric features, supported by consistent de novo variant findings in multiple unrelated patients and robust neurological phenotypes in mouse knockout models. The primary limitation is the small patient cohort and the absence of a fully peer-reviewed clinical series with functional variant validation. Publication of the Shinde et al. dataset in a peer-reviewed venue, combined with identification of additional patients through international data-sharing initiatives, would be sufficient to elevate the evidence to STRONG and support formal disease-gene classification.

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. De novo variants in KLF7 are a potential novel cause of developmental delay/intellectual disability, neuromuscular and psychiatric symptoms (PMID: 29251763)
2. Autistic and Rett-like features associated with 2q33.3-q34 interstitial deletion (PMID: 25899208)
3. 2q33 Deletions Underlying Syndromic and Non-syndromic CTLA4 Deficiency (PMID: 39578275)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. Transcription factor KLF7 is important for neuronal morphogenesis in selected regions of the nervous system. (PMID: 15964824)
2. KLF7 orchestrates hippocampal development through neurogenesis and Draxin-mediated neuronal migration. (PMID: 40762575)
3. Krüppel-like factor 7 deficiency causes autistic-like behavior in mice via regulating Clock gene. (PMID: 36207723)
4. Brn3a and Klf7 cooperate to control TrkA expression in sensory neurons. (PMID: 17011544)
5. Identification of genes regulated by transcription factor KLF7 in differentiating olfactory sensory neurons. (PMID: 17123745)
6. Transcription factor KLF7 regulates differentiation of neuroectodermal and mesodermal cell lineages. (PMID: 20580711)
7. The KLF7/PFKL/ACADL axis modulates cardiac metabolic remodelling during cardiac hypertrophy in male mice. (PMID: 36810848)
8. KLF7 promotes adipocyte inflammation and glucose metabolism disorder by activating the PKCζ/NF-κB pathway. (PMID: 37342904)

### Biological Evidence
1. KLF7-transfected Schwann cell graft transplantation promotes sciatic nerve regeneration. (PMID: 27826105)
2. KLF7 Blocks MKNK2/HIF-1 Pathway-Mediated M1 Microglia Polarization to Ameliorate Ischemic Stroke-Induced Neurological Injury. (PMID: 40923147)
