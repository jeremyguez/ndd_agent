# PPP6R3 Evidence Report: Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene Assessment

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

PPP6R3 (Protein Phosphatase 6 Regulatory Subunit 3) encodes one of three regulatory subunits of the PP6 serine/threonine phosphatase holoenzyme, which directs substrate specificity for the catalytic subunit PPP6C. PPP6R3 participates in diverse cellular processes including mRNA translation regulation, cell death signaling, and germ cell differentiation, and is reported to be ubiquitously expressed across tissues including bone, muscle, and brain.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*) and zebrafish (*Danio rerio*).

**Key findings:**

In mouse, a conditional knockout of *Ppp6r3* demonstrated a critical role in male germ cell biology. Mutant males were infertile due to abnormal spermatogonial differentiation, with mechanistic studies revealing that PPP6R3-mediated dephosphorylation of translation initiation factors EIF3C (at S39) and EIF4G1 (at S1217) is required for proper translational activation of differentiation-associated mRNAs in KIT+ spermatogonia. Animals were viable with no reported lethality (PMID: 40721635).

In zebrafish, full knockout of *ppp6r3* produced two notable phenotypes. First, all mutant fish developed as males, indicating a role in gonadal sex determination, and these males were infertile due to a block in the spermatocyte-to-sperm transition (PMID: 40962474). Second, a separate study examining deletion of a developmentally regulated microexon within *ppp6r3* reported mild neural phenotypes in larval brain activity, identified as part of a systematic screen of 45 microexons (PMID: 41252186). Animals were viable in both zebrafish studies, with no Mendelian ratio deviations reported.

**Consistency with a human neurodevelopmental phenotype:** The evidence is weak. The predominant and reproducible phenotype across both mouse and zebrafish models is male infertility and disrupted spermatogenesis, not neurological dysfunction. The only neural signal comes from a microexon-specific zebrafish mutant showing mild larval brain activity changes (PMID: 41252186), which is a subtle finding from a secondary screen rather than a primary neurodevelopmental model. No cognitive, behavioral, or structural brain abnormalities were reported in the mouse conditional knockout.

**Strength of model organism evidence (for neurodevelopmental disease relevance): LIMITED**

The model organism data are internally consistent and methodologically credible, but they point primarily toward a reproductive/gametogenic function rather than a neurodevelopmental one. The mild neural phenotype in zebrafish microexon mutants provides only weak, indirect support for brain involvement.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

PPP6R3 functions as a regulatory subunit of the PP6 phosphatase holoenzyme, directing dephosphorylation of specific substrates. Documented molecular roles include: (1) regulation of mRNA translation initiation through dephosphorylation of EIF3C and EIF4G1 during spermatogonial differentiation (PMID: 40721635); (2) promotion of RIPK1-dependent PANoptosis by facilitating pro-death S166 auto-phosphorylation of RIPK1 and reducing pro-survival S321 phosphorylation, identified through a cell death CRISPR screen (PMID: 38807188); and (3) interaction with the influenza A virus RNA-dependent RNA polymerase subunits PB1 and PB2, implicating PP6 in viral replication (PMID: 25187537).

**Expression pattern:**

PPP6R3 is described as ubiquitously expressed across tissues, including bone, muscle, and brain (ASHG 2012 – The PPP6R3/LRP5 locus influences lean mass in children of different ethnic background). An expression study identified PPP6R3 as a stably expressed reference gene in natural killer cells and granulocytes under inflammatory conditions (PMID: 31075840), consistent with broad constitutive expression. No brain-specific or neurodevelopmentally enriched expression pattern has been specifically documented in the available evidence.

**GWAS and functional genomic associations:**

Multiple GWAS studies have implicated the PPP6R3/LRP5 locus in musculoskeletal traits. A meta-analysis of up to 18,719 individuals identified a genome-wide significant association of rs7102273 near PPP6R3 with femoral neck geometry and fracture risk, with PPP6R3 confirmed as an eQTL in bone transcriptome data (PMID: 30888730). A bivariate GWAS meta-analysis of 10,414 children identified the same locus as having pleiotropic effects on both lean mass and bone mineral density (PMID: 28743860). A TWAS/eQTL colocalization study identified PPP6R3 as the gene with the strongest support for causal involvement in BMD, with in vivo validation showing that *Ppp6r3* deletion in mice decreased BMD (PMID: 36416764). Importantly, these associations are with musculoskeletal rather than neurodevelopmental traits, and the locus is in high LD with *LRP5*, complicating causal gene attribution.

**Oncological relevance:**

A novel PPP6R3-USP6 gene fusion was identified in a single case of malignant nodular fasciitis with recurrent and metastatic behavior, where amplification of the fusion drove transcriptional upregulation of USP6 (PMID: 27113271). This is an isolated somatic finding with no direct relevance to germline neurodevelopmental disease.

**Neurodevelopmental relevance of biological evidence:** The biological evidence does not specifically support a neurodevelopmental role. The gene is broadly expressed, and its documented molecular functions relate to phosphatase-mediated substrate regulation in germ cells, immune cells, and bone. No neurodevelopmentally relevant interactors, pathways, or expression enrichment have been reported.

**Strength of biological evidence (for neurodevelopmental disease relevance): LIMITED**

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
- PPP6R3 has well-characterized molecular function as a PP6 phosphatase regulatory subunit with credible in vivo validation in two vertebrate model organisms.
- A mild neural phenotype was observed in zebrafish microexon mutants (PMID: 41252186), providing at least a preliminary biological signal in the nervous system.
- The gene is ubiquitously expressed, including in brain tissue, and its role in regulating phosphorylation of translation initiation factors is biologically plausible in a neurodevelopmental context.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- There is a complete absence of clinical evidence: no patients with PPP6R3 variants and neurodevelopmental phenotypes have been reported in any published study or cohort.
- The dominant and reproducible phenotype in model organisms is male infertility and spermatogenic failure, not neurological dysfunction; the neural phenotype in zebrafish is mild and derived from a microexon-specific allele in a secondary screen.
- The GWAS associations implicate PPP6R3 in musculoskeletal traits, not neurodevelopmental disorders, and the causal gene at the PPP6R3/LRP5 locus remains uncertain due to linkage disequilibrium.
- No heterozygous dominant-negative or haploinsufficiency mechanism has been proposed or tested.

**Conclusion:**

At present, the evidence is insufficient to support PPP6R3 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous autosomal dominant pathogenic variants. The gene has credible biological function and model organism data, but these point primarily toward reproductive and musculoskeletal roles rather than neurodevelopmental ones. Establishing PPP6R3 as a neurodevelopmental disease gene would require identification and functional characterization of pathogenic variants in affected individuals, ideally through large-scale sequencing cohorts, combined with targeted neurological phenotyping in existing or new animal models.
