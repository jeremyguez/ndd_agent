# Literature Evidence Report: SECISBP2L as a Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

SECISBP2L (SECIS-binding protein 2-like) is a paralog of SBP2 (SECISBP2) that functions as a component of the selenocysteine incorporation machinery, facilitating the translation of selenoproteins by binding SECIS (selenocysteine insertion sequence) elements in the 3′ UTR of selenoprotein mRNAs. It is proposed to act as a functional backup or cell-type-specific complement to SBP2, promoting essential selenoprotein synthesis particularly when SBP2 activity is limiting, and is notably highly expressed in differentiating oligodendrocytes in the central nervous system.

- **Predominant variant type:** Not applicable
- **Known cohort representation (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL):** None identified
- **Strength of clinical evidence: NONE**

No published clinical case reports, case series, or patient cohort studies describing individuals with pathogenic heterozygous or homozygous variants in SECISBP2L were identified in the available evidence. There is currently no direct human genetic evidence linking SECISBP2L variants to a neurodevelopmental or any other Mendelian disorder.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*) and zebrafish (*Danio rerio*)

**Key findings:**

In mouse, a conditional knockout of *Secisbp2l* restricted to the oligodendrocyte (OL) lineage resulted in decreased DIO2 (deiodinase 2) protein levels, reduced active thyroid hormone (T3) in the CNS, impaired oligodendrocyte differentiation, hypomyelination, and motor deficits in both sexes. Importantly, the differentiation defect was rescued by exogenous T3 or a T3 analog but not by T4, establishing a SECISBP2L→DIO2→T3 axis as essential for autonomous regulation of myelination (PMID: 35760530). Animals were viable, and no lethality or infertility data were reported for this conditional model.

In zebrafish, full (*secisbp2l −/−*) single knockouts were viable and retained the full complement of selenoproteins, indicating that SECISBP2L is not individually essential for global selenoprotein synthesis under normal conditions. However, double knockout of both *sbp2* and *secisbp2l* completely abolished selenoprotein synthesis and caused juvenile lethality at 14 days post fertilization, demonstrating functional redundancy between the two paralogs and an essential collective role (PMID: 35210313).

**Consistency with human neurodevelopmental phenotype:** The mouse conditional knockout data are directly relevant to neurodevelopmental disease, demonstrating that loss of SECISBP2L in OL-lineage cells causes hypomyelination and motor deficits — phenotypes consistent with leukodystrophy or hypomyelinating disorders in humans. However, these models reflect homozygous or conditional biallelic loss, not heterozygous dominant mechanisms, and no haploinsufficiency phenotype has been reported.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**

The mouse model provides compelling evidence for a CNS-specific role in myelination, but the absence of heterozygous phenotype data and the reliance on cell-type-restricted rather than germline knockouts limit direct extrapolation to autosomal dominant human disease.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Molecular function and pathways:**

SECISBP2L functions within the selenocysteine incorporation machinery as a paralog of SBP2, binding SECIS elements to facilitate Sec-tRNA^[Ser]Sec) decoding at UGA codons during selenoprotein translation (PMID: 35210313). Its most clearly defined downstream target in the CNS is DIO2, a selenoprotein deiodinase that converts T4 to the active T3 form locally within oligodendrocytes. Loss of SECISBP2L in OL-lineage cells reduces DIO2 translation, depletes local T3, and impairs OL differentiation and myelination (PMID: 35760530). This places SECISBP2L at the intersection of selenoprotein biology and thyroid hormone signaling in the CNS.

**Expression pattern:**

SECISBP2L is specifically and highly expressed in differentiating oligodendrocytes in the CNS, distinguishing it from the more broadly expressed SBP2 and suggesting a cell-type-specific role during myelin development (PMID: 35760530). In a separate study, mRNA sequencing of hippocampal tissue from LPS-treated mice identified SECISBP2L among differentially expressed genes in the context of neuroinflammation and cognitive impairment, with expression partially reversed by Pyrolae herba treatment (PMID: 37739108), providing additional, albeit indirect, evidence for hippocampal expression relevance.

**GWAS and cancer associations:**

Multiple large GWAS studies have implicated SECISBP2L at lung cancer susceptibility loci through eQTL analysis. A GWAS of 29,266 lung cancer cases and 56,450 controls identified SECISBP2L as a candidate gene at a genome-wide significant locus via eQTL analysis in normal lung tissue (PMID: 28604730), and a cross-trait GWAS meta-analysis of headache and free thyroxine (fT4) identified SECISBP2L in gene-based analysis at p < 2.09×10⁻⁶ (PMID: 36672757), consistent with its role in thyroid hormone metabolism. These associations are not directly informative for neurodevelopmental disease but support the gene's functional relevance to thyroid hormone biology and its expression in accessible tissues.

**Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE**

The mechanistic link between SECISBP2L, selenoprotein synthesis, DIO2 activity, and oligodendrocyte-autonomous T3 signaling is well-supported by experimental data. The cell-type-specific expression in differentiating OLs is particularly relevant to neurodevelopmental disease. However, no direct biochemical studies of human variant effects, protein interaction data in human neural cells, or transcriptomic evidence from patient-derived material are available.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
- The mouse conditional knockout model provides a clear mechanistic and phenotypic basis for SECISBP2L involvement in CNS myelination, demonstrating hypomyelination and motor deficits via a SECISBP2L→DIO2→T3 pathway (PMID: 35760530).
- Zebrafish double-knockout data establish that SECISBP2L is collectively essential for selenoprotein synthesis and survival, confirming its functional importance in vertebrate development (PMID: 35210313).
- Cell-type-specific expression in differentiating oligodendrocytes provides a biologically plausible mechanism for a hypomyelinating or leukodystrophy-like neurodevelopmental phenotype.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- There is a complete absence of human clinical evidence: no patients with SECISBP2L variants and a neurodevelopmental phenotype have been reported.
- All animal models employ homozygous or conditional biallelic loss; no heterozygous (haploinsufficiency) phenotype has been described in any organism, making the proposed autosomal dominant mechanism entirely unsupported by current data.
- The gene has not been identified in established neurodevelopmental disorder gene discovery cohorts (e.g., DDD, GEL, UDN), and no de novo variant data are available.

**Conclusion:**

SECISBP2L has a compelling biological rationale as a candidate neurodevelopmental gene, given its essential role in selenoprotein-mediated thyroid hormone activation within oligodendrocytes and the demonstration of hypomyelination and motor deficits in mouse conditional knockouts. However, the complete absence of human clinical evidence and the lack of any heterozygous phenotypic data in model organisms mean that the hypothesis of autosomal dominant pathogenicity remains entirely speculative at this time. Systematic variant screening in hypomyelination or leukodystrophy cohorts and functional haploinsufficiency studies are needed before SECISBP2L can be considered a candidate neurodevelopmental disorder gene with confidence.
