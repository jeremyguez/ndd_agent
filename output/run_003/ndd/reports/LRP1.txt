# LRP1 as a Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene: Literature Evidence Report

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

LRP1 (Low-Density Lipoprotein Receptor-Related Protein 1) is a large, multifunctional endocytic receptor and signaling co-receptor expressed broadly across tissues, with particularly high expression in the brain, peripheral nervous system, and skeletal progenitor cells. Its primary known functions include endocytosis and lysosomal degradation of diverse ligands, regulation of multiple signaling pathways (including Wnt, Notch, and TGF-β), modulation of extracellular matrix remodeling, and facilitation of intercellular protein spread — including pathological aggregates such as tau and α-synuclein.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent case reports / case series:** Four independent reports were identified that describe patients with heterozygous or de novo LRP1 variants.

**Total approximate number of patients:** Approximately 12 patients across all studies, though most reports are small.

**Most common phenotypes observed:** The clinical evidence for LRP1 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene specifically is limited. Two studies report patients with **autism spectrum disorder (ASD)**: one de novo loss-of-function variant in a single patient from a US cohort (ASHG 2014 – Banks et al.) and one de novo or inherited missense variant in a single patient from a Chinese trio-based whole-genome sequencing study (PMID: 39126614). One additional patient harbored a missense variant in the context of **refractory epilepsy**, identified by trio-based whole-exome sequencing, with functional validation in a Drosophila oligogenic model (PMID: 40374006). The largest clinical series (9 patients) concerns **developmental dysplasia of the hip (DDH)** — a skeletal, not neurodevelopmental, phenotype — with both missense and loss-of-function variants identified by whole-exome sequencing (PMID: 36067312). Taken together, the neurodevelopmental phenotypes (ASD, epilepsy) are represented by only 3 patients across 3 independent reports.

**Predominant variant type:** Both loss-of-function and missense variants are represented. The ASD-associated variant from the US cohort was classified as a severe de novo LoF (ASHG 2014 – Banks et al.); the Chinese ASD case involved a missense variant (PMID: 39126614); and the epilepsy case involved a missense variant (PMID: 40374006).

**Cohort origin:** None of the neurodevelopmental cases originate from established large-scale diagnostic cohorts such as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL. The ASD cases derive from a local Ohio registry (CORA, USA) and a Chinese trio cohort, respectively.

**Strength of clinical evidence for neurodevelopmental disorder: LIMITED**
The total number of patients with neurodevelopmental phenotypes is very small (n=3), variant-level details are largely absent from the available abstracts, and no case has been reported with full clinical characterization in a peer-reviewed primary case report. The epilepsy case is further complicated by an oligogenic model involving two additional genes (UBR4 and OPHN1), making it difficult to attribute causality to LRP1 alone.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (multiple knockout strategies) and zebrafish (morpholino knockdown).

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

- **Heterozygous knockout and knock-in mice** recapitulate developmental dysplasia of the hip, with premature triradiate cartilage development, acetabular and femoral head malformation, impaired chondrogenic differentiation, decreased autophagy, and β-catenin upregulation (PMID: 36067312). Homozygous knock-in mice showed more severe skeletal phenotypes.
- **Neuronal conditional knockout mice** demonstrated significantly reduced spread of α-synuclein in the hippocampus compared to wild-type littermates (PMID: 36056345), and knockdown models showed reduced tau propagation between neurons in vivo (PMID: 32296178). These findings are relevant to neurodegeneration but do not directly model neurodevelopmental phenotypes.
- **Schwann cell-specific LRP1 deletion** caused mechanical allodynia, accelerated nerve degeneration after crush injury, impaired remyelination, and central sensitization with p38MAPK and microglial activation in the spinal cord — demonstrating a clear role in peripheral nervous system integrity (PMID: 23536074).
- **Astrocytic knockdown** worsened ischemia-reperfusion injury and reduced mitochondria transfer to damaged neurons (PMID: 38906140).
- **Zebrafish knockdown** produced developmental defects comparable to those seen with mutations in known Noonan syndrome genes (ASHG 2012 – Yntema et al.), suggesting a broader developmental role, though this finding is from a conference abstract and lacks full peer-reviewed detail.
- Conditional knockout models in vascular smooth muscle and skin fibroblasts demonstrated roles in aortic integrity and inflammatory signaling, respectively (PMID: 35143327; PMID: 40177548), which are not directly relevant to neurodevelopment.

**Lethality / Mendelian ratio deviations:** Heterozygous and conditional knockout mice are reported as viable. No Mendelian ratio deviations or embryonic lethality were reported in the heterozygous state, which is consistent with an autosomal dominant mechanism with incomplete penetrance or variable expressivity.

**Consistency with human neurodevelopmental phenotype:** The mouse models most relevant to neurodevelopment demonstrate roles in neuronal protein uptake and spread, peripheral nerve myelination, and astrocyte-neuron communication. However, no mouse model has been reported to display ASD-like behavioral phenotypes or epilepsy as a primary outcome of Lrp1 loss. The skeletal (DDH) phenotype in heterozygous mice is well-validated and directly consistent with the human skeletal clinical series.

**Strength of model organism evidence for neurodevelopmental disorder: LIMITED**
While the nervous system expression and function of LRP1 are well-supported in mouse models, no model organism study has directly recapitulated a neurodevelopmental behavioral or seizure phenotype attributable to heterozygous Lrp1 loss. The zebrafish data (ASHG 2012 – Yntema et al.) are intriguing but remain unpublished in full peer-reviewed form.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
LRP1 participates in multiple signaling and trafficking pathways with neurodevelopmental relevance:
- **Wnt signaling:** LRP1 directly binds Wnt5a, facilitates its endocytic degradation and recycling, and regulates non-canonical Wnt/PCP components Vangl2 in developing limbs; skeletal progenitor-specific deficiency causes joint fusion and bone malformation resembling Wnt pathway phenotypes (PMID: 39865089). Given the established role of Wnt signaling in brain development, this pathway connection is biologically plausible for neurodevelopmental phenotypes.
- **Notch signaling:** LRP1 mediates ubiquitination chain linkage switching of Delta ligands, affecting ligand recycling and Notch pathway activation; LRP1 inhibition suppressed Notch signaling and tumorigenesis in leukemia models (PMID: 34626540). Notch signaling is critical for neural progenitor maintenance and cortical development.
- **TGF-β/SMAD signaling:** LRP1 knockout in iPSC-derived smooth muscle cells enhanced TGF-β/SMAD2/3 signaling and remodeled the extracellular matrix (PMID: 39355906).
- **Endocytosis and protein clearance:** LRP1 mediates neuronal uptake and trans-neuronal spread of tau (PMID: 32296178) and α-synuclein (PMID: 36056345), with direct binding demonstrated by co-immunoprecipitation and surface plasmon resonance.
- **Intracellular trafficking:** LRP1 regulates ABCA1 trafficking to the plasma membrane via modulation of lysosomal enzymes Cathepsin D and Prosaposin, with missense variants in LRP1 associated with extremely low HDL-c levels (ASHG 2016).

**Expression pattern:**
LRP1 is expressed in neurons, astrocytes, Schwann cells, and skeletal progenitor cells. Schwann cell expression is essential for normal axon myelination and Remak bundle integrity (PMID: 23536074). Astrocytic LRP1 suppresses glycolysis and promotes mitochondria transfer to neurons (PMID: 38906140). Neuronal LRP1 mediates uptake of extracellular aggregation-prone proteins. The gene is thus broadly expressed throughout the nervous system at multiple developmental and adult stages.

**GWAS and population-level evidence:**
The LRP1 locus (rs11172113, 12q13.3) has been robustly associated with migraine susceptibility in a large-scale GWAS meta-analysis of ~20,000 cases and ~90,000 controls (p=3.94×10⁻¹⁹, strengthened to p=1.41×10⁻²⁶ by conditional analysis) (ASHG 2011). Multitrait colocalization analyses further implicate the same variant in fibromuscular dysplasia, pulse pressure, and spontaneous coronary artery dissection, with the T allele associated with higher LRP1 expression (PMID: 39355906). These associations are with common variants and complex traits, not Mendelian neurodevelopmental disease, but they support the biological plausibility of LRP1 haploinsufficiency affecting neurological and vascular phenotypes.

**Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE**
LRP1 has well-characterized roles in multiple pathways (Wnt, Notch, TGF-β, endocytosis) that are directly relevant to neurodevelopment, and it is expressed throughout the nervous system. However, the direct mechanistic link between heterozygous LRP1 loss-of-function and a specific neurodevelopmental disorder phenotype (ASD or epilepsy) has not been experimentally established in cell or animal models.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: LIMITED**

**Key strengths:**
- LRP1 is biologically plausible as a neurodevelopmental gene, given its expression in neurons, astrocytes, and Schwann cells, and its participation in Wnt and Notch signaling pathways critical for brain development.
- De novo variants (both LoF and missense) have been identified in ASD patients in two independent studies from different populations (ASHG 2014 – Banks et al.; PMID: 39126614), providing some cross-ethnic support.
- The heterozygous DDH phenotype is well-validated by both human genetics and mouse models (PMID: 36067312), demonstrating that heterozygous LRP1 variants are pathogenic in a developmental context, which supports the plausibility of haploinsufficiency as a mechanism.
- Functional validation in a Drosophila model supports a role for LRP1 in epilepsy susceptibility within an oligogenic framework (PMID: 40374006).

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- The total number of patients with neurodevelopmental phenotypes (ASD or epilepsy) attributable to LRP1 is very small (n=3), with no detailed clinical descriptions, no recurrent variants, and no variant-level functional validation for the neurodevelopmental cases specifically.
- No mouse or other model organism has been shown to exhibit ASD-like behavior or spontaneous seizures as a result of heterozygous Lrp1 loss.
- The epilepsy case involves an oligogenic model, making it impossible to assign causality to LRP1 alone.
- None of the neurodevelopmental cases originate from large, well-characterized diagnostic cohorts, limiting confidence in phenotypic ascertainment and variant interpretation.

**Conclusion:**
Current evidence is insufficient to classify LRP1 as a confirmed autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene. While the gene's biological functions, nervous system expression, and involvement in key developmental signaling pathways make it a credible candidate, the clinical evidence for neurodevelopmental phenotypes rests on only three patients across three small or conference-level reports, without recurrent variants or robust functional validation in relevant model systems. Accumulation of additional cases with detailed phenotyping, identification of recurrent de novo variants, and targeted neurodevelopmental behavioral studies in heterozygous mouse models would be required to elevate the evidence classification.
