# MIDEAS – Literature Evidence Report for Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene Assessment

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

MIDEAS (Mediator of DNA Damage Checkpoint Protein 1-like, also known as ELMSAN1) is a scaffold protein and core subunit of the Mitotic Deacetylase Complex (MiDAC), a cell cycle-regulated histone deacetylase complex containing HDAC1/2 and DNTTIP1. Its primary known function is to mediate assembly of the MiDAC complex, which regulates gene expression through histone deacetylation—particularly at promoters and enhancers of genes governing neurodevelopment, chromosome alignment during mitosis, and embryonic development.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent case reports / case series:** One published report (PMID: 41290615).

**Total approximate number of patients:** 2 unrelated individuals, both carrying the identical de novo heterozygous missense variant.

**Most common phenotypes observed:** Both individuals presented with a multisystem neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by delayed speech development, joint contractures, dysmorphic features, gut dysmotility, and a broader multisystem involvement (PMID: 41290615).

**Predominant variant type:** Missense (p.Tyr654Ser); no loss-of-function variants have been reported in affected individuals. The mechanism is proposed to be gain-of-function (increased deacetylase activity) rather than haploinsufficiency, based on functional studies demonstrating displacement of a conserved auto-inhibitory loop covering the HDAC active site and reciprocal gene expression changes in patient fibroblasts compared to MiDAC-depleted cell lines (PMID: 41290615).

**Known cohort affiliation:** The cohort affiliation of the two patients is not specified in the available data; no explicit linkage to UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL is reported.

**Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED**
The identification of the same de novo missense variant in two unrelated individuals with overlapping phenotypes, supported by robust functional evidence, is compelling. However, the total patient number is very small (n=2), there is only a single published report, and no additional independent replication in larger cohorts has been documented to date. The evidence is promising but remains limited by sample size.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*) — full knockout model (PMID: 32591534).

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:** Homozygous loss of MIDEAS in mice results in embryonic lethality during late embryogenesis, with heart malformation and haematopoietic failure as the primary structural defects. The knockout phenotype is identical to that of DNTTIP1 knockout mice, consistent with both proteins functioning as obligate components of the same MiDAC complex. Perturbations in gene expression were documented as the underlying molecular mechanism (PMID: 32591534).

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The mouse knockout data are not directly comparable to the human gain-of-function mechanism proposed for the p.Tyr654Ser variant, as complete loss of MIDEAS causes embryonic lethality rather than a viable neurodevelopmental syndrome. Nevertheless, the mouse data confirm that MIDEAS is essential for normal embryonic development and gene regulation, and the cardiac and systemic involvement in knockout mice is broadly consistent with the multisystem nature of the human disorder. The neurodevelopmental relevance is further supported by cell-based studies (see Section 4) rather than the knockout model per se.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**
The mouse knockout establishes biological essentiality and developmental relevance of MIDEAS, but the embryonic lethal phenotype of the full knockout does not model the heterozygous gain-of-function human condition. No heterozygous or knock-in mouse models recapitulating the human variant have been reported.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:** MIDEAS is the central scaffold of the MiDAC histone deacetylase complex, which also contains HDAC1/2 and DNTTIP1. The complex is upregulated during mitosis and is associated with cyclin A (PMID: 25653165). CryoEM structural studies reveal that MiDAC has a unique multivalent architecture with four copies of HDAC1 positioned at the periphery with outward-facing active sites, suggesting a processive deacetylase function potentially targeting multiple nucleosomes simultaneously (PMID: 32591534). MIDEAS interacts with HDAC1 via its ELM2/SANT domain, with specific residues on the HDAC1 surface (including Y48 and E63) critical for MiDAC assembly (PMID: 40966515). The pathogenic variant p.Tyr654Ser is located in a conserved auto-inhibitory loop that normally covers the deacetylase active site; its displacement is proposed to cause elevated deacetylase activity, establishing a gain-of-function mechanism (PMID: 41290615).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:** MiDAC functions as a modulator of a neurodevelopmental gene expression program. Loss of MiDAC in neuronal cell models results in defective neurite outgrowth, mediated through dysregulation of pro-neural guidance cues including SLIT3 and NETRIN1 (via H4K20ac removal at their promoters and enhancers) and suppression of negative regulators of neurogenesis (via H3K27ac reduction). These neurite outgrowth defects can be rescued by supplementation with SLIT3 and/or NTN1 (PMID: 32297854). This establishes a direct mechanistic link between MiDAC activity and neurodevelopmental gene regulation, providing a plausible biological basis for the delayed speech and neurodevelopmental features observed in patients.

**Additional experimental findings:** MIDEAS has been identified as a cell cycle-regulated host transcription factor that recognizes the HIV-1 core promoter TASHET sequence, further supporting its role as a sequence-selective chromatin regulator (PMID: 38781120). Differential DNA methylation of MIDEAS has been reported in peripheral blood leukocytes of Alzheimer's disease patients, though this finding is associative and of uncertain functional significance in the context of neurodevelopmental disease (PMID: 40343304).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
There is a well-characterized molecular function for MIDEAS as a scaffold of the MiDAC complex, supported by high-resolution structural data, protein interaction studies, and functional cell-based experiments. The neurodevelopmental gene expression program regulated by MiDAC is directly relevant to the human phenotype, and the structural basis for the pathogenic variant's gain-of-function effect has been elucidated by cryoEM (PMID: 41290615).

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths:**
- Identification of the identical de novo heterozygous missense variant (p.Tyr654Ser) in two unrelated individuals with overlapping multisystem neurodevelopmental phenotypes provides strong genetic evidence for causality given the rarity of the variant and the de novo occurrence (PMID: 41290615).
- Robust functional evidence supports a gain-of-function mechanism: cryoEM structural data localizes the variant to a conserved auto-inhibitory loop, biochemical assays demonstrate elevated deacetylase activity, and patient fibroblast transcriptomics show reciprocal gene expression changes relative to MiDAC-depleted controls (PMID: 41290615).
- Strong biological plausibility is established by independent studies demonstrating MiDAC's essential role in neurodevelopmental gene regulation, including control of SLIT3 and NETRIN1 expression and neurite outgrowth (PMID: 32297854), and by mouse knockout data confirming developmental essentiality (PMID: 32591534).

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- The clinical evidence base is very small (n=2 patients, single publication), and independent replication in additional unrelated individuals or larger cohorts is lacking.
- No heterozygous or variant-specific knock-in animal model has been reported to directly model the human gain-of-function mechanism in vivo.
- The cohort source of the two patients is unspecified, and the full phenotypic spectrum of MIDEAS-related disorder remains poorly defined given the limited case number.

**Conclusion:** MIDEAS meets criteria for a candidate dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene, supported by recurrent de novo missense variants in two unrelated individuals, a well-characterized gain-of-function molecular mechanism, and strong biological evidence for a role in neurodevelopmental gene regulation. However, the overall evidence remains moderate rather than strong due to the very limited number of reported patients and the absence of independent replication. Prioritization for further case-finding through international matchmaking platforms and functional validation in animal models is warranted to elevate the evidence classification.

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. A de novo missense variant in MIDEAS results in increased deacetylase activity of the MiDAC HDAC complex causing a neurodevelopmental syndrome (PMID: 41290615)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. The MiDAC histone deacetylase complex is essential for embryonic development and has a unique multivalent structure. (PMID: 32591534)

### Biological Evidence
1. Structural and functional characterization of a cell cycle associated HDAC1/2 complex reveals the structural basis for complex assembly and nucleosome targeting. (PMID: 25653165)
2. Mutations on the surface of HDAC1 reveal molecular determinants of specific complex assembly and their requirement for gene regulation. (PMID: 40966515)
3. The histone deacetylase complex MiDAC regulates a neurodevelopmental gene expression program to control neurite outgrowth. (PMID: 32297854)
4. Mitotic deacetylase complex (MiDAC) recognizes the HIV-1 core promoter to control activated viral gene expression. (PMID: 38781120)
5. DNA methylation in peripheral blood leukocytes in late onset Alzheimer's disease. (PMID: 40343304)
