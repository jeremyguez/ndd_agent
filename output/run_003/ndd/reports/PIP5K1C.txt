# PIP5K1C – Literature Evidence Report for Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene Assessment

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

PIP5K1C (Phosphatidylinositol-4-Phosphate 5-Kinase Type I Gamma) encodes a lipid kinase responsible for the synthesis of phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate [PI(4,5)P2] from phosphatidylinositol 4-phosphate [PI(4)P], a critical second messenger regulating membrane trafficking, endocytosis, actin cytoskeleton dynamics, and calcium signaling. It is broadly expressed and plays essential roles in neuronal endocytosis, synaptic vesicle recycling, and intracellular signal transduction.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent case reports/series:** One published case series has been identified in the literature.

**Total number of patients:** Nine unrelated children were reported across this single study (PMID: 37451268).

**Most common phenotypes observed:** The core clinical phenotype described in all nine patients includes intellectual disability, global developmental delay, acquired microcephaly, seizures, visual abnormalities, and dysmorphic features. This constellation is consistent with a syndromic neurodevelopmental disorder.

**Predominant variant type:** All identified pathogenic variants are de novo heterozygous missense variants. Three distinct missense variants in PIP5K1C were identified across the nine patients. No loss-of-function (nonsense, frameshift, or splice-site) variants were reported in this cohort, suggesting a gain-of-function or dominant-negative mechanism rather than haploinsufficiency.

**Known cohort affiliation:** The study does not specify enrollment through named cohorts such as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL. Patient origins and recruiting centers are not detailed in the available abstract data.

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**
The identification of nine unrelated patients with de novo heterozygous missense variants in PIP5K1C and a consistent, recognizable neurodevelopmental phenotype provides meaningful clinical evidence. However, the evidence is currently limited to a single publication, and the absence of independent replication in a second cohort, combined with the lack of specific variant-level annotations (nucleotide/amino acid changes) in the available data, prevents an upgrade to STRONG at this time.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Both zebrafish (*Danio rerio*) and mouse (*Mus musculus*) models have been employed across multiple independent studies.

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

- **Zebrafish (knockdown):** Morpholino-based knockdown of PIP5K1C in zebrafish recapitulated key features of the human neurodevelopmental syndrome, including developmental defects affecting the forebrain, eye abnormalities, and craniofacial abnormalities (PMID: 37451268). This direct phenotypic concordance with the human disorder is a meaningful point of support.

- **Mouse – Full knockout:** Homozygous deletion of Pip5k1c in mice results in perinatal lethality, accompanied by a neuron-specific endocytosis defect and reduced agonist-induced calcium signaling (PMID: 22976293). This demonstrates that PIP5K1C is essential for neuronal function and survival, and that complete loss is not compatible with postnatal life.

- **Mouse – Heterozygous knockout:** Heterozygous Pip5k1c knockout mice are viable and fertile, with at least a 50% reduction in PI(4,5)P2 levels in dorsal root ganglion (DRG) neurons, reduced pronociceptive receptor signaling, and reduced thermal and mechanical hypersensitivity in chronic pain models (PMID: 24853942). While this model focuses on pain signaling rather than neurodevelopment, it confirms that heterozygous reduction of PIP5K1C is sufficient to produce measurable neuronal phenotypes.

- **Mouse – Conditional knockouts:** Sensory ganglion-specific conditional deletion produced proprioceptive deficits and altered inflammatory pain sensitization (PMID: 29020859). Adipocyte-specific deletion reduced diet-induced obesity and insulin resistance (PMID: 34996482), and osteocyte-specific deletion caused low bone mass (PMID: 38495744), demonstrating broad tissue-specific roles but with limited direct neurodevelopmental relevance beyond the sensory neuron studies.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The zebrafish knockdown model directly recapitulates the forebrain and eye abnormalities seen in human patients (PMID: 37451268). The mouse full knockout confirms neuronal endocytosis dysfunction as a core pathomechanism. The heterozygous mouse model demonstrates neuronal sensitivity to PIP5K1C dosage reduction, which is relevant to the autosomal dominant human disease context.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**
Multiple independent model organism studies across two species confirm neurological roles for PIP5K1C, and the zebrafish model directly mirrors the human phenotype. However, no mouse model specifically recapitulating the human gain-of-function/dominant-negative missense variants has been reported, and the heterozygous mouse knockout models focus primarily on pain rather than neurodevelopment.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:** PIP5K1C is the primary enzyme responsible for generating PI(4,5)P2 at cellular membranes. PI(4,5)P2 is a master regulator of clathrin-mediated endocytosis, actin polymerization, focal adhesion assembly, and calcium signaling via IP3 generation. The protein exists in multiple splice isoforms with distinct subcellular localizations and binding partners, including talin, FAK, and integrins (PMID: 34541033).

**Pathomechanism of disease-associated variants:** The de novo missense variants identified in patients cause increased endosomal PI(4,5)P2 production, leading to ectopic recruitment of filamentous actin at early endosomes and dysfunction of early endosome trafficking (PMID: 37451268). This represents a gain-of-function or dominant-negative mechanism distinct from simple haploinsufficiency, consistent with the missense variant spectrum and the viability of heterozygous knockout mice.

**Neurodevelopmental relevance of expression and function:**
- EZH2 directly suppresses PIP5K1C transcription in proliferating human mesenchymal stem cells; relief of this suppression increases PI(4,5)P2 and intracellular Ca²⁺, promoting neuronal differentiation. Conversely, PIP5K1C knockdown disrupts neuronal differentiation both in vitro and in vivo (PMID: 21216957). This establishes a direct role for PIP5K1C in neuronal fate determination.
- The full mouse knockout demonstrates a neuron-specific endocytosis defect and impaired calcium signaling (PMID: 22976293), further supporting neuronal dependency on PIP5K1C activity.

**Additional biological roles (non-neurodevelopmental context):** PIP5K1C has been implicated in cancer biology, including lysosome homeostasis and autophagy in PIKFYVE inhibitor sensitivity (PMID: 36803256), tumor suppression in triple-negative breast cancer via the PI3K/AKT axis (PMID: 37803350), and melanoma-specific survival through a GWAS-identified SNP (PMID: 33163277). While these findings confirm broad biological importance, they are not directly relevant to the neurodevelopmental disease assessment.

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
The molecular mechanism linking PIP5K1C variants to endosomal trafficking dysfunction is well-characterized and experimentally validated. The role of PIP5K1C in neuronal differentiation, endocytosis, and calcium signaling is supported by multiple independent experimental systems. The specific pathomechanism (ectopic PI(4,5)P2 at early endosomes causing actin misrecruitment and trafficking defects) provides a coherent and biologically plausible explanation for the human neurodevelopmental phenotype.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths of the evidence:**
- Nine unrelated patients with de novo heterozygous missense variants and a consistent, recognizable neurodevelopmental syndrome provide meaningful clinical signal (PMID: 37451268).
- The zebrafish knockdown model directly recapitulates the human forebrain, eye, and craniofacial phenotypes, providing strong cross-species phenotypic concordance.
- The molecular mechanism is well-defined: disease variants cause gain-of-function PI(4,5)P2 dysregulation at early endosomes, disrupting endosomal trafficking—a pathway with established neurological importance.
- Multiple independent lines of evidence (mouse knockouts, cell culture, stem cell differentiation studies) confirm that PIP5K1C is essential for neuronal function and differentiation.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- All clinical evidence derives from a single publication; independent replication in a second cohort is lacking.
- Specific variant-level data (nucleotide and amino acid changes) were not available in the extracted data, limiting variant-level pathogenicity assessment.
- No mouse model carrying the specific human disease-associated missense variants has been reported, leaving the precise dominant mechanism (gain-of-function vs. dominant-negative) incompletely resolved in a mammalian system.
- The heterozygous mouse knockout, while viable and neurologically informative, does not model the neurodevelopmental syndrome directly.

**Conclusion:** PIP5K1C represents an emerging autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene supported by a coherent body of clinical, model organism, and biological evidence. The identification of nine unrelated patients with de novo missense variants and a consistent syndromic phenotype, combined with a validated molecular mechanism and cross-species phenotypic recapitulation, provides a moderate level of overall confidence. Independent replication of the clinical findings and generation of knock-in mouse models carrying patient-specific variants would substantially strengthen the evidence base and support definitive disease gene classification.

### Clinical Evidence
1. De novo missense variants in phosphatidylinositol kinase PIP5KIγ underlie a neurodevelopmental syndrome associated with altered phosphoinositide signaling (PMID: 37451268)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. Comparative phenotypic analysis of the two major splice isoforms of phosphatidylinositol phosphate kinase type Iγ in vivo (PMID: 22976293)
2. The lipid kinase PIP5K1C regulates pain signaling and sensitization (PMID: 24853942)
3. Conditional deletion of Pip5k1c in sensory ganglia and effects on nociception and inflammatory sensitization (PMID: 29020859)
4. Adipocyte-specific deletion of PIP5K1c reduces diet-induced obesity and insulin resistance by increasing energy expenditure (PMID: 34996482)

### Biological Evidence
1. Pip5k1c expression in osteocytes regulates bone remodeling in mice. (PMID: 38495744)
2. Loss of CDCP1 triggers FAK activation in detached prostate cancer cells. (PMID: 34541033)
3. EZH2 regulates neuronal differentiation of mesenchymal stem cells through PIP5K1C-dependent calcium signaling. (PMID: 21216957)
4. PIP5K1C phosphoinositide kinase deficiency distinguishes PIKFYVE-dependent cancer cells from non-malignant cells. (PMID: 36803256)
5. MiR-4649-5p acts as a tumor-suppressive microRNA in triple negative breast cancer by direct interaction with PIP5K1C, thereby potentiating growth-inhibitory effects of the AKT inhibitor capivasertib. (PMID: 37803350)
6. Novel genetic variants of PIP5K1C and MVB12B of the endosome-related pathway predict cutaneous melanoma-specific survival. (PMID: 33163277)
