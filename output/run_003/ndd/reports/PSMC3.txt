# PSMC3 Literature Evidence Report: Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene Assessment

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

PSMC3 (Proteasome 26S Subunit, ATPase 3; also known as Rpt5) encodes an AAA-ATPase subunit of the 19S regulatory particle of the 26S proteasome, which is essential for ATP-dependent unfolding and translocation of ubiquitinated substrates into the 20S catalytic core for degradation. As a core component of the ubiquitin-proteasome system, PSMC3 plays a fundamental role in protein homeostasis, and its dysfunction leads to proteotoxic stress with broad cellular consequences, particularly in post-mitotic neurons.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent case reports/series:** Three independent primary clinical reports were identified, with two additional supporting references that cite PSMC3 as an established disease gene.

**Total approximate number of patients:** At least 23 patients are documented across the primary literature. The largest single study (PMID: 37256937) reported 23 unrelated heterozygous individuals with de novo missense variants. A second study (PMID: 32500975) described patients with a recessive deep intronic variant, representing a distinct inheritance mode and phenotypic spectrum. Two additional reports from a Bangladeshi neurodevelopmental disorder cohort (PMID: 36959829; ASHG 2022 – Akter et al.) identified PSMC3 as a candidate gene within pathogenic copy number variants, though precise patient numbers were not extractable from the available abstracts.

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Neurodevelopmental delay and intellectual disability (dominant inheritance; PMID: 37256937)
- Dysregulation of type I interferon signaling (PMID: 37256937)
- Severe sensorineural deafness and early-onset cataracts (recessive inheritance; PMID: 32500975)
- Neurological, sensorial, and cutaneous involvement (PMID: 32500975)
- Autism spectrum disorder and broader neurodevelopmental disorder (PMID: 36959829)

**Predominant variant type:** For the autosomal dominant (heterozygous) disease mechanism, the predominant variant type is de novo missense variants; 15 distinct de novo missense variants were identified across 23 unrelated patients (PMID: 37256937). The recessive phenotype is caused by a deep intronic variant leading to cryptic exon inclusion (PMID: 32500975), representing a distinct allelic series. No truncating loss-of-function variants have been reported as pathogenic in heterozygous state, consistent with the mouse full-knockout being embryonic lethal and suggesting that complete haploinsufficiency may not be the dominant mechanism.

**Known cohort affiliation:** No patients were explicitly identified as belonging to named research cohorts such as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL based on the available evidence. The Bangladeshi cohort studies (PMID: 36959829; ASHG 2022) represent an independent population-based NDD cohort.

**Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG**
The identification of 15 distinct de novo missense variants in 23 unrelated individuals (PMID: 37256937), combined with a consistent neurodevelopmental phenotype, autosomal dominant inheritance, and corroborating functional data, constitutes strong clinical evidence. The gene is further cited as an established primary proteasomopathy gene in subsequent literature (PMID: 38866022).

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*) and zebrafish (*Danio rerio*).

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**
- **Mouse (full knockout):** Homozygous Psmc3 knockout mice die before implantation, with defective blastocyst development, demonstrating early embryonic lethality (PMID: 10945464). This finding confirms the essential, non-redundant role of PSMC3 in mammalian development and is consistent with the observation that no homozygous loss-of-function variants have been reported in humans.
- **Zebrafish (full knockout):** Psmc3-null zebrafish recapitulate the human neurosensory syndrome, exhibiting inner ear development anomalies and cataracts (PMID: 32500975). This directly mirrors the deafness and cataract phenotype observed in patients with the recessive deep intronic variant.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The zebrafish model shows high phenotypic concordance with the recessive human syndrome (deafness, cataracts, CNS involvement). The mouse embryonic lethality is consistent with the biological indispensability of PSMC3 and explains the absence of homozygous null individuals in the human population. The dominant neurodevelopmental phenotype has been partially modeled in mouse neuronal cultures, where expression of patient-derived PSMC3 variants caused altered dendrite development (PMID: 37256937), though a dedicated heterozygous knock-in mouse model was not described in the available evidence.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**
The zebrafish knockout provides direct phenotypic validation of the recessive syndrome, and the mouse knockout confirms essential developmental function. However, a dedicated heterozygous knock-in model recapitulating the dominant neurodevelopmental phenotype has not been reported, which represents a gap in the model organism evidence for the AD disease mechanism specifically.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
PSMC3 is a core AAA-ATPase subunit (Rpt5) of the 19S proteasome regulatory particle. Its primary function is ATP-dependent substrate unfolding and translocation into the 20S catalytic core. Beyond canonical proteasomal degradation, PSMC3 has been shown to stabilize AGO2 by facilitating its interaction with the deubiquitylase USP14, thereby promoting RNAi activity (PMID: 36528617). PSMC3 also participates in substrate recognition for proteasomal degradation of specific targets such as FRA1 (PMID: 38011562) and, in a non-canonical role, can stabilize substrates such as the Hepatitis B virus HBx protein in a ubiquitin-independent manner (PMID: 35695579).

**Pathomechanism in neurodevelopmental disease:** Pathogenic PSMC3 variants cause impaired 26S proteasome assembly, leading to proteotoxic stress, accumulation of ubiquitinated proteins, and constitutive activation of the TCF11/Nrf1 proteasome recovery pathway (PMID: 32500975). In patient-derived T cells, proteomic and transcriptomic analyses revealed dysregulated type I interferon signaling mediated by PKR (PMID: 37256937), a mechanism shared with other primary proteasomopathy genes including PSMB1, PSMC1, and PSMD12 (PMID: 38866022). Expression of patient variants in mouse neuronal cultures caused altered dendrite morphology, directly linking proteasome dysfunction to neuronal structural abnormalities (PMID: 37256937).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:** PSMC3 expression has been linked to frontal cortex thickness, volume, and cerebrospinal fluid volume changes in neuroimaging genetics analyses, implicating it in cortical structure relevant to neurodegenerative disease (PMID: 41732108). siRNA-mediated knockdown of Psmc3 in murine microglial cells significantly altered cellular phenotypes and reversed Alzheimer's disease-associated proteomic patterns (PMID: 41388808), further supporting brain-relevant expression and function. PSMC3 mRNA is also overexpressed in breast cancer tissues relative to normal tissues (PMID: 34329194), indicating broad tissue expression with particular functional relevance in proliferating and post-mitotic cells.

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent experimental systems — patient-derived T cells, fibroblasts, mouse neuronal cultures, zebrafish, and microglial cell lines — converge on a consistent mechanistic model of proteasome dysfunction, proteotoxic stress, and type I interferon dysregulation. The molecular pathway is well-characterized and shared with other established proteasomopathy genes.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths:**
1. A large, well-powered primary study (PMID: 37256937) identified 15 distinct de novo missense variants in 23 unrelated patients with a consistent autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental phenotype, supported by structural modeling, proteomics, and transcriptomics.
2. A second independent study (PMID: 32500975) established a recessive allelic series with a distinct but mechanistically related phenotype (neurosensory syndrome), validated by zebrafish knockout.
3. The pathomechanism — proteasome dysfunction, proteotoxic stress, and type I interferon dysregulation via PKR — is biologically coherent, experimentally validated in multiple systems, and shared with other established primary proteasomopathy genes (PMID: 38866022).

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. A dedicated heterozygous knock-in mouse model recapitulating the dominant neurodevelopmental phenotype has not been reported, leaving the in vivo dominant mechanism incompletely modeled.
2. Specific variant-level HGVS notations were not available in the extracted abstracts, limiting precise genotype-phenotype correlation analysis.
3. No patients were identified from large, well-characterized research cohorts (e.g., DDD, GEL, UDN), and the total patient number, while substantial for a rare disease gene, remains relatively modest.

**Conclusion:** PSMC3 is a well-supported neurodevelopmental disorder gene with strong clinical, biological, and model organism evidence. De novo heterozygous missense variants cause an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental syndrome characterized by intellectual disability and type I interferon dysregulation, while biallelic variants cause a distinct neurosensory syndrome with deafness and cataracts. The gene should be considered a definitive disease gene for autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental proteasomopathy, with the caveat that further delineation of the full phenotypic spectrum and genotype-phenotype correlations would benefit from larger cohort studies and dedicated knock-in animal models.
