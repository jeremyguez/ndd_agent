# ZMYND8 Literature Evidence Report: Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene Assessment

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

ZMYND8 (Zinc Finger MYND-Type Containing 8; also known as RACK7) encodes a multidomain chromatin reader protein containing PHD, bromodomain (BRD), and PWWP domains that recognizes modified histone marks (including H3K4me1, H3K14ac, and H3K36me2) to regulate transcriptional activation and repression at enhancers and promoters. It functions as a key epigenetic regulator involved in DNA damage response, transcriptional elongation, super-enhancer decommissioning, and developmental gene regulation across multiple tissue types including the nervous system.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports:** Two independent publications were identified reporting ZMYND8 variants in patients with neurodevelopmental phenotypes.

**Total patients:** Approximately 12 patients in total — 11 patients from a dedicated case series (PMID: 35916866) and 1 additional patient from a broader neurodevelopmental cohort study (PMID: 32530565).

**Most common phenotypes observed:** The core phenotypic spectrum, as defined in the primary case series (PMID: 35916866), includes intellectual disability/neurodevelopmental delay, cardiovascular malformations, ophthalmologic anomalies, and minor skeletal anomalies. The single patient reported in the Japanese cohort study (PMID: 32530565) presented with a neurodevelopmental disorder, consistent with this spectrum.

**Predominant variant type:** Both loss-of-function (LoF) truncating variants and missense variants have been reported. In the primary series of 11 patients (PMID: 35916866), 2 carried truncating (LoF) variants and 9 carried missense variants, including one recurrent missense variant. Missense variants were specifically localized to functionally critical domains: the PWWP domain (abolishing interaction with Drebrin) and the MYND domain (disrupting interaction with GATAD2A), providing strong functional support for pathogenicity. The single patient in PMID: 32530565 carried a de novo truncating variant.

**De novo status:** Of the 11 patients in the primary series, 10 variants were confirmed de novo and 1 was suspected de novo (PMID: 35916866). The variant in PMID: 32530565 was also de novo. The consistent de novo occurrence across all reported cases is a critical indicator of pathogenicity for an autosomal dominant disorder.

**Cohort origin:** Patients were ascertained through an international multi-center collaboration using exome sequencing (PMID: 35916866); no patients were explicitly identified from named cohorts such as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL, though the international nature of the collaboration may overlap with some of these programs. The second report originated from a Japanese cohort (PMID: 32530565).

**Supporting functional evidence:** The primary study (PMID: 35916866) provided substantial functional validation including molecular modeling, yeast two-hybrid protein interaction assays, transcriptomic analysis, and a Drosophila knockdown model, substantially strengthening the causal interpretation of the identified variants.

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**
*(Rationale: 12 patients across 2 publications, all with de novo variants, consistent phenotype, and functional validation — sufficient for moderate confidence, but the total patient number remains relatively small and detailed variant-level data were not fully extractable from available abstracts.)*

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Multiple model organisms have been used to study ZMYND8 function, including mouse (*Mus musculus*), zebrafish (*Danio rerio*), *Xenopus laevis*, and *Drosophila melanogaster* (the latter referenced in the clinical study, PMID: 35916866).

**Key phenotypes relevant to neurodevelopment:**
- In *Xenopus*, overexpression of XZMYND8 inhibited neural differentiation, and XZMYND8 was shown to act as a transcriptional repressor co-expressed with XRCOR2 in the nervous system (PMID: 20331974). This is the most directly relevant model organism finding for neurodevelopmental function.
- In mouse, a conditional knockout of ZMYND8 (RACK7) in astrocytes produced overt developmental defects associated with aberrant astrocyte development, a remarkable genome-wide decrease in H3K27me3 chromatin localization, overactivation of Wnt signaling, and dysregulation of astrocyte differentiation genes (PMID: 40125808). This finding directly implicates ZMYND8 in glial cell development and CNS maturation.
- The Drosophila knockdown model referenced in the primary clinical paper (PMID: 35916866) provided additional in vivo support for the neurodevelopmental role, though detailed phenotypic data were not available from the abstract.

**Other model organism findings (primarily oncology-focused):** Several mouse and zebrafish studies examined ZMYND8 in cancer contexts (PMIDs: 25117453, 33148660, 34462784, 28232094, 40223119), demonstrating roles in angiogenesis, immune evasion, and tumor growth. While these studies confirm ZMYND8's broad biological importance, they are not directly informative for the neurodevelopmental phenotype.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The *Xenopus* neural differentiation data (PMID: 20331974) and the mouse astrocyte conditional knockout data (PMID: 40125808) are broadly consistent with a role for ZMYND8 in nervous system development, supporting the intellectual disability and neurodevelopmental features observed in human patients. However, no model organism study has yet recapitulated the full human syndrome (including cardiac malformations and ophthalmologic anomalies) in a heterozygous loss-of-function context.

**Notable gaps:** No published germline heterozygous knockout mouse model with systematic phenotyping has been identified. Mendelian ratio data, lethality information, and fertility data are not reported in the available studies. The absence of a dedicated vertebrate neurodevelopmental knockout model is a limitation.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**
*(Rationale: Relevant neurological phenotypes have been demonstrated in Xenopus and mouse conditional knockout models, and Drosophila knockdown was used in the primary clinical study, but a comprehensive heterozygous vertebrate model recapitulating the human syndrome is lacking.)*

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Molecular function and known pathways:**
ZMYND8 is a multifunctional chromatin reader whose PHD-BRD-PWWP cassette recognizes combinatorial histone modifications (H3K4me1/H3K14ac at active enhancers; H3K36me2 at gene bodies) to regulate transcription. Key mechanistic roles include:

- **Enhancer regulation and transcriptional control:** ZMYND8 occupies lineage-specific enhancers and interacts with BRD4 via its chromatin reader cassette to regulate oncogenic transcription factors including IRF8 and MYC in AML (PMID: 34358447). It also drives enhancer-promoter looping with SREBP2 and the Mediator complex to regulate metabolic gene programs (PMID: 33932349).
- **Super-enhancer decommissioning:** ZMYND8 forms liquid-liquid phase-separated condensates with NF-κB/p65 to silence latent super-enhancers during macrophage polarization, recruiting LSD1 for histone demethylation (PMID: 34764296).
- **DNA damage response:** ZMYND8 maintains genomic integrity and facilitates DNA damage repair; its knockout sensitizes cells to ionizing radiation and is associated with sustained γH2AX activation and ATM phosphorylation (PMID: 36692427).
- **Protein interactions relevant to neurodevelopment:** The PWWP domain mediates interaction with Drebrin (an actin-binding protein critical for dendritic spine morphology), and the MYND domain mediates interaction with GATAD2A (a component of the NuRD repressor complex); both interactions are disrupted by patient-derived missense variants (PMID: 35916866). ZMYND8 also interacts with RCOR2 (REST corepressor 2) in the nervous system (PMID: 20331974) and with PRC2 to regulate H3K27me3 deposition in astrocytes (PMID: 40125808).
- **Post-translational regulation:** ZMYND8 protein stability is regulated by ubiquitination (FBXW7-mediated degradation at K1034) and deubiquitination (USP7-mediated stabilization) (PMID: 39128723), and by lncRNA-mediated proteasomal degradation (PMID: 30854423).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**
ZMYND8 is co-expressed with RCOR2 in the *Xenopus* nervous system (PMID: 20331974), and its conditional deletion in mouse astrocytes produces CNS developmental defects (PMID: 40125808), indicating expression and functional relevance in the developing brain. The interaction with Drebrin — a protein with established roles in synaptic plasticity and dendritic spine development — provides a plausible molecular mechanism linking ZMYND8 dysfunction to intellectual disability (PMID: 35916866).

**Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE**
*(Rationale: Strong mechanistic data exist for ZMYND8's role as a chromatin reader and transcriptional regulator, with specific protein interactions (Drebrin, GATAD2A, RCOR2, PRC2) directly relevant to neurodevelopment. However, the majority of detailed mechanistic studies have been conducted in cancer cell lines, and direct mechanistic dissection in neuronal or cardiac developmental contexts remains limited.)*

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths:**
1. **De novo variant recurrence:** All 12 reported patients carry de novo (or suspected de novo) variants, with 10 confirmed de novo in a single well-characterized series (PMID: 35916866). This is the strongest genetic indicator of autosomal dominant pathogenicity.
2. **Functional validation of variants:** The primary clinical study (PMID: 35916866) provided multi-level functional evidence including molecular modeling, yeast two-hybrid assays, transcriptomic analysis, and a Drosophila model, demonstrating that patient variants specifically disrupt biologically relevant protein-protein interactions (Drebrin via PWWP domain; GATAD2A via MYND domain).
3. **Biological plausibility:** ZMYND8's interactions with Drebrin (dendritic spine morphology), GATAD2A/NuRD complex (transcriptional repression in neural contexts), RCOR2 (neural gene regulation), and PRC2 (astrocyte development) provide a coherent mechanistic framework for the observed neurodevelopmental phenotype.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. **Small total patient cohort:** Only ~12 patients have been reported across two publications, limiting phenotypic delineation and genotype-phenotype correlation.
2. **Absence of a dedicated vertebrate heterozygous knockout model:** No published mouse model with heterozygous germline loss of ZMYND8 and systematic neurodevelopmental and cardiac phenotyping has been identified, leaving the in vivo haploinsufficiency mechanism incompletely characterized.
3. **Predominance of cancer-focused biological studies:** The majority of mechanistic data derive from oncology research, and direct experimental evidence for ZMYND8's role in human neuronal differentiation, synaptogenesis, or cardiac morphogenesis remains limited.

**Conclusion:**
ZMYND8 meets criteria for a moderate-confidence autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene, supported by a consistent series of de novo variants (both LoF and missense) in patients with intellectual disability and cardiac malformations, functional validation of variant effects on key protein interactions, and biological evidence for roles in neural differentiation and CNS development. The primary limitation is the relatively small number of reported patients and the absence of a comprehensive vertebrate heterozygous model. Identification of additional patients through large-scale sequencing cohorts and development of dedicated neurodevelopmental animal models would substantially strengthen the evidence base.

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. De Novo ZMYND8 variants result in an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder with cardiac malformations (PMID: 35916866)
2. A recurrent PJA1 variant in trigonocephaly and neurodevelopmental disorders (PMID: 32530565)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. Xenopus RCOR2 (REST corepressor 2) interacts with ZMYND8, which is involved in neural differentiation (PMID: 20331974)
2. RACK7 Interacts with PRC2 Complex to Regulate Astrocyte Development (PMID: 40125808)
3. Zinc finger MYND-type containing 8 promotes tumour angiogenesis via induction of vascular endothelial growth factor-A expression (PMID: 25117453)
4. ZMYND8 Expression in Breast Cancer Cells Blocks T-Lymphocyte Surveillance to Promote Tumor Growth (PMID: 33148660)
5. Validation of ZMYND8 as a new treatment target in hepatocellular carcinoma (PMID: 34462784)
6. Chromatin reader ZMYND8 is a key target of all trans retinoic acid-mediated inhibition of cancer cell proliferation (PMID: 28232094)
7. The RBM39 degrader indisulam inhibits acute megakaryoblastic leukemia by altering the alternative splicing of ZMYND8 (PMID: 40223119)

### Biological Evidence
1. ZMYND8-regulated IRF8 transcription axis is an acute myeloid leukemia dependency (PMID: 34358447)
2. The ZMYND8-regulated mevalonate pathway endows YAP-high intestinal cancer with metabolic vulnerability (PMID: 33932349)
3. ZMYND8 mediated liquid condensates spatiotemporally decommission the latent super-enhancers during macrophage polarization (PMID: 34764296)
4. Zinc Finger MYND-Type Containing 8 (ZMYND8) Is Epigenetically Regulated in Mutant Isocitrate Dehydrogenase 1 (IDH1) Glioma to Promote Radioresistance (PMID: 36692427)
5. USP7 deubiquitinates epigenetic reader ZMYND8 to promote breast cancer cell migration and invasion (PMID: 39128723)
6. The endogenous retrovirus-derived long noncoding RNA TROJAN promotes triple-negative breast cancer progression via ZMYND8 degradation (PMID: 30854423)
