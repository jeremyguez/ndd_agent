# KIF5B as a Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene: Literature Evidence Report

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

KIF5B (Kinesin Family Member 5B) encodes the ubiquitous heavy chain isoform of kinesin-1, a microtubule-based motor protein responsible for anterograde intracellular transport of organelles, vesicles, mRNA-protein complexes, and cytoskeletal components along microtubules. It is expressed broadly across tissues—including neurons, cardiomyocytes, adipocytes, and chondrocytes—and plays essential roles in cargo trafficking, organelle positioning, synaptic plasticity, and cellular homeostasis.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports and patients:**
Six independent reports were identified. Across the three most directly relevant publications describing point variants in KIF5B, a total of approximately 11 patients with heterozygous de novo variants are documented: four patients with kyphomelic dysplasia (PMID: 35342932), four patients with osteogenesis imperfecta (PMID: 37934770), and three patients with variable phenotypes including dilated cardiomyopathy, ophthalmoplegia, skeletal myopathy, and neurodevelopmental conditions with hypotonia and seizures (PMID: 36018820). Two additional reports contribute single patients each: one fetus with malformations detected on antenatal ultrasound (ASHG 2023 – Saxena et al.) and one individual with neurologic disease identified through exome sequencing in a consanguineous Arab cohort (PMID: 27435318). A sixth report describes a single patient with a large chromosomal microdeletion encompassing KIF5B among multiple other genes (PMID: 41622991); this case is of limited interpretive value for KIF5B-specific pathogenicity given the multigenic nature of the deletion.

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
The phenotypic spectrum associated with heterozygous KIF5B variants is notably broad and pleiotropic, reflecting the gene's ubiquitous expression. Skeletal manifestations are prominent, including kyphomelic dysplasia with sharp angulation of long bones (PMID: 35342932) and osteogenesis imperfecta with fractures (PMID: 37934770). Neurodevelopmental features—including severe hypotonia, seizures, and intellectual disability—are reported in at least two of the three patients described in PMID: 36018820. Additional phenotypes include dilated cardiomyopathy, adult-onset ophthalmoplegia, progressive skeletal myopathy, neonatal respiratory distress, distinctive facial features, and postnatal osteoporosis. The neurodevelopmental phenotype (hypotonia, seizures, neurodevelopmental delay) is specifically documented in patients carrying variants in the stalk/coiled-coil domain (p.Leu498Pro, p.Leu537Pro) rather than the motor domain.

**Predominant variant type:**
The overwhelming majority of pathogenic variants are heterozygous missense variants (e.g., p.Lys91Arg, p.Thr195Lys, p.Gly234Val [PMID: 35342932]; p.Thr87Ile [PMID: 37934770]; p.Leu498Pro, p.Leu537Pro [PMID: 36018820]). One in-frame deletion (p.Asn255del) was also reported (PMID: 36018820). All confirmed pathogenic variants are de novo and heterozygous, consistent with an autosomal dominant mechanism. Variants cluster in the highly conserved kinesin motor domain (ATPase-related motifs) or in the stalk domain. No truncating loss-of-function variants have been reported as disease-causing in these studies, and the mechanism appears to be dominant negative rather than haploinsufficiency, as confirmed experimentally in a C. elegans model for the p.Thr87Ile variant (PMID: 37934770).

**Cohort affiliation:**
None of the patients are explicitly identified as belonging to established rare disease cohorts such as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL. The Saudi Arabia cohort (PMID: 27435318) is a consanguineous Arab family series, and the ASHG 2023 abstract (Saxena et al.) describes a fetal cohort from India. Cohort provenance for the remaining studies is not specified.

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**
The evidence is strengthened by multiple independent publications, consistent de novo occurrence, variant clustering in functionally critical domains, and functional validation in model organisms. However, the total number of patients remains small (~11 with point variants), the phenotypic spectrum is highly variable (complicating genotype-phenotype correlation), and the neurodevelopmental phenotype specifically is documented in only two to three patients across a single publication (PMID: 36018820). The absence of patients from large, well-characterized rare disease cohorts and the lack of detailed clinical data for some cases (PMID: 27435318; ASHG 2023) further limit confidence.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:**
KIF5B has been studied extensively in mouse (Mus musculus) using conditional knockout strategies targeting specific cell types, and in zebrafish (Danio rerio) using full knockout and knock-in approaches.

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

*Mouse:* Neuronal conditional knockout of Kif5b produces a range of phenotypes directly relevant to neurodevelopmental disease. Cortex-specific deletion results in heightened dendritic spine turnover, reduced spine stability, impaired PSD95 acquisition, and abolished FMRP transport to newly formed spines—with disrupted learning-dependent FMRP localization after fear extinction (PMID: 38451812). A separate neuronal conditional knockout demonstrates deficits in dendritic spine morphogenesis, impaired synaptic plasticity, and memory formation deficits (PMID: 31961321). Striatum-targeted deletion causes hypo-locomotion, movement initiation deficits, coordination impairments, and reduced dopamine D2 receptor presentation at synaptic membranes (PMID: 30664247). Pancreatic beta-cell-specific deletion abolishes glucose-stimulated calcium transients and impairs insulin secretion (PMID: 39322740). Adipose-specific deletion causes hyperlipidemia, insulin resistance, and thermogenic dysfunction (PMID: 28242773). None of the conditional knockouts reported lethality or infertility, indicating that complete loss in specific cell types is tolerated but produces tissue-relevant dysfunction.

*Zebrafish:* Full knockout of kif5b in zebrafish produces impaired muscle integrity, defective cartilage remodeling during craniofacial morphogenesis, disrupted lysosomal function, impaired matrix secretion, and chondrocyte apoptosis—phenotypes consistent with the skeletal dysplasia observed in human patients (PMID: 28715414). Knock-in of patient-derived variants (p.Asn255del, p.Leu498Pro, p.Leu537Pro) in zebrafish recapitulates disrupted lysosomal, autophagosome, and mitochondrial organization and impairs cilium biogenesis, affecting multiple developmental processes (PMID: 36018820).

**Consistency with human phenotype:**
The mouse neuronal knockout data are highly consistent with the neurodevelopmental phenotype (hypotonia, synaptic dysfunction, learning/memory deficits) observed in human patients. The zebrafish skeletal phenotypes are consistent with the kyphomelic dysplasia and osteogenesis imperfecta presentations. The pleiotropic nature of KIF5B dysfunction in model organisms mirrors the variable clinical expressivity seen across human cases.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent conditional knockout studies in mice across different neuronal and non-neuronal cell types, combined with zebrafish knock-in studies using patient-derived variants, provide robust and convergent support for KIF5B's role in neuronal function, skeletal development, and organelle trafficking. The neuronal phenotypes in mice are particularly compelling given their direct relevance to the human neurodevelopmental presentation.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
KIF5B functions as the motor subunit of the kinesin-1 complex, driving anterograde transport along microtubules. Its cargo repertoire is exceptionally broad and includes GLUT4- and CD36-containing vesicles (PMID: 40057204), FMRP-mRNA complexes (PMID: 38451812), Piezo2 mechanosensitive ion channels via an EndoA2 adaptor complex (PMID: 38475827), lysosomes and autophagosomes (PMID: 36018820), mitochondria (PMID: 27094714), and early endocytic vesicles (PMID: 17360972). KIF5B also participates in unconventional protein secretion via ERGIC compartmentalization in a Rab2A-dependent manner (PMID: 38926505) and in DNA double-strand break repair through formation of nuclear envelope tubules (PMID: 38632359). Post-translational regulation of KIF5B includes ubiquitin-like modification by TUGUL in adipocytes and muscle to regulate insulin-stimulated GLUT4 trafficking (PMID: 36246906), and stabilization via Pds5A/USP14-mediated deubiquitination during oocyte meiosis (PMID: 40215310).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**
KIF5B is the ubiquitously expressed kinesin-1 heavy chain isoform, in contrast to the neuron-enriched paralogs KIF5A and KIF5C. Nevertheless, it is abundantly expressed in neurons and plays non-redundant roles in dendritic transport, spine morphogenesis, and synaptic plasticity (PMID: 38451812; PMID: 31961321). Its role in transporting FMRP—a protein whose loss causes Fragile X syndrome—to newly formed dendritic spines is particularly noteworthy for neurodevelopmental disease relevance (PMID: 38451812). KIF5B is also expressed in cardiomyocytes, chondrocytes, adipocytes, and immune cells, consistent with the multi-system phenotypes observed in patients.

**Key experimental findings:**
Functional studies in patient-derived cells and model systems have demonstrated that pathogenic KIF5B variants disrupt lysosomal, autophagosome, and mitochondrial organization and impair cilium biogenesis (PMID: 36018820). The dominant negative mechanism for motor domain variants was confirmed in C. elegans (PMID: 37934770). Intracellular trafficking defects, including dilated endoplasmic reticulum, intracellular vacuoles, and abnormal Golgi distribution, were observed in patient cells (PMID: 37934770). Downregulation of mTOR signaling was identified as a downstream consequence of KIF5B dysfunction in osteogenesis imperfecta patients (PMID: 37934770). The KIF5B-RET fusion oncogene, while not directly relevant to germline neurodevelopmental disease, further illustrates the functional importance of KIF5B's structural domains in signaling contexts (PMID: 36895965).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
KIF5B has an extensively characterized molecular function with direct experimental evidence linking its cargo transport activities to neuronal, skeletal, and metabolic phenotypes. Patient-derived functional studies confirm that disease-associated variants disrupt intracellular trafficking through a dominant negative mechanism. The breadth and depth of mechanistic data across multiple experimental systems is exceptional for a recently described disease gene.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths:**
- Multiple independent publications (PMID: 35342932; PMID: 37934770; PMID: 36018820) report de novo heterozygous variants in KIF5B in patients with overlapping but variable phenotypes, with variants clustering in functionally critical domains.
- Dominant negative mechanism is experimentally confirmed, and patient-derived cell studies demonstrate consistent intracellular trafficking defects.
- Extensive model organism data—particularly neuronal conditional knockouts in mice and patient-variant knock-in zebrafish—provide strong biological plausibility and phenotypic consistency with human disease.
- The molecular function of KIF5B in neuronal dendritic transport, spine morphogenesis, and FMRP trafficking directly supports a neurodevelopmental disease mechanism.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- The total number of patients with neurodevelopmental phenotypes specifically attributable to KIF5B variants is small (approximately two to three individuals from a single publication [PMID: 36018820]), limiting the ability to define a consistent neurodevelopmental syndrome.
- The phenotypic spectrum is exceptionally broad (skeletal dysplasia, osteogenesis imperfecta, cardiomyopathy, neurodevelopmental delay), and genotype-phenotype correlations remain poorly defined, raising questions about whether neurodevelopmental features represent a distinct KIF5B-associated entity or a variable feature of a broader multisystem disorder.
- No patients are reported from large, systematic rare disease cohorts, and detailed clinical characterization is lacking for some cases.

**Conclusion:**
KIF5B is a well-characterized motor protein with compelling biological and model organism evidence supporting its role in neuronal function, and multiple independent reports of de novo heterozygous variants in patients with overlapping multisystem phenotypes—including neurodevelopmental features—establish it as a credible candidate autosomal dominant disease gene. However, the evidence specifically supporting KIF5B as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene is currently moderate, constrained by small patient numbers, phenotypic heterogeneity, and the absence of a clearly delineated neurodevelopmental syndrome. Identification of additional patients through large-scale sequencing cohorts and systematic genotype-phenotype analyses will be essential to elevate the evidence classification and define the neurodevelopmental phenotype with greater precision.

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. De novo heterozygous variants in KIF5B cause kyphomelic dysplasia. (PMID: 35342932)
2. Dominant negative variants in KIF5B cause osteogenesis imperfecta via down regulation of mTOR signaling. (PMID: 37934770)
3. Dominantly acting KIF5B variants with pleiotropic cellular consequences cause variable clinical phenotypes. (PMID: 36018820)
4. Exome sequencing in mostly consanguineous Arab families with neurologic disease provides a high potential molecular diagnosis rate. (PMID: 27435318)
5. A Patient With Intellectual Disability, Agenesis of Corpus Callosum, and Congenital Heart Disease Associated With Chromosome 10p11.2 Microdeletion. (PMID: 41622991)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. KIF5B plays important roles in dendritic spine plasticity and dendritic localization of PSD95 and FMRP in the mouse cortex in vivo. (PMID: 38451812)
2. Specific depletion of the motor protein KIF5B leads to deficits in dendritic transport, synaptic plasticity and memory. (PMID: 31961321)
3. Neuronal KIF5b deletion induces striatum-dependent locomotor impairments and defects in membrane presentation of dopamine D2 receptors. (PMID: 30664247)
4. Kinesin-1 mediates proper ER folding of the Ca(V)1.2 channel and maintains mouse glucose homeostasis. (PMID: 39322740)
5. Adipose-specific deletion of Kif5b exacerbates obesity and insulin resistance in a mouse model of diet-induced obesity. (PMID: 28242773)
6. Kinesin-1 promotes chondrocyte maintenance during skeletal morphogenesis. (PMID: 28715414)
7. Kif5B and Kifc1 interact and are required for motility and fission of early endocytic vesicles in mouse liver. (PMID: 17360972)

### Biological Evidence
1. Motor protein KIF5B inhibition as a novel strategy of controlled reperfusion against myocardial ischemia/reperfusion injury (PMID: 40057204)
2. Endophilin A2 controls touch and mechanical allodynia via kinesin-mediated Piezo2 trafficking (PMID: 38475827)
3. Hypertrophy induced KIF5B controls mitochondrial localization and function in neonatal rat cardiomyocytes (PMID: 27094714)
4. A dual role of ERGIC-localized Rabs in TMED10-mediated unconventional protein secretion (PMID: 38926505)
5. DNA double-strand break-capturing nuclear envelope tubules drive DNA repair (PMID: 38632359)
6. Ubiquitin-like processing of TUG proteins as a mechanism to regulate glucose uptake and energy metabolism in fat and muscle (PMID: 36246906)
7. The cohesin-associated protein Pds5A governs the meiotic spindle assembly via deubiquitination of Kif5B in oocytes (PMID: 40215310)
8. FOXA2 and STAT5A regulate oncogenic activity of KIF5B-RET fusion (PMID: 36895965)
