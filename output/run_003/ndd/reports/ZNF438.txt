# ZNF438 Evidence Report: Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene Assessment

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

ZNF438 encodes a C2H2-type zinc finger protein of 828 amino acids containing five zinc finger domains, mapped to human chromosome 10p11.2. Its primary known function is transcriptional repression, with nuclear localization confirmed in cell-based assays (PMID: 17669267).

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
ZNF438 functions as a transcriptional repressor. Dual luciferase reporter assays demonstrated transcription inhibition activity, and immunofluorescence confirmed nuclear localization of the protein in COS-7 cells (PMID: 17669267). In androgen-independent prostate cancer cells (LNCaP-AI), ZNF438 was identified as a negatively regulated target of the androgen receptor (AR): ChIP-PCR demonstrated significant AR enrichment at the ZNF438 locus, and DHT stimulation reduced ZNF438 expression while AR knockdown increased it (PMID: 27122218). In pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, 4C-seq chromatin conformation analysis nominated ZNF438 as a candidate transcriptional repressor of ZEB1, a key driver of epithelial-mesenchymal transition (PMID: 41391024).

**Expression pattern:**
RT-PCR across 18 human adult tissues demonstrated ubiquitous expression of ZNF438 without tissue-specific restriction (PMID: 17669267). No brain-specific or neurodevelopmentally enriched expression data were identified. The ubiquitous expression pattern is consistent with a broadly acting transcriptional regulator but does not specifically implicate neurodevelopmental contexts.

**Key experimental findings from other domains:**
- In osteosarcoma, ZNF438 was identified as one of seven prognosis-related zinc finger genes; lower expression correlated with high-risk classification and worse overall survival, with associations to immune cell infiltration patterns (PMID: 35281811).
- Multi-trait GWAS analysis identified ZNF438 as one of five genes shared between rheumatoid arthritis and stroke susceptibility, implicating immune-inflammatory pathways (PMID: 39953635; 8,255 RA cases / 409,001 controls; 43,132 stroke cases).
- Bayesian colocalization identified a shared ZNF438/ZEB1 locus among 26 colocalizing signals between type 1 diabetes and multiple sclerosis, representing a novel general autoimmune susceptibility locus (PMID: 42194988; 18,942 T1D cases / 14,802 MS cases).
- The CpG site cg01656216, located near ZNF438, was one of only two CpG sites referenced in six or more publications in a systematic review of DNA methylation and cardiovascular disease, associating ZNF438 methylation with vascular disease and epigenetic aging across 87,827 individuals across 99 studies (PMID: 36991458).
- A genome-wide significant differentially methylated CpG site nearest to ZNF438 was identified following high-dose dexamethasone administration, with >10% DNAm change (PMID: 30821055; n=30).
- Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) of pediatric sepsis datasets identified ZNF438 among the top 20 hub genes in a module enriched for neutrophil degranulation pathways (PMID: 33274532).

**Neurodevelopmental relevance of biological evidence:**
The biological evidence does not specifically support a neurodevelopmental role for ZNF438. The gene's associations are predominantly in immune/inflammatory, oncological, and epigenetic/cardiovascular contexts. No brain-enriched expression, neuronal function, synaptic pathway involvement, or neurodevelopmental regulatory network membership has been reported.

**Strength of biological evidence (for neurodevelopmental disease): LIMITED**

While there is a modest body of biological evidence establishing ZNF438 as a transcriptional repressor with roles in immune regulation and cancer biology, none of this evidence directly supports a neurodevelopmental function or mechanism relevant to autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorders.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

- **Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
- ZNF438 has a defined molecular function as a nuclear transcriptional repressor (PMID: 17669267), a class of proteins well-represented among neurodevelopmental disorder genes.
- GWAS-level signals implicate the ZNF438/ZEB1 locus in shared autoimmune susceptibility (PMID: 42194988), and its methylation is among the most consistently reported epigenetic signals in cardiovascular disease research (PMID: 36991458), indicating the locus is biologically active and detectable in population-scale studies.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- There is a complete absence of clinical genetic evidence: no patients with neurodevelopmental disorders carrying pathogenic variants in ZNF438 have been reported.
- No model organism studies exist to provide functional validation of a neurodevelopmental phenotype.
- The existing biological evidence is entirely in non-neurological contexts (cancer, autoimmunity, cardiovascular disease, sepsis), and no brain-enriched expression or neuronal pathway involvement has been demonstrated.
- The GWAS signals identified are at a locus shared with ZEB1, making it unclear whether ZNF438 itself or the neighboring ZEB1 gene is the functional driver.

**Conclusion:**
At present, there is insufficient evidence to support ZNF438 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous autosomal dominant pathogenic variants. While the gene encodes a transcriptional repressor with broad expression and emerging roles in immune and epigenetic regulation, no clinical cases, model organism phenotypes, or neurodevelopmentally relevant functional data have been reported. Systematic evaluation in large neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts and targeted functional studies in neuronal models would be required before any disease association could be considered.
