# Evidence Report: ANK3 as a Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene (Autosomal Dominant / Heterozygous Variants)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

ANK3 encodes ankyrin-G, a multifunctional molecular scaffold protein critical for localizing ion channels, cell adhesion molecules, and cytoskeletal components to the axon initial segment (AIS) and nodes of Ranvier in neurons, as well as to dendritic shafts and spines (PMID: 35794211). Multiple developmentally regulated isoforms are expressed in the brain, with distinct roles in neuronal excitability, GABAergic synapse maintenance, and axoglial integrity.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports/series:** Six independent publications or conference reports were identified, ranging from single case reports to multi-center case series.

**Total approximate number of patients:** Approximately 26 patients with heterozygous or mosaic ANK3 variants are described across the peer-reviewed literature, with additional single cases reported at ASHG conferences. The largest peer-reviewed series identified 16 patients with mono- or biallelic variants (PMID: 38988293), followed by a series of 4 patients with heterozygous loss-of-function (LoF) variants (PMID: 34218362), one mosaic case (PMID: 37263801), and three patients with heterozygous interstitial deletions encompassing ANK3 (PMID: 39904608). Single cases were reported from ASHG 2013 (compound heterozygous, epilepsy) and ASHG 2022 (ASD cohort in Qatar).

**Most common phenotypes observed:** Across studies, the most consistently reported phenotypes in heterozygous LoF carriers include:
- Intellectual disability / neurodevelopmental delay
- Autism spectrum disorder (ASD)
- Language and speech delay
- Behavioral problems (ADHD, aggressivity, self-injury)
- Hypotonia and motor delay
- Epilepsy / seizures
- Sleep disturbances

These phenotypes are reported across all major series (PMID: 38988293; PMID: 34218362; PMID: 37263801).

**Predominant variant type:** The overwhelming majority of pathogenic variants in the heterozygous (autosomal dominant) context are predicted loss-of-function (LoF) variants — specifically frameshift and nonsense variants predicted to result in truncated protein or nonsense-mediated decay. Several are confirmed de novo. One mosaic de novo nonsense variant (22% allelic fraction, confirmed by digital PCR) was reported (PMID: 37263801). Chromosomal deletions encompassing ANK3 at 10q21.1q21.3 have also been described (PMID: 39904608), though these are multi-gene deletions and cannot be attributed solely to ANK3 haploinsufficiency.

**Cohort origin:** Patients are predominantly identified through clinical exome or genome sequencing in multi-center international settings. None of the peer-reviewed studies explicitly identify patients from named cohorts such as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL, though the 100,000 Genomes Project (GEL) is referenced in the context of ocular coloboma families (PMID: 35034853). The ASHG 2022 abstract describes a Qatar-based ASD whole-genome sequencing cohort.

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**

The evidence is supported by multiple independent publications totaling approximately 20–26 heterozygous/mosaic cases, consistent phenotypic overlap across studies, de novo status confirmed in several cases, and isoform-specific functional analyses. However, the total patient numbers remain modest, formal segregation data are limited in most reports, and some evidence derives from conference abstracts without full peer review. The multi-gene deletion cases (PMID: 39904608) cannot be used to attribute causality to ANK3 alone.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** The majority of functional studies have been conducted in mouse (*Mus musculus*), with additional evidence from zebrafish (*Danio rerio*) and *C. elegans*.

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

- **Heterozygous knockout mice (Ank3+/-):** Multiple independent groups have demonstrated neuropsychiatric-relevant behavioral phenotypes in heterozygous mice, including increased or decreased anxiety (depending on the specific paradigm and genetic background), altered reward motivation, elevated stress reactivity with elevated serum corticosterone, and psychiatric-related behaviors in novelty-suppressed feeding and passive avoidance tasks (PMID: 23237312; PMID: 28411148; PMID: 28109561). Structural brain changes including a smaller cingulate cortex, retrosplenial cortex, primary motor cortex, and hippocampal fimbria were observed (PMID: 28411148). At the molecular level, heterozygous mice show altered hippocampal gene expression enriched for microtubule-related functions, increased GSK3 activity, elevated CRMP2 phosphorylation, and impaired microtubule dynamics in primary neurons — phenotypes rescued by lithium or GSK3 inhibition (PMID: 30046097). Disturbance of kinesin-mediated axonal transport and glutamate receptor trafficking was also demonstrated, again ameliorated by lithium (PMID: 28109561).

- **Full knockout mice (Ank3-/-):** Homozygous knockout mice exhibit absence epilepsy, hyperactivity, repetitive behaviors, abnormal sleep architecture with decreased REM sleep, and increased slow gamma oscillatory power correlated with seizure severity (PMID: 38123552). A microexon-specific full knockout revealed increased interneuron excitability and somatic calcium activity without disruption of the AIS, implicating isoform-specific mechanisms (PMID: 41688438).

- **Conditional knockout mice:** Oligodendrocyte-specific conditional knockout produced age-dependent destabilization of axoglial interactions with histological, electrophysiological, and behavioral pathophysiologies in aged mice (PMID: 38617359). Neuron-specific conditional knockout demonstrated reduced dendrite complexity and dendritic spine number in pyramidal neurons, rescued by lithium, GSK-3β inhibition, or adenylate cyclase activation (PMID: 36376465).

- **Knock-in mice (p.W1989R):** A BD-associated ANK3 variant disrupting the ankyrin-G/GABARAP interaction produced striking reductions in forebrain GABAergic synapses, pyramidal cell hyperexcitability, and disrupted network synchronization (PMID: 30504823). Lithium treatment selectively enhanced presynaptic GABAergic neurotransmission and reduced neuronal hyperexcitability in this model (PMID: 40849089).

- **Zebrafish knockdown:** Morpholino-mediated knockdown of *ank3a* in zebrafish produced coloboma and microphthalmia phenotypes, with expression localized to the optic fissure during early eye development (PMID: 35034853).

- **Lethality / Mendelian ratios:** No lethality was reported in any heterozygous or conditional knockout model. Full knockout mice are viable. No Mendelian ratio deviations were reported.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The mouse models are highly consistent with the human neurodevelopmental phenotype. Behavioral abnormalities (anxiety, hyperactivity, repetitive behaviors), epilepsy, sleep disturbances, and structural brain changes in heterozygous and homozygous mice closely mirror the intellectual disability, ASD, ADHD, epilepsy, and sleep disturbances observed in human patients. The lithium-responsiveness observed across multiple mouse models is biologically coherent with the known role of ANK3 in bipolar disorder.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**

There is a large body of convergent evidence from multiple independent groups using diverse mouse models (heterozygous knockout, full knockout, conditional knockout, and knock-in), with consistent neurological and behavioral phenotypes that parallel the human disorder. Mechanistic insights into GABAergic dysfunction, microtubule dynamics, axonal transport, and neuronal excitability are well-supported.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:** Ankyrin-G functions as a master organizer of the axon initial segment and nodes of Ranvier, recruiting voltage-gated sodium channels (Nav), potassium channels, and cell adhesion molecules (e.g., neurofascin) to these specialized membrane domains, thereby regulating neuronal excitability and action potential generation (PMID: 35794211). It also localizes to dendritic shafts and spines, where it interacts with GABARAP to maintain inhibitory GABAergic synapses (PMID: 30504823). Posttranslational modifications — including ubiquitination regulated by the deubiquitinase OTUD7A — control ankyrin-G stability and AIS localization; disruption of this regulatory axis in 15q13.3 microdeletion syndrome reduces ankyrin-G at the AIS and impairs neuronal excitability (PMID: 36604605). Ankyrin-G also participates in GSK-3β/CRMP2 signaling pathways governing microtubule dynamics and dendritic morphology (PMID: 30046097; PMID: 36376465), and in kinesin-mediated axonal transport of ion channels and glutamate receptors (PMID: 28109561). Additionally, ANK3 has been implicated in renal magnesium reabsorption (PMID: 29344503) and, through zebrafish studies, in ocular development (PMID: 35034853).

**Expression pattern:** ANK3 is broadly expressed in the brain, with isoform-specific expression patterns that vary by developmental stage and brain region. A BD-associated isoform shows significantly higher expression in the corpus callosum than frontal cortex, and is upregulated during adolescence relative to early childhood, suggesting a role in myelin maturation (PMID: 35091539). ANK3 mRNA is also detectable in whole blood, where expression levels moderate the relationship between childhood trauma and affective outcomes in patients with bipolar disorder and schizophrenia spectrum disorder (PMID: 37620394). High cerebellar expression of specific ANK3 isoforms is consistent with the cerebellar atrophy and ataxia observed in biallelic LoF cases (PMID: 40879451).

**Key experimental findings:**
- The AnkyrinG protein-protein interaction (PPI) network encompasses 17 direct interactors including sodium/potassium channel subunits, β4-spectrin, cell adhesion molecules, and Casein kinase 2 components. Targeted sequencing of all PPI network genes in 1,009 patient-parent trios identified 14 confirmed de novo variants across network members, a significant overrepresentation (p=0.007, Fisher's exact test), demonstrating that the broader ANK3 interactome is under selective constraint and relevant to neurodevelopmental disorders (ASHG 2018 - Genetic variation in the AnkyrinG interactome causes a range of neurological disorders).
- Disruption of the ankyrin-G/GABARAP interaction (via the p.W1989R variant) is sufficient to produce GABAergic synapse loss and pyramidal neuron hyperexcitability, with rescue by lithium acting through GSK-3β inhibition (PMID: 40849089; PMID: 30504823).
- OTUD7A-mediated deubiquitination stabilizes ankyrin-G at the AIS; loss of this interaction leads to increased polyubiquitination and reduced AIS ankyrin-G, with restoration of ankyrin-G expression reversing axonal growth and excitability deficits (PMID: 36604605).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The molecular function of ankyrin-G is well-characterized across multiple experimental systems. The mechanistic links between ANK3 dysfunction and neuronal excitability, GABAergic signaling, dendritic morphology, and axonal transport are supported by convergent biochemical, cell biological, and in vivo data from multiple independent laboratories.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE–STRONG**

**Key strengths:**
1. Multiple independent peer-reviewed publications report heterozygous LoF variants (frameshift, nonsense, de novo) in patients with a consistent neurodevelopmental phenotype encompassing intellectual disability, ASD, language delay, behavioral problems, and epilepsy, with de novo status confirmed in several cases (PMID: 38988293; PMID: 34218362; PMID: 37263801).
2. An extensive and internally consistent body of mouse model data — spanning heterozygous knockouts, full knockouts, conditional knockouts, and knock-in models from multiple independent groups — recapitulates key aspects of the human phenotype including behavioral abnormalities, epilepsy, sleep disturbances, and structural brain changes, with well-characterized molecular mechanisms (PMID: 23237312; PMID: 28411148; PMID: 38123552; PMID: 30504823).
3. The molecular function of ankyrin-G at the AIS and inhibitory synapses provides a compelling and mechanistically coherent explanation for the observed neurodevelopmental and neuropsychiatric phenotypes (PMID: 35794211; PMID: 40849089).

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. The total number of published patients with heterozygous LoF variants and a well-characterized neurodevelopmental phenotype remains modest (~20–26), and formal segregation data are limited in most reports. Some evidence derives from conference abstracts (ASHG 2013, ASHG 2022) that have not undergone full peer review.
2. The chromosomal deletion cases (PMID: 39904608) involve multiple genes and cannot be used to attribute causality specifically to ANK3 haploinsufficiency. Genotype-phenotype correlations, particularly regarding isoform specificity, remain incompletely defined.
3. Functional validation of individual patient variants (e.g., in patient-derived iPSC neurons or in vivo models) is largely absent from the clinical reports, and the precise mechanism by which heterozygous LoF leads to haploinsufficiency — as opposed to dominant-negative effects — has not been formally established for most reported variants.

**Conclusion:** ANK3 has moderate-to-strong evidence supporting its role as an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene, driven by heterozygous predicted loss-of-function variants. The clinical evidence, while based on a relatively small total patient cohort, is supported by a robust and mechanistically detailed body of model organism and biological data that is highly consistent with the observed human phenotype. Continued ascertainment of additional patients, formal functional validation of individual variants, and clearer delineation of isoform-specific genotype-phenotype correlations would further strengthen the evidence base.
