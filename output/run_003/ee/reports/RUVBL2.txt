# RUVBL2 as an Epileptic Encephalopathy Gene: Literature Evidence Report

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

RUVBL2 (RuvB-like AAA ATPase 2; also known as Reptin, TIP49b) encodes a highly conserved AAA+ ATPase that functions as a core component of multiple chromatin-remodeling and protein assembly complexes, including the R2TP complex, INO80, and SWR1/SRCAP. Its primary known functions encompass transcriptional regulation, DNA damage repair, nonsense-mediated mRNA decay, circadian clock regulation, and assembly of macromolecular complexes including axonemal dynein and HSP90 client proteins.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports:** Two independent reports were identified linking RUVBL2 variants to human disease.

**Total approximate number of patients:** Neither report provided a precise patient count. The evidence is therefore based on an unquantified number of individuals across both studies.

**Most common phenotypes observed:** The reported phenotypes span two partially overlapping clinical domains:
- *Neurodevelopmental impairments* (unspecified), associated with rare de novo variants identified in the context of cerebral organoid proteomics research (PMID: 35613588).
- *Noonan syndrome-like features*, including unique facial features, postnatal growth failure, psychomotor retardation, ectodermal abnormalities, congenital heart disease, chest and skeletal deformities, and delayed puberty, identified by whole exome sequencing in Korean patients without mutations in known RASopathy genes (ASHG 2015 – Kim et al.).

Critically, **neither report describes epileptic encephalopathy as a phenotype**. No seizures, epilepsy, or encephalopathy are mentioned in either clinical source.

**Predominant variant type:** Both reports describe variants of unspecified molecular type (neither loss-of-function nor missense classification was provided). The ASHG 2015 abstract describes "novel sequence variants," and the PMID: 35613588 study references "rare de novo genetic variants" without further characterization.

**Known cohorts:** Neither report is affiliated with recognized large-scale rare disease cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL). The ASHG 2015 report originates from a Korean clinical genetics center.

**Strength of clinical evidence for epileptic encephalopathy: NONE**
There is no published clinical evidence linking RUVBL2 variants to epileptic encephalopathy specifically. The available clinical reports describe neurodevelopmental impairment and Noonan syndrome-like features, and both are preliminary in nature — the ASHG 2015 abstract explicitly notes that functional studies are needed to verify pathogenicity, and the PMID: 35613588 study uses RUVBL2 primarily as a functional validation target in organoid models rather than as a primary clinical finding.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*) and zebrafish (*Danio rerio*) have been studied across multiple independent investigations.

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

- *Neurodevelopmental/neurological:* Conditional knockout of Ruvbl2 in paraventricular nucleus (PVN) hypothalamic neurons in mice abolished circadian amplitude restoration and hormonal rhythm recovery (PMID: 41785851). A separate PVN-specific conditional knockout produced hyperphagic obesity with impaired excitatory synaptic transmission, reduced presynaptic boutons and synaptic vesicles, and disrupted projections to arcuate hypothalamus, dorsomedial hypothalamus, and parabrachial complex (PMID: 39414808). These findings demonstrate a clear role for RUVBL2 in hypothalamic neurocircuit integrity and synaptic function, but no seizure or epileptic phenotype was reported.
- *Cardiac:* Full knockout of ruvbl2 in zebrafish caused ventricular hyperplasia due to cardiomyocyte hyperproliferation (PMID: 35178388).
- *Hepatic:* Liver-specific conditional knockout in mice produced hypoglycemia, hypolipidemia, decreased body and fat mass, and complete rescue of high-fat diet-induced insulin resistance phenotypes through opposing effects on mTORC1 and mTORC2 (PMID: 29074727). A separate hepatic conditional knockout demonstrated impaired hepatocyte proliferation and liver regeneration (PMID: 33792165).
- *Ciliary:* Zebrafish ruvbl2 loss-of-function models showed defective cilia motility, reduced axonemal dynein arm density, and multiple developmental defects consistent with primary ciliary dyskinesia (PMID: 23858445; PMID: 29113992).
- *Immune:* A knock-in mouse model demonstrated impaired T-cell development and defective humoral immune responses (PMID: 22761313).
- *Lethality:* Germline (full) knockout of RUVBL2 is embryonic lethal in mice, as reported in the context of the Fanconi anaemia core complex study (PMID: 25428364), consistent with its essential cellular functions.

**Consistency with human epileptic encephalopathy phenotype:** The model organism data do not recapitulate an epileptic encephalopathy phenotype. While synaptic transmission defects and hypothalamic neurocircuit disruption are observed in conditional mouse knockouts (PMID: 39414808), these have not been associated with seizures or cortical hyperexcitability. The embryonic lethality of full knockout is consistent with haploinsufficiency being a plausible disease mechanism, but this has not been demonstrated in a neurological context.

**Strength of model organism evidence for epileptic encephalopathy: LIMITED**
Model organism studies demonstrate broad and essential roles for RUVBL2 in multiple organ systems, including neuronal synaptic function, but no model has produced an epileptic or encephalopathic phenotype.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:** RUVBL2 participates in a remarkably diverse set of molecular processes:
- *Circadian clock regulation:* RUVBL2 interacts with core clock proteins (BMAL1) at E-box chromatin loci (PER1, PER2, DBP, NR1D1) to regulate circadian phase; pharmacological disruption of this interaction causes a 12-hour phase shift (PMID: 32376767). RUVBL2 has been identified as a conserved circadian clock component across eukaryotes, with its slow intrinsic ATPase activity (~13 ATP/day) proposed as a molecular timer (PMID: 40140583).
- *DNA damage repair:* RUVBL2 forms a complex with RUVBL1 and RAD51 for DNA repair; this complex is targeted by *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* UreC to suppress host repair and promote intracellular survival (PMID: 37848028).
- *mRNA surveillance:* DHX34 directly interacts with the RUVBL1-RUVBL2 complex and induces conformational changes specifically in RUVBL2 subunits to regulate NMD initiation (PMID: 33205750).
- *Oncogenic signaling:* RUVBL1/2 are required for MYC-driven oncogenic and immunoevasive gene expression in pancreatic cancer (PMID: 38821858) and mediate YTHDF1-dependent oncogenic translation in colorectal cancer (PMID: 38900944).
- *Metabolic regulation:* RUVBL2 is transcriptionally activated by PPARγ during adipogenesis and promotes Adiponectin polymerization and secretion (PMID: 32877748).
- *Neurodegenerative disease:* RuvBL2 overexpression reduced C9orf72-associated dipeptide repeat proteins in cell lines, primary neurons, and patient-derived iPSC motor neurons, and rescued motor phenotypes in a Drosophila C9ALS/FTD model; notably, C9ALS/FTD patients show reduced RUVBL2 expression compared to controls (PMID: 39638345).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:** RUVBL2 chemical inactivation in human cerebral organoids led to precursor cell displacement and apoptosis (PMID: 35613588), suggesting a role in cortical neurogenesis. Reduced RUVBL2 expression is observed in C9ALS/FTD patient tissue (PMID: 39638345). Hypothalamic neuronal expression is well-documented in mouse studies (PMID: 39414808; PMID: 41785851). However, no study has specifically characterized RUVBL2 expression in epilepsy-relevant cortical or hippocampal circuits, nor in the context of epileptic encephalopathy.

**Key experimental findings relevant to neurological disease:** The synaptic transmission defects observed upon PVN-specific conditional knockout (reduced presynaptic boutons, reduced synaptic vesicles, impaired excitatory transmission; PMID: 39414808) are mechanistically relevant to neurological disease broadly, but have not been linked to epilepsy. The organoid data (PMID: 35613588) provide the most direct neurodevelopmental evidence but are functional/pharmacological rather than genetic.

**Strength of biological evidence for epileptic encephalopathy: LIMITED**
RUVBL2 has well-characterized roles in neurodevelopment, synaptic function, and neurodegeneration, but none of the biological evidence specifically implicates it in epileptic encephalopathy mechanisms (e.g., channelopathy, cortical excitability, GABAergic/glutamatergic imbalance in epilepsy-relevant circuits).

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level for RUVBL2 as an epileptic encephalopathy gene with heterozygous (autosomal dominant) pathogenic variants: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths of the evidence:**
- RUVBL2 has a well-established and essential role in neurodevelopment, demonstrated by organoid studies (PMID: 35613588) and conditional neuronal knockout models (PMID: 39414808).
- De novo variants in RUVBL2 have been reported in patients with neurodevelopmental impairments (PMID: 35613588), providing a preliminary genetic signal.
- The embryonic lethality of full knockout in mice (PMID: 25428364) is consistent with a gene that could cause severe neurodevelopmental disease through heterozygous loss-of-function, and synaptic transmission defects in conditional neuronal knockouts provide a plausible neurological disease mechanism.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- **No clinical evidence for epileptic encephalopathy:** Neither clinical report describes seizures or epilepsy. The neurodevelopmental phenotypes reported are non-specific, and the Noonan syndrome-like features are not typically associated with epileptic encephalopathy.
- **No epileptic phenotype in any animal model:** Despite multiple conditional knockout studies in mice and zebrafish, no model has demonstrated seizures, cortical hyperexcitability, or an encephalopathic phenotype.
- **Variant-level evidence is absent:** No specific heterozygous pathogenic variants have been characterized at the molecular level, and no genotype-phenotype correlation has been established.
- **No cohort-level validation:** Neither clinical report is embedded in a recognized rare disease cohort, and patient numbers are not reported.

**Conclusion:** The current published literature does not support RUVBL2 as an established epileptic encephalopathy gene. While RUVBL2 has compelling roles in neurodevelopment and synaptic function, and preliminary clinical reports associate de novo variants with neurodevelopmental impairment, there is no direct clinical, animal model, or mechanistic evidence linking RUVBL2 to epileptic encephalopathy specifically. Rigorous case-level documentation of epileptic encephalopathy patients with characterized heterozygous RUVBL2 variants, combined with targeted neurological phenotyping in conditional cortical or hippocampal knockout models, would be required to substantiate this gene-disease association.
