# Evidence Report: PLN as an Epileptic Encephalopathy Gene (Autosomal Dominant / Heterozygous)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

PLN (Phospholamban) encodes a small 52-amino acid transmembrane regulatory peptide expressed predominantly in cardiac muscle, where it functions as a reversible inhibitor of the sarco/endoplasmic reticulum Ca²⁺-ATPase 2a (SERCA2a), thereby regulating calcium reuptake into the sarcoplasmic reticulum and modulating cardiac contractility and relaxation (PMID: 29464562). PLN is most extensively characterized as a cardiac gene, with its best-known pathogenic variant (R14del) associated with inherited dilated cardiomyopathy and arrhythmogenic disease.

- **Predominant variant type:** Not applicable
- **Known cohort representation (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL):** None identified
- **Strength of clinical evidence: NONE**

No clinical evidence was identified linking heterozygous PLN variants to epileptic encephalopathy or any neurodevelopmental disorder. The clinical evidence database provided for this report is entirely empty. There are no published case reports, case series, or cohort studies describing patients with PLN heterozygous pathogenic variants presenting with epileptic encephalopathy.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*) and zebrafish (*Danio rerio*).

**Key phenotypes from knockouts and knock-in models:**

The overwhelming majority of phenotypes documented in PLN animal models are cardiac in nature. Homozygous PLN R14del knock-in mice develop severe ventricular dilation, cardiac fibrosis, PLN protein aggregation, and premature death due to heart failure at approximately 8 weeks of age (PMID: 37955153; PMID: 41545752). Heterozygous R14del mice show a milder, age-dependent cardiac phenotype, with significantly reduced left ventricular ejection fraction emerging only at 18 months (PMID: 38334971), and no accelerated disease under pressure overload (PMID: 41068173). PLN full knockout mice are viable and demonstrate improved baseline cardiac function, though PLN-deficient hiPSC-derived cardiomyocytes progress to a failure phenotype with mitochondrial damage and impaired Ca²⁺ handling at later time points (PMID: 35334214). PLN knockout mice also show increased thermal inactivation of SERCA in cardiac tissue (PMID: 33111944).

**Neurological phenotypes:** One study using full PLN knockout mice reported hyperactivity, impulsivity, and sleep deficits attributed to aberrant thalamic reticular nucleus (TRN) function and perturbed SERCA2 regulation in TRN neurons (PMID: 38493225). This is a notable finding suggesting a non-cardiac role for PLN in the brain; however, these phenotypes (hyperactivity, impulsivity, sleep disruption) are not equivalent to epileptic encephalopathy, and no seizure activity, epileptiform discharges, or encephalopathic features were reported. This study used full (homozygous) knockout animals, not heterozygous models relevant to autosomal dominant disease.

**Zebrafish:** Downregulation of *pln* mRNA in larval zebrafish exposed to the cardiotoxicant 6PPD was associated with cardiac malformation and oxidative stress (PMID: 37094682), but no neurological phenotypes were described.

**Consistency with human epileptic encephalopathy phenotype:** The model organism data are not consistent with an epileptic encephalopathy phenotype. Cardiac phenotypes dominate across all models. The single study reporting neurological findings (PMID: 38493225) describes behavioral abnormalities in homozygous knockout mice that do not recapitulate epileptic encephalopathy.

**Strength of model organism evidence: NONE** (for epileptic encephalopathy specifically; evidence is STRONG for cardiac disease)

---

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

PLN is a well-characterized regulator of the SERCA2a-calcium cycling axis in cardiomyocytes. It is phosphorylated downstream of β-adrenergic signaling via PKA and CaMKII, relieving SERCA2a inhibition and accelerating calcium reuptake (PMID: 29464562). PLN also interacts with HAX-1, an anti-apoptotic protein, linking calcium homeostasis to cardiomyocyte survival pathways (PMID: 18415121). In the context of heart failure, cAMP dynamics within the PLN/SERCA2a microdomain are substantially remodeled (PMID: 38099489). PLN expression is transcriptionally regulated by ZBTB20 in cardiomyocytes (PMID: 38166470), and PLN serves as a downstream effector in the CAMK2-PLN-SERCA2a pathway mediating cardiotoxicity (PMID: 40568844). GWAS data from massively parallel reporter assays have identified functional non-coding regulatory variants at the PLN locus associated with QT interval variation (ASHG 2022 - Massively parallel reporter assays of QT interval GWAS enhancer variants), further supporting a cardiac-specific regulatory role.

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**

PLN expression is described as specific to cardiac muscle in the primary functional literature (PMID: 29464562). The single mouse knockout study reporting neurological phenotypes (PMID: 38493225) does document PLN expression in thalamic reticular nucleus neurons and a role for SERCA2 regulation therein, which represents a biologically plausible but largely unexplored non-cardiac function. There is no published evidence of PLN expression in cortical neurons, hippocampus, or other brain regions classically associated with epileptic encephalopathy, and no mechanistic data linking PLN dysfunction to epileptogenesis or neurodevelopmental impairment.

**Strength of biological evidence: NONE** (for epileptic encephalopathy; evidence is STRONG for cardiac calcium regulation)

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:** There is one mouse knockout study (PMID: 38493225) demonstrating that PLN is expressed in thalamic reticular nucleus neurons and that its loss produces behavioral abnormalities including hyperactivity and sleep deficits, providing a minimal biological foothold for a neurological role. The gene is well-characterized at the molecular level, and its role in SERCA-mediated calcium regulation is theoretically relevant to neuronal calcium homeostasis.

**Key gaps and limitations:** There are no clinical cases linking heterozygous PLN variants to epileptic encephalopathy or any neurodevelopmental phenotype. No animal model recapitulates epileptic encephalopathy, and the single study reporting neurological findings used homozygous full knockouts — a model not relevant to autosomal dominant heterozygous disease. PLN expression in epilepsy-relevant brain regions has not been established, and no mechanistic pathway connecting PLN dysfunction to epileptogenesis has been proposed or tested.

**Conclusion:** The current body of evidence provides no support for PLN as an epileptic encephalopathy gene with autosomal dominant heterozygous pathogenic variants. PLN is a well-validated cardiac disease gene, with robust evidence for its role in dilated cardiomyopathy and arrhythmogenic disease, but the neurological evidence is limited to a single behavioral study in homozygous knockout mice that does not model epileptic encephalopathy. This gene should not be considered a candidate for epileptic encephalopathy without the emergence of clinical case evidence and dedicated neurological mechanistic studies.
