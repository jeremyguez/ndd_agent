# Literature Evidence Report: MED13L as an Epileptic Encephalopathy Gene (Autosomal Dominant / Heterozygous)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

MED13L (Mediator Complex Subunit 13-Like) encodes a structural subunit of the CDK8 kinase module of the Mediator transcriptional coactivator complex, where it physically bridges the kinase module to the core Mediator and thereby regulates RNA Polymerase II-dependent transcription of a broad array of developmental genes. Heterozygous pathogenic variants in MED13L cause MED13L syndrome (also termed MED13L-related intellectual disability with facial dysmorphism and absent or severely impaired speech; OMIM #616,789), a neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by intellectual disability, severe speech impairment, hypotonia, and variable additional features including epilepsy.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports and patient totals:**
Ten independent publications or conference reports were identified, spanning case reports, case series, and cohort studies. Across these sources, the cumulative number of reported patients is approximately 139 individuals, comprising: 8 patients (PMID: 25758992), 36 patients (PMID: 29511999), 67 patients (PMID: 39257968), 5 patients (PMID: 40500968), 1 patient (PMID: 32646507), 2 patients (PMID: 34654706), 2 patients (PMID: 34713510), 8 patients (PMID: 29959045), 1 patient (ASHG 2014), and 9 patients (ASHG 2017). This represents a substantial and growing body of clinical evidence across multiple independent centers and countries.

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
The core phenotypic spectrum is highly consistent across studies. Intellectual disability (ranging from mild to severe) and severe language/speech impairment are universal or near-universal findings. Motor developmental delay and muscular hypotonia are reported in the large majority of patients, with hypotonia documented in 83% of the largest cohort (PMID: 39257968). Recognizable facial dysmorphism — including open mouth appearance, macroglossia, and macrostomia — is a consistent feature (PMID: 29959045). Speech apraxia and dysarthria are highlighted as particularly characteristic in the most recent large cohort (PMID: 39257968). Epilepsy or seizures are a recurrent but variable feature: reported in 31% of patients in the largest cohort (PMID: 39257968), noted as more frequent among patients with de novo missense variants clustering in exons 15–17 and 25–31 (PMID: 29511999), and documented in individual cases including a patient with a de novo p.(Lys1474*) nonsense variant who presented with seizures and epileptiform EEG activity (ASHG 2014) and a patient with p.Pro869Ser who had abnormal EEG (PMID: 32646507). Additional features include vision problems (72%), recurrent otitis media (58%), gastrointestinal problems (57%), ataxia, behavioral difficulties, and — in a subset — complex congenital heart defects. Epilepsy is thus a clinically significant but incompletely penetrant feature of MED13L syndrome, present in roughly one-third of patients.

**Predominant variant type:**
Both loss-of-function (LoF) variants (nonsense, frameshift, intragenic deletions) and missense variants are well-represented and causative. De novo missense variants clustering in specific exonic hotspots (exons 15–17 and 25–31) appear to be associated with a more severe phenotype including higher epilepsy frequency (PMID: 29511999). Specific recurrent missense variants include p.Pro869Ser (reported independently in PMID: 32646507 and PMID: 40500968) and p.Pro866Leu (PMID: 40500968). Intragenic copy number deletions are also pathogenic (ASHG 2017; PMID: 29959045). The vast majority of variants are de novo heterozygous, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance; rare instances of germline mosaicism have been documented (PMID: 34654706; PMID: 34713510).

**Cohort affiliation:**
None of the identified studies explicitly report patients from named research cohorts such as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL. The largest studies represent international collaborations or single-center series without specific cohort designation.

**Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG**
The evidence is strong based on: (1) a large cumulative patient number (~139 individuals) across at least ten independent reports; (2) consistent and recognizable phenotypic spectrum across studies from multiple countries; (3) predominantly de novo occurrence of heterozygous variants; (4) both LoF and missense variant types confirmed as pathogenic; (5) specific recurrent variants (e.g., p.Pro869Ser) reported independently by multiple groups; and (6) ACMG pathogenic classifications applied in multiple studies.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:**
MED13L has been studied in mouse (*Mus musculus*) and zebrafish (*Danio rerio*).

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

*Mouse:* Homozygous Med13l knockout mice exhibit perinatal lethality with reduced brain volume, reduced cortical thickness, and severe cortical neurogenesis deficits driven by impaired neural progenitor differentiation (PMID: 40775066). Heterozygous Med13l mice — the genotype directly analogous to human patients — are viable and display impaired learning and memory, reduced motor coordination, heightened anxiety, microcephaly, and simplified neuronal morphology in the motor cortex (PMID: 40775066). A separate heterozygous knockout mouse study documented smaller body size, developmental growth delay, and craniofacial anomalies (including midface hypoplasia and crooked snout) in over 60% of heterozygous animals (PMID: 40919805). A conditional cardiomyocyte-specific double knockout of Med13 and Med13L resulted in lethal heart failure within a median of 6 weeks, with dysregulation of fibrotic and calcium handling pathways (PMID: 40989238), consistent with the cardiac involvement seen in a subset of human patients. MED13L was also shown to partially compensate for loss of MED13 during preimplantation development (PMID: 29325037), underscoring its fundamental developmental role.

*Zebrafish:* Morpholino-mediated knockdown of the MED13L zebrafish orthologue (med13b) caused defective cranial neural crest cell migration, cartilage deformities, craniofacial anomalies, and abnormal neuronal distribution in the brain — phenotypes rescued by introduction of full-length human MED13L mRNA, confirming functional conservation (PMID: 25137640). Expression of the med13l orthologue was enriched in the brain and branchial arches, consistent with the human syndrome phenotypes (ASHG 2012 - Utami et al.).

**Consistency with human phenotype:**
The model organism data are highly consistent with the human phenotype. Heterozygous mouse models recapitulate the core neurodevelopmental features (cognitive impairment, motor deficits, microcephaly, craniofacial anomalies), and zebrafish models reproduce the craniofacial and neurological aspects. The haploinsufficiency mechanism operative in human patients is directly modeled by the heterozygous mouse.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent model systems (mouse full knockout, mouse heterozygous knockout, mouse conditional knockout, zebrafish knockdown) across multiple laboratories converge on neurological and craniofacial phenotypes that closely mirror the human syndrome, with functional rescue experiments in zebrafish confirming specificity.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
MED13L is a structural component of the CDK8 kinase module of the Mediator complex, where it physically links the kinase module (CDK8, CDK19, Cyclin C, MED12/MED12L) to the core Mediator. This interaction is regulated post-translationally: the SCF-Fbw7 ubiquitin ligase targets MED13 and MED13L for proteasomal degradation, thereby controlling the dynamic association of the kinase module with core Mediator (PMID: 23322298). MED13L also regulates superenhancer-associated gene expression — depletion of MED13 and MED13L in colon cancer cells disproportionately reduced expression of superenhancer-driven oncogenes including MYC (PMID: 29507187), indicating a structural role in superenhancer activity independent of CDK8 kinase function. In lung cancer cells, miR-4497-mediated silencing of MED13L attenuated CDK8 module-Mediator interaction, reduced P300 acetyltransferase recruitment, and diminished global H3K27ac signals (PMID: 32802198), further demonstrating MED13L's role in epigenetic regulation of transcription.

**Expression pattern (neurodevelopmental relevance):**
MED13L exhibits tissue-dependent and developmental stage-dependent expression in the mouse brain, with high expression in the ventricular zone of the cerebral cortex at embryonic day 14 — a critical period of neurogenesis — and diffuse expression throughout the cortex postnatally (PMID: 33794529). Partial colocalization with pre- and post-synaptic markers (synaptophysin and PSD95) in hippocampal neurons suggests a role in synaptic function (PMID: 33794529). Single-cell transcriptomics in Med13l knockout mice revealed that MED13L orchestrates cortical neurogenesis by priming transcriptional activation of key developmental genes including Neurod2, Sox5, Auts2, and Nfib through facilitation of RNA Pol II association with the core Mediator (PMID: 40775066).

**Key experimental findings:**
- Disease-associated MED13L missense variants (p.P866L, p.T2162M, p.S2163L, p.S2177Y) show aberrant subcellular localization in cortical neurons and impair dendritic growth in vivo; MED13L knockdown abrogated dendritic branching, rescued by wild-type but not p.S2163L MED13L (PMID: 36798993).
- Patient fibroblasts with MED13L S1497F/fs alleles showed aberrant cytoplasmic cyclin C localization, mitochondrial fragmentation, a 6-fold reduction in mitochondrial respiration, reduced mtDNA copy number, and hypersensitivity to oxidative stress — corrected by preventing cyclin C mitochondrial localization (PMID: 35198885). This mitochondrial dysfunction mechanism may contribute to neuronal vulnerability and epilepsy susceptibility.
- MED13L knockout in human iPSC-derived cerebral organoids produced neuroretinal rather than cortical neurons, with coordinated activation of OTX2/PAX6 cis-regulatory elements and failure to activate forebrain-specific enhancers, demonstrating MED13L's essential role in proper enhancer activation during neural induction (ASHG 2023 - Ghoumid et al.).
- Pathogenic missense variants p.Pro866Leu and p.Pro869Ser reduce protein stability and disrupt CDK8 kinase module interaction, while p.Cys1131Tyr causes aberrant cytoplasmic localization; variants p.Gly1899Arg and p.Thr2162Met are associated with milder phenotypes (PMID: 40500968).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent experimental approaches — protein interaction studies, patient-derived cell lines, in utero electroporation, iPSC-derived organoids, transcriptomics, and epigenomics — converge on a coherent mechanistic model in which MED13L loss disrupts Mediator-dependent transcriptional regulation of neurodevelopmental gene programs, impairs dendritic morphogenesis, and causes mitochondrial dysfunction, all of which are plausible contributors to the epileptic encephalopathy phenotype.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths:**
1. *Robust clinical evidence:* Approximately 139 patients across ten independent reports from multiple countries demonstrate a consistent, recognizable neurodevelopmental syndrome caused by heterozygous de novo MED13L variants (both LoF and missense), with epilepsy documented in ~31% of patients and enriched among those with missense variants in specific hotspot regions.
2. *Convergent model organism data:* Heterozygous mouse knockouts directly recapitulate the human cognitive, motor, and craniofacial phenotypes; zebrafish knockdown reproduces neurological and craniofacial features with human mRNA rescue, confirming functional conservation and haploinsufficiency as the operative mechanism.
3. *Mechanistic depth:* Multiple biological studies elucidate how MED13L loss disrupts Mediator-dependent transcriptional priming of neurodevelopmental genes, impairs dendritic morphogenesis, and causes mitochondrial dysfunction — providing plausible cellular mechanisms for both the intellectual disability and the epileptic component of the syndrome.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- Epilepsy is a variable rather than universal feature (~31% prevalence), and the specific mechanisms by which MED13L haploinsufficiency lowers seizure threshold have not been directly investigated in electrophysiological or circuit-level studies.
- No patients in the identified literature are drawn from named large-scale research cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, GEL), which limits systematic genotype-phenotype correlation and ascertainment bias assessment.
- Specific variant-level data (HGVS notation) are unavailable for the majority of patients in the larger cohort studies, limiting precise genotype-phenotype analysis for the epilepsy subphenotype.

**Conclusion:**
MED13L is a well-established autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disease gene, with strong and convergent clinical, model organism, and biological evidence supporting its role in causing intellectual disability, severe speech impairment, hypotonia, and — in a clinically significant subset of approximately one-third of patients — epilepsy. The haploinsufficiency mechanism is directly supported by heterozygous mouse models that recapitulate the human phenotype, and multiple molecular studies demonstrate that MED13L loss disrupts Mediator-dependent transcriptional regulation of cortical neurogenesis and dendritic development, providing a plausible biological basis for epileptic encephalopathy. MED13L should be considered a confirmed epileptic encephalopathy gene with autosomal dominant inheritance, warranting inclusion in diagnostic gene panels for neurodevelopmental disorders with epilepsy.
