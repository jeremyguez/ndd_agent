# Evidence Report: RAB5C as an Epileptic Encephalopathy Gene (Autosomal Dominant)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

RAB5C encodes a member of the RAB5 subfamily of small GTPases (alongside RAB5A and RAB5B), which are master regulators of early endosomal trafficking, controlling vesicle fusion, receptor internalization, and endolysosomal sorting. RAB5C cycles between GDP-bound (inactive) and GTP-bound (active) states and plays isoform-specific roles in growth factor receptor trafficking, cell motility, Notch and AKT signaling, and synaptic receptor endocytosis.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports:** Two independent publications were identified.

**Total patients:** Approximately 13 patients across both studies — 12 patients in a dedicated case series (PMID: 37552066) and 1 patient in a chromosomal duplication report (PMID: 23337768).

**Most common phenotypes observed:** The primary case series (PMID: 37552066) identified two partially distinct phenotypic clusters depending on variant type. Among the nine patients with heterozygous de novo missense variants, the predominant features were macrocephaly and mild-to-moderate developmental delay. Among the two patients with heterozygous de novo loss-of-function (LoF) variants, the phenotype was notably more severe, characterized by refractory epilepsy and intellectual disability, with normal head circumference — a phenotype most directly relevant to epileptic encephalopathy. The single patient with a de novo 17q21.2 duplication encompassing RAB5C and seven other genes presented with severe developmental delay and dysmorphic features, though RAB5C was not considered the primary candidate gene in that report (PMID: 23337768).

**Predominant variant type:** The majority of characterized variants are heterozygous de novo missense variants (n=9 patients). Two patients carried heterozygous de novo LoF variants. All variants were de novo, consistent with an autosomal dominant mechanism.

**Cohort affiliation:** The case series (PMID: 37552066) is described as a multinational study, but specific cohort affiliations (e.g., UDN, DDD, GEL, GREGoR, RUMC) are not explicitly stated in the available data.

**Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED**

While the de novo occurrence of variants across 12 unrelated individuals is meaningful, the total patient number remains small. Critically, only two patients with LoF variants exhibited the refractory epilepsy phenotype most relevant to epileptic encephalopathy, and the genotype–phenotype correlation between variant type and epilepsy versus macrocephaly is not yet fully established. The chromosomal duplication case (PMID: 23337768) provides minimal additional support for RAB5C specifically, given the multi-gene nature of the rearrangement.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Zebrafish (*Danio rerio*), *Xenopus laevis*, mouse (*Mus musculus*), and *C. elegans* (the latter referenced within the clinical study, PMID: 37552066).

**Key phenotypes:**

- **Zebrafish:** Multiple independent knockdown studies demonstrate that Rab5c is essential for diverse developmental processes, including hematopoietic stem and progenitor cell specification via Notch and AKT signaling (PMID: 32275659), gastrulation and mesendodermal cell cohesion via E-cadherin endocytosis (PMID: 16198297), developmental angiogenesis via VEGFR2 stabilization (PMID: 33983539), and epiboly via E-cadherin localization at adherens junctions (PMID: 20405038). Most directly relevant to the human phenotype, expression of human RAB5C missense variants in zebrafish embryos caused defective development (PMID: 37552066), providing in vivo validation of variant pathogenicity.

- **Xenopus:** Rab5c-dependent endocytosis of AMPA receptor subunits (GluA1 and GluA2) was shown to mediate synaptic downscaling in response to prolonged visual experience, with knockdown altering dendritic architecture and increasing intrinsic neuronal excitability (PMID: 41507541). This finding is particularly relevant to the neurodevelopmental and epilepsy phenotypes observed in humans.

- **Mouse:** Conditional triple depletion of Rab5A, Rab5B, and Rab5C using an auxin-inducible degron system caused cell death within 48 hours, with loss of early and late endosomes and dramatic reduction in endocytic cargo trafficking (PMID: 34817057), indicating that Rab5 family function is essential for cell viability. A separate study showed that RAB5C knockout in chondrogenic progenitor cells enhanced chondrogenic differentiation markers (PMID: 34013174), and reduced RAB5C protein levels in Sorcin knockout mice were associated with impaired lung alveolarization (PMID: 41148307).

- **C. elegans:** As reported within the clinical study, in vivo testing of RAB5C variants confirmed endocytic pathway defects, with two variants acting through a dominant negative mechanism (PMID: 37552066).

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The Xenopus data showing Rab5c-mediated AMPA receptor endocytosis and altered neuronal excitability (PMID: 41507541) is directly consistent with a synaptic mechanism for epilepsy and neurodevelopmental delay. The zebrafish variant expression data (PMID: 37552066) directly validates the pathogenicity of human disease-associated variants. However, no model organism study has specifically recapitulated the macrocephaly or refractory epilepsy phenotypes observed in human patients with isolated RAB5C variants.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**

Multiple organisms demonstrate that Rab5c is required for normal development and that human disease variants are damaging in vivo. The neuronal excitability data from Xenopus provides a plausible mechanistic link to epilepsy. However, no dedicated neurological knockout model has been reported, and the available zebrafish and mouse studies primarily address non-neurological developmental processes.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:** RAB5C is a core regulator of early endosomal trafficking, with isoform-specific functions distinct from RAB5A and RAB5B. Key documented roles include: (1) regulation of growth factor receptor trafficking and downstream signaling — specifically EGFR/Rac1/AKT-mediated cell motility (PMID: 24587345) and VEGFR2 stabilization to sustain ERK and PI3-K signaling (PMID: 33983539); (2) regulation of Notch ligand/receptor endocytosis and AKT signaling in hematopoietic development (PMID: 32275659); (3) LC3-associated phagocytosis (LAP) in macrophages via V-ATPase and ATG16L1 recruitment (PMID: 40196584); (4) von Willebrand Factor exocytosis from endothelial cells via SNAP29 interaction (PMID: 41537263); and (5) AMPA receptor endocytosis mediating synaptic downscaling in neurons (PMID: 41507541).

**Neurodevelopmental relevance:** The Xenopus study (PMID: 41507541) directly implicates Rab5c in synaptic AMPA receptor trafficking, with knockdown reducing mEPSC amplitudes, altering dendritic architecture, and increasing intrinsic neuronal excitability — a profile consistent with epileptic encephalopathy. The biochemical studies in the clinical paper (PMID: 37552066) showed that disease-associated missense variants increase the nucleotide exchange rate and attenuate responsivity to guanine exchange factors (GEFs), with heterogeneous effects on effector protein interactions, suggesting gain-of-function or dominant negative mechanisms depending on the specific variant.

**GWAS and expression data:** RAB5C was identified as one of six genes containing SNPs within coronary artery disease GWAS loci that affect smooth muscle cell-specific alternative splicing, with sQTLs colocalizing with CAD risk in aortic smooth muscle cells from 151 heart transplant donors (ASHG 2023 - Coronary artery disease-associated splicing variants in vascular smooth muscle cells). Two additional GWAS-identified variants (rs9915255 and rs9912088) were shown to regulate RAB5C expression in stem cell-derived endothelial cells (PMID: 41537263). These findings indicate that RAB5C expression is subject to regulatory variation with phenotypic consequences, though these data pertain to cardiovascular rather than neurological traits.

**Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE**

RAB5C has well-characterized roles in endosomal trafficking with direct neuronal relevance demonstrated in Xenopus. The biochemical characterization of disease variants (PMID: 37552066) provides mechanistic insight into pathogenicity. However, direct evidence for RAB5C function in mammalian brain development or neuronal circuit formation remains limited, and the precise mechanism linking RAB5C dysfunction to epileptic encephalopathy in humans has not been fully elucidated.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: LIMITED**

**Key strengths:**
- All 12 characterized patients carry heterozygous de novo variants, strongly supporting an autosomal dominant mechanism (PMID: 37552066).
- Biochemical studies demonstrate that missense variants alter RAB5C GTPase activity and effector interactions, and C. elegans studies confirm in vivo endocytic pathway defects including dominant negative mechanisms for two variants (PMID: 37552066).
- The Xenopus data showing Rab5c-dependent AMPA receptor endocytosis and increased neuronal excitability provides a biologically plausible mechanism for epilepsy (PMID: 41507541).
- Expression of human disease variants in zebrafish caused developmental defects, providing cross-species validation (PMID: 37552066).

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- The epileptic encephalopathy phenotype specifically is supported by only two patients with LoF variants in a single study (PMID: 37552066); the larger group of missense variant carriers presented primarily with macrocephaly and developmental delay rather than epilepsy, raising questions about whether RAB5C LoF specifically drives epileptic encephalopathy.
- No dedicated neurological model organism study (e.g., brain-specific knockout in mouse) has been reported, and no study has directly recapitulated the human epilepsy phenotype in an animal model.
- The total patient count (n=12, with only n=2 having epilepsy) is small, and cohort affiliations are not specified, limiting assessment of ascertainment bias.

**Conclusion:** RAB5C harbors heterozygous de novo variants — predominantly missense — associated with macrocephaly and developmental delay in a cohort of 12 individuals, with refractory epilepsy and intellectual disability observed specifically in the two patients carrying LoF variants (PMID: 37552066). Functional studies across multiple model organisms and biochemical assays support variant pathogenicity and provide a plausible synaptic mechanism via AMPA receptor trafficking (PMID: 41507541), but the evidence base for RAB5C as an epileptic encephalopathy gene specifically remains limited by small patient numbers and the absence of a dedicated neurological animal model. Further case ascertainment and mechanistic studies in mammalian neuronal systems are needed to firmly establish RAB5C as a cause of epileptic encephalopathy.

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. Macrocephaly and developmental delay caused by missense variants in RAB5C (PMID: 37552066)
2. A de novo 17q21.2 duplication in a boy with developmental delay and dysmorphic features (PMID: 23337768)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. Rab5c-mediated endocytic trafficking regulates hematopoietic stem and progenitor cell development via Notch and AKT signaling (PMID: 32275659)
2. Wnt11 functions in gastrulation by controlling cell cohesion through Rab5c and E-cadherin (PMID: 16198297)
3. The endosomal RIN2/Rab5C machinery prevents VEGFR2 degradation to control gene expression and tip cell identity during angiogenesis (PMID: 33983539)
4. A vertebrate-specific Chp-PAK-PIX pathway maintains E-cadherin at adherens junctions during zebrafish epiboly (PMID: 20405038)
5. Prolonged visual experience accelerates developmental synaptic downscaling via epigenetic regulation and Rab5c mediated AMPA receptor trafficking (PMID: 41507541)
6. Establishment and analysis of conditional Rab1- and Rab5-knockout cells using the auxin-inducible degron system (PMID: 34817057)
7. Enhancing the chondrogenic potential of chondrogenic progenitor cells by deleting RAB5C (PMID: 34013174)
8. Sorcin regulates alveolarization and airway tissue remodeling during lung morphogenesis (PMID: 41148307)

### Biological Evidence
1. Rab5 isoforms orchestrate a 'division of labor' in the endocytic network; Rab5C modulates Rac-mediated cell motility (PMID: 24587345)
2. RAB5c controls the assembly of non-canonical autophagy machinery to promote phagosome maturation and microbicidal function of macrophages (PMID: 40196584)
3. RAB5C Increases Endothelial Release of VWF by Regulating Vesicle Trafficking (PMID: 41537263)
