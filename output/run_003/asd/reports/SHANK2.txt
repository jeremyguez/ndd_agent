# Literature Evidence Report: SHANK2 as an Autism Spectrum Disorder Gene (Autosomal Dominant / Heterozygous Pathogenic Variants)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

SHANK2 (SH3 and multiple ankyrin repeat domains protein 2; also known as ProSAP1) encodes a postsynaptic scaffolding protein that organizes the postsynaptic density of excitatory glutamatergic synapses by linking neurotransmitter receptors, signaling enzymes, and the actin cytoskeleton. It plays a critical role in synapse formation, dendritic spine morphology, and synaptic plasticity, and is highly expressed in the brain, particularly in regions relevant to social cognition and learning.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports/series:** Approximately 10 independent publications or conference reports were identified, spanning peer-reviewed articles and ASHG abstracts (2010–2022).

**Total approximate number of patients:** Across all studies, roughly 45–50 individual patients with SHANK2 variants are described, though some studies report overlapping cohorts and not all provide exact patient counts. Key series include 11 patients (PMID: 32987185), 10 patients (PMID: 40307697), 13 patients (ASHG 2011 – Leblond et al.), 9 patients (ASHG 2010 – Berkel et al.), and single-patient reports from Italy (ASHG 2015 – Grisolia et al.) and Jordan (ASHG 2022 – Alkhateeb et al.).

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Autism spectrum disorder (ASD): present in virtually all case series; reported in 90% of patients in one dedicated cohort (PMID: 40307697)
- Intellectual disability, ranging from mild to moderate: consistently reported across series (PMID: 32987185; ASHG 2010 – Berkel et al.; ASHG 2015 – Grisolia et al.)
- Speech and language delay / poor communication skills (PMID: 32987185; PMID: 40307697)
- ADHD (50% in one cohort; PMID: 40307697)
- Repetitive and stereotyped behaviors (PMID: 40307697)
- Sensory abnormalities including hyperreactivity and sensory seeking (PMID: 40307697)
- Hypotonia, gastrointestinal abnormalities, and recurrent ear infections reported in a subset (PMID: 40307697)
- Delayed motor milestones in isolated cases (ASHG 2015 – Grisolia et al.)

**Predominant variant type:** Loss-of-function (LoF) variants — including protein-truncating variants, nonsense mutations, and copy number deletions — represent the most clinically significant and functionally validated class. De novo CNVs and de novo nonsense mutations are reported across multiple independent studies (ASHG 2010 – Berkel et al.; ASHG 2011 – Leblond et al.; PMID: 40307697). Rare inherited missense variants have also been identified in ASD patients, though their individual contribution is less certain and a "multiple hit" model has been proposed for some (ASHG 2011 – Leblond et al.; ASHG 2018 – Rosan et al.). A patient-derived isoform-specific nonsense mutation (SHANK2B Y29X) was functionally validated in iPSC and mouse models (PMID: 38704506).

**Known cohort membership:** None of the identified patients are explicitly reported as belonging to established diagnostic cohorts such as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL. Patients originate from research cohorts in Europe, Brazil, and the Middle East, as well as from the Autism Genome Project (ASHG 2011 – Leblond et al.).

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**
Multiple independent groups across different countries have identified de novo LoF variants and deletions in SHANK2 in ASD and intellectual disability patients, with consistent phenotypic profiles. However, total patient numbers remain modest, specific variant notations are often not provided, and some studies are conference abstracts rather than peer-reviewed publications. The inherited nature of some variants and the presence of similar variants in controls (ASHG 2011 – Leblond et al.) introduce some uncertainty for missense variants specifically, though de novo LoF variants are well-supported.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*) and zebrafish (*Danio rerio*).

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

*Mouse models* (multiple independent lines, full and conditional knockouts):
- Social behavior deficits, including reduced sociability and altered social dominance, are consistently observed across multiple Shank2 knockout lines (PMID: 30072871; PMID: 27903723; PMID: 33126897)
- Repetitive and stereotyped behaviors (PMID: 30072871; PMID: 27903723)
- Hyperactivity and altered locomotion (PMID: 30072871; PMID: 29046483; PMID: 38853567)
- Abnormal ultrasonic vocalizations, a proxy for communication deficits (PMID: 23994547; PMID: 27903723)
- Mania-like behavior responsive to mood stabilizers lithium and valproate, with altered NMDA/AMPA receptor composition (PMID: 29046483)
- Impaired motor coordination in cerebellar-specific knockouts (PMID: 27903723)
- Reduced excitatory synapse density in cerebellar Purkinje cells (PMID: 27903723)
- Pain hypersensitivity and altered nociception mediated by glycinergic interneurons (PMID: 37316943)
- Decreased bone mass in male mice (PMID: 36751416)
- Partial rescue of hyperactivity and social preference deficits by dietary zinc supplementation, without rescue of working memory or NMDA/AMPA transmission deficits (PMID: 38853567)
- Gene dosage-dependent transcriptomic changes in prefrontal cortex, hippocampus, and striatum, with heterozygous and homozygous mutations producing distinct and sometimes opposite expression patterns (PMID: 36311025)

*Zebrafish model* (full knockout):
- Reduced social preference and kin preference, enhanced behavioral stereotypy, auditory hypersensitivity, and greater susceptibility to drug-induced seizures (PMID: 36768529)
- Reduced GABAergic signaling (reduced GABA receptor subunit expression), consistent with inhibitory/excitatory imbalance (PMID: 36768529)

**Lethality / Mendelian ratio:** All reported knockout models are viable with no reported lethality or Mendelian ratio deviations, supporting that heterozygous and homozygous loss of Shank2 is compatible with survival.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The mouse and zebrafish models recapitulate the core features of human SHANK2-related neurodevelopmental disorder with high fidelity — ASD-like social deficits, communication abnormalities, repetitive behaviors, and hyperactivity are consistently observed. The mania-like phenotype in mice (PMID: 29046483) is consistent with the broader neuropsychiatric spectrum reported in human patients (PMID: 30072871). The spectrum of phenotypes across multiple independently generated mouse lines strengthens confidence in the gene-phenotype relationship.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent mouse lines and a zebrafish model, generated by different laboratories, consistently reproduce ASD-relevant behavioral phenotypes. Conditional knockout studies dissect region-specific contributions. Rescue experiments (zinc supplementation, mood stabilizers) provide mechanistic insight and translational relevance.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
- SHANK2 is a core postsynaptic density scaffolding protein at excitatory glutamatergic synapses, organizing receptor complexes (NMDA, AMPA) and linking them to downstream signaling via its PDZ, SH3, and ankyrin repeat domains.
- SHANK2 directly interacts with CFTR via its PDZ domain, negatively regulating CFTR-mediated ion transport in epithelial cells (PMID: 14679199), demonstrating functional roles beyond the CNS.
- SHANK2 has been identified as a regulator of the Hippo signaling pathway through competitive binding for a LATS1 activator, with relevance to cell proliferation and oncogenesis (PMID: 32661924).
- CRISPR-generated frameshift mutations in SHANK2 in SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cells impair neurite outgrowth, neuronal differentiation, and apoptosis, and increase AKT phosphorylation, implicating downstream tyrosine kinase receptor signaling (PMID: 33483523).
- SHANK2 protein levels are post-transcriptionally regulated by microRNA miR-137 via translational repression, with evidence that this regulatory axis is relevant to schizophrenia (ASHG 2017 – Berkel et al.), further linking SHANK2 to broader neuropsychiatric disease mechanisms.

**Expression pattern:**
- SHANK2 is highly expressed in the brain, with particular relevance to postsynaptic compartments of excitatory neurons. Shank2 expression is detected in the prefrontal cortex, hippocampus, striatum, and cerebellum, all regions implicated in ASD-related behaviors (PMID: 36311025; PMID: 27903723).
- Shank2 is expressed in a subpopulation of glycinergic inhibitory interneurons in the dorsal spinal cord of both mice and humans, contributing to nociceptive processing (PMID: 37316943).
- Shank2 is expressed in cochlear hair cells, where it regulates bundle architecture and is required for high-frequency hearing (PMID: 40627398).
- Shank2 is expressed in bone tissue and increases during osteoblastogenesis; patient-derived iPSC osteoblasts with LoF SHANK2 mutations show reduced differentiation (PMID: 36751416).

**Key experimental findings:**
- Heterozygous LoF mutations in SHANK2 derived from ASD patients, when introduced into human iPSC-derived neurons, cause hyperconnectivity — increased dendrite length, complexity, synapse number, and spontaneous excitatory postsynaptic current frequency — with phenotypic rescue upon gene correction (PMID: 30911184). This is a particularly strong piece of evidence directly linking patient-derived variants to a neuronal cellular phenotype.
- The patient-derived SHANK2B Y29X mutation impairs dopaminergic neuron differentiation from iPSCs and reduces dopamine release in the ventral tegmental area of knock-in mice, with rescue by genetic correction (PMID: 38704506).
- Functional studies of de novo and rare inherited SHANK2 variants in heterologous systems demonstrate impaired dendritic spine enlargement (ASHG 2010 – Berkel et al.) and reduced synapse density at dendrites (ASHG 2011 – Leblond et al.), providing mechanistic support for pathogenicity.
- Transcriptomic analysis reveals gene dosage-dependent effects: heterozygous Shank2 mutation in prefrontal cortex increases synaptic gene expression (a reverse-ASD pattern), while homozygous mutation decreases it (ASD-like pattern), suggesting that haploinsufficiency and complete loss have distinct molecular consequences (PMID: 36311025).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
Convergent evidence from human iPSC-derived neurons, patient-derived cell lines, CRISPR models, protein interaction studies, and transcriptomic analyses robustly supports SHANK2's role in synaptic organization and neuronal development. Direct rescue of cellular phenotypes by gene correction in human neurons (PMID: 30911184; PMID: 38704506) is particularly compelling.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths:**
1. *Convergent multi-system evidence:* De novo LoF variants and deletions in SHANK2 have been independently identified in ASD and intellectual disability patients by multiple research groups across different countries and methodologies (sequencing, array-CGH, WES), with a consistent core phenotype of ASD, intellectual disability, and speech delay.
2. *Robust model organism validation:* Multiple independently generated mouse lines and a zebrafish model consistently reproduce ASD-relevant behavioral phenotypes (social deficits, repetitive behaviors, communication abnormalities, hyperactivity), with no lethality, directly paralleling the human condition.
3. *Human cellular rescue experiments:* Patient-derived iPSC neuronal models carrying SHANK2 LoF mutations exhibit measurable synaptic and differentiation phenotypes that are rescued by genetic correction (PMID: 30911184; PMID: 38704506), providing the highest level of functional validation.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- Total patient numbers with well-characterized, individually documented de novo LoF variants remain modest (estimated <50 across all studies), and many reports lack precise variant-level notation.
- The pathogenicity of inherited missense variants is less certain; a "multiple hit" model has been proposed for some, and similar variants are occasionally found in controls (ASHG 2011 – Leblond et al.; ASHG 2018 – Rosan et al.).
- Several key reports are conference abstracts (ASHG 2010, 2011, 2013, 2015, 2017, 2018, 2022) rather than peer-reviewed publications, limiting the depth of available methodological detail.
- No patients are reported from large, well-characterized diagnostic cohorts (UDN, DDD, GEL), which would strengthen genotype-phenotype correlation.

**Conclusion:** SHANK2 is a well-supported ASD and neurodevelopmental disorder gene, with the strongest evidence for heterozygous de novo loss-of-function variants (truncating mutations and deletions) acting through haploinsufficiency. The convergence of clinical genetics, multiple independent animal models, and human iPSC-based functional studies — including direct phenotypic rescue by gene correction — establishes a compelling and coherent gene-disease relationship. Expansion of patient cohorts with precise variant documentation and inclusion in large-scale diagnostic sequencing programs would further consolidate the evidence base and refine genotype-phenotype correlations, particularly for inherited missense variants.
