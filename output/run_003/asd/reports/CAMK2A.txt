# Literature Evidence Report: CAMK2A as an Autism Spectrum Disorder / Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene (Autosomal Dominant, Heterozygous)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

CAMK2A (Calcium/Calmodulin-Dependent Protein Kinase II Alpha) encodes the alpha isoform of CaMKII, a serine/threonine kinase that is among the most abundant proteins in the postsynaptic density of excitatory neurons. It plays a central role in synaptic plasticity, learning, and memory by transducing calcium signals—primarily through autophosphorylation at Thr286—into long-lasting changes in synaptic strength, including the regulation of AMPA receptor trafficking, dendritic spine morphology, and long-term potentiation (LTP).

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports and patient totals:**
Across the structured evidence reviewed, there are at least seven independent publications and two conference abstracts (ASHG 2017, ASHG 2018) reporting heterozygous de novo CAMK2A variants in individuals with neurodevelopmental disorders. The total number of reported patients with CAMK2A variants is approximately 21–25 unique individuals when accounting for overlap between the published paper (PMID: 29100089) and the ASHG 2017 abstract, plus additional cases from PMID: 29560374, PMID: 28130356, PMID: 37510258, PMID: 40140673, PMID: 38835877, and PMID: 23695276. One additional de novo CAMK2A variant was identified in an ASD trio-sequencing cohort from India (ASHG 2023).

**Most common phenotypes:**
The predominant phenotype across all reports is intellectual disability (ID) / global developmental delay, present in the vast majority of cases. Seizures or epileptic encephalopathy are reported in a substantial subset (PMID: 29560374; PMID: 37510258; PMID: 38835877). Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is explicitly reported in at least two independent cases: a single proband with a de novo p.Glu183Val (E183V) variant (PMID: 28130356) and a de novo SNV identified in an Indian ASD cohort (ASHG 2023). Speech delay and psychomotor delay are consistently noted as associated features. One large deletion case presented with Treacher Collins syndrome plus ID, with CAMK2A haploinsufficiency proposed as the contributor to the cognitive phenotype (PMID: 23695276).

**Predominant variant type:**
The majority of pathogenic variants are heterozygous de novo missense variants, predominantly affecting the kinase domain or autoregulatory domain. Loss-of-function variants (splice site, in-frame deletion) are also represented. Notably, both gain-of-function (e.g., p.Pro212Leu causing hyperactivation; PMID: 40140673) and loss-of-function/dominant-negative mechanisms (e.g., p.Glu183Val; PMID: 28130356) have been demonstrated, indicating that precise regulation of CaMKIIα activity is critical and that perturbation in either direction is pathogenic.

**Cohort origin:**
Patients are drawn from multi-center international collaborations (PMID: 29100089; ASHG 2017), Japanese multi-center studies (PMID: 29560374; ASHG 2018), and individual case reports from Italy (PMID: 37510258), France (PMID: 23695276), and the USA (PMID: 28130356). None are explicitly identified as originating from named cohorts such as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL, though the multi-center nature of the largest studies suggests overlap with large-scale sequencing programs.

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**
The evidence is strengthened by recurrence of de novo variants across multiple independent international studies, functional validation in several cases, and phenotypic consistency. However, the total number of patients with ASD specifically (as opposed to ID/NDD broadly) remains small (approximately 2–3 individuals), and the gene is more robustly established for ID/NDD than for ASD as a primary diagnosis. The overall clinical evidence for CAMK2A as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene is strong, but evidence specifically for ASD is moderate.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:**
The primary model organism is the mouse (Mus musculus), with additional functional validation in C. elegans (PMID: 29784083, biological evidence section).

**Key phenotypes from mouse models:**

- *Full Camk2a knockout mice* exhibit profound spatial and conditional learning deficits and impaired hippocampal LTP, phenotypes that are fully rescued by adult re-expression of CAMK2A, demonstrating a direct and reversible role in cognitive function (PMID: 36304100).
- *Heterozygous Camk2a knockout mice* display deficits in both recent and remote spatial memory and an immature dentate gyrus (iDG) phenotype (PMID: 40847814), directly relevant to haploinsufficiency as a disease mechanism.
- *Camk2a/Camk2b double knockout* results in perinatal lethality, while single Camk2a knockout mice are viable and fertile, consistent with autosomal dominant heterozygous pathogenicity in humans (PMID: 31064859).
- *Knock-in mice carrying the human ID-associated p.Pro212Leu variant* show increased CaMKIIα autophosphorylation, dendritic spine abnormalities, exaggerated hippocampal LTP, and learning and memory impairments, directly recapitulating the human neurodevelopmental phenotype (PMID: 40140673).
- *Camk2a knockout mice* also show reduced basal sleep and impaired sleep homeostasis (PMID: 39757286), a feature sometimes observed in ASD/NDD.
- *Heterozygous Camk2a knockout mice* display infradian locomotor oscillations rescued by carbamazepine, suggesting relevance to neuropsychiatric phenotypes (PMID: 31150948).

**Consistency with human phenotype:**
The mouse model data are highly consistent with the human phenotype. Learning and memory deficits, synaptic plasticity abnormalities, and dendritic spine pathology in mice mirror the intellectual disability, developmental delay, and synaptic dysfunction observed in human patients. The viability of heterozygous knockouts supports an autosomal dominant mechanism without embryonic lethality.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
CAMK2A encodes a central node in calcium-dependent neuronal signaling. Upon calcium/calmodulin binding, CaMKIIα undergoes autophosphorylation at Thr286, converting it to a calcium-independent autonomous state. This molecular switch is critical for LTP induction, AMPA receptor (GluA1) phosphorylation and trafficking, and synaptic strengthening. Downstream targets include GluA1, CREB, and multiple postsynaptic density proteins including Shank3, L-type calcium channels, and NMDA receptor subunits (PMID: 28130356). Dysregulation of Thr286 autophosphorylation—in either direction—disrupts neuronal migration and synaptic function (PMID: 29100089).

**Expression pattern:**
CAMK2A is highly expressed in mature excitatory glutamatergic neurons of the brain, particularly in the hippocampus and cortex. A CAMK2A::GFP reporter marks the most differentiated excitatory neurons in human iPSC-derived cultures (PMID: 29791859), confirming its role as a marker and functional mediator of mature neuronal identity. In vivo, plasma CaMK2A levels positively correlate with cognitive ability in adolescents, and elevated CaMK2A increases autophosphorylation and upregulates GluA1 (PMID: 34607601).

**Key experimental findings:**
- De novo CAMK2A variants in ID patients alter Thr286 autophosphorylation and disrupt neuronal migration in vitro (PMID: 29100089).
- The p.Glu183Val (E183V) ASD-associated variant causes loss of substrate phosphorylation, dominant-negative suppression of wild-type CaMKIIα, reduced dendritic spine density, increased dendritic arborization, decreased excitatory synaptic transmission, and ASD-related behaviors in knock-in mice (PMID: 28130356).
- The p.His477Tyr homozygous variant (recessive case) impairs self-oligomerization and fails to rescue neuronal defects in C. elegans unc-43 mutants, with patient iPSC neurons showing profound synaptic defects (PMID: 29784083).
- Gain-of-function variants (e.g., p.Pro212Leu) cause hyperactivation of CaMKIIα, exaggerated LTP, and learning impairments in mice (PMID: 40140673).
- Splice site variants causing in-frame deletion of the regulatory segment increase Thr286 phosphorylation and A-type K+ currents in hippocampal neurons (PMID: 29560374).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE (for ASD specifically) / STRONG (for CAMK2A-related NDD broadly)**

**Key strengths:**
- Multiple independent de novo variants identified across international cohorts, with strong recurrence in ID/NDD.
- Robust functional validation across multiple variant types and mechanisms (gain-of-function, loss-of-function, dominant-negative), all converging on disruption of CaMKIIα autophosphorylation and synaptic function.
- Highly consistent mouse model phenotypes, including a human variant knock-in model directly recapitulating the neurodevelopmental phenotype.
- CAMK2A is listed in OMIM as the causative gene for Intellectual Developmental Disorder, Autosomal Dominant 53 (OMIM #617798), providing formal disease gene recognition.
- At least two independent ASD cases with de novo CAMK2A variants, with one extensively characterized at the molecular, cellular, and behavioral level (PMID: 28130356).

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- The number of patients with ASD as the primary diagnosis is small (approximately 2–3), limiting the strength of ASD-specific evidence relative to the broader ID/NDD phenotype.
- No large-scale ASD cohort study (e.g., SPARK, SSC) has been cited explicitly identifying CAMK2A as a recurrently mutated ASD gene, and the gene does not appear in the most commonly cited high-confidence ASD gene lists.
- Phenotypic overlap between ASD and ID/NDD makes it difficult to determine whether ASD is a distinct presentation or a feature of a broader neurodevelopmental syndrome caused by CAMK2A variants.

**Conclusion:**
CAMK2A is a well-established neurodevelopmental disorder gene with strong evidence for autosomal dominant pathogenicity via heterozygous de novo variants, supported by consistent clinical, functional, and animal model data. ASD is a recognized phenotypic feature of CAMK2A-related disorders, with at least one deeply characterized case demonstrating molecular and behavioral recapitulation of ASD in a knock-in mouse model (PMID: 28130356); however, the ASD-specific evidence base remains limited in patient numbers. The gene should be considered a moderate-confidence ASD gene and a high-confidence ID/NDD gene, with the caveat that ASD features likely occur within the spectrum of a broader CAMK2A-related neurodevelopmental syndrome.
