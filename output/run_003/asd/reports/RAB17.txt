# RAB17 — Evidence Report for Autism Spectrum Disorder (Autosomal Dominant)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

RAB17 encodes a member of the RAS superfamily of small GTPases that functions as a regulator of vesicular trafficking, specifically controlling cargo sorting through recycling endosomes and mediating transcytosis in polarized epithelial cells. It has additionally been implicated in dendritic membrane trafficking in neurons, antibacterial autophagy, and tumor suppression across multiple tissue contexts.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
RAB17 is a small GTPase that regulates endosomal membrane trafficking, particularly through recycling endosomes. It has been shown to mediate basolateral-to-apical transcytosis of the polymeric immunoglobulin receptor in polarized epithelial cells (PMID: 9624171), to regulate maturation of nascent endosomes in a manner dependent on its GEF RABGEF1 and interacting partner ALS2 (PMID: 31959474), and to direct apoptotic cell-derived antigens through recycling endosomes during efferocytosis in macrophages (PMID: 28005073). RAB17 also contributes membrane supply from recycling endosomes to autophagosomes during antibacterial autophagy against Group A Streptococcus (PMID: 25052408). In cancer biology, RAB17 acts as a tumor suppressor in hepatocellular carcinoma partly through suppression of ERK pathway activity (PMID: 25707355), while ERK2 reciprocally suppresses RAB17 expression to promote breast cancer cell invasion (PMID: 22328529). In endometrial cancer, RAB17 is paradoxically overexpressed and promotes tumor survival by suppressing TFRC-dependent ferroptosis (PMID: 39242574).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**
The original characterization of RAB17 described its mRNA as restricted to epithelial organs — kidney, liver, and intestine — with expression absent from non-epithelial tissues and fibroblasts, and induced during mesenchymal-to-epithelial differentiation in the developing kidney (PMID: 8486736). However, one cell-culture study in mouse hippocampal neurons demonstrated that Rab17 localizes specifically to dendritic growth cones, shafts, filopodia, and mature spines, and that shRNA-mediated knockdown reduces dendrite growth, branching, and dendritic spine number (PMID: 22291024). This finding provides a degree of neurobiological plausibility, as dendritic spine morphogenesis is a process frequently implicated in ASD pathophysiology. Additionally, RAB17 was identified as a potential regulator of angiogenesis in diabetic wound healing through MAPK/ERK signaling (PMID: 37605780), further illustrating its broad tissue roles.

**Key experimental findings relevant to neurodevelopment:**
The most directly relevant finding is the hippocampal neuron knockdown study (PMID: 22291024), which showed that Rab17 is dendrite-specific and required for normal dendritic arborization and spine formation in vitro. This is a cell-culture observation without in vivo validation or genetic disease association.

**Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED**

While there is a mechanistically coherent role for RAB17 in dendritic trafficking that is conceptually relevant to neurodevelopmental disorders, the evidence is restricted to a single in vitro neuronal study. The broader body of RAB17 biology is centered on epithelial transcytosis, cancer biology, and immune function, with no established connection to ASD-relevant pathways at the systems or organismal level.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

- **Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
- RAB17 has a demonstrated role in dendritic morphogenesis and spine formation in cultured hippocampal neurons (PMID: 22291024), providing a biologically plausible mechanism by which loss of RAB17 function could theoretically affect synaptic development.
- The gene is well-characterized at the molecular level, with known interactors (ALS2, RABGEF1, Rab11) and defined roles in endosomal recycling pathways.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- There is a complete absence of clinical evidence: no human patients with ASD or any neurodevelopmental disorder have been reported to carry pathogenic variants in RAB17.
- No model organism data exist to support a neurological or behavioral phenotype upon Rab17 disruption in vivo.
- The neuronal expression and function data derive from a single in vitro study; the original expression characterization described RAB17 as epithelial-specific, raising questions about its quantitative importance in the brain.
- There is no genetic evidence from large ASD cohort studies (e.g., SPARK, SSC, DDD) implicating RAB17.

**Conclusion:**
The current body of evidence is insufficient to support RAB17 as an ASD gene with autosomal dominant pathogenicity. Although one in vitro study suggests a role for Rab17 in dendritic development that is conceptually relevant to ASD, this finding has not been validated in vivo, and there are no human genetic data linking RAB17 variants to ASD or any related neurodevelopmental phenotype. RAB17 should not be considered a candidate ASD gene without substantial additional clinical and functional evidence.

6. ERK2 drives tumour cell migration in three-dimensional microenvironments by suppressing expression of Rab17 and liprin-β2. (PMID: 22328529)
7. RAB17 promotes endometrial cancer progression by inhibiting TFRC-dependent ferroptosis. (PMID: 39242574)
8. Rab17, a novel small GTPase, is specific for epithelial cells and is induced during cell polarization. (PMID: 8486736)
9. Small GTPase Rab17 regulates dendritic morphogenesis and postsynaptic development of hippocampal neurons. (PMID: 22291024)
10. Single-cell RNA-seq and bulk-seq identify RAB17 as a potential regulator of angiogenesis by human dermal microvascular endothelial cells in diabetic foot ulcers. (PMID: 37605780)
