# Literature Evidence Report: GABRA1 as an Epilepsy/Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene with Heterozygous (Autosomal Dominant) Pathogenic Variants

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

GABRA1 encodes the α1 subunit of the type A γ-aminobutyric acid receptor (GABA-A receptor), the principal mediator of fast inhibitory neurotransmission in the central nervous system. The α1 subunit is the most abundantly expressed GABA-A receptor subunit in the mature brain and plays a critical role in synaptic inhibition, neuronal excitability, and neurodevelopment.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports/series:** At least 10 independent publications or conference reports were identified, ranging from large multi-center cohort studies to single case reports and conference abstracts.

**Total approximate number of patients:** Across all identified studies, approximately 50+ patients with GABRA1 variants have been reported, with the two largest cohort studies contributing 27 (PMID: 37606373) and 15 (PMID: 35718920) patients, respectively.

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
The predominant phenotype across all studies is epilepsy, spanning a broad severity spectrum. Milder presentations include idiopathic generalized epilepsy, fever-related epilepsy, and focal seizures with mild cognitive impairment (PMID: 37606373; PMID: 35718920; ASHG 2009 - Novel mutations found by resequencing multiple subunits of GABAA receptor). Severe presentations include early infantile developmental and epileptic encephalopathy (DEE), Dravet syndrome (SCN1A-negative), epilepsy of infancy with migrating focal seizures (EIMFS), infantile spasms, and Lennox-Gastaut syndrome (PMID: 37606373; PMID: 31618474; PMID: 23934111; ASHG 2011 - Whole exome sequencing in SCN1A-negative Dravet syndrome; ASHG 2013 - Targeted resequencing of 101 known and candidate epilepsy genes). A single patient with a contiguous microdeletion of GABRA1 and GABRG2 presented with intractable epilepsy combined with blindness due to bilateral optic nerve atrophy (PMID: 37703949). A clear genotype-phenotype correlation has emerged: variants in the extracellular domain and small transmembrane loops tend to cause loss-of-function (LoF) effects associated with milder epilepsy, while variants in pore-forming transmembrane helices cause gain-of-function (GoF) or LoF effects associated with severe early-onset DEE (PMID: 37606373; PMID: 35718920).

**Predominant variant type:**
The vast majority of reported variants are heterozygous missense mutations, consistent with an autosomal dominant mechanism. De novo occurrence has been confirmed in multiple cases (PMID: 23934111; PMID: 31618474; ASHG 2011; ASHG 2015). A small number of LoF variants (frameshift/insertion) and one contiguous gene deletion have also been reported (ASHG 2009; PMID: 37703949). Functional studies have demonstrated both GoF and LoF molecular mechanisms depending on variant location (PMID: 37606373; PMID: 39642202).

**Cohort origin:**
Patients were identified through multiple international and national collaborative efforts, including the French EPIGENE network (PMID: 35718920), an international EIMFS collaborative study (PMID: 31618474), the Epi25 Consortium (ASHG 2018 - Feng et al.), and multi-center exome sequencing studies (PMID: 23934111). No patients were specifically noted as originating from UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL cohorts in the available data.

**Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent cohorts across different countries, functional validation of variants, consistent genotype-phenotype correlations, de novo occurrence confirmed in multiple cases, and large-scale burden analysis (OR=21.5, p=3×10⁻⁴ in 4,443 GGE patients vs. 17,668 controls; ASHG 2018 - Feng et al.) collectively provide strong clinical evidence.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Both zebrafish (*Danio rerio*) and mouse (*Mus musculus*) models have been employed.

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

*Zebrafish:* A germline homozygous loss-of-function allele (sa43718) of *gabra1* in zebrafish produced light-induced hypermotility consistent with seizure-like behavior, decreased expression of paralogous GABA-A receptor subunits (*gabra2*, *gabra3*, *gabra5*), and proteomic abnormalities in proteins regulating synaptic vesicle fusion, vesicle transport, synapse development, and mitochondrial complexes (PMID: 38908344). A morpholino-based knockdown model similarly showed hypoactivity and defects in GABA-A receptor subunit expression, with partial rescue by wild-type human GABRA1 but not by a pathogenic variant (c.875C>T), providing direct functional validation (PMID: 32205311). Seizure-like hyperactivity in gabra1-null zebrafish was also suppressed by cannabidiol (CBD) and Δ-9-tetrahydrocannabinol (THC), individually and synergistically (PMID: 30949046).

*Mouse:* A heterozygous double-knockout mouse model (Gabra1+/-/Gabrg2+/-) recapitulating the human contiguous gene deletion phenotype exhibited spontaneous seizures, reduced GABA-A receptor subunit expression across multiple brain regions, reduced electroretinography oscillatory potentials, and optic nerve degeneration with impaired axonal myelination — closely mirroring the human patient's phenotype of intractable epilepsy and blindness (PMID: 37703949). A knock-in mouse model (Gabra1em1) with altered palmitoylation homeostasis showed enhanced inhibitory synaptic transmission, disrupted hippocampal oscillatory phase coupling, and impaired learning and memory (PMID: 39695089). Conditional knockout studies in specific neuronal populations (petrosal neurons, dorsal motor nucleus of vagus) demonstrated roles for GABRA1 in sickness behavior and metabolic regulation, though these are less directly relevant to the epilepsy phenotype (PMID: 36890237; PMID: 39261733).

**Consistency with human phenotype:**
The zebrafish and mouse models are highly consistent with the human epilepsy phenotype. Seizure-like behavior, abnormal inhibitory synaptic function, and neurodevelopmental abnormalities are recapitulated across species. The mouse model also reproduced the rare combined epilepsy-blindness phenotype observed in the human microdeletion case (PMID: 37703949).

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent model systems (zebrafish full knockout, zebrafish knockdown with human rescue experiment, mouse heterozygous knockout, mouse knock-in) consistently demonstrate neurological phenotypes relevant to the human disease, with direct functional rescue experiments strengthening causal inference.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
GABRA1 encodes the α1 subunit of the pentameric GABA-A receptor, the primary ionotropic receptor mediating fast inhibitory neurotransmission via chloride ion conductance. The α1 subunit is the dominant subunit in mature synaptic GABA-A receptors and is critical for receptor assembly, trafficking, and gating. Functional studies have established that pathogenic GABRA1 variants can cause either LoF (reduced surface expression, impaired gating) or GoF (increased GABA sensitivity, enhanced receptor activation) depending on their structural location within the protein (PMID: 37606373; PMID: 39642202). Specifically, variants at the conserved M1 proline residue (e.g., α1P260L, α1P260S) increase GABA sensitivity 3- to 23-fold, confirmed by electrophysiology and molecular dynamics simulations (PMID: 39642202). GABRA1 is also subject to post-translational regulation via palmitoylation by PPT1, which controls synaptic transmission and circuit oscillation (PMID: 39695089).

**Expression pattern:**
GABRA1 is broadly expressed in the central nervous system, with particularly high expression in mature cortical and hippocampal neurons, consistent with its role in epilepsy. Expression is also noted in specific brainstem neuronal populations (petrosal ganglia, dorsal motor nucleus of vagus) relevant to autonomic and sensory functions (PMID: 36890237; PMID: 39261733). In disease contexts, GABRA1 expression is downregulated in somatostatin-expressing interneurons in tuberous sclerosis complex brain tissue (PMID: 38714540) and in the hippocampus in Alzheimer's disease models (PMID: 39752101), suggesting broader relevance to neurological disease beyond epilepsy.

**Key experimental findings:**
- Large-scale WES burden analysis in the Epi25 Consortium identified GABRA1 as one of the strongest genetic risk genes for genetic generalized epilepsy (OR=21.5, p=3×10⁻⁴; ASHG 2018 - Feng et al.).
- Functional analysis of 19 variants in 27 patients established a structural framework linking variant location to molecular mechanism (GoF vs. LoF) and clinical severity (PMID: 37606373).
- Human GABRA1 rescue of zebrafish knockdown phenotype, with failure of rescue by a pathogenic variant, provides direct in vivo functional evidence (PMID: 32205311).
- GABRA1 has been identified as a downstream target of miR-139-5p in glioma, suggesting additional roles in cell proliferation pathways (PMID: 34001135), though this is not directly relevant to the epilepsy/neurodevelopmental phenotype.

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
Convergent evidence from electrophysiology, molecular dynamics, proteomics, in vivo rescue experiments, and large-scale genetic burden analysis robustly supports GABRA1 as a functionally critical gene whose disruption causes neurological disease through well-characterized molecular mechanisms.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths:**
- Multiple independent cohorts totaling >50 patients with heterozygous GABRA1 variants and epilepsy/neurodevelopmental phenotypes, including confirmed de novo mutations.
- Highly significant genetic burden in large-scale WES studies (OR=21.5 for GGE; ASHG 2018 - Feng et al.).
- Robust functional characterization of variants demonstrating GoF and LoF mechanisms with clear genotype-phenotype correlations (PMID: 37606373; PMID: 39642202).
- Consistent neurological phenotypes (seizures, inhibitory synaptic dysfunction) across zebrafish and mouse models, with direct human gene rescue experiments (PMID: 32205311; PMID: 37703949).

**Key gaps or limitations:**
- The available evidence is overwhelmingly focused on epilepsy and developmental epileptic encephalopathy; the specific question of autism spectrum disorder (ASD) as a primary phenotype is not well-supported by the current literature, with no studies in this dataset specifically reporting ASD as a primary outcome for GABRA1 variants. Cognitive impairment and neurodevelopmental delay are reported as comorbidities in severe cases, but ASD per se is not highlighted.
- Specific variant-level data (HGVS notation) are unavailable for many reported cases, limiting precise genotype-phenotype analysis.
- Most conference abstracts (ASHG 2009, 2011, 2013, 2015) lack full peer-reviewed publication details, and patient overlap between studies cannot be excluded.

**Conclusion:**
GABRA1 is a well-established, strongly supported autosomal dominant epilepsy and neurodevelopmental disorder gene, with robust clinical, functional, and model organism evidence linking heterozygous pathogenic variants — predominantly de novo missense mutations — to a spectrum of epilepsy phenotypes ranging from idiopathic generalized epilepsy to severe early infantile DEE. However, the current evidence base does not specifically support GABRA1 as an autism spectrum disorder gene; the neurodevelopmental features reported are primarily epilepsy-related encephalopathy rather than ASD. Further prospective studies with systematic ASD phenotyping in GABRA1 variant carriers would be required to evaluate this specific association.
