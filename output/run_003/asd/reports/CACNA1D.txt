# Literature Evidence Report: CACNA1D as an Autism Spectrum Disorder Gene (Autosomal Dominant / Heterozygous Pathogenic Variants)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

CACNA1D encodes the pore-forming α1 subunit (Cav1.3) of L-type voltage-gated calcium channels, which are widely expressed in the brain, inner ear, heart, and adrenal gland. Cav1.3 channels play critical roles in neuronal excitability, pacemaking, synaptic transmission, auditory hair cell function, and excitability-transcription coupling, making them essential for normal neurodevelopment and sensory processing.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports and patient counts:**
Across the structured evidence reviewed, at least seven independent publications or conference reports describe patients with pathogenic CACNA1D variants, encompassing an approximate total of 21 individually described patients (with additional patients in cohort-level analyses). These include: two patients with the p.G1169D variant (PMID: 39246741); twelve patients across nine distinct variants in a dedicated case series (PMID: 32583268); one patient with p.F747S (PMID: 36208199); two twins with p.S652L identified through the Deciphering Developmental Disorders (DDD) cohort (PMID: 31921405); two patients with p.A749G and p.G407R (PMID: 25620733); three patients with p.S652L/S672L in a case report and literature review (PMID: 37122292); and one patient with p.L271H studied via iPSC modeling (PMID: 41513972). Additional conference-level data implicate CACNA1D in ASD cohorts of over 4,000 probands (ASHG 2013) and in a whole-exome sequencing study of 3,871 ASD cases (ASHG 2014).

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
Across all reports, the most consistently observed phenotypes are autism spectrum disorder (ASD), intellectual disability/developmental delay, and epilepsy/seizures. Additional recurrent features include primary aldosteronism, hyperinsulinemic hypoglycemia, sensorineural deafness, hypotonia, and congenital tremor/jittering. Psychiatric features including psychosis, bipolar disorder, and major depressive disorder have also been reported in at least one family (PMID: 37122292). The phenotypic spectrum is broad, and expressivity is variable — one putative mosaic carrier of p.G1169D had a notably milder neurodevelopmental phenotype compared to the fully heterozygous proband (PMID: 39246741).

**Predominant variant type:**
The overwhelming majority of reported pathogenic variants are heterozygous de novo missense variants. All functionally characterized variants have demonstrated gain-of-function (GoF) effects via in vitro electrophysiology, including hyperpolarized shifts in channel activation and/or inactivation, slowed inactivation and deactivation kinetics, and increased window currents — collectively resulting in enhanced Ca²⁺ influx. No loss-of-function variants have been causally linked to ASD or the neurodevelopmental syndrome; loss-of-function variants in CACNA1D are instead associated with deafness and bradycardia (PMID: 36592224). One conference abstract (ASHG 2015) reported a LoF variant (p.C1471*) in a Japanese ASD/schizophrenia cohort, but this remains unconfirmed at the functional level.

**Known cohort representation:**
At least two patients (twins with p.S652L) were identified through the Deciphering Developmental Disorders (DDD) cohort (PMID: 31921405). Additional patients were identified through large-scale ASD exome sequencing efforts (ASHG 2013; ASHG 2014). No patients from UDN, GREGoR, RUMC, or GEL cohorts were explicitly identified in the reviewed evidence.

**Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent de novo missense variants have been identified in unrelated individuals with overlapping neurodevelopmental phenotypes including ASD, intellectual disability, and epilepsy. Functional validation has been performed for the majority of reported variants, and recurrence of specific variants (e.g., p.S652L in multiple unrelated families) further strengthens the causal inference.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:**
CACNA1D/Cacna1d has been studied in mouse (multiple full knockout, conditional knockout, and knock-in models) and zebrafish (heterozygous splice-variant knockout). Rat models have also been used for blood pressure and cardiac phenotype studies.

**Key neurological and neurodevelopmental phenotypes:**
- In full Cacna1d knockout mice, severe impairments in hippocampal dentate gyrus neurogenesis were observed, including a significantly smaller dentate gyrus, reduced survival of newly generated cells, and impaired hippocampus-dependent spatial memory (PMID: 26459417). These findings are directly relevant to the intellectual disability and learning impairments seen in human patients.
- Full knockout mice also showed deficits in motor learning (rotarod and Erasmus Ladder tasks) despite normal baseline locomotion, and impaired associative learning (PMID: 35044095).
- Heterozygous zebrafish carrying a splice-variant mutation in cacna1d displayed hyperlocomotion and psychosis-like behaviors (altered locomotor responses to light transitions and startle stimuli) that were reversed by antipsychotics risperidone and haloperidol (PMID: 33057948). Homozygous fish showed low viability, consistent with the essential role of the channel. These behavioral phenotypes are broadly consistent with the neuropsychiatric features (ASD, hyperactivity, psychosis) observed in human patients.
- A patient-derived iPSC model of the p.L271H variant demonstrated increased spontaneous calcium transients in neural progenitor cells, depolarized resting membrane potential, accelerated neuronal differentiation, and impaired cortical organoid organization (PMID: 41513972), providing a human cellular correlate to the mouse neurogenesis findings.
- Conditional cochlear knockout mice recapitulated the profound hearing loss and inner hair cell differentiation arrest seen in systemic knockouts (PMID: 31178698), consistent with sensorineural deafness reported in some human patients.

**Lethality and Mendelian ratio:**
Full knockout mice are viable and fertile. Homozygous zebrafish show sub-Mendelian viability, suggesting that complete loss of function is poorly tolerated in this organism. No Mendelian ratio deviations were reported for heterozygous mouse models.

**Consistency with human phenotype:**
The model organism data are broadly consistent with the human neurodevelopmental phenotype. Loss-of-function models recapitulate cognitive, learning, and neurogenesis deficits; zebrafish heterozygous models recapitulate hyperactivity and psychosis-like behaviors; and the hearing loss phenotype is well-modeled in conditional cochlear knockouts. However, it should be noted that the human disease is caused by gain-of-function variants, whereas most animal models employ loss-of-function approaches — the zebrafish heterozygous model and the iPSC model are therefore the most directly translatable to the human GoF disease mechanism.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent model systems across two species demonstrate neurological, cognitive, and behavioral phenotypes consistent with the human neurodevelopmental syndrome. The iPSC model provides direct human cellular evidence for the GoF mechanism.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
CACNA1D encodes the Cav1.3 L-type voltage-gated calcium channel α1 subunit. Cav1.3 channels activate at relatively hyperpolarized membrane potentials compared to other L-type channels (Cav1.2), making them particularly important for neuronal pacemaking, excitability-transcription coupling, and synaptic transmission. In the inner ear, Cav1.3 is essential for inner hair cell ribbon synapse function and auditory signal encoding (PMID: 39718549). In the adrenal gland, somatic gain-of-function mutations in CACNA1D are a well-established cause of aldosterone-producing adenomas, implicating the CaMK/calcineurin/NFAT pathway in aldosterone synthase upregulation (PMID: 31695023). This somatic mutation database has been leveraged to predict pathogenicity of germline variants (PMID: 32583268).

**Expression pattern:**
CACNA1D is broadly expressed in the brain, with particular relevance to neurons involved in pacemaking (e.g., dopaminergic neurons of the substantia nigra, hippocampal neurons), as well as in the cochlea, adrenal cortex, heart, and pancreatic β-cells. Its expression in neural stem cells (Nestin+) and mature granular neurons of the dentate gyrus (PMID: 26459417) is directly relevant to its role in hippocampal neurogenesis and cognitive function. Transcriptomic analyses of patient-derived iPSCs showed dysregulation of CNS development and neurogenesis gene networks (PMID: 41513972), further supporting neurodevelopmental relevance.

**Key experimental findings:**
- Patch-clamp electrophysiology across multiple independent laboratories has consistently demonstrated that ASD/neurodevelopmental disease-associated de novo CACNA1D variants cause gain-of-function gating changes: hyperpolarized activation shifts (up to −28 mV for p.F747S; PMID: 36208199), slowed inactivation and deactivation, increased window currents, and enhanced Ca²⁺ influx during action potential-like stimulations (PMID: 31921405; PMID: 25620733; PMID: 39246741).
- In vitro sensitivity to dihydropyridine Ca²⁺ channel blockers (isradipine) has been demonstrated for several variants, suggesting a potential therapeutic avenue, though clinical benefit has not yet been established (PMID: 39246741; PMID: 36592224).
- Evolutionary co-variation analysis has been validated as a tool to predict pathogenicity of novel CACNA1D variants, with electrophysiological confirmation (PMID: 38553610).
- GWAS data from ~59,900 African American participants identified CACNA1D as a genome-wide significant locus for diastolic blood pressure (p = 1.26×10⁻¹²; ASHG 2017), consistent with its role in vascular smooth muscle and adrenal function.

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
The molecular mechanism (GoF Ca²⁺ influx via gating changes) has been rigorously and reproducibly demonstrated across multiple variants, multiple laboratories, and multiple expression systems. The pathway from enhanced Ca²⁺ influx to neuronal hyperexcitability, altered neurogenesis, and downstream transcriptional dysregulation is mechanistically coherent and supported by both in vitro and in vivo data.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths:**
1. Multiple independent de novo heterozygous missense variants have been identified in unrelated individuals with overlapping ASD, intellectual disability, and epilepsy phenotypes, with functional validation (patch-clamp electrophysiology) demonstrating a consistent gain-of-function mechanism for each characterized variant.
2. The biological mechanism is well-defined and reproducible: GoF gating changes lead to enhanced Ca²⁺ influx, neuronal hyperexcitability, and downstream neurodevelopmental disruption, supported by cell culture, iPSC, mouse, and zebrafish models.
3. The gene has been independently implicated in large-scale ASD exome sequencing cohorts (ASHG 2013; ASHG 2014), and specific variants have been identified recurrently in unrelated families (e.g., p.S652L), further strengthening causal inference.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. The total number of individually described patients remains relatively small (~21 across all reports), and most studies are case reports or small series rather than large prospective cohorts. Larger systematic registries would strengthen genotype-phenotype correlations.
2. All functionally characterized pathogenic variants are gain-of-function missense variants; the contribution of loss-of-function or other variant types to ASD risk remains uncertain, and the single LoF variant reported in a Japanese ASD cohort (ASHG 2015) lacks functional validation.
3. No randomized or prospective therapeutic trials have been reported; the potential clinical utility of dihydropyridine Ca²⁺ channel blockers (e.g., isradipine) remains exploratory.

**Conclusion:**
CACNA1D is a well-supported autosomal dominant ASD/neurodevelopmental disease gene in which heterozygous de novo gain-of-function missense variants cause a recognizable syndrome encompassing ASD, intellectual disability, epilepsy, and variable endocrine features (primary aldosteronism, hyperinsulinemic hypoglycemia). The evidence base is strong across clinical, functional, and model organism domains, with a coherent and reproducible molecular mechanism. Continued patient ascertainment through large-scale sequencing cohorts and prospective natural history studies are the primary unmet needs to further characterize this channelopathy.
