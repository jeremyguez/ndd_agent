# SOX5 Evidence Report: Autism Spectrum Disorder and Heterozygous Autosomal Dominant Pathogenic Variants

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

SOX5 (SRY-box transcription factor 5) encodes a member of the SoxD family of transcription factors, characterized by a conserved HMG-box DNA-binding domain and a coiled-coil dimerization domain (PMID: 19647094). SOX5 plays critical roles in multiple developmental processes, most notably in chondrogenesis (as part of the "SOX trio" with SOX6 and SOX9), neocortical neuron migration and differentiation, and neural circuit formation, making it an important regulator of both skeletal and central nervous system development.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent studies:** Ten independent reports or case series were identified, ranging from conference abstracts (ASHG 2009, ASHG 2010) to peer-reviewed cohort studies and case reports.

**Total approximate number of patients:** Approximately 94 patients across all studies, including 41 patients (PMID: 31578471), 20 patients (PMID: 37702321), 16 patients (PMID: 36861937), 8 patients (ASHG 2009), 4 patients (PMID: 39075495), and several single case reports (PMID: 40500800; PMID: 26111154; PMID: 36759900; PMID: 40163633; ASHG 2010).

**Most common phenotypes observed:** The core phenotype across all studies is consistent with Lamb-Shaffer syndrome (LSS), a neurodevelopmental disorder. The most frequently reported features include:
- Global developmental delay and intellectual disability (mild to severe)
- Language/speech delay (often disproportionately severe)
- Behavioral abnormalities, including autistic-like features and autism spectrum disorder
- Subtle facial dysmorphic features
- Additional variable features including scoliosis, visual problems (strabismus, hypermetropia, optic atrophy), microcephaly, and hypotonia

Autistic-like behavior and severe language delay were highlighted as cardinal features in the original ASHG 2009 series, and behavioral abnormalities were consistently noted across the UK (PMID: 36861937) and Spanish (PMID: 37702321) cohorts.

**Predominant variant type:** All three major variant classes — loss-of-function (LoF; truncating variants and splice-site variants), copy number variants/deletions (intragenic and larger chromosomal deletions), and missense variants (particularly clustering in the HMG domain) — have been reported. The mechanism is haploinsufficiency in all cases. Missense variants outside the HMG domain tend to produce milder phenotypes (PMID: 31578471). Functional validation has been performed for select variants: minigene assays confirmed aberrant splicing for c.1772-1C>A (PMID: 39075495), and in vitro transcriptional activation assays confirmed reduced activity for p.Leu590Ser (PMID: 40500800) and HMG-domain missense variants (PMID: 31578471). The majority of variants are de novo, though inheritance from an affected parent and parental gonadal mosaicism have been documented (PMID: 36861937; PMID: 39075495).

**Known cohort representation:** One study explicitly draws from the DDD (Deciphering Developmental Disorders) cohort (PMID: 36861937). The largest international series used GeneMatcher and international clinical/genetic networks (PMID: 31578471). No patients from UDN, GREGoR, RUMC, or GEL cohorts were explicitly identified in the available evidence.

**Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strong based on: (1) a large cumulative patient number (~94) across geographically diverse, independent cohorts from the UK, Spain, China, and the United States; (2) consistent core phenotype across all studies; (3) multiple variant types all converging on haploinsufficiency; (4) predominantly de novo occurrence; (5) functional validation in multiple studies; and (6) ACMG class IV/V classification applied in at least two independent series (PMID: 36861937; PMID: 37702321).

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*), zebrafish (*Danio rerio*), and *Xenopus* have all been used to study SOX5 function.

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

The most directly relevant model organism finding for the human neurodevelopmental phenotype comes from a mouse full knockout study demonstrating that SOX5 is required postmitotically for the migration, differentiation, and axonal projections of subplate and deep-layer neocortical neurons (PMID: 18840685). Sox5-null mice exhibit failed preplate partitioning, laminar inversion of early-born neurons, loss of layer 5 subcerebral axons, and misrouting of corticothalamic axons — phenotypes consistent with disrupted cortical circuit formation underlying intellectual disability and autism. SOX5 was shown to bind and repress a conserved enhancer near Fezf2, a key regulator of corticofugal neuron identity.

In the context of skeletal development, double Sox5/Sox6 knockout mice fail to develop growth plate columnar and hypertrophic zones, with impaired endochondral bone formation (PMID: 14993235), consistent with the skeletal features (scoliosis, growth parameters) occasionally observed in LSS patients.

Sox5 knockout mice are viable (PMID: 18703590), which is consistent with the autosomal dominant (haploinsufficiency) mechanism in humans rather than a recessive lethal mechanism. No Mendelian ratio deviations or infertility were reported in the available knockout studies.

In a mouse brain tumor model, Sox5 loss dramatically increased the capacity of subventricular zone cells to form glioma-like tumors, demonstrating an antiproliferative role in neural stem/progenitor cells (PMID: 28687615), further supporting SOX5's role in brain development and neural cell fate regulation.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The mouse cortical neuron migration and differentiation phenotypes are highly consistent with the human neurodevelopmental phenotype of LSS, particularly the intellectual disability, language delay, and autistic features. The viable knockout is consistent with haploinsufficiency as the human disease mechanism.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:** SOX5 is a SoxD transcription factor that functions primarily through its HMG-box DNA-binding domain and coiled-coil dimerization domain (PMID: 19647094). It acts as a context-dependent transcriptional modulator — enhancing transactivation by SOX9 in chondrocytes (forming the chondrogenic SOX trio; PMID: 27771362), while antagonizing SOX9 and other SoxE proteins in oligodendrocytes and melanocytes. In neural contexts, SOX5 controls the pace of neurogenesis and promotes neural crest generation. SOX5 can also act as a transcriptional repressor, as demonstrated by its repression of Fezf2 in cortical neurons (PMID: 18840685) and Dmrt1 in zebrafish (PMID: 15981199).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:** Integrative functional genomic analysis of human brain development identified SOX5 as converging with other established neuropsychiatric risk genes — including MEF2C, SATB2, TCF4, and TSHZ3 — within specific gene coexpression modules and distinct cell types during prenatal and postnatal brain development (PMID: 30545854). This places SOX5 within a network of transcription factors critical for human neurodevelopment and neuropsychiatric risk. Additionally, a chromosome translocation disrupting SOX5 and two SOX5 mutations were identified in patients with autism and intellectual disability, with ZBTB20 identified as a transcriptional repressor of SOX5 in mouse brain (ASHG 2013 - ZBTB20-dependent transcription regulation imbalance in autism and intellectual disability).

**Key experimental findings:**
- HMG-domain missense variants in LSS patients abolish DNA binding and transactivation in vitro, directly linking variant pathogenicity to loss of transcriptional function (PMID: 31578471).
- A large GWAS meta-analysis of suicide attempt (43,871 cases; 915,025 controls) identified a genome-wide significant locus implicating SOX5 (p < 5×10⁻⁸), suggesting broader psychiatric relevance beyond neurodevelopmental disability (PMID: 37777856).
- SOX5 regulates pancreatic beta-cell gene expression and insulin secretion, with relevance to type 2 diabetes (PMID: 28585545), though this is not directly relevant to the neurodevelopmental phenotype.
- In cancer biology, SOX5 has been implicated in oncogenic contexts (PMID: 35880568; PMID: 40274769), but these findings are not relevant to the germline haploinsufficiency mechanism in LSS.

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths:**
1. **Large, replicated clinical dataset:** Approximately 94 patients across at least 10 independent studies from multiple countries, with a consistent core neurodevelopmental phenotype (intellectual disability, language delay, autistic features) and convergent haploinsufficiency mechanism across LoF, deletion, and HMG-domain missense variants.
2. **Mechanistically coherent model organism data:** Mouse Sox5 knockout studies directly demonstrate disrupted cortical neuron migration, laminar organization, and axonal connectivity — phenotypes that mechanistically explain the human intellectual disability and autism spectrum features.
3. **Functional validation:** Multiple studies have performed in vitro and in vivo functional assays confirming that pathogenic variants reduce or abolish SOX5 transcriptional activity, and human brain transcriptomic data place SOX5 within neuropsychiatric risk gene networks.

**Key gaps or limitations:**
1. Autism spectrum disorder is a variable and not universally reported feature of LSS; it is part of a broader neurodevelopmental phenotype rather than the sole or defining manifestation, and formal ASD diagnostic criteria are not consistently applied across studies.
2. The majority of large cohort studies do not provide individual-level variant data, limiting precise genotype-phenotype correlation, and some evidence derives from conference abstracts (ASHG 2009, ASHG 2010, ASHG 2013) that have not been published as full peer-reviewed articles.
3. No patients from UDN, GREGoR, or GEL cohorts were identified, and the representation of non-European ancestries remains limited.

**Conclusion:** SOX5 haploinsufficiency is a well-established cause of Lamb-Shaffer syndrome, a neurodevelopmental disorder in which autism spectrum features, intellectual disability, and severe language delay are core manifestations, supported by strong and replicated clinical, model organism, and biological evidence. The autosomal dominant mechanism is robustly supported by the predominance of de novo variants, functional validation of pathogenic alleles, and mechanistically consistent mouse knockout phenotypes in cortical neuron development. SOX5 meets criteria for a definitive autism-associated gene in the context of a broader neurodevelopmental syndrome caused by heterozygous loss-of-function variants.
