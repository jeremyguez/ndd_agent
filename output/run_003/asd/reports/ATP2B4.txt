# Evidence Report: ATP2B4 as an Autism Spectrum Disorder Gene (Autosomal Dominant / Heterozygous)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

ATP2B4 encodes Plasma Membrane Calcium ATPase 4 (PMCA4), a P-type ATPase responsible for actively extruding calcium ions from the cytoplasm across the plasma membrane, thereby maintaining intracellular calcium homeostasis. It is ubiquitously expressed but has well-characterized roles in erythrocytes, sperm, cardiac and smooth muscle, and has emerging evidence for expression and function in neural tissues.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports:** 1 case series.

**Total patients:** 3 affected individuals from a single family.

**Phenotypes observed:** Developmental dysplasia of the hip (DDH). No autism spectrum disorder (ASD) or neurodevelopmental phenotype was reported in any affected individual.

**Variant type:** A rare heterozygous missense variant in ATP2B4 was identified by whole exome sequencing (PMID: 28327142). The specific nucleotide or amino acid change was not provided in the available abstract. The variant was predicted damaging by multiple in silico tools and segregated with affected status on a shared chromosome 1 haplotype; however, an asymptomatic mother also carried the variant, indicating incomplete penetrance.

**Cohort affiliation:** The family was ascertained in Saudi Arabia. No affiliation with recognized large-scale cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL) was reported.

**Critical note:** The single published clinical report links ATP2B4 to DDH, not to autism spectrum disorder or any neurodevelopmental condition. Furthermore, the evidence is complicated by proposed digenic inheritance (co-occurring HSPG2 variant), incomplete penetrance, and the absence of functional validation of the ATP2B4 variant itself. There are **no published clinical reports** associating heterozygous ATP2B4 variants with ASD.

**Strength of clinical evidence for ASD (autosomal dominant): NONE**

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*); pharmacological inhibition studies in mouse pancreatic acinar cells; Drosophila melanogaster (PMCA ortholog, not ATP2B4-specific knockout).

**Key phenotypes from mouse knockouts:**
- Homozygous *Atp2b4*-null mice are viable, born at Mendelian ratios, and outwardly normal in appearance (PMID: 15178683; PMID: 34215257; PMID: 33098669).
- The most prominent phenotype is **male infertility** due to failure of sperm hyperactivation, attributed to calcium overload in sperm mitochondria (PMID: 15178683).
- Erythrocyte phenotypes include reduced red blood cell size and reduced hemoglobin content (PMID: 34215257), consistent with the known role of PMCA4 in RBC calcium homeostasis.
- No neurological, behavioral, or neurodevelopmental phenotypes have been reported in *Atp2b4* knockout mice across any of the studies reviewed.
- One study noted strain-dependent impairment of portal vein smooth muscle contractility in vitro (PMID: 15178683), but this was not a consistent finding.
- No lethality, no Mendelian ratio deviations, and no brain-specific phenotypes were documented.

**Consistency with ASD or neurodevelopmental human phenotype:** Poor. The mouse knockout data do not recapitulate any neurological or behavioral phenotype relevant to ASD. While one cell-culture study identified Atp2b4 as a regulator of oxidative stress resistance in mouse neural stem cells (PMID: 38666459), this was an in vitro finding and no in vivo neurological phenotype was described in knockout animals.

**Strength of model organism evidence for ASD: NONE**

---

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:** ATP2B4/PMCA4 is a plasma membrane calcium pump that extrudes Ca²⁺ from the cytosol in exchange for H⁺, playing a central role in intracellular calcium signaling and homeostasis. It participates in pathways including calcium-dependent cell signaling, sperm motility, erythrocyte volume regulation, and epithelial polarity.

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**
- The strongest expression and functional data for ATP2B4 relate to **erythrocytes** (PMID: 28714864; PMID: 36604655), **sperm** (PMID: 15178683), **adrenal tissue** (PMID: 32002807), **pancreatic acinar cells** (PMID: 29042438), and **breast epithelium** (PMID: 40075218).
- One cell-culture study demonstrated that Atp2b4 overexpression in mouse haploid neural stem cells increased survival under oxidative stress (PMID: 38666459), and a separate study identified Atp2b4 as a direct target of miR-129-5p in hippocampal neurons, where it was shown to be a key effector of homeostatic synaptic downscaling (PMID: 28487411). These findings suggest a functional role in neurons, but neither study was conducted in the context of ASD or heterozygous loss-of-function.
- Expression of ATP2B4 in enamel organ ion regulation (PMID: 20929715) and chicken uterine calcium transport (PMID: 34702171) further illustrates broad tissue expression without specific neurodevelopmental enrichment.

**Key experimental findings relevant to disease:**
- GWAS and functional studies have robustly linked common regulatory variants in ATP2B4 to **mean corpuscular hemoglobin concentration (MCHC)** and **severe malaria susceptibility** (PMID: 28714864; PMID: 35563239; PMID: 36604655). This represents the strongest disease-gene association in the literature for ATP2B4, but it is entirely unrelated to ASD.
- Rare variants in ATP2B4 were investigated in bilateral hyperaldosteronism but a pathogenic role was not confirmed (PMID: 32002807).
- The synaptic plasticity role (PMID: 28487411) is the most biologically plausible connection to neurodevelopment, but it remains indirect and has not been linked to ASD in any human genetic or functional study.

**Strength of biological evidence for ASD: LIMITED**
(There is indirect biological plausibility via calcium signaling in neurons and synaptic plasticity, but no direct experimental or human genetic evidence connecting ATP2B4 to ASD pathogenesis.)

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level for ATP2B4 as an ASD gene (autosomal dominant, heterozygous): INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
- ATP2B4 encodes a calcium pump with documented expression and functional roles in hippocampal neurons and neural stem cells (PMID: 28487411; PMID: 38666459), providing a theoretical biological rationale for neurodevelopmental involvement.
- Calcium dysregulation is a recognized mechanism in several established ASD-associated genes, lending indirect plausibility.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- There are **no published clinical reports** linking heterozygous ATP2B4 variants to ASD or any neurodevelopmental disorder. The sole clinical genetic report associates ATP2B4 with DDH in a digenic inheritance model with incomplete penetrance (PMID: 28327142).
- Mouse knockout models show no neurological or behavioral phenotypes; the predominant phenotypes are male infertility and erythrocyte abnormalities, which are inconsistent with an ASD-relevant mechanism.
- No functional validation of any ATP2B4 variant in a neurodevelopmental context has been published.

**Conclusion:** Based on the totality of available evidence, there is currently **insufficient evidence** to classify ATP2B4 as an ASD gene with autosomal dominant pathogenicity. The gene has well-established roles in erythrocyte calcium homeostasis and male fertility, with emerging but preliminary data suggesting neuronal calcium regulatory functions. Establishing ATP2B4 as an ASD candidate would require independent identification of de novo or segregating heterozygous variants in ASD cohorts, functional validation in neuronal models, and replication across multiple unrelated families.

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. Exome sequencing identified rare variants in genes HSPG2 and ATP2B4 in a family segregating developmental dysplasia of the hip (PMID: 28327142)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. Targeted ablation of plasma membrane Ca2+-ATPase (PMCA) 1 and 4 indicates a major housekeeping function for PMCA1 and a critical role in hyperactivated sperm motility and male fertility for PMCA4. (PMID: 15178683)
2. The plasma membrane calcium ATPase 4 does not influence parasite levels but partially promotes experimental cerebral malaria during murine blood stage malaria. (PMID: 34215257)
3. Plasma membrane Ca(2+) ATPase 1 (PMCA1) but not PMCA4 is critical for B-cell development and Ca(2+) homeostasis in mice. (PMID: 33098669)
4. The serum protein renalase reduces injury in experimental pancreatitis. (PMID: 29042438)

### Biological Evidence
1. Genetic Screening of Haploid Neural Stem Cells Reveals that Nfkbia and Atp2b4 are Key Regulators of Oxidative Stress in Neural Precursors. (PMID: 38666459)
2. An erythroid-specific ATP2B4 enhancer mediates red blood cell hydration and malaria susceptibility. (PMID: 28714864)
3. Genetic variations in human ATP2B4 gene alter Plasmodium falciparum in vitro growth in RBCs from Gambian adults. (PMID: 36604655)
4. Molecular and Electrophysiological Analyses of ATP2B4 Gene Variants in Bilateral Adrenal Hyperaldosteronism. (PMID: 32002807)
5. The calcium pump PMCA4b promotes epithelial cell polarization and lumen formation. (PMID: 40075218)
6. A microRNA-129-5p/Rbfox crosstalk coordinates homeostatic downscaling of excitatory synapses. (PMID: 28487411)
7. Altered ion-responsive gene expression in Mmp20 null mice. (PMID: 20929715)
8. Circadian miR-449c-5p regulates uterine Ca(2+) transport during eggshell calcification in chickens. (PMID: 34702171)
9. Identification of ATP2B4 Regulatory Element Containing Functional Genetic Variants Associated with Severe Malaria. (PMID: 35563239)
