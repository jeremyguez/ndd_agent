# EHMT1 Evidence Report: Autism Spectrum Disorder Gene Assessment (Autosomal Dominant / Heterozygous)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

EHMT1 (Euchromatic Histone Methyltransferase 1) encodes a nuclear histone methyltransferase that catalyzes mono- and dimethylation of histone H3 at lysine 9 (H3K9me1/me2), a repressive epigenetic mark critical for transcriptional regulation in euchromatic regions. It is the causative gene for Kleefstra syndrome (OMIM #610253), a neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder (ASD), hypotonia, and characteristic facial dysmorphisms, inherited in an autosomal dominant manner through heterozygous loss-of-function variants or deletions.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports/series:** Ten independent publications or conference abstracts were identified, ranging from large multicenter cohort studies to single case reports.

**Total approximate number of patients:** Across all studies, approximately 270+ patients with pathogenic or likely pathogenic heterozygous EHMT1 variants were reported. The two largest series contributed 191 patients (PMID: 39013458) and 65 patients (PMID: 39746677), respectively.

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
The core phenotypic spectrum is highly consistent across studies and includes:
- Global developmental delay and intellectual disability (present in virtually all patients)
- Autism spectrum disorder / autistic features
- Hypotonia (infantile and persistent)
- Characteristic facial dysmorphisms
- Behavioral problems (aggression, anxiety, stereotypies)
- Epilepsy (reported in a significant subset; detailed electroclinical characterization in PMID: 37802085)
- Structural cardiac defects and hearing loss (particularly in deletion carriers; PMID: 39746677)
- Constipation and feeding difficulties

Additional, less common phenotypes include psychosis and schizophrenia-like features (PMID: 36617745), syndromic obesity (PMID: 35597848), cortical visual impairment, progressive cerebral atrophy, and rare features such as thrombocytopenia and adrenal carcinoma (PMID: 39746677). Two patients presenting with CdLS-like features were ultimately diagnosed with EHMT1 variants, underscoring phenotypic overlap with other chromatin disorders (PMID: 40677927).

**Predominant variant type:**
Loss-of-function (LoF) variants predominate, including protein-truncating variants (nonsense, frameshift, splice-site), intragenic deletions, and large/small 9q34 chromosomal deletions encompassing EHMT1. Pathogenic missense variants disrupting the ankyrin repeat domain (reader function) are also well-documented and produce a milder but recognizable phenotype with the Kleefstra-specific DNA methylation (DNAm) signature. Notably, missense variants disrupting only the SET domain methyltransferase activity produce an atypical phenotype without the DNAm signature, and N-terminal truncating variants produce a mild phenotype without the signature — revealing important genotype-phenotype correlations (PMID: 39013458). A novel splice-site variant (c.2382+1G>T) and a previously reported missense variant (c.2426C>T, p.Pro809Leu) were confirmed pathogenic by exome sequencing and protein modeling in a recent Chinese cohort (PMID: 39696517).

**Cohort origin:**
Patients are drawn from international multicenter studies, a dedicated Kleefstra Clinic at Boston Children's Hospital (USA; PMID: 39746677), centers in China (PMID: 39696517), Italy (PMID: 37802085; PMID: 39985057), the UK (PMID: 40677927), and two ASHG-presented adult intellectual disability WGS cohorts from the USA (ASHG 2022 and ASHG 2023, A. Sabo et al.). None of the patients are explicitly identified as originating from the UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL cohorts in the available citations, though the multicenter study (PMID: 39013458) likely includes European research center participants.

**Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG**
The evidence base is large (>270 patients), multi-ethnic, and multi-institutional. ASD/autistic features are a consistent and well-documented component of the Kleefstra syndrome phenotype across independent cohorts. De novo occurrence is the rule for most LoF variants, with rare familial cases also documented (PMID: 39013458). Variants have been classified as pathogenic or likely pathogenic using ACMG criteria, functional assays, and DNAm episignature analysis.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*) is the primary model organism, with additional evidence from *Drosophila melanogaster* and *C. elegans*.

**Key phenotypes from mouse models:**

*Heterozygous knockout mice* (modeling the human haploinsufficiency state) display a rich neurobehavioral phenotype:
- Deficits in fear extinction learning, novel object recognition, and spatial object recognition (PMID: 23175442)
- Reduced dendritic arborization and mature dendritic spine density in hippocampal CA1 pyramidal neurons, with reduced mEPSC frequency and increased paired-pulse facilitation suggesting presynaptic deficiency (PMID: 23175442)
- Deficits in sensory-motor gating (abnormal prepulse inhibition), complete absence of object recognition memory, and electrophysiological deficits (reduced beta/gamma oscillations, impaired phase synchronization, reduced mismatch negativity) resembling those induced by NMDA-R antagonists; reduced hippocampal *Grin1* mRNA expression (PMID: 32954001)
- Aberrant social behaviors including increased aggression, defensive posturing, and autistic-like traits during social interactions (PMID: 36877418)
- Impaired cortical neural network development with transiently reduced spontaneous bursting and excitatory synaptic currents (PMID: 27767173)
- Interestingly, one study reported enhanced pattern separation performance and increased hippocampal cell proliferation in heterozygous knockouts, suggesting domain-specific cognitive effects (PMID: 28071689)

*Conditional knockout models* demonstrated that EHMT1 loss in oocytes causes meiotic defects, impaired oocyte maturation, and mid-gestation embryonic lethality of resulting embryos, with depletion of H3K9me1/me2 and transcriptional derepression (PMID: 36690445). Conditional deletion in oligodendrocyte progenitor cells (OPCs) resulted in fewer oligodendrocyte lineage cells, reduced myelin basic protein, and derepression of genes regulating cell death and electrical excitability (PMID: 40134232).

*Knock-in models* validated that specific Kleefstra syndrome-associated missense substitutions (C1073Y and R1197W in the mouse GLP/EHMT1 ortholog) impair heterocomplex formation with EHMT2 and severely reduce histone methyltransferase activity in vivo (PMID: 29459631).

*Drosophila* disruption of EHMT1 produced impaired neurodevelopment and defects in learning and memory, with genetic interaction studies supporting a chromatin modification module (ASHG 2011, T. Kleefstra et al.). In *C. elegans*, the EHMT1 ortholog SET-6 was identified as a neuronal H3K9 methyltransferase whose activity accelerates behavioral deterioration during aging by repressing mitochondrial function genes (PMID: 32103178).

**Consistency with human phenotype:**
The mouse heterozygous knockout phenotype is highly consistent with the human Kleefstra syndrome phenotype, recapitulating cognitive impairment, social/behavioral abnormalities, synaptic dysfunction, and neurodevelopmental network deficits. The autistic-like social behavior and sensory processing deficits in mice directly parallel the ASD features observed in patients.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent laboratories have characterized heterozygous Ehmt1 knockout mice across diverse behavioral, electrophysiological, and neuroanatomical paradigms, with consistent and reproducible findings. Cross-species conservation is supported by Drosophila and C. elegans data.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
EHMT1 functions as the catalytic subunit of a histone methyltransferase complex (with its paralog EHMT2/G9a) that deposits the repressive H3K9me1 and H3K9me2 marks in euchromatic regions, silencing gene expression. It contains two functionally distinct domains: an N-terminal ankyrin repeat domain that acts as a "reader" of methylated histones, and a C-terminal SET domain that acts as the "writer" (methyltransferase). Domain-specific genotype-phenotype correlations in Kleefstra syndrome patients demonstrate that reader and writer functions are non-redundant in vivo (PMID: 39013458).

Beyond histone substrates, EHMT1 methylates non-histone proteins: methylation of DNA ligase 1 (LIG1) by EHMT1 regulates DNA replication fork progression and chemotherapy sensitivity, distinguishing its function from that of EHMT2 (PMID: 37873068). EHMT1 also interacts physically with BRD4 to co-repress target genes including RhoB in erythroblasts (PMID: 40598222), and regulates SOX8 expression via BRD4 occupancy in rhabdomyosarcoma cells (PMID: 40467997).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**
EHMT1 is expressed in the developing and mature brain, with particular relevance to cortical and hippocampal neurons. In human pluripotent stem cell-derived cortical neurons, EHMT1 is part of an epigenetic barrier in neural progenitor cells that restrains transcriptional maturation programs; transient inhibition of EHMT1 at the progenitor stage enables precocious neuronal maturation, identifying it as a cell-intrinsic regulator of the prolonged timeline of human cortical neuron maturation (PMID: 38297124). EHMT1 expression in the human frontal cortex increases with age and correlates positively with Alzheimer's disease progression (PMID: 32103178). In OPCs, EHMT1/2-deposited H3K9me2 increases in response to neuronal activity, linking EHMT1 to activity-dependent myelination (PMID: 40134232).

**Key experimental findings:**
- Domain-specific functional dissection in patient-derived variants demonstrates that the ankyrin repeat reader domain is required for the Kleefstra DNAm episignature and full phenotypic expression (PMID: 39013458)
- Biochemical validation of missense variants confirms loss of EHMT1-EHMT2 heterocomplex formation and methyltransferase activity (PMID: 29459631)
- EHMT1 depletion in RPE1 cells disrupts Golgi morphology, lysosomal distribution, cell adhesion, and migration, providing cellular mechanistic insights into the neurodevelopmental phenotype (PMID: 37257768)
- Pharmacological inhibition of EHMT1/2 rescues glutamate receptor expression and synaptic/cognitive function in Alzheimer's disease mouse models, demonstrating the functional importance of EHMT1-mediated H3K9me2 at neuronal signaling gene loci (PMID: 30668640)

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
The molecular function of EHMT1 is well-characterized at the biochemical, cellular, and systems level. Its role as an epigenetic regulator of neurodevelopmental gene expression programs is supported by convergent evidence from human iPSC-derived neurons, mouse models, invertebrate models, and patient variant functional studies.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths:**
1. *Large, replicated clinical evidence base:* Over 270 patients with heterozygous pathogenic EHMT1 variants have been reported across independent international cohorts, with ASD/autistic features consistently documented as a core phenotypic feature alongside intellectual disability, hypotonia, and dysmorphisms. Functional validation using DNA methylation episignatures provides an orthogonal diagnostic tool.
2. *Highly consistent model organism data:* Multiple independent heterozygous Ehmt1 knockout mouse studies recapitulate the human neurodevelopmental and behavioral phenotype, including cognitive impairment, social dysfunction, and synaptic deficits, with cross-species conservation demonstrated in Drosophila and C. elegans.
3. *Well-defined molecular mechanism:* EHMT1's role as an H3K9 methyltransferase regulating neurodevelopmental gene expression is mechanistically well-understood, with domain-specific genotype-phenotype correlations providing direct links between molecular function and clinical outcome.

**Key gaps or limitations:**
- The majority of clinical studies focus on the broader Kleefstra syndrome phenotype; prospective studies specifically quantifying ASD prevalence and severity using standardized diagnostic instruments (e.g., ADOS-2, ADI-R) across large EHMT1 cohorts are limited.
- Genotype-phenotype correlations for ASD specifically (as distinct from the broader neurodevelopmental phenotype) have not been systematically analyzed across variant classes.
- Some conference abstracts (ASHG 2022, ASHG 2023) lack full peer-reviewed publication details, and several clinical reports do not provide specific variant notations, limiting precise variant-level curation.

**Conclusion:**
EHMT1 is a well-established, high-confidence autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene. Heterozygous loss-of-function variants cause Kleefstra syndrome, in which ASD/autistic features are a consistent and defining component of the phenotype, supported by robust clinical, model organism, and mechanistic biological evidence. The gene meets criteria for a strong causal association with ASD as part of a broader syndromic neurodevelopmental disorder, and EHMT1 should be considered a definitive ASD-associated gene in the context of Kleefstra syndrome.
