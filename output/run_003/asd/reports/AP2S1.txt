# Evidence Report: AP2S1 as an Autism Spectrum Disorder Gene (Autosomal Dominant / Heterozygous)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

AP2S1 encodes the sigma (σ2) subunit of the heterotetrameric adaptor protein-2 (AP-2) complex, which is essential for clathrin-mediated endocytosis. Its primary established function is to facilitate internalization of cell-surface receptors — most notably the calcium-sensing receptor (CaSR) — and it is the causative gene for Familial Hypocalciuric Hypercalcemia type 3 (FHH3) when heterozygous gain-of-function missense mutations impair CaSR endocytosis.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports and patients:**
The structured clinical evidence retrieved contains 2 independent case reports, each describing a single patient (total n = 2). Both reports describe heterozygous missense variants at the same codon (p.Arg15Leu) in the context of FHH3 (PMID: 39949382; PMID: 41501259). Broader published cohort studies (referenced in the biological evidence) have identified AP2S1 mutations in up to 22 FHH3 patients across 19 families (PMID: 26963950) and in 5 of 39 FHH patients lacking CASR mutations (PMID: 24731014), but these studies were not captured as primary clinical evidence for ASD.

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
Across all retrieved clinical evidence, the phenotypes are uniformly those of FHH3: hypercalcemia, hypocalciuria, elevated or inappropriately normal PTH, and reduced bone mineral density. Notably, both case reports also document neurodevelopmental features — specifically "developmental disorder" and "neurodevelopmental difficulties" — in the affected individuals (PMID: 39949382; PMID: 41501259). However, these neurodevelopmental features are reported as associated findings rather than as the primary indication for genetic testing, and neither report provides formal ASD diagnostic criteria or standardized neurodevelopmental assessments.

**Predominant variant type:**
All clinically reported variants are heterozygous missense mutations, specifically at the Arg15 residue of AP2σ2 (p.Arg15Leu, p.Arg15Cys, p.Arg15His). These are recurrent, gain-of-function-like variants that impair CaSR internalization rather than classical loss-of-function alleles.

**Cohort origin:**
Neither patient was reported as part of a recognized neurodevelopmental disease cohort (e.g., UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL). One case was inferred to be from Japan (PMID: 39949382); the other had no stated country of origin (PMID: 41501259).

**Strength of clinical evidence for ASD: NONE**
There are no published case reports, case series, or cohort studies that identify AP2S1 heterozygous variants as a cause of autism spectrum disorder specifically. The neurodevelopmental features noted in FHH3 patients are incompletely characterized and have not been systematically evaluated for ASD. No patient in the retrieved evidence received a formal ASD diagnosis attributed to AP2S1.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:**
AP2S1/Ap2s1 has been studied in mouse (Mus musculus) and zebrafish (Danio rerio).

**Key findings in mouse:**
Two distinct knock-in mouse models have been generated. A loss-of-function model carrying an ENU-induced splice-site mutation (Ap2s1+/del17) showed that heterozygous mice are viable with entirely normal biochemistry — including normal plasma calcium, PTH, and urinary calcium excretion — indicating AP2σ haplosufficiency in mice under baseline conditions (PMID: 29479578). Homozygous Ap2s1del17/del17 mice were embryonic lethal (E3.5–E9.5), demonstrating that complete AP2S1 loss is incompatible with early embryonic development. A second CRISPR/Cas9 knock-in model carrying the human FHH3 variant p.Arg15Leu (Ap2s1+/L15) recapitulated the human FHH3 biochemical phenotype, with marked hypercalcemia, hypermagnesemia, and hypophosphatemia in heterozygous mice; homozygotes died perinatally (PMID: 33729479). Cinacalcet treatment reduced plasma calcium and PTH in Ap2s1+/L15 mice, providing pharmacological validation. Neither mouse model reported neurological or behavioral phenotypes.

**Key findings in zebrafish:**
Multiple zebrafish studies implicate ap2s1 in neurological function. Loss of ap2s1 impaired acoustically evoked habituation learning and altered sensorimotor behavioral choice (PMID: 38550987; PMID: 29681477). In a molecular dissection of habituation, ap2s1 was identified as part of a molecular module that drives habituation by antagonizing dopamine signaling (PMID: 36972301). These behavioral phenotypes are consistent with a role for AP2S1 in synaptic vesicle recycling and neurotransmitter receptor endocytosis in the nervous system. Importantly, the zebrafish studies link ap2s1 to CaSR endocytosis in the context of sensorimotor decision-making (PMID: 29681477), providing a mechanistic bridge between the calcium-regulatory and neurological functions.

**Consistency with human ASD phenotype:**
The zebrafish behavioral phenotypes (habituation deficits, altered behavioral plasticity, dopaminergic dysregulation) are broadly consistent with features seen in neurodevelopmental disorders including ASD, but the connection is indirect. No mouse model has demonstrated ASD-relevant behavioral phenotypes. The primary human phenotype modeled in mice is FHH3, not ASD.

**Strength of model organism evidence for ASD: LIMITED**
Zebrafish data provide suggestive neurological evidence, but no mammalian model has demonstrated ASD-relevant behavioral phenotypes, and the zebrafish findings have not been replicated in higher organisms.

---

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
AP2S1 is a core component of the AP-2 clathrin adaptor complex (with AP2α, AP2β, and AP2μ subunits), which is the principal mediator of clathrin-mediated endocytosis at the plasma membrane. Its established role is to recognize tyrosine-based and dileucine sorting motifs on cargo receptors, facilitating their internalization. In the context of calcium homeostasis, AP2S1 directly interacts with the CaSR to mediate its endocytosis; mutations at Arg15 disrupt this interaction and impair CaSR internalization, causing FHH3 (PMID: 24731014; PMID: 26963950). AP2S1 has also been identified as an interaction partner of RNF41 (an E3 ubiquitin ligase), with functional roles in leptin and LIF receptor signaling (PMID: 29560723). Additionally, AP2S1 has been identified as a host factor exploited by Zika virus (PMID: 40156834) and Listeria monocytogenes (PMID: 40847839) for intracellular entry, further confirming its broad role in receptor-mediated endocytosis.

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**
AP2S1 mRNA is expressed in adult parathyroid adenoma tissue (PMID: 32666007), and AP2S1 protein has been detected in boar sperm proteomics (PMID: 38029685). No brain-specific or neuron-specific expression data were retrieved in the structured evidence. However, the AP-2 complex is well established in the broader literature as a key regulator of synaptic vesicle recycling and neurotransmitter receptor endocytosis in neurons, which provides a plausible biological rationale for neurological involvement. The zebrafish studies specifically implicate ap2s1 in dopaminergic signaling modulation in the nervous system (PMID: 36972301).

**Key experimental findings relevant to neurodevelopment:**
The most directly relevant biological finding is that AP2S1 mutations impair protein-protein interactions between AP2σ2 and other AP-2 subunits and with CaSR (PMID: 33729479). Given that the AP-2 complex mediates endocytosis of multiple neurotransmitter receptors (e.g., AMPA receptors, dopamine receptors), disruption of AP2S1 function could plausibly affect synaptic plasticity. However, no direct experimental evidence linking AP2S1 variants to synaptic receptor trafficking deficits in neurons has been retrieved.

**Strength of biological evidence for ASD: LIMITED**
The molecular function of AP2S1 in clathrin-mediated endocytosis is well characterized, and there is a plausible mechanistic pathway to neurological dysfunction via impaired neurotransmitter receptor internalization. However, no direct experimental evidence from neuronal systems, brain expression studies, or synaptic physiology experiments was retrieved to support a specific role in ASD pathogenesis.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level for AP2S1 as an ASD gene: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
- AP2S1 has a well-established molecular function in clathrin-mediated endocytosis, a pathway with known relevance to synaptic plasticity and neurodevelopmental disorders.
- Zebrafish loss-of-function studies demonstrate neurological phenotypes including habituation deficits and dopaminergic dysregulation (PMID: 38550987; PMID: 36972301), providing biological plausibility.
- Two clinical case reports note neurodevelopmental features (developmental disorder, neurodevelopmental difficulties) in FHH3 patients carrying p.Arg15Leu (PMID: 39949382; PMID: 41501259), raising the possibility of a broader neurodevelopmental phenotype.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- No patient in the retrieved evidence has received a formal ASD diagnosis attributed to AP2S1 variants; neurodevelopmental features in FHH3 cases are incompletely characterized and may reflect secondary effects of chronic hypercalcemia rather than a primary neurogenetic mechanism.
- No mammalian (mouse) model has demonstrated ASD-relevant behavioral phenotypes; the established mouse models recapitulate FHH3 biochemistry only.
- AP2S1 is absent from recognized ASD gene databases (e.g., SFARI Gene) and has not been identified in large-scale ASD sequencing cohorts (e.g., SPARK, ASC).
- The predominant variant mechanism in FHH3 (gain-of-function missense at Arg15) differs from the typical loss-of-function mechanism expected for haploinsufficiency-driven neurodevelopmental disorders.

**Conclusion:**
The current evidence does not support AP2S1 as an established autism spectrum disorder gene. While its role in clathrin-mediated endocytosis provides a biologically plausible link to neurodevelopmental function — supported by zebrafish behavioral data — the clinical evidence is limited to FHH3 case reports with incidental neurodevelopmental features, and no systematic ASD-focused genetic or functional studies have been conducted. Reclassification of AP2S1 as an ASD candidate gene would require identification of AP2S1 variants in ASD cohorts, formal neurodevelopmental phenotyping of FHH3 patients, and neuronal functional studies in mammalian systems.
