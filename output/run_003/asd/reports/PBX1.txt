# PBX1 Evidence Report: Heterozygous (Autosomal Dominant) Pathogenic Variants and Neurodevelopmental Disease

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

PBX1 (Pre-B-cell leukemia homeobox 1) encodes a TALE (Three Amino acid Loop Extension) homeodomain transcription factor that functions as a master developmental regulator, forming multimeric complexes with HOX and MEIS/PREP cofactors to control transcription of target genes across multiple organ systems. PBX1 plays critical roles in embryonic patterning, organogenesis, skeletal development, hematopoiesis, and neural crest cell-derived tissue formation.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent case reports/series:** One case series was identified, presented at ASHG 2017 (Slavotinek A, Risolino M, Losa M, et al.; ASHG 2017 – De novo, deleterious sequence variants that alter the transcriptional activity of the homeoprotein PBX1 are associated with intellectual disability and pleiotropic developmental defects).

**Total approximate number of patients:** 7 patients across multiple international centers (USA, Australia, Netherlands), including UCSF, University of Groningen, and Sydney Children's Hospital.

**Most common phenotypes observed:** All seven patients presented with intellectual disability and developmental delay as core neurodevelopmental features. Additional recurrent findings included external ear anomalies, branchial arch derivative abnormalities, heart malformations, diaphragmatic hernia, pulmonary hypoplasia, renal hypoplasia, and cryptorchidism. Notably, while intellectual disability and developmental delay are consistently reported, autism spectrum disorder (ASD) per se is not explicitly listed among the documented phenotypes in the available evidence. The neurodevelopmental phenotype is therefore best characterized as intellectual disability/global developmental delay rather than ASD specifically.

**Predominant variant type:** A mixture of missense variants (5/7: p.Arg184Pro, p.Met224Lys, p.Arg227Pro, p.Arg234Pro, p.Arg235Gln) and loss-of-function variants (2/7: p.Ser262Glnfs*2, p.Arg288*). All variants were de novo and heterozygous, consistent with an autosomal dominant mechanism. The clustering of missense variants within the homeodomain (residues 184–235) suggests a critical functional region, and functional studies on five variants confirmed impaired PBX-dependent transactivation, altered nuclear translocation, and disrupted TALE/HOX cofactor interactions (ASHG 2017).

**Cohort origin:** Patients were recruited from multiple international clinical centers. The report does not explicitly identify patients as originating from named research cohorts such as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL, though the multi-center international composition is consistent with collaborative rare disease networks.

**Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED**
The evidence is limited by the small patient number (n=7), the single publication source (a conference abstract rather than a peer-reviewed full paper), and the absence of ASD as a specifically documented phenotype. While intellectual disability and developmental delay are robustly supported, the direct link to ASD as a discrete diagnosis is not established in the available clinical data.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*) and zebrafish (*Danio rerio*).

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

- **Full knockout (mouse):** Homozygous *Pbx1*-null mice die at embryonic day E15–16 with severe multi-organ hypoplasia or aplasia, widespread axial and appendicular skeletal patterning defects, malformations of branchial arch-derived structures, and premature ossification (PMID: 11566859). Lethality is embryonic and Mendelian ratios are sub-Mendelian, indicating strong developmental constraint. Critically, the multi-organ phenotype — including branchial arch, skeletal, and visceral defects — closely mirrors the human clinical presentation.

- **Conditional knockout (mouse, Leydig cells):** Loss of *Pbx1* in Leydig cells caused complete male sterility, spermatogenic defects, disorganization of seminiferous tubules, and impaired testosterone synthesis (PMID: 38724675), consistent with the cryptorchidism observed in human patients.

- **Conditional knockout (mouse, cornea):** Corneal-specific deletion produced progressive corneal dystrophy, stromal disorganization, and loss of barrier function (PMID: 19797217), demonstrating tissue-specific roles in epithelial morphogenesis.

- **Heterozygous knockout (mouse):** Heterozygous *Pbx1+/-* mice showed pancreatic islet malformations, impaired glucose tolerance, and hypoinsulinemia; compound heterozygosity with *Pdx1+/-* produced overt diabetes (PMID: 30002646), indicating haploinsufficiency effects in some tissues.

- **Diaphragm-specific evidence:** Ex vivo analysis of *Pbx1* knockout embryos at E15.5 revealed diaphragmatic defects in 3/3 animals examined, directly validating PBX1 as a congenital diaphragmatic hernia gene (ASHG 2011 – Analysis of time-series embryonic diaphragm transcriptomes identifies Pbx1 as a candidate gene for diaphragmatic defects).

- **Zebrafish:** Morpholino-mediated knockdown of *pbx1* abrogated primitive erythropoiesis with failure to produce primitive erythrocytes (PMID: 34496088). A knock-in of the oncogenic hE2A-PBX1 fusion produced myeloid hyperplasia and progression to AML (PMID: 38385270).

**Neurological phenotypes:** Notably absent from the available model organism data is a well-characterized neurological or behavioral phenotype in *Pbx1* mutant mice. No studies reporting cognitive impairment, autism-like behavior, or brain structural anomalies in *Pbx1* knockout or heterozygous mice were identified in the provided evidence. This represents a significant gap given the neurodevelopmental phenotype in human patients.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The multi-organ developmental defects (branchial arch, skeletal, diaphragm, gonadal) observed in *Pbx1*-null mice are highly consistent with the human clinical phenotype. However, the neurological/intellectual disability component — the most clinically prominent feature in patients — lacks direct corroboration from mouse models in the available evidence.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**
The model organism data strongly support PBX1's role in multi-organ embryonic development and validate several specific human phenotypes (diaphragmatic hernia, branchial arch defects, cryptorchidism). However, the absence of documented neurological phenotypes in animal models limits the strength of evidence specifically for the neurodevelopmental/ASD component.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:** PBX1 functions as a transcriptional regulator within TALE-HOX multimeric complexes. It acts as a pioneer transcription factor, facilitating chromatin accessibility at regulatory elements prior to activation of downstream transcriptional programs (PMID: 30078726). PBX1 directly binds target gene promoters (confirmed by ChIP assays) and regulates transcription in diverse developmental and disease contexts, including myoblast differentiation, endometrial carcinoma suppression via SFRP4/Wnt pathway modulation (PMID: 37054536), and hepatic stellate cell activation via IL7R transcription (PMID: 40080840). The dominant-negative isoform PBX1D, which lacks DNA- and HOX-binding domains, impairs regulatory T cell homeostasis and is implicated in systemic lupus erythematosus susceptibility (PMID: 37486226), illustrating the functional importance of intact DNA-binding and cofactor interaction domains — precisely the domains disrupted by the patient-derived missense variants.

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:** Direct evidence for PBX1 expression in the human brain or developing nervous system is not prominently represented in the provided biological evidence. PBX1 expression has been documented in hematopoietic stem cells (PMID: 34724565), cochlear hair cells (PMID: 41581416), hepatic stellate cells, and myoblasts. The cochlear hair cell data (PMID: 41581416) — showing age-dependent reduction of PBX1 and its role in preserving auditory function — provides indirect evidence for PBX1 function in sensory neurobiology, but direct brain expression data are not available in the current evidence base.

**Key experimental findings:**
- Functional studies on five of the seven patient-derived variants demonstrated impaired PBX-dependent transactivation, altered nuclear translocation, and disrupted interactions with TALE/HOX cofactors, confirming pathogenicity at the molecular level (ASHG 2017).
- PBX1 acts as a pioneer factor for chromatin remodeling during differentiation (PMID: 30078726), a mechanism increasingly implicated in neurodevelopmental gene regulation.
- Family-based association analysis in 829 NSCL/P families identified PBX1 SNP rs1044263 as associated with nonsyndromic cleft lip/palate in Hispanic multiplex families (p=0.002; ASHG 2013 – Interrogating the PBX-WNT-TP63 pathway in human nonsyndromic cleft lip/palate), providing independent human genetic evidence for PBX1's role in craniofacial morphogenesis consistent with the branchial arch phenotypes in patients.
- HBO1-mediated H3K14 acetylation at the *Pbx1* locus is required for its expression in hematopoietic stem cells (PMID: 34724565), indicating epigenetic regulation of PBX1 that may be relevant across cell types.

**Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE**
The molecular function of PBX1 as a transcriptional regulator and pioneer factor is well established, and functional validation of patient variants has been performed. However, direct biological evidence for PBX1's role in brain development, neuronal function, or ASD-relevant pathways is absent from the current evidence base.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: LIMITED**

**Key strengths:**
- Seven patients with de novo heterozygous PBX1 variants and a consistent multi-organ developmental phenotype including intellectual disability provide a coherent clinical signal.
- Functional studies directly demonstrate that patient-derived variants impair PBX1's transcriptional activity, nuclear localization, and cofactor interactions, establishing molecular pathogenicity.
- Mouse knockout models recapitulate multiple specific human phenotypes (branchial arch defects, diaphragmatic hernia, cryptorchidism), strongly supporting biological plausibility.
- The clustering of missense variants within the homeodomain and the presence of LoF variants are consistent with a haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative mechanism.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- The primary clinical evidence derives from a single conference abstract (ASHG 2017) with n=7 patients; no peer-reviewed full publication with detailed clinical characterization was identified.
- **Autism spectrum disorder is not explicitly documented** as a phenotype in any of the identified patients or studies; the neurodevelopmental phenotype is intellectual disability and developmental delay, which, while overlapping with ASD, is distinct. The evidence base does not directly support PBX1 as an ASD gene based on available data.
- Neurological or behavioral phenotypes have not been characterized in *Pbx1* mouse models, leaving the mechanism linking PBX1 dysfunction to cognitive impairment unresolved.
- Brain-specific expression data and neurodevelopmental pathway involvement for PBX1 are not represented in the current evidence.

**Conclusion:** PBX1 harbors de novo heterozygous variants — both missense and loss-of-function — in patients with intellectual disability and pleiotropic developmental defects, supported by functional validation and consistent mouse model phenotypes. However, the current evidence is insufficient to classify PBX1 specifically as an autism spectrum disorder gene, as ASD has not been documented as a discrete phenotype in reported patients, and neurodevelopmental biological mechanisms remain uncharacterized. The gene warrants further investigation through larger patient cohorts with detailed neurodevelopmental phenotyping, and dedicated neurological characterization of heterozygous *Pbx1* mouse models, before a definitive ASD gene classification can be made.
