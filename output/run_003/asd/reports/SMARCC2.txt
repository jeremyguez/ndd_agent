# Literature Evidence Report: SMARCC2 as an Autism Spectrum Disorder / Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene (Autosomal Dominant)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

SMARCC2 (SWI/SNF-related matrix-associated actin-dependent regulator of chromatin subfamily C member 2; also known as BAF170) encodes an invariable core structural subunit of the BAF (BRG1/BRM-associated factor) chromatin-remodeling complex, which is essential for regulating gene expression through ATP-dependent nucleosome repositioning. SMARCC2 plays a critical role in embryogenesis and corticogenesis, and its SANT and SWIRM domains mediate direct DNA interaction and chromatin remodeling activity (PMID: 30580808).

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports and total patients:**
Ten independent publications or conference reports were identified, collectively describing approximately 92 or more affected individuals with pathogenic or likely pathogenic heterozygous SMARCC2 variants. The largest single cohort comprised 65 individuals (PMID: 37551667), followed by a foundational series of 15 patients (PMID: 30580808). Additional contributions include a series of 3 Chinese patients (PMID: 41532374), two separate 2-patient reports (PMID: 34881817; PMID: 39901255), and individual case reports from China (PMID: 35536477), an Arab-descent patient identified by long-read sequencing (PMID: 41291750), and a French learning disorders cohort (PMID: 41168819). A conference abstract from Baylor College of Medicine (ASHG 2019) further corroborated de novo SMARCC2 variants in a clinical whole-exome sequencing database.

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
Across studies, the core phenotypic spectrum includes:
- Intellectual disability and/or global developmental delay (present in the vast majority of patients)
- Speech and language delay (near-universal)
- Muscular hypotonia
- Behavioral abnormalities, including autism spectrum disorder (ASD), ADHD, and obsessive-compulsive features
- Craniofacial dysmorphisms (thick eyebrows, upturned nose, thin upper lip vermilion)
- Feeding difficulties
- Seizures (reported in a subset)
- Short stature / growth retardation

Notably, ASD and ADHD are recurrent behavioral diagnoses. One patient presented with a primary diagnosis of ASD, OCD, and ADHD following identification of a de novo chromosomal inversion disrupting SMARCC2 (PMID: 41291750). Two patients in a French cohort presented with severe specific learning and language disorders without frank intellectual disability (PMID: 41168819), suggesting the phenotypic spectrum extends to milder neurodevelopmental presentations.

**Predominant variant type and genotype-phenotype correlation:**
Both missense and loss-of-function (LoF) variants are represented. The largest cohort study (PMID: 37551667) established an important genotype-phenotype correlation: non-truncating (predominantly missense) variants, mostly de novo and clustering in functional domains, are associated with severe developmental delay and intellectual disability, while likely gene-disrupting (LoF) variants are predominantly inherited and associated with milder or normal cognitive development. This suggests that severe missense variants may act through a dominant-negative or gain-of-function mechanism rather than simple haploinsufficiency. Additional variant types include splice-site variants (PMID: 39901255; PMID: 41532374), an in-frame deletion (PMID: 34881817), and a structural chromosomal inversion (PMID: 41291750), broadening the mutational spectrum.

**Known cohort representation:**
One study explicitly recruited through the Undiagnosed Diseases Network (UDN) (PMID: 30580808). The remaining studies were conducted at multiple international centers or individual clinical genetics centers, including centers in China, France, and the United States (Baylor College of Medicine, ASHG 2019).

**Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG**
The evidence is supported by a large cumulative patient number (>90), multiple independent international replication studies, consistent core phenotypes, established genotype-phenotype correlations, and predominantly de novo occurrence of the most severe variants.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:**
No formal model organism studies (e.g., knockout mouse lines, zebrafish, Drosophila) were identified in the structured evidence provided for this report.

**Key phenotypes and consistency with human phenotype:**
One biological study (PMID: 40492195) used conditional Smarcc2 deficiency in the mouse prefrontal cortex as a functional model, demonstrating impaired working memory, downregulation of synaptic transmission genes, and impaired GABAergic and glutamatergic synaptic currents — findings directly relevant to the ASD and intellectual disability phenotypes observed in human patients. However, this represents a targeted regional knockdown rather than a germline knockout model, and no data on lethality, Mendelian ratio deviations, or whole-organism developmental phenotypes were reported in the available evidence.

**Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED**
While the mouse prefrontal cortex Smarcc2-deficiency model provides relevant neurobiological data, the absence of formal germline knockout or knock-in studies with systematic phenotypic characterization limits the strength of this evidence tier.

---

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
SMARCC2 is a core, invariable subunit of the BAF (SWI/SNF) chromatin-remodeling complex. Its primary molecular function is to facilitate ATP-dependent nucleosome repositioning, thereby regulating chromatin accessibility and transcriptional programs. Key mechanistic findings include:

- **BAF complex integrity and variant pathomechanism:** Co-immunoprecipitation and proximity-ligation assays in the 65-patient cohort study demonstrated that N-terminal missense variants reduce SMARCC2 protein expression to levels comparable to LoF variants, suggesting protein instability as one pathomechanism (PMID: 37551667). Three-dimensional protein modeling confirmed clustering of pathogenic variants in functional domains.
- **Neuronal gene regulation:** Transcriptomic analysis of patient-derived fibroblasts identified differentially expressed genes with roles in neuronal development and function, including RELN, H19, SCRG1, and CACNB4 (PMID: 30580808).
- **Synaptic function and ASD relevance:** SMARCC2 was found to be significantly diminished in iPSC-derived neurons from idiopathic ASD patients. Smarcc2 deficiency in mouse prefrontal cortex caused downregulation of synaptic transmission genes and impaired GABAergic and glutamatergic synaptic currents, with rescue by the HDAC inhibitor romidepsin (PMID: 40492195). This directly links SMARCC2 to synaptic mechanisms underlying ASD.
- **Inflammatory and signaling pathway interactions:** Nuclear RIPK1 mediates phosphorylation of SMARCC2 to promote BAF complex-dependent chromatin remodeling at proinflammatory gene loci, implicating SMARCC2 in neuroinflammatory pathways relevant to ASD and ALS (PMID: 35661830).
- **Post-translational regulation:** FBXO28 was identified as an E3 ubiquitin ligase targeting SMARCC2 for proteasomal degradation (PMID: 37348029), and functionally important phosphorylation sites in SMARCC2 relevant to neuronal differentiation were identified by large-scale phosphoproteomics (PMID: 31819260).
- **Chromatin remodeling specificity:** The SWIRM domain of SMARCC2 plays a more important role than the SANT domain in chromatin remodeling function, as demonstrated in glioblastoma models (PMID: 36418306), consistent with the clustering of pathogenic NDD variants in these domains.

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**
SMARCC2 is described as an invariable core subunit of the BAF complex critical for embryogenesis and corticogenesis (PMID: 30580808). Network analysis identified SMARCC2 as part of a gene module showing highest expression early during embryonic development and enriched for de novo mutations in ASD and intellectual disability patients, with overlap to schizophrenia risk genes and association with Wnt and Notch signaling pathways (ASHG 2014 - Penn et al.).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent experimental approaches — including transcriptomics, proteomics, iPSC-derived neuronal models, mouse prefrontal cortex deficiency models, protein structural modeling, and co-immunoprecipitation — converge to support a critical role for SMARCC2 in neurodevelopmental gene regulation, synaptic function, and ASD-relevant biology.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths:**
1. **Large, replicated clinical dataset:** More than 90 patients across at least 10 independent publications from multiple international centers establish SMARCC2 as a bona fide NDD/ASD gene with a consistent core phenotype. The largest cohort (PMID: 37551667) provides robust genotype-phenotype correlations and functional validation of variant pathogenicity.
2. **De novo occurrence of most severe variants:** The predominance of de novo heterozygous variants — particularly missense variants in functional domains — strongly supports autosomal dominant pathogenicity rather than incidental findings.
3. **Convergent biological evidence:** Independent lines of evidence from patient fibroblast transcriptomics, iPSC-derived ASD neurons, mouse prefrontal cortex models, and protein interaction studies all point to disruption of BAF complex-mediated chromatin remodeling in neuronal development and synaptic function as the underlying disease mechanism (PMID: 30580808; PMID: 37551667; PMID: 40492195).

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. **Absence of formal germline model organism studies:** No germline knockout or knock-in mouse or zebrafish models with systematic neurodevelopmental phenotyping have been reported, which limits mechanistic understanding of the full developmental consequences of heterozygous SMARCC2 loss.
2. **ASD as a primary diagnosis is underrepresented:** While ASD and autistic behaviors are recurrently reported as part of the phenotypic spectrum, most studies frame the condition primarily as intellectual disability / NDD (Coffin-Siris syndrome 8), and dedicated ASD cohort studies are lacking.
3. **Variant-level functional data remain incomplete:** Functional validation (e.g., protein expression, complex assembly) has been performed for only a subset of reported variants, and the precise dominant-negative versus haploinsufficiency mechanisms for different variant classes require further clarification.

**Conclusion:**
SMARCC2 is a well-established autosomal dominant NDD/ASD gene, with strong and replicated clinical evidence from over 90 patients demonstrating that de novo heterozygous variants — particularly missense variants in the SANT and SWIRM domains — cause a syndrome characterized by intellectual disability, developmental delay, speech impairment, hypotonia, and behavioral abnormalities including ASD and ADHD. Biological evidence robustly supports a mechanism involving disruption of BAF complex-mediated chromatin remodeling in corticogenesis and synaptic gene regulation. The primary gap in the evidence base is the absence of formal germline animal model studies and dedicated ASD cohort analyses, which would further strengthen the gene-disease association and clarify pathomechanisms.
