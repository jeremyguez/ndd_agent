# Evidence Report: RFX3 as an Autism Spectrum Disorder Gene (Autosomal Dominant / Haploinsufficiency Mechanism)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

RFX3 (Regulatory Factor X 3) is a member of the RFX family of transcription factors characterized by a highly conserved winged-helix DNA-binding domain that recognizes X-box promoter motifs. It is best known as a master regulator of ciliogenesis — controlling the differentiation and function of both motile and primary cilia — and has more recently been recognized as a critical transcriptional regulator of neurodevelopmental gene networks in the developing and adult brain.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports/series:** Seven independent clinical reports or series were identified, spanning peer-reviewed publications and major conference presentations (ASHG 2017, 2019, 2023).

**Total approximate number of patients:** Across the studies with reported patient counts, at least 65 individuals with heterozygous RFX3 variants have been described: 38 individuals in the largest published cohort (PMID: 33658631), 17 in an earlier conference series (ASHG 2019 – Nakayama et al.), 8 in the initial syndrome delineation report (ASHG 2017 – Hodges et al.), and single case reports in three additional publications (PMID: 40200712; PMID: 37717291; PMID: 25844147). Two additional studies implicate RFX3 through chromosomal deletions in 9p deletion syndrome without specifying individual patient counts (ASHG 2023 – Turner et al.; PMID: 41137173).

**Most common phenotypes observed:** Across all reports, the core phenotypic spectrum associated with heterozygous RFX3 variants includes:
- Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) — present in virtually all series
- Intellectual disability (mild to moderate)
- Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD)
- Hypersensitivity to sensory stimuli and sleep problems
- Hypotonia and joint laxity
- Subtle dysmorphic features
- Behavioral difficulties and, in at least one case, psychiatric decompensation requiring hospitalization (PMID: 40200712)
- Infantile epileptic spasms syndrome has been reported in one patient (PMID: 37717291)

**Predominant variant type:** Both loss-of-function (LoF) variants (nonsense, frameshift, splice-site) and missense variants have been reported. The functional and population-genetic evidence strongly supports a haploinsufficiency mechanism; RFX3 is noted to be under marked haploinsufficient constraint in human populations across multiple studies (PMID: 33658631; ASHG 2019 – Nakayama et al.). De novo occurrence has been documented in index cases (ASHG 2017 – Hodges et al.; PMID: 25844147), with additional inherited variants also reported. One case was identified specifically by long-read genome sequencing, highlighting that some LoF variants may be missed by standard short-read approaches (PMID: 40200712).

**Chromosomal deletion evidence:** RFX3 is deleted in approximately 80% of individuals with 9p minus syndrome, and machine learning dosage-phenotype correlation analysis identified RFX3 as the top implicated gene in that syndrome (ASHG 2023 – Turner et al.). A complementary whole-genome sequencing study of 9p deletion syndrome independently prioritized RFX3 among 24 genes accounting for the majority (83%) of the phenotypic burden in affected individuals (PMID: 41137173). A de novo single-gene deletion of RFX3 was also identified by SNP array in an individual with ASD carrying an apparently balanced chromosomal rearrangement (PMID: 25844147).

**Known cohort membership:** Cases were assembled through GeneMatcher (ASHG 2017 – Hodges et al.) and multiple international centers. The 9p deletion cohort was studied through the 9P-ARCH research network (PMID: 41137173). No explicit membership in UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL cohorts was specified in the available abstracts.

**Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG**
The convergence of multiple independent international series, a total of at least 65 patients, consistent core phenotype, de novo variant documentation, haploinsufficiency constraint data, and corroborating chromosomal deletion evidence collectively constitute strong clinical evidence.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*) has been the primary model organism, with multiple independent full knockout and conditional knockout studies. Xenopus has also been used in a knockdown context.

**Key phenotypes from mouse knockouts:**

- *Neurological/brain:* Rfx3 full knockout mice develop **agenesis of the corpus callosum**, attributed to marked disorganization of guidepost neurons required for callosal axon pathfinding, ectopic Fgf8 expression, and altered GLI3 repressor/activator ratios (PMID: 22479201; PMID: 26071364). This structural brain malformation is directly relevant to the neurodevelopmental phenotype observed in humans.
- *Hydrocephalus:* Rfx3-null mice develop hydrocephalus associated with defective ependymal cell differentiation and severe agenesis of the subcommissural organ (PMID: 16930429), consistent with the ciliogenic role of RFX3 in brain ventricular lining cells.
- *Ciliogenesis:* Full knockout mice show stunted nodal monocilia with frequent left-right asymmetry defects and situs inversus (PMID: 15121860), and impaired growth and beating efficiency of motile cilia in multiciliated brain cells with reduced FOXJ1 and axonemal dynein expression (PMID: 19671664).
- *Pancreatic/metabolic:* Rfx3-null mice show reduced insulin-producing beta-cells, impaired glucose tolerance, and disorganized islets (PMID: 17229940; PMID: 20413507) — findings relevant to potential metabolic comorbidities but less directly tied to the ASD phenotype.
- *Lethality/Mendelian ratio:* The full knockout is associated with sub-Mendelian survival ratios and embryonic lethality (PMID: 15121860), indicating that complete biallelic loss is not well tolerated — consistent with the haploinsufficiency model in humans where heterozygous variants cause disease.
- *Xenopus:* Rfx3 knockdown in Xenopus caused abnormal heart looping and defective left-right asymmetry, rescued by exogenous rfx3, placing RFX3 downstream of the RNF20/H2Bub1 chromatin remodeling pathway (PMID: 31235600).

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The mouse corpus callosum agenesis phenotype is particularly relevant, as structural brain anomalies and neurodevelopmental delay are core features of the human syndrome. The embryonic lethality of full knockouts supports haploinsufficiency as the operative mechanism in heterozygous human patients. Ciliogenesis defects in mouse brain cells are consistent with emerging evidence that primary cilia play roles in neuronal differentiation and cortical development.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent mouse knockout studies from different laboratories demonstrate reproducible, severe neurological and developmental phenotypes, with direct mechanistic relevance to the human neurodevelopmental syndrome.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
RFX3 functions as a transcription factor binding X-box DNA motifs to regulate gene expression in ciliated cells. Key molecular mechanisms include:
- **Ciliogenesis regulation:** RFX3 directly controls expression of intraflagellar transport genes, axonemal dynein subunits, and FOXJ1, a master regulator of motile ciliogenesis. RFX3 physically interacts with FOXJ1 to co-activate ciliated gene promoters in human airway epithelium (PMID: 23822649).
- **Auto-fatty acylation:** RFX3 undergoes enzyme-independent S-fatty acylation at a conserved cysteine in its dimerization domain; a fatty acylation-deficient mutant shows impaired homodimerization, fails to promote ciliary gene expression, and disrupts Hedgehog signaling (PMID: 30127002).
- **Neuronal activity-dependent gene regulation:** In human iPSC-derived neurons and forebrain organoids, monoallelic loss of RFX3 disrupted synaptic target gene expression and diminished activity-dependent gene expression. RFX3 binding sites co-localize with CREB binding sites near activity-dependent genes, and RFX3 deficiency reduces CREB binding and impairs induction of CREB targets upon neuronal depolarization (PMID: 40060598). This is a mechanistically novel and ASD-relevant finding.
- **Interneuron development:** RFX3 knockdown in human cortical interneuron progenitor models caused precocious neuronal differentiation and disrupted interneuron progenitor specification, further supporting its role as an ASD risk gene through interneuron circuit development (PMID: 38827400).
- **Pancreatic islet differentiation:** RFX3 is essential for human iPSC-derived pancreatic islet organoid differentiation; CRISPR knockout impaired beta cell function and insulin secretion (PMID: 40263183), consistent with mouse data.
- **Epigenetic regulation:** H2B monoubiquitination at the Rfx3 locus is tissue-specifically regulated, placing RFX3 within a broader chromatin remodeling pathway (PMID: 31235600).

**Expression pattern:**
RFX3 is strongly expressed in developing and adult human brain (PMID: 33658631). X-box binding motifs and RFX ChIP-seq peaks are enriched in cis-regulatory regions of established ASD risk genes, suggesting RFX3 acts as a transcriptional hub upstream of a broader ASD gene network.

**Key experimental findings relevant to ASD:**
The iPSC neuron study (PMID: 40060598) is particularly compelling: it demonstrates that even monoallelic (heterozygous) loss of RFX3 — mirroring the human haploinsufficiency state — is sufficient to disrupt synaptic gene expression and neuronal plasticity mechanisms. The interneuron progenitor study (PMID: 38827400) adds a developmental cellular mechanism. Together, these studies provide a plausible molecular and cellular basis for the ASD phenotype observed in heterozygous human carriers.

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
Convergent evidence from human iPSC-derived neurons, forebrain organoids, human airway epithelium, mouse primary cells, and biochemical studies establishes RFX3 as a multifunctional transcription factor with clear neurodevelopmental roles directly relevant to ASD pathophysiology.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths:**
1. *Clinical:* At least 65 patients across multiple independent international series share a consistent neurodevelopmental phenotype (ASD, intellectual disability, ADHD) associated with heterozygous LoF and missense variants; de novo occurrence is documented; RFX3 is under haploinsufficient constraint in the human population; and chromosomal deletion studies independently converge on RFX3 as the top candidate gene in 9p deletion syndrome.
2. *Model organism:* Multiple independent mouse knockout studies demonstrate corpus callosum agenesis, hydrocephalus, and ciliogenesis defects — phenotypes directly relevant to human neurodevelopmental disease — with sub-Mendelian survival of full knockouts supporting haploinsufficiency.
3. *Biological:* Human iPSC-derived neuron studies demonstrate that monoallelic RFX3 loss disrupts synaptic and activity-dependent gene expression via impaired CREB co-activation, providing a direct mechanistic link between RFX3 haploinsufficiency and ASD-relevant neuronal dysfunction (PMID: 40060598).

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. Specific variant-level data (HGVS notations, segregation analyses, functional validation of individual variants) are not available from the conference abstracts, limiting precise genotype-phenotype correlation.
2. The total patient count, while substantial, is still relatively modest for a newly emerging gene-disease association, and formal statistical enrichment analyses (e.g., comparing de novo variant rates to genome-wide expectations) have not been reported in detail in the available sources.
3. No patients are explicitly identified from large well-characterized cohorts (DDD, GEL, GREGoR), which would strengthen the epidemiological evidence base.

**Conclusion:**
RFX3 haploinsufficiency is supported by strong and convergent evidence across clinical, model organism, and biological domains as a cause of autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder characterized primarily by autism spectrum disorder, intellectual disability, and ADHD. The gene functions as a transcriptional regulator of both ciliogenesis and neuronal gene networks, with monoallelic loss demonstrated to disrupt synaptic and activity-dependent gene expression in human neurons. RFX3 meets criteria for a well-supported ASD risk gene and warrants inclusion in clinical neurodevelopmental gene panels.

4. The transcription factor RFX3 directs nodal cilium development and left-right asymmetry specification. (PMID: 15121860)
5. RFX3 governs growth and beating efficiency of motile cilia in mouse and controls the expression of genes involved in human ciliopathies. (PMID: 19671664)
6. Novel function of the ciliogenic transcription factor RFX3 in development of the endocrine pancreas. (PMID: 17229940)
7. The transcription factor Rfx3 regulates beta-cell differentiation, function, and glucokinase expression. (PMID: 20413507)
8. Histone H2B monoubiquitination regulates heart development via epigenetic control of cilia motility. (PMID: 31235600)

### Biological Evidence
