# Evidence Report: GABRB3 as an Autism Spectrum Disorder Gene (Heterozygous / Autosomal Dominant)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

GABRB3 encodes the β3 subunit of the type-A γ-aminobutyric acid receptor (GABA-A receptor), a ligand-gated chloride channel that mediates the principal fast inhibitory neurotransmission in the central nervous system. The β3 subunit is among the most abundantly expressed GABA-A receptor subunits in the developing brain and is critical for the establishment and maintenance of inhibitory GABAergic signaling during neurodevelopment.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports/series:** Ten independent publications or conference abstracts were identified, ranging from large multicenter cohort studies to single case reports.

**Total approximate number of patients:** Across the studies with reported patient counts, at least 148 individuals with pathogenic or likely pathogenic GABRB3 variants have been described (26 patients, PMID: 34698933; 71 patients, PMID: 34906499; 3 patients, PMID: 33585817; 37 patients, PMID: 35718920; 4 patients, PMID: 23934111; 4 patients, PMID: 39642202; 1 patient, PMID: 25356899; 1 patient, PMID: 31618474; 1 patient, ASHG 2015 – Guella et al.), with additional patients from the Chinese GEFS+ cohort (PMID: 41366559) not individually enumerated.

**Most common phenotypes observed:** The predominant phenotype across all studies is epilepsy, most commonly developmental and epileptic encephalopathy (DEE), including West syndrome, Lennox-Gastaut syndrome, epilepsy of infancy with migrating focal seizures (EIMFS), and epilepsy with myoclonic-atonic seizures (PMID: 34698933; PMID: 23934111). Seizure onset is typically in infancy (median 3.5–12 months depending on variant location). Intellectual disability ranging from mild to severe, global developmental delay, and hypotonia are consistently reported. Fever sensitivity and febrile seizures plus (GEFS+) represent a milder end of the phenotypic spectrum, particularly associated with N-terminal variants (PMID: 35718920; PMID: 41366559). Abnormal neuroimaging findings (cortical dysplasia, delayed myelination, corpus callosum dysplasia) are noted in a subset (PMID: 34698933). Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) features, including social interaction deficits and communication impairments, are reported in association with GABRB3 variants, though ASD is not always the primary reported diagnosis in clinical series focused on epilepsy.

**Predominant variant type:** Missense variants predominate across all cohorts. The majority are de novo (25 of 26 in PMID: 34698933; confirmed de novo in PMID: 23934111; PMID: 39642202). Loss-of-function variants (nonsense, e.g., p.Arg194*) are reported but less common (PMID: 33585817). One mosaic variant was identified in an EIMFS patient (PMID: 31618474). Functional studies have further classified missense variants as gain-of-function (GOF) or loss-of-function (LOF), with GOF variants associated with earlier onset and more severe phenotypes (PMID: 35383156; PMID: 33585817).

**Cohort membership:** Patients are drawn from the international Epi4K/EPGP consortium (PMID: 23934111), the French EPIGENE network (PMID: 35718920), a Chinese multicenter collaboration (PMID: 34698933), and an international structural mapping collaboration (PMID: 34906499). None are explicitly identified as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL cohort patients in the available abstracts.

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**
The evidence is substantial for GABRB3 as a cause of epileptic encephalopathy and intellectual disability, with strong de novo enrichment and large patient numbers. However, ASD is not consistently the primary reported phenotype in these epilepsy-focused cohorts; it appears as a co-occurring feature rather than the primary indication for genetic testing in most series.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*) and zebrafish (*Danio rerio*) have both been used to model GABRB3 dysfunction.

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

- *Full knockout mice* exhibit spontaneous seizures, EEG abnormalities, increased mortality, and behavioral features overlapping with Angelman syndrome (PMID: 10515160). Homozygous deletion of a large region including Gabrb3 causes perinatal lethality due to cleft palate (PMID: 20808828), while maternal deletion heterozygotes show spontaneous seizures, abnormal EEG, impaired motor function, and learning and memory deficits.

- *Full knockout mice* also display deficits in sociability, social novelty recognition, impaired nesting behavior, and hypoplasia of cerebellar vermal lobules — phenotypes directly relevant to ASD (PMID: 17983671).

- *Heterozygous knockout mice* show increased seizure susceptibility, tactile and heat hypersensitivity, sensorimotor deficits, and altered prepulse inhibition, with gender- and parent-of-origin-dependent effects (PMID: 20699105). A separate heterozygous knockout study demonstrated that Gabrb3 deficiency in the context of maternal immune activation exacerbates impaired neocortical progenitor proliferation and neuronal subtype specification (ASHG 2017 – Moon et al.).

- *Knock-in mice* carrying the N328D variant recapitulate Lennox-Gastaut syndrome with spontaneous seizures, spatial learning and memory deficits, and locomotion impairment (PMID: 37176165). A GOF knock-in model (PMID: 42017314) shows spike-wave discharges, cortical hyperexcitability, hypoactivity, and embryonic lethality in homozygotes, with the heterozygous state producing a viable epileptic phenotype.

- *Conditional endothelial knockout mice* display impaired social interactions, communication deficits, heightened anxiety, and reduced short-term memory alongside vascular abnormalities (PMID: 35318369), suggesting non-neuronal contributions to the behavioral phenotype.

- *Zebrafish full knockouts* exhibit unprovoked electrographic seizure activity confirmed by local field potential recordings (PMID: 34083748).

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The mouse and zebrafish models are highly consistent with the human phenotype, recapitulating epilepsy, intellectual disability, social deficits, and anxiety — the core features of GABRB3-related neurodevelopmental disorder. The heterozygous mouse models are particularly relevant to the autosomal dominant human condition.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:** GABRB3 encodes a structural subunit of pentameric GABA-A receptors, which mediate fast inhibitory neurotransmission via chloride ion flux. Pathogenic variants alter receptor function in two mechanistically distinct directions: LOF variants reduce inhibitory GABAergic tone (e.g., reduced miniature inhibitory post-synaptic current amplitude, impaired receptor surface expression and synaptic clustering as shown for N328D and E357K in PMID: 31435640; D120N in PMID: 32467926), while GOF variants increase GABA potency and tonic chloride conductance, paradoxically causing hyperexcitability through collapse of feed-forward inhibition onto pyramidal neurons (PMID: 42017314; PMID: 33585817). The direction of functional change has direct clinical implications: GOF variants are associated with vigabatrin hypersensitivity, whereas LOF variants may benefit from GABAergic augmentation (PMID: 35383156).

**Expression pattern:** GABRB3 maps to chromosome 15q11-q13, within the Prader-Willi/Angelman syndrome imprinted genomic region, in a cluster with GABRA5 and GABRG3 (PMID: 9126483). The β3 subunit is highly expressed in the developing brain, particularly in the neocortex, hippocampus, thalamus, and cerebellum. In vivo calcium imaging studies demonstrate that Gabrb3 is enriched in contralaterally projecting pyramidal neurons and is required for inhibitory synapse formation and normal cortical network synchrony (PMID: 36446382). Human transcriptomic data confirm that GABRB3 expression correlates spatially with atypical functional connectivity patterns observed in ASD subjects (PMID: 36446382).

**Key experimental findings relevant to ASD:**
- Deletion of Gabrb3 specifically in peripheral somatosensory neurons causes mechanosensory dysfunction and, when occurring during development, produces social interaction deficits and anxiety-like behavior, directly linking peripheral GABAergic signaling to ASD-relevant behaviors (PMID: 27293187).
- A genome-wide CNV analysis of over 3,000 ASD cases and 7,000 controls identified GABA-A receptor signaling as the most significantly disrupted canonical pathway in ASD (P ≤ 2.1×10⁻²¹, OR=9.9), driven substantially by CNVs encompassing GABRB3, GABRA5, and GABRG3 (ASHG 2011 – Hadley et al.).
- Gabrb3 ablation in cortical pyramidal neurons leads to decreased GABAergic synapses, increased local network synchrony, and enhanced functional connectivity — a pattern mirroring the atypical connectivity observed in human ASD (PMID: 36446382).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG** (for GABRB3 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with epilepsy and intellectual disability as primary features; MODERATE specifically for ASD as the primary phenotypic indication)

**Key strengths:**
1. Large numbers of patients with de novo GABRB3 variants and well-characterized epileptic encephalopathy phenotypes across multiple independent international cohorts, with robust genotype-phenotype correlations established.
2. Multiple mouse models — full knockout, heterozygous knockout, and knock-in — consistently recapitulate epilepsy, social deficits, cognitive impairment, and anxiety, directly supporting autosomal dominant pathogenicity in the heterozygous state.
3. Strong mechanistic understanding at the molecular level, with electrophysiological classification of variants into GOF and LOF categories that predict distinct clinical outcomes, and genome-wide evidence implicating the GABA-A receptor pathway in ASD.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. ASD is not the primary reported phenotype in most clinical series, which are predominantly epilepsy-focused; systematic ascertainment of ASD features (e.g., ADOS/ADI-R scores) is largely absent from the published cohorts.
2. Specific variant-level data (HGVS notations) are unavailable for the majority of patients in the larger cohort studies, limiting precise genotype-phenotype analysis for ASD-specific features.
3. The genomic location of GABRB3 within the 15q11-q13 imprinted region introduces interpretive complexity, as some phenotypes (particularly Angelman syndrome features) may reflect contiguous gene effects rather than isolated GABRB3 haploinsufficiency.

**Conclusion:** GABRB3 is a well-established autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene, with the strongest evidence supporting its role in epileptic encephalopathy, intellectual disability, and developmental delay. ASD features — including social deficits, communication impairments, and anxiety — are consistently observed in both human patients and multiple mouse models, and are mechanistically supported by the gene's role in GABAergic inhibitory synapse formation and cortical network development. The evidence is sufficient to classify GABRB3 as a high-confidence ASD-associated gene, though epilepsy and intellectual disability typically dominate the clinical presentation and ASD-specific phenotyping in patient cohorts remains incomplete.
