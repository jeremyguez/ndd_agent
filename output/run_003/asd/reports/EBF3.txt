# EBF3 Evidence Report: Autism Spectrum Disorder and Autosomal Dominant Neurodevelopmental Disorder

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

EBF3 (Early B-Cell Factor 3) is a member of the COE family of transcription factors characterized by a highly conserved atypical zinc finger DNA-binding domain and a helix-loop-helix dimerization domain. It plays critical roles in neuronal differentiation, transcriptional regulation of cell cycle arrest and apoptosis, and neurodevelopmental gene expression programs, with particularly high relevance in the developing brain and nervous system.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent case reports/series:** Ten independent publications reporting clinical cohorts or case series were identified, spanning 2017 to 2025.

**Total approximate number of patients:** Across all studies, approximately 186+ individuals with pathogenic EBF3 variants have been reported, including 10 patients (PMID: 28017373), 3 patients (PMID: 28017372), an unspecified number from the DDD/CAUSES cohorts (PMID: 28017370), 83 patients in a large genotype-phenotype study (PMID: 35340043), 8 patients from Japan (PMID: 34050706), 6 patients from Italy (PMID: 37090941), 12 Chinese patients (PMID: 36937983), 11 patients including one mosaic duplication/triplication case (PMID: 34999443), 1 case report (PMID: 40073166), and 52 patients in a urologic manifestations study (PMID: 37718233).

**Most common phenotypes observed:** The core phenotypic spectrum — collectively termed Hypotonia, Ataxia, and Delayed Development Syndrome (HADDS) — includes:
- Global developmental delay and intellectual disability (present in virtually all reported cases)
- Speech/expressive language delay
- Cerebellar ataxia
- Hypotonia (congenital)
- Facial dysmorphism
- Cerebellar malformation (including vermis hypoplasia and a distinctive "dandelion sign" on MRI, described in PMID: 37090941)
- Genitourinary abnormalities, particularly neurogenic bladder and vesicoureteral reflux (67% of a literature review cohort; PMID: 37718233)
- Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) features, especially in individuals with zinc finger domain (ZNF) missense variants (PMID: 35340043)
- Behavioral problems including oppositional defiant disorder and hyperactivity (PMID: 40073166)

**Predominant variant type:** Both loss-of-function (LoF) variants (nonsense, frameshift, splice-site) and missense variants are well-represented. Haploinsufficiency is supported by the recurrence of 10q26 deletions encompassing EBF3 producing the same HADDS phenotype (PMID: 34050706; PMID: 37090941). A genotype-phenotype correlation has been established: missense variants disrupting the zinc finger domain (ZNF) are associated with greater phenotypic severity and higher rates of ASD, while missense variants in the DNA-binding domain (DBD), such as the recurrent p.Arg209Trp, and LoF variants are associated with comparatively milder or more variable severity (PMID: 35340043). The majority of variants are de novo; one notable exception is p.Arg209Trp (c.625C>T), which was inherited from a mosaic mother in two affected siblings (PMID: 28017373). Duplication/triplication mosaicism of the EBF3 locus has also been reported, further underscoring gene-dosage sensitivity (PMID: 34999443).

**Cohort representation:** Patients from the DDD (Deciphering Developmental Disorders) study are explicitly represented (PMID: 28017370), as is the CAUSES study (Canada). Additional patients derive from international GeneMatcher-enabled collaborations (PMID: 28017373), institutional cohorts in Japan, Italy, and China, and a large international registry-based cohort (PMID: 35340043).

**Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG**
The evidence is supported by multiple independent publications across diverse international cohorts, a large cumulative patient count (>180), consistent core phenotype, recurrent de novo variants, functional validation of pathogenicity, and an established genotype-phenotype correlation including ASD as a prominent feature of ZNF-domain variants.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*), zebrafish (*Danio rerio*), *Xenopus laevis*, and *Drosophila melanogaster* have all been used to study EBF3 function.

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

- **Mouse full knockout:** Homozygous Ebf3 knockout mice die perinatally due to respiratory failure caused by a hypercontractile diaphragm; the lungs fail to unfold at birth due to impaired calcium efflux in muscle, with downregulation of Serca1 (Atp2a1). Transgenic rescue of Serca1 expression corrects the phenotype, confirming the mechanistic link (PMID: 24786561). A separate full knockout demonstrated sternum ossification defects with defective Runx2+ pre-osteoblast generation (PMID: 32398354).
- **Mouse enhancer knockout:** Deletion of a brain-expressed EBF3 enhancer (Rr169617) produced sub-Mendelian ratios (p=0.02), with depletion of the deletion allele (p=5.8×10⁻⁴), suggesting developmental disadvantage. Heterozygous and homozygous enhancer knockout mice showed 10% and 20% reductions in Ebf3 expression, respectively, with dysregulation of brain development genes (including *Chd5* and *Ntng1*) and histone genes in E12.5 forebrain, midbrain, and hindbrain. Sex-specific behavioral differences were observed, with homozygous knockout males showing reduced mobility (PMID: 39829799).
- **Mouse conditional knockout:** Loss of Ebf3 in CAR/LepR+ mesenchymal stem cells impaired hematopoietic stem cell niche function and caused osteosclerosis (PMID: 29593065), and Ebf3-expressing neurons in the medial amygdala failed to be generated in Pax6 knockout mice (PMID: 29869132), placing Ebf3 within a neuronal specification pathway.
- **Mouse knockdown/overexpression:** Suppression of Ebf3 in an embryonic stem cell dopaminergic differentiation model significantly reduced TH+ dopaminergic neuron numbers, while overexpression increased them, demonstrating a direct role in dopaminergic neurogenesis (PMID: 25192643).
- **Xenopus knockdown:** Morpholino-mediated knockdown disrupted somite organization, impaired hypaxial muscle migration, and downregulated key myogenic transcription factors (PMID: 21839736). Ebf3 overexpression drove ectopic neurogenesis via induction of Maturin, and Maturin loss-of-function blocked Ebf3-driven neurogenesis (PMID: 24095902).
- **Drosophila:** EBF3 ZNF-domain missense variants failed to restore viability in UAS-GAL4 rescue assays, while the DBD variant p.Arg209Trp partially rescued viability, directly paralleling the human genotype-phenotype correlation (PMID: 35340043).

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The neurological and neurodevelopmental phenotypes observed in model organisms — including impaired neuronal differentiation, dopaminergic neuron development, brain gene dysregulation, and behavioral abnormalities — are broadly consistent with the human HADDS/ASD phenotype. Perinatal lethality in full mouse knockouts reflects the essential nature of the gene, while heterozygous and enhancer models more closely recapitulate the human haploinsufficiency context.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent organisms and experimental approaches converge on EBF3's essential role in neuronal differentiation, brain development, and gene regulation, with direct functional validation of human disease variants in Drosophila.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:** EBF3 is a sequence-specific transcriptional activator that binds DNA through its atypical zinc finger domain and regulates downstream gene expression programs. Key molecular functions include:
- Direct transcriptional regulation of cell cycle regulators p21(cip1/waf1), p27(kip1), and p57(kip2), inducing cell cycle arrest and apoptosis (PMID: 17018599)
- Genome-wide chromatin binding and transcriptional activation, with mutant EBF3 proteins showing cytoplasmic mislocalization, reduced chromatin association, and loss of transcriptional activation of CDKN1A (PMID: 28017373)
- Direct transcriptional activation of CNTNAP4 in dopaminergic neurons, with neuroprotective effects in Parkinson's disease models (PMID: 38479556)
- Downstream mediation of Notch signaling in skeletal stem and progenitor cells, regulating cell fate decisions (PMID: 37752132)
- Inhibition of osteoblast differentiation and maintenance of hematopoietic stem cell niches in bone marrow (PMID: 29563184)

**Expression pattern:** EBF3 is expressed in the developing and adult brain, with particularly high relevance in neuronal populations. A brain-specific enhancer (hs737) drives EBF3 expression in human fetal brain, and de novo variants in this enhancer were identified in autism families, with in vitro assays confirming reduced enhancer activity (PMID: 34256850). EBF3 is also expressed in retinal amacrine interneurons (PMID: 29258872), dopaminergic neurons of the midbrain (PMID: 25192643), and medial amygdala neurons (PMID: 29869132), consistent with its broad neurodevelopmental role.

**Key experimental findings:**
- EBF3 is genome-wide significant for coding de novo variants in neurodevelopmental disorders: missense p=8.12×10⁻³⁵, LoF p=2.26×10⁻¹³ (PMID: 34256850)
- ChIP-seq in neuronal cells showed EBF3 promoter binding is enriched for NDD-associated genes (p=7.43×10⁻⁶, OR=1.87; PMID: 34256850)
- In vitro luciferase assays confirmed that ZNF-domain variants impair transcriptional activation, while p.Arg209Trp preserves partial activity (PMID: 35340043)
- Four novel missense variants in Chinese patients showed altered subcellular localization and significantly decreased transcriptional activity (p<0.01) in vitro (PMID: 36937983)
- EBF3 locus is deleted or methylated in 73% of brain tumors, and its silencing is observed across multiple tumor types, indicating it functions as a tumor suppressor with broad transcriptional regulatory importance (PMID: 17018599)
- Noncoding de novo variants in the brain-specific EBF3 enhancer hs737 were identified in autism families, with a combined enhancer significance of p=1.1×10⁻⁴, and individuals with these noncoding variants presented with autism and hypotonia rather than full HADDS (PMID: 34256850)

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
Convergent evidence from ChIP-seq, RNA-seq, luciferase reporter assays, genome-wide statistical analyses, and cell-based functional studies robustly supports EBF3's role as a transcriptional regulator with direct relevance to neurodevelopmental gene networks and ASD.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths:**
1. **Large, replicated clinical dataset:** More than 180 individuals with pathogenic EBF3 variants have been reported across at least 10 independent publications from multiple continents, with consistent core phenotypes and a well-established genotype-phenotype correlation linking ZNF-domain missense variants specifically to ASD and greater severity (PMID: 35340043).
2. **Convergent functional validation:** Mutant EBF3 proteins consistently demonstrate loss of nuclear localization, reduced chromatin binding, and impaired transcriptional activation across multiple independent functional assays (PMID: 28017373; PMID: 36937983; PMID: 35340043), and Drosophila in vivo rescue assays directly recapitulate the human genotype-phenotype gradient.
3. **Genome-wide statistical significance and noncoding evidence:** EBF3 achieves genome-wide significance for de novo coding variants in NDDs (PMID: 34256850), and noncoding de novo variants in a brain-specific EBF3 enhancer have been independently linked to simplex autism, broadening the mutational spectrum.

**Key gaps or limitations:**
- The number of patients with ASD as a primary or isolated diagnosis (rather than as a feature within HADDS) remains relatively small, and prospective systematic ASD assessment across the full EBF3-NDD cohort is lacking.
- Full knockout mouse models are perinatally lethal, limiting direct neurological phenotyping; heterozygous mouse models with neurological readouts are undercharacterized relative to the human haploinsufficiency phenotype.
- Specific variant-level functional data (e.g., for individual LoF variants) and longitudinal natural history data are limited, making precise genotype-phenotype predictions for individual patients challenging.

**Conclusion:** EBF3 is a well-established autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene with strong, multi-layered evidence supporting its role in intellectual disability, ataxia, hypotonia, and ASD. Heterozygous pathogenic variants — particularly missense variants disrupting the zinc finger domain — are associated with ASD as a prominent feature, supported by genome-wide statistical significance, consistent functional impairment of transcriptional activity, and direct in vivo validation in Drosophila. The gene meets criteria for a definitive disease-gene relationship for HADDS/EBF3-related neurodevelopmental disorder, with ASD representing a clinically significant and genotype-enriched component of the phenotypic spectrum.
