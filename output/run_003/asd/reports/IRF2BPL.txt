# Literature Evidence Report: IRF2BPL as a Candidate Gene for Autosomal Dominant Neurodevelopmental Disorder

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

IRF2BPL (Interferon Regulatory Factor 2 Binding Protein-Like) encodes a member of the IRF2BP family of transcriptional regulators that contains a conserved N-terminal zinc finger domain and a C-terminal RING-finger domain; it functions as both a transcriptional repressor and an E3 ubiquitin ligase, with established roles in repressing Wnt/β-catenin signaling and maintaining neuronal integrity. It is broadly expressed, including prominently in the nervous system, and is highly intolerant to loss-of-function variation (pLI = 0.97).

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports/series:** Ten independent publications reporting patients with IRF2BPL variants were identified, spanning 2018–2025.

**Total approximate number of patients:** Approximately 61 patients across all studies (7 from PMID: 30057031; 11 from PMID: 30166628; 18 from PMID: 38235039; 10 from PMID: 39843638; 3 from PMID: 38481258; 3 from PMID: 37114479; 1 from PMID: 39224955; 6 from PMID: 34102826; 1 from PMID: 38650104; 1 from PMID: 36670390).

**Most common phenotypes observed:** The predominant clinical presentation is a neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by:
- Global developmental delay or intellectual disability (present across virtually all series)
- Epilepsy/seizures, including infantile spasms, developmental epileptic encephalopathy, and progressive myoclonus epilepsy
- Neurodevelopmental regression following a period of apparently normal development
- Hypotonia
- Progressive ataxia and cerebellar involvement
- Abnormal movements including dystonia and myoclonus
- Loss of speech

This constellation has been formally designated **NEDAMSS** (Neurodevelopmental disorder with regression, Abnormal Movements, loss of Speech, and Seizures). Notably, phenotypic variability exists: some patients present with severe early-onset epileptic encephalopathy (PMID: 30166628; PMID: 34102826), others with progressive myoclonus epilepsy (PMID: 37114479), and at least one large pedigree demonstrated late-onset cerebellar ataxia with mild intellectual disability and incomplete penetrance (PMID: 38235039). A single case presented with isolated rapid-progressive dystonia without epilepsy or developmental delay (PMID: 36670390), further expanding the phenotypic spectrum.

**Important note regarding autism spectrum disorder (ASD):** None of the identified publications report ASD as a primary or prominent phenotype associated with IRF2BPL variants. The established phenotype is a progressive neurological disorder centered on regression, epilepsy, movement abnormalities, and intellectual disability — not ASD. There is no direct clinical evidence in the current literature linking IRF2BPL to ASD specifically.

**Predominant variant type:** Loss-of-function (LoF) variants — including nonsense, frameshift, and truncating variants — predominate and are consistently associated with the most severe phenotypes. Missense variants have also been reported across multiple studies (PMID: 30057031; PMID: 39843638; PMID: 38481258; PMID: 34102826) and are associated with overlapping but sometimes milder phenotypes. All reported pathogenic variants are heterozygous, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. The vast majority are de novo, though one family demonstrated inherited truncating variants with incomplete penetrance (PMID: 38235039).

**Cohort affiliations:** One study drew patients from the Undiagnosed Diseases Network (UDN) (PMID: 30057031). The remaining studies were conducted through international data-sharing collaborations, single-center series, or unspecified cohorts. No patients from GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL cohorts were explicitly identified in the available abstracts.

**Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG** (for IRF2BPL as a cause of autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder/NEDAMSS; however, **NONE** specifically for ASD).

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Drosophila melanogaster, zebrafish (Danio rerio), and mouse (Mus musculus).

**Key findings:**

- **Drosophila:** The IRF2BPL ortholog *pits* is broadly expressed including in the nervous system. Complete loss of *pits* is lethal early in development, while partial neuronal RNAi knockdown causes progressive neurodegeneration (PMID: 30057031). Human nonsense variants behave as severe loss-of-function alleles in complementation assays, while missense variants produce a range of phenotypic severities, directly mirroring the human genotype-phenotype correlation.

- **Zebrafish:** Two independent zebrafish knockdown studies have been reported. Morpholino-mediated knockdown of *irf2bpl* produced neurological defects associated with increased Wnt1 transcription and downstream Wnt signaling, which were suppressed by pharmacological Wnt inhibition (PMID: 35044823). A second zebrafish knockdown model demonstrated reduced body length and spontaneous ictal-like and interictal-like electrographic discharges, directly recapitulating the human seizure phenotype of NEDAMSS (PMID: 38481258).

- **Mouse:** AAV-mediated knockdown of *Irf2bpl* in a destabilization of medial meniscus model worsened osteoarthritis severity (PMID: 35733354), confirming the gene's role in β-catenin ubiquitination in non-neurological tissues, though this model is not directly relevant to the neurological phenotype.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The Drosophila and zebrafish models are highly consistent with the human neurological phenotype, demonstrating neurodegeneration, seizures, and electrographic abnormalities that parallel NEDAMSS. No ASD-specific behavioral phenotypes have been reported in any model organism.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG** (for neurological disease mechanism; not specifically for ASD).

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

IRF2BPL operates at the intersection of transcriptional regulation and ubiquitin-mediated protein degradation. Its best-characterized molecular role is as a repressor of Wnt/β-catenin signaling: IRF2BPL and its Drosophila ortholog Pits repress Wnt target gene transcription, physically interact with the Wnt antagonist CKIα, and promote ubiquitination and degradation of β-catenin independently of GSK-3β (PMID: 35044823; PMID: 29374064). Loss of IRF2BPL leads to excess Wnt signaling, which has been mechanistically linked to neuronal loss in both fly and zebrafish models (PMID: 35044823).

Additional mechanistic insights include:
- Truncating IRF2BPL variants sequester wild-type protein in the cytoplasm and cause protein aggregation, suggesting a dominant-negative mechanism in addition to haploinsufficiency (PMID: 36476864). Patient-derived astrocytes harboring truncating variants fail to support neuronal survival in co-culture and exhibit mitochondrial dysfunction and aberrant respiratory function; these defects were rescued by CuATSM treatment in vitro (PMID: 36476864).
- In cells with truncating mutations, the level of ubiquitinated proteins is significantly decreased, implicating impairment of the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway (PMID: 33278788).
- Postmortem neuropathological analysis of one NEDAMSS patient revealed polyglutamine (polyQ) inclusions and findings consistent with dentatorubral-pallidoluysian atrophy (DRPLA), suggesting the polyQ repeat tract of IRF2BPL may contribute to a novel aggregation-based disease mechanism (PMID: 39224955).
- IRF2BPL also regulates the FOSL2/PI3K/AKT/mTOR axis through ubiquitin-mediated degradation of FOSL2 in osteosarcoma (PMID: 40834975), and acts as a transcriptional activator of IGFBP2 in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (PMID: 41413713), indicating context-dependent functional versatility.

**Expression pattern:** IRF2BPL is broadly expressed with prominent expression in the nervous system, as demonstrated in Drosophila (PMID: 30057031) and supported by its elevated expression in patient-derived astrocytes (PMID: 35044823). Proteomic studies have also detected IRF2BPL regulation in retinal ganglion cells under neurodegenerative conditions (PMID: 34445296), further supporting neurological relevance.

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG** (for neuronal function and Wnt/ubiquitin pathway involvement; no specific biological evidence linking IRF2BPL to ASD pathways such as synaptic development or social behavior circuits).

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level for IRF2BPL as an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disease gene: STRONG**

**Overall evidence level for IRF2BPL as an autism spectrum disorder gene specifically: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths of the evidence:**
- Robust, replicated clinical evidence across ten independent publications and approximately 61 patients from multiple international centers, with consistent autosomal dominant (predominantly de novo) inheritance
- Strong genotype-phenotype correlation, with LoF variants causing the most severe phenotypes (epileptic encephalopathy, NEDAMSS) and missense variants associated with milder or overlapping presentations
- Convergent model organism evidence in Drosophila and zebrafish directly recapitulating human neurological phenotypes including neurodegeneration and seizures
- Well-characterized molecular mechanism involving Wnt/β-catenin repression, ubiquitin-proteasome pathway function, and mitochondrial integrity in neurons

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- No published evidence specifically links IRF2BPL variants to autism spectrum disorder; ASD is not reported as a phenotypic feature in any of the identified studies
- Incomplete penetrance has been documented in at least one family (PMID: 38235039), complicating variant interpretation in some contexts
- Most model organism studies use knockdown rather than true knockout approaches, and no mammalian knockout model with detailed behavioral phenotyping has been reported
- The mechanistic basis for phenotypic variability (severe epileptic encephalopathy vs. late-onset ataxia vs. isolated dystonia) remains incompletely understood

**Conclusion:** IRF2BPL is a well-established autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disease gene, with strong and replicated clinical, model organism, and biological evidence supporting its role in causing NEDAMSS and related neurological phenotypes through heterozygous, predominantly de novo loss-of-function variants. However, the available literature provides **no direct evidence** supporting IRF2BPL as an autism spectrum disorder gene; the clinical phenotype is characterized by neurological regression, epilepsy, and movement disorders rather than the social communication deficits and restricted/repetitive behaviors that define ASD. Any assertion of an IRF2BPL-ASD association would require dedicated clinical and functional studies not currently present in the published literature.
