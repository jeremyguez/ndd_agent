# Literature Evidence Report: GABBR2 as an Autism Spectrum Disorder / Neurodevelopmental Disorder Gene (Autosomal Dominant)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

GABBR2 encodes the GB2 subunit of the metabotropic GABA-B receptor (GABABR), a heterodimeric G-protein-coupled receptor that mediates slow, sustained inhibitory neurotransmission in the central nervous system. The GB2 subunit is essential for receptor surface trafficking, G-protein coupling, and downstream signal transduction, making it a critical regulator of neuronal excitability, synaptic plasticity, and neurodevelopment.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports and patient counts:**
Across the structured evidence reviewed, at least seven independent publications or conference reports describe patients with pathogenic or likely pathogenic de novo GABBR2 variants. These include a primary case series of four patients (PMID: 28856709; corroborated by ASHG 2017 – Yoo et al.), a single-patient case report with detailed phenotyping (PMID: 35414446), a Portuguese cohort study identifying one GABBR2 case among 19 RTT-like patients (PMID: 26740508), a pediatric epilepsy cohort yielding one additional case (PMID: 31957018), a large whole-genome sequencing meta-analysis of developmental and epileptic encephalopathy (DEE) cohorts reporting multiple GABBR2 de novo mutations (PMID: 29100083), a functional study characterizing seven de novo GABBR2 variants from individuals with autism spectrum disorder (ASD), intellectual disability (ID), and/or ADHD (PMID: 41803176), and a Landau-Kleffner syndrome cohort identifying one likely pathogenic GABBR2 variant (PMID: 40944498). The total number of individually described patients is at minimum 12–15, with additional uncounted cases contributing to the meta-analysis in PMID: 29100083 and the variant series in PMID: 41803176.

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
The predominant phenotypes span a severity spectrum. At the milder end, patients present with a Rett syndrome-like (RTT-like) phenotype characterized by neurodevelopmental regression, poor language, loss of hand skills, and hypotonia (PMID: 28856709; PMID: 26740508; PMID: 35414446). At the more severe end, patients present with developmental and epileptic encephalopathy (DEE), pharmaco-resistant seizures, infantile spasms with hypsarrhythmia, intellectual disability, and developmental plateauing or regression (PMID: 28856709; PMID: 29100083; PMID: 31957018). Additional phenotypes include paroxysmal limb dystonia, motor stereotypies, non-epileptic hyperventilation, breath-holding episodes, and feeding difficulties requiring gastrostomy (PMID: 35414446). ASD, ID, and ADHD have also been reported in association with de novo GABBR2 missense variants (PMID: 41803176). One patient presented with Landau-Kleffner syndrome with epileptic encephalopathy with spike-wave activation in sleep and speech/language impairment (PMID: 40944498).

**Predominant variant type:**
All reported pathogenic variants are heterozygous de novo missense variants. No truncating loss-of-function (LoF) variants have been reported as disease-causing in this dataset. Specific variants with recurrent documentation include p.G693W (NM_005458:c.G2077T; PMID: 35414446; PMID: 39028675), p.S695I (PMID: 39028675; PMID: 40994051), p.I705N (PMID: 39028675; PMID: 40994051), and p.A567T (PMID: 40994051). The functional consequences are heterogeneous, encompassing both gain-of-function (constitutive receptor activation) and loss-of-function (reduced surface expression, reduced GABA potency) mechanisms, with phenotypic severity correlating with the degree of functional disruption (PMID: 28856709).

**Cohort origins:**
Patients originate from Korean and European centers (PMID: 28856709), Portugal (PMID: 26740508), and unspecified international centers contributing to large DEE sequencing consortia (PMID: 29100083). No patients are explicitly identified as belonging to named cohorts such as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL based on the available abstracts.

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**
Multiple independent de novo reports across distinct populations, functional correlation with phenotypic severity, and recurrence of specific variants support a moderate-to-strong clinical signal. The evidence is rated MODERATE rather than STRONG primarily because the total number of individually characterized patients remains relatively small, variant-level details are incompletely reported in several abstracts, and the ASD-specific patient numbers from PMID: 41803176 are not individually enumerated.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:**
Three model systems have been employed: mouse (*Mus musculus*), African clawed frog (*Xenopus tropicalis*), and zebrafish (*Danio rerio*; ASHG 2015 abstract, knockdown studies described as planned/in progress).

**Key phenotypes from knockouts and knock-ins:**

*Mouse:* Conditional knockout mice with ubiquitous Cre-mediated deletion of a floxed *Gabbr2* allele fully recapitulated the germline knockout phenotype, including spontaneous epileptiform activity, hyperlocomotor activity, hyperalgesia, impaired memory, increased neuronal cell death, altered neuronal architecture throughout cortex, hippocampus, and cerebellum, and premature death (PMID: 41397015). A knock-in mouse carrying the epileptic encephalopathy-associated p.I705N variant showed abnormal high-amplitude delta-band EEG synchronization, increased constitutive GABABR activity, reduced agonist responsiveness, and compensatory downregulation of GB1/GB2 subunits and G-protein signaling components (PMID: 40994051). Treatment with a positive allosteric modulator normalized spontaneous network activity in this model, providing proof-of-concept for precision therapeutic intervention.

*Xenopus:* Knock-in expression of patient-derived GABBR2 variants in *Xenopus* oocytes demonstrated that milder loss-of-function variants recapitulate RTT-like receptor dysfunction, while more severe variants produce epileptic encephalopathy-like functional profiles; functional rescue was achieved with a GABA-B receptor agonist (PMID: 28856709). A separate *Xenopus* study confirmed that p.G693W, p.S695I, and p.I705N variants impair neuronal cell surface expression of GABABRs, reduce signaling efficacy, and elevate presynaptic Ca²⁺ signaling, with reversal by positive allosteric modulation (PMID: 39028675).

**Consistency with human phenotype:**
The model organism data are highly consistent with the human phenotype. Epileptiform activity, cognitive impairment, and neurodevelopmental abnormalities observed in mice mirror the DEE and RTT-like presentations in patients. The genotype-phenotype correlation (severity of functional disruption predicting clinical severity) is reproduced across both *Xenopus* and mouse systems.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent model systems, including germline knockout, conditional knockout, and patient-variant knock-in models, consistently reproduce neurological phenotypes relevant to the human disease. Therapeutic rescue experiments further validate the mechanistic link.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
GABBR2 encodes the obligate signaling subunit of the heterodimeric GABA-B receptor. Upon GABA binding to the GB1 subunit, GB2 couples to Gi/o proteins, inhibiting adenylyl cyclase, activating inwardly rectifying K⁺ channels (GIRK), and inhibiting voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels, collectively suppressing neuronal excitability and neurotransmitter release. De novo variants in GABBR2 produce both gain-of-function (constitutive receptor activation, increased constitutive activity reaching 50–100% of maximal GABA-induced activity for variants p.A567T, p.S695I, and p.I705N) and loss-of-function (reduced surface expression, reduced GABA potency) effects, as characterized in heterologous cell systems (PMID: 40994051; PMID: 41803176; PMID: 39028675).

**Expression pattern:**
GABBR2 is broadly expressed throughout the central nervous system, with particularly high levels in cortex, hippocampus, and cerebellum — regions critical for cognition, memory, and motor function. The conditional knockout mouse data confirm that GABBR2 disruption in these regions produces the core neurological phenotype (PMID: 41397015). Beyond the CNS, GABBR2 expression has been documented in enteric neurons (PMID: 40033120), vascular endothelium (PMID: 34422926), and various cancer cell lines, though these peripheral roles are not directly relevant to the neurodevelopmental disease mechanism.

**Key experimental findings:**
Functional characterization of seven de novo GABBR2 missense variants from ASD/ID/ADHD patients revealed a spectrum of molecular consequences — increased constitutive activity, reduced GABA potency, and reduced surface expression — all converging on decreased net GABAergic efficacy (PMID: 41803176). The p.I705N knock-in mouse model demonstrated that constitutive GABABR activity produces pathological EEG synchronization and that this can be normalized pharmacologically (PMID: 40994051). Transcriptome-wide analysis showed that GABBR2 bridges RTT- and EE-associated gene networks, providing a molecular explanation for the phenotypic overlap (PMID: 28856709). Presynaptic Ca²⁺ dysregulation caused by impaired GABABR surface expression was identified as a key pathomechanism for loss-of-function variants, reversible by positive allosteric modulation (PMID: 39028675).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent functional studies in heterologous cell systems and in vivo models have defined clear molecular mechanisms linking GABBR2 variants to neuronal dysfunction. The convergence of gain- and loss-of-function mechanisms on a common endpoint of disrupted GABAergic inhibition provides a coherent biological framework.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE–STRONG**

**Key strengths:**
- Multiple independent de novo reports across geographically distinct cohorts, with recurrence of specific pathogenic variants (e.g., p.G693W, p.S695I, p.I705N), strongly supporting causality.
- Robust model organism data from mouse knock-in and knockout models that faithfully recapitulate human neurological phenotypes, including epileptiform activity, cognitive impairment, and premature death.
- Mechanistically well-characterized variants with defined gain- and loss-of-function consequences, a clear genotype-phenotype severity correlation, and demonstrated pharmacological rescue, providing both biological plausibility and translational relevance.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- The total number of individually characterized patients with ASD as the primary phenotype remains small; most patients in the literature present with DEE or RTT-like phenotypes, and the ASD/ADHD-specific cases (PMID: 41803176) lack individual clinical detail in the available abstract.
- Variant-level molecular data (specific HGVS notation, segregation, population frequency) are incompletely reported across several studies, limiting precise variant-level classification.
- No patients have been identified from large, well-characterized named cohorts (e.g., DDD, GEL), and the absence of truncating LoF variants as disease-causing raises questions about the full mutational spectrum.

**Conclusion:**
GABBR2 is a well-supported neurodevelopmental disorder gene in which heterozygous de novo missense variants cause a phenotypic spectrum ranging from ASD and intellectual disability to Rett syndrome-like disorder and severe developmental epileptic encephalopathy, with phenotypic severity correlating with the degree of receptor dysfunction. The convergence of clinical genetics, functional characterization, and in vivo model data provides moderate-to-strong evidence for autosomal dominant pathogenicity. Further large-cohort studies with detailed ASD-specific phenotyping and comprehensive variant-level functional data are needed to fully delineate the ASD-specific contribution of GABBR2 and to support definitive gene-disease classification.
