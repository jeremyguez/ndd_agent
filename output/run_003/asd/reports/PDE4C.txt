# Evidence Report: PDE4C as an Autism Spectrum Disorder Gene (Autosomal Dominant)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

PDE4C (Phosphodiesterase 4C) encodes a cyclic AMP (cAMP)-specific phosphodiesterase belonging to the PDE4 family, which hydrolyzes cAMP to 5'-AMP and thereby regulates intracellular cAMP signaling. PDE4C exists as multiple tissue-specific splice variants and plays a role in modulating downstream cAMP/PKA-dependent processes including transcription, cell proliferation, apoptosis, and inflammatory signaling (PMID: 9349724).

- **Total approximate number of patients:** 0
- **Most common phenotypes observed:** None reported in the context of autism spectrum disorder (ASD) or neurodevelopmental disorders.
- **Predominant variant type:** No pathogenic or likely pathogenic heterozygous variants in PDE4C have been reported in ASD or related neurodevelopmental cohorts based on the available evidence.
- **Known cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL):** No cases identified from any of these cohorts.
- **Strength of clinical evidence: NONE**

No clinical evidence was identified linking heterozygous pathogenic variants in PDE4C to autism spectrum disorder or any other Mendelian neurodevelopmental condition. The structured clinical evidence set returned no entries.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
PDE4C functions as a cAMP-hydrolyzing enzyme, and its activity directly modulates the cAMP/PKA signaling axis. Multiple studies confirm this core function across tissue contexts. In spinal neurons, suppression of PDE4C by miR-615-3p activated cAMP/PKA signaling and reduced cellular stress (PMID: 41526970). In endometrial stromal cells, TCF21-driven upregulation of PDE4C reduced intracellular cAMP and suppressed FOXO1-dependent transcription (PMID: 39326465). In thyroid carcinoma, PDE4C was shown to regulate adenylate cyclases (ADCY1, ADCY5, ADCY6, ADCY8, ADCY9) within cAMP metabolic processes (PMID: 38514754). In papillary thyroid carcinoma, PDE4C participates in P-AKT and EMT pathway activation downstream of a ceRNA regulatory network (PMID: 40427523).

**Expression pattern:**
PDE4C is expressed in a tissue-specific manner. Multiple splice variants were originally cloned from human fetal lung and testis (PMID: 9349724), and PDE4B and PDE4C are co-expressed in rodent Leydig cells where they regulate steroidogenesis (PMID: 26643407). PDE4C was found to be hypermethylated and silenced in high-grade glioma brain tissue, with expression detectable in normal brain (PMID: 24842301). PDE4C was also overexpressed in peripheral blood of children with Down Syndrome, suggesting a role in inflammatory signaling in a neurodevelopmental context, though this finding did not survive FDR correction (PMID: 27293319). There is no dedicated evidence of PDE4C expression in developing human brain regions relevant to ASD (e.g., prefrontal cortex, hippocampus during embryogenesis).

**Key experimental findings:**
- In glioma cell lines, PDE4C siRNA knockdown reduced wild-type p53 expression, while overexpression promoted apoptosis and inhibited migration, suggesting tumor suppressor activity via the p53 pathway (PMID: 24842301).
- In osteosarcoma, M2 macrophage-derived PDE4C mRNA transferred via exosomes increased tumor cell proliferation and collagen expression (PMID: 38774995).
- ELAVL1, an RNA-binding protein, stabilizes PDE4C mRNA and promotes lymph node metastasis in papillary thyroid cancer (PMID: 41868681).
- ChIP-seq and dual-luciferase assays confirmed direct transcriptional regulation of PDE4C by TCF21 in human endometrial stromal cells (PMID: 39326465).

None of these experimental findings are directly relevant to ASD pathogenesis, synaptic function, neuronal development, or the mechanisms typically implicated in autosomal dominant ASD genes (e.g., haploinsufficiency of synaptic scaffolding proteins, chromatin regulators, or ion channels).

**Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED**

While PDE4C has a well-characterized molecular function in cAMP regulation — a pathway with some relevance to neurodevelopment and synaptic plasticity — the available biological evidence is derived entirely from cancer biology, reproductive biology, and injury models. There is no experimental evidence directly implicating PDE4C in neuronal development, synaptogenesis, or ASD-relevant biology.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

- **Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
- PDE4C has a well-defined molecular function as a cAMP phosphodiesterase, and cAMP/PKA signaling is broadly relevant to neuronal function and plasticity.
- PDE4C expression has been detected in brain tissue (normal brain in glioma studies; PMID: 24842301) and in a neurodevelopmental condition (Down Syndrome peripheral blood; PMID: 27293319), providing a minimal biological foothold for neurodevelopmental relevance.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- There are **no clinical reports** of heterozygous pathogenic PDE4C variants in individuals with ASD or any neurodevelopmental disorder.
- There are **no model organism studies** (mouse, zebrafish, Drosophila) characterizing the neurological or behavioral consequences of PDE4C loss or gain of function.
- The existing biological evidence is derived from oncology and reproductive contexts with no direct experimental link to ASD-relevant mechanisms such as synaptic development, neuronal migration, or excitatory/inhibitory balance.

**Conclusion:**
At present, there is **insufficient evidence** to support PDE4C as an autism spectrum disorder gene with autosomal dominant pathogenic variants. The gene's role in cAMP hydrolysis is biologically plausible in a broad neurological context, but no clinical, genetic, or model organism data exist to substantiate a causal relationship with ASD. Establishing PDE4C as an ASD candidate gene would require, at minimum, the identification of de novo or inherited heterozygous loss-of-function variants in ASD cohorts and functional validation in neuronal model systems.
