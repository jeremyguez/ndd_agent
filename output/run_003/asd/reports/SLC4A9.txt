# Literature Evidence Report: SLC4A9 as an Autism Spectrum Disorder Gene (Autosomal Dominant / Heterozygous)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

SLC4A9 (also known as AE4) encodes a member of the SLC4 bicarbonate transporter family that functions as an electroneutral, monovalent cation-dependent Cl⁻/HCO₃⁻ exchanger, mediating Cl⁻ influx in exchange for Na⁺ (or K⁺) and HCO₃⁻ (PMID: 27114614). It is expressed in secretory epithelia including salivary gland acinar cells, renal intercalated cells, and the avian uterus, where it plays a central role in fluid secretion, acid-base regulation, and ion homeostasis.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

- **Number of independent case reports / case series:** 0
- **Total approximate number of patients:** 0
- **Most common phenotypes observed:** None reported in the context of autism spectrum disorder (ASD) or any neurodevelopmental condition.
- **Predominant variant type:** Not applicable; no clinical variants have been reported.
- **Known cohort representation (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL):** None identified.
- **Strength of clinical evidence: NONE**

No clinical evidence was identified linking heterozygous pathogenic variants in SLC4A9 to autism spectrum disorder or any related neurodevelopmental phenotype. The clinical evidence database returned no case reports, case series, or cohort studies associating SLC4A9 with ASD.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

- **Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*)
- **Key phenotypes from knockouts:**
  - Full knockout (Ae4/Slc4a9⁻/⁻) mice are viable with no reported lethality or gross developmental abnormalities (PMID: 25745107).
  - The predominant phenotype is a 35% reduction in saliva secretion stimulated by muscarinic and β-adrenergic receptor agonists, attributable to reduced Cl⁻ uptake in submandibular gland acinar cells (PMID: 25745107).
  - In double Ae4/Ae2 knockout mice, Cl⁻/HCO₃⁻ exchanger activity in acinar cells is nearly abolished, confirming the functional role of Ae4 in salivary secretion (PMID: 25745107).
  - No neurological, behavioral, or neurodevelopmental phenotypes have been reported in Slc4a9 knockout mice.
  - Knockout mice show no significant effect on duodenal Cl⁻/HCO₃⁻ exchange activity (<5% reduction), indicating tissue-specific functional relevance (PMID: 17170027).
- **Consistency with human ASD phenotype:** None. The knockout phenotype is restricted to exocrine secretory function and renal acid-base physiology, with no reported neurological correlates.
- **Strength of model organism evidence: NONE** (with respect to ASD or neurodevelopmental phenotypes; evidence exists only for non-neurological epithelial phenotypes)

---

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

- **Known pathways and molecular function:** SLC4A9/AE4 is an electroneutral cation-driven Cl⁻/HCO₃⁻ exchanger activated by PKA-mediated phosphorylation at serine residue S173 in response to β-adrenergic/cAMP signaling (PMID: 34585968; PMID: 27114614). It is a direct transcriptional target of the forkhead transcription factor Foxi1, which activates the SLC4A9 promoter approximately 100-fold via a single binding site in a 462 bp upstream regulatory region (PMID: 16159312). Foxi1 overexpression in murine cortical collecting duct M-1 cells is sufficient to induce Slc4a9 expression alongside other intercalated cell markers (PMID: 36603001).
- **Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:** SLC4A9 expression is documented in salivary gland acinar cells, renal β-intercalated cells of the cortical collecting duct, and the avian uterine epithelium (PMID: 24990900; PMID: 29115887). Expression is regulated by acid-base status (reduced during acidosis, restored during recovery) in renal intercalated cells (PMID: 24990900) and is induced by hypoxia in cancer cell lines via HIF1α/HIF2α (PMID: 27197160). An epigenome-wide association study identified differential methylation at a SLC4A9 CpG probe associated with all-cause mortality in a cardiovascular cohort (PMID: 31642367). **No evidence of brain expression, neuronal function, or neurodevelopmental role has been reported.**
- **Key experimental findings:** In cancer biology, RNAi-mediated knockdown of SLC4A9 acidified intracellular pH, increased apoptosis in breast cancer spheroids, and suppressed tumor growth in murine xenograft models (PMID: 27197160), suggesting a role in intracellular pH regulation that is context-dependent. In the kidney, Slc4a9 is coordinately upregulated with Slc26a4 and Slc4a8 as part of a compensatory NaCl reabsorption pathway in SPAK-deficient mice (PMID: 25893600).
- **Strength of biological evidence: NONE** (with respect to ASD or neurodevelopmental relevance; the gene has well-characterized epithelial transport functions but no established neurological or synaptic role)

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

- **Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:** SLC4A9 is a well-characterized ion transporter with defined molecular function, regulatory mechanisms, and tissue-specific expression, providing a solid biological foundation for future investigation.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- There is a complete absence of clinical evidence linking SLC4A9 heterozygous variants to autism spectrum disorder or any neurodevelopmental condition.
- Model organism studies demonstrate viability and non-neurological phenotypes only; no behavioral, synaptic, or brain-related phenotypes have been described in Slc4a9 knockout mice.
- No expression data, functional studies, or pathway analyses support a role for SLC4A9 in brain development, neuronal signaling, or processes relevant to ASD pathogenesis.

**Conclusion:** Based on the totality of available evidence, there is currently no credible support for SLC4A9 as an autism spectrum disorder gene with autosomal dominant pathogenic variants. The gene's established biology is confined to epithelial ion transport in secretory and renal tissues, and neither clinical reports nor model organism studies provide any neurological or neurodevelopmental signal. Designation of SLC4A9 as an ASD candidate gene would require, at minimum, the identification of de novo or inherited heterozygous variants in ASD-affected individuals from well-characterized cohorts, supported by neurological phenotypes in animal models or functional evidence of a role in neuronal biology.
