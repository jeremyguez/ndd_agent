# TAOK1 Evidence Report: Autism Spectrum Disorder and Neurodevelopmental Disorder (Autosomal Dominant)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

TAOK1 (Thousand And One Amino Acid Protein Kinase 1) encodes a serine/threonine kinase of the STE20 family that functions as a core component of the Hippo signaling pathway and plays critical roles in neuronal maturation, cortical development, microtubule dynamics, and DNA damage response. In the nervous system, TAOK1 is highly expressed in neurons and regulates plasma membrane remodeling, dendritic arbor growth, and neural migration during embryonic brain development.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports and patient totals:**
At least eight independent publications or conference reports describe TAOK1-associated neurodevelopmental disorder (NDD). The total number of reported patients across these studies is approximately 95 or more: 50 patients (PMID: 39737487), 23 patients (PMID: 33565190), 8 patients (PMID: 31230721), 4 patients (PMID: 35091509), 7 patients (ASHG 2017 – McLaughlin et al.), 1 patient (PMID: 38443934), 1 patient (PMID: 37656623), and 1 patient (PMID: 41001785). Additional patients are described in phenotypic expansion reports (PMID: 37696715; PMID: 36841152) for which exact patient numbers were not available from abstracts. Collectively, well over 90 unique individuals have been reported.

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
Across all studies, the core phenotype of TAOK1-related NDD consistently includes:
- Global developmental delay and intellectual disability (present in virtually all cohorts)
- Muscular hypotonia (reported in PMID: 33565190; PMID: 31230721; PMID: 35091509; ASHG 2017)
- Macrocephaly (PMID: 39737487; PMID: 38443934; ASHG 2017; PMID: 37696715)
- Feeding difficulties in infancy (PMID: 33565190; PMID: 35091509; ASHG 2017)
- Behavioral abnormalities including autism spectrum disorder (ASD) (ASHG 2017; PMID: 37656623)
- Language and motor delay (ASHG 2017; PMID: 33565190)
- Facial dysmorphisms (PMID: 33565190; PMID: 35091509; ASHG 2017)
- Joint hypermobility/laxity (ASHG 2017; PMID: 35091509)

Additional features reported in subsets of patients include hypoglycemia, male genital anomalies (PMID: 39737487), periventricular nodular heterotopia (PMID: 38443934), childhood-onset tremor (PMID: 36841152), prenatal macrocephaly (PMID: 37696715), and short stature/small for gestational age (PMID: 41001785).

**Predominant variant type:**
Both loss-of-function (LoF) variants (truncating, nonsense, frameshift, splice site) and missense variants are reported across all major studies. The largest cohort (PMID: 39737487) identified 37 unique variants, with the majority being missense variants clustered in the protein kinase domain at intolerance-constrained residues, alongside truncating variants. The first report (PMID: 31230721) and the foundational cohort study (PMID: 33565190) emphasized de novo LoF variants, while missense variants with demonstrated loss-of-function or dominant-negative effects were also described. Inherited variants with incomplete penetrance and variable expressivity have been reported (PMID: 35091509), though de novo occurrence predominates. The inheritance pattern is autosomal dominant.

**Known cohort affiliations:**
The studies do not explicitly identify patients from named cohorts such as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL. Patients were ascertained through international matchmaking platforms (e.g., GeneMatcher; ASHG 2017; PMID: 35091509), clinical and research exome/genome sequencing at multiple international centers, and a Korean multicenter exome study (PMID: 41001785). One patient was identified in a Chinese ASD cohort (PMID: 37656623).

**Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG**
The evidence is strong based on: (1) more than 90 reported patients across at least eight independent publications and conference reports; (2) consistent core phenotype across geographically diverse centers; (3) predominantly de novo occurrence of variants; (4) variants absent from population databases (gnomAD) (PMID: 31230721); (5) functional validation in multiple studies; and (6) the largest cohort reporting 37 unique variants with 30 novel additions (PMID: 39737487).

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:**
Model organism studies relevant to TAOK1 neurodevelopmental function include mouse (*Mus musculus*), *Xenopus tropicalis*, and *Drosophila melanogaster* (referenced in PMID: 31230721 for Tao1 knockdown studies).

**Key phenotypes from knockouts/knockdowns:**
- *Mouse*: Altered TAOK1 expression in the embryonic mouse brain disrupted neural migration in vivo and neuronal maturation in vitro (PMID: 33565190). Taok1 haploinsufficiency in mice produced autistic-like behaviors, with mechanistic studies implicating the dorsal raphe nucleus (PMID: 37656623). A separate mouse study using AAV-mediated TAOK1 silencing demonstrated effects on cardiomyocyte survival and cardiac function (PMID: 39964965), and TAOK1 knockdown exacerbated colitis in an inflammatory bowel disease model (PMID: 29400705) — findings less directly relevant to NDD but demonstrating broad physiological roles.
- *Xenopus tropicalis*: TAOK1 knockdown impaired motile cilia formation and caused heart development defects (PMID: 40552535), suggesting a role in ciliary biology that may intersect with neurodevelopmental mechanisms.
- *Drosophila*: Tao1 knockdown in *Drosophila* produced neurodevelopmental phenotypes consistent with the human disorder (PMID: 31230721).

**Consistency with human phenotype:**
The mouse haploinsufficiency model directly recapitulates the ASD-related behavioral phenotype observed in human patients (PMID: 37656623). The embryonic mouse brain studies showing impaired neural migration and neuronal maturation (PMID: 33565190) are highly consistent with the human cortical malformation phenotype, including the periventricular nodular heterotopia reported in one patient (PMID: 38443934). The *Drosophila* Tao1 knockdown data provided early functional support for pathogenicity of human variants (PMID: 31230721).

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**
The available model organism data are supportive and mechanistically informative, particularly the mouse haploinsufficiency ASD behavioral model and the embryonic brain migration studies. However, formal knockout studies with detailed Mendelian ratio analysis, lethality data, and comprehensive neurological phenotyping in constitutive knockout mice are not fully described in the available abstracts, limiting the strength rating to moderate rather than strong.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
TAOK1 is a multifunctional serine/threonine kinase with roles in several key signaling pathways:
- **Hippo signaling**: TAOK1 is described as a core member of the Hippo pathway, linking it to cell growth, proliferation, and organ size regulation (PMID: 38479581).
- **Neuronal plasma membrane remodeling**: TAOK1 directly associates with phosphoinositides to remodel the neuronal plasma membrane; NDD-associated mutations are catalytically inactive and aberrantly membrane-bound, inducing abnormal membrane protrusions and disrupted dendritic arbor growth in cultured hippocampal neurons (PMID: 36595571).
- **Microtubule dynamics and checkpoint signaling**: TAOK1 regulates microtubule-associated protein MAP4 and interacts with dynein for intracellular trafficking of signaling complexes (PMID: 36649698; PMID: 38479581).
- **DNA damage response**: TAOK1 phosphorylates USP7, stabilizing RAD51 and promoting homologous recombination repair; its depletion impairs HR repair and sensitizes cells to PARP inhibition (PMID: 40106350).
- **Innate immune signaling**: TAOK1 promotes TBK1-IRF3 complex formation for type I interferon production (PMID: 36649698) and negatively regulates IL-17-mediated signaling via interaction with IL-17RA (PMID: 29400705).
- **MAPK signaling**: TAOK1 modulates p38 MAPK, ERK, JNK, and NF-κB pathways in multiple cellular contexts (PMID: 39964965; PMID: 29400705; PMID: 36930872).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**
TAOK1 is abundant in neurons in the mammalian brain (PMID: 36595571) and is expressed during embryonic cortical development (PMID: 33565190). Its expression in the embryonic mouse brain is required for proper neural migration and neuronal maturation, directly linking its molecular function to the cortical development phenotypes observed in patients.

**Key experimental findings directly relevant to NDD:**
The most mechanistically informative study for NDD (PMID: 36595571) demonstrated that four NDD-associated TAOK1 mutations are catalytically inactive and become aberrantly trapped in a membrane-bound state, causing abnormal dendritic arbor growth — providing a direct molecular mechanism linking patient variants to neuronal dysfunction. Autophosphorylation at threonine-440 and threonine-443 normally prevents membrane trapping, and NDD mutations disrupt this regulatory mechanism. Additionally, nonsense-mediated mRNA decay was demonstrated in a patient fibroblast line, and absence of phosphorylated TAO1 kinase and phosphorylated tau was shown, supporting loss-of-function as a pathogenic mechanism (PMID: 31230721).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
The biological evidence is strong, with direct mechanistic studies linking NDD-associated patient variants to specific molecular defects in neuronal function (PMID: 36595571), functional validation in patient-derived cells (PMID: 31230721), and in vivo evidence of TAOK1's essential role in cortical development (PMID: 33565190).

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths:**
1. *Large, replicated clinical dataset*: More than 90 patients reported across at least eight independent studies from multiple international centers, with a consistent core neurodevelopmental phenotype including developmental delay, intellectual disability, ASD, hypotonia, macrocephaly, and feeding difficulties.
2. *De novo variant predominance and functional validation*: The majority of variants are de novo, absent from population databases, and multiple studies have provided direct functional evidence — including demonstration of NMD in patient fibroblasts (PMID: 31230721), catalytic inactivity and aberrant membrane trapping of NDD-associated mutant proteins in neurons (PMID: 36595571), and impaired neural migration in mouse embryonic brain (PMID: 33565190).
3. *Convergent model organism data*: Mouse haploinsufficiency recapitulates ASD-like behaviors (PMID: 37656623), and *Drosophila* Tao1 knockdown produces neurodevelopmental phenotypes consistent with the human disorder (PMID: 31230721).

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. Specific variant-level data (HGVS notations) were not available from the abstracts reviewed, limiting precise genotype-phenotype correlation analysis.
2. Formal constitutive knockout mouse studies with complete neurological phenotyping, Mendelian ratio analysis, and brain imaging data are not fully described in the available literature, leaving some gaps in the model organism evidence.
3. Incomplete penetrance and variable expressivity have been documented (PMID: 35091509), and the contribution of inherited versus de novo variants to overall disease burden requires further characterization in larger cohorts.

**Conclusion:**
TAOK1 is a well-established autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene with strong clinical, functional, and model organism evidence supporting its role in ASD and broader NDD. The gene meets criteria for definitive disease-gene association based on the number of independent reports, the de novo nature of most variants, direct molecular mechanistic studies in patient-derived cells and neurons, and recapitulation of the human phenotype in mouse models. Ongoing phenotypic expansion — including prenatal macrocephaly, childhood-onset tremor, and periventricular nodular heterotopia — underscores the importance of TAOK1 testing in individuals with unexplained NDD and ASD.
