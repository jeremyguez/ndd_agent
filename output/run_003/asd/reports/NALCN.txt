# NALCN – Literature Evidence Report: Heterozygous (Autosomal Dominant) Pathogenic Variants and Autism Spectrum Disorder

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

NALCN (Sodium Leak Channel, Non-Selective) encodes a voltage-independent, non-selective cation channel that mediates a constitutive sodium leak current responsible for setting and maintaining neuronal resting membrane potential and excitability. It operates as part of a large channelosome complex with the accessory proteins UNC79, UNC80, and FAM155A, and is regulated by multiple G protein-coupled receptors and second-messenger pathways, making it a central modulator of neuronal firing across the central and peripheral nervous systems.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports and patient totals:**
Eight independent reports or case series were identified. Across these, the total number of patients with NALCN variants is approximately 58, though this figure is dominated by a single large genotype-phenotype study (PMID: 40048676, n=43). The remaining reports contribute smaller numbers: three patients (PMID: 26763878), six patients (ASHG 2014 – Shively et al.), two patients (PMID: 37046053), and single patients from Italy (PMID: 41153398), Turkey (PMID: 41153369), Bulgaria (PMID: 40558542), and the USA (ASHG 2014 – Powis et al.).

**Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) specifically:**
The evidence directly linking heterozygous NALCN variants to ASD is limited to two reports. One patient identified via trio diagnostic exome sequencing presented with ASD, seizures, intellectual disability, and dysmorphic features, though this was reported as part of a dual molecular diagnosis with ANO3 (ASHG 2014 – Powis et al.), complicating attribution. A second patient identified via whole exome sequencing with segregation analysis presented with ASD and neurodevelopmental impairment, with NALCN listed as a critical gene for synaptic signaling imbalance (PMID: 40558542). Neither report provides detailed variant-level data for NALCN in the ASD context.

**Most common phenotypes across all heterozygous (dominant) NALCN cases:**
The predominant phenotypes associated with heterozygous de novo NALCN variants are neurodevelopmental delay, intellectual disability (ranging from mild to profound), hypotonia, facial dysmorphologies, and, in more severe cases, distal arthrogryposis and congenital contractures — collectively constituting the CLIFAHDD syndrome (Congenital contractures of the Limbs and Face, Hypotonia, and Developmental Delay). Additional features include episodic ataxia, paroxysmal dystonia, epilepsy, and, in a minority, premature death (PMID: 40048676). A milder presentation with cerebellar ataxia and cognitive impairment has also been reported (PMID: 41153398). A heterozygous exonic deletion (exon 27) was associated with temporal lobe epilepsy in a father-son pair, broadening the phenotypic spectrum (PMID: 37046053).

**Predominant variant type:**
Among heterozygous dominant cases, missense variants are overwhelmingly predominant, with the majority classified as gain-of-function (GoF) based on their clustering around the S6 gate and pore-forming helices. De novo occurrence has been confirmed in multiple reports (PMID: 26763878; ASHG 2014 – Shively et al.; PMID: 41153398). One structural variant (heterozygous exon 27 deletion) has also been reported (PMID: 37046053).

**Known cohort membership:**
None of the identified patients are explicitly reported as belonging to established research cohorts such as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL.

**Strength of clinical evidence for ASD specifically: LIMITED**
While NALCN is well-established as a dominant neurodevelopmental disease gene (CLIFAHDD/IHPRF1), the direct evidence linking heterozygous NALCN variants specifically to ASD rests on only two patients across two reports, one of which involves a confounding dual diagnosis. The broader clinical literature robustly supports NALCN haploinsufficiency and GoF variants in neurodevelopmental disorders that may include ASD-overlapping features (intellectual disability, developmental delay), but ASD as a primary or isolated phenotype has not been systematically demonstrated.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:**
NALCN has been studied extensively in mouse (*Mus musculus*), rat, and invertebrates (*C. elegans*, *Drosophila melanogaster* via the related UNC79 subunit).

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

- *Full knockout (mouse):* Homozygous Nalcn-null mice die within 24 hours of birth due to severely disrupted respiratory rhythm, with complete absence of the TTX- and Cs⁺-resistant background Na⁺ leak current in hippocampal neurons (PMID: 17448995). This perinatal lethality precludes behavioral assessment of neurodevelopmental phenotypes in homozygous animals but powerfully validates the channel's essential role in neuronal excitability and respiratory control. A second full knockout study confirmed loss of extracellular Ca²⁺-dependent modulation of neuronal excitability (PMID: 21040849).

- *Heterozygous knockout (mouse):* Heterozygous Nalcn mice are viable and display hypernatremia relative to wild-type littermates, suggesting a role in osmoregulation (PMID: 21177381). Notably, no behavioral or neurodevelopmental phenotypes are explicitly reported for heterozygous animals in the available literature, which is a gap relevant to the autosomal dominant disease model.

- *Conditional knockouts (mouse):* Neuron-specific conditional knockouts have demonstrated that NALCN is required for spontaneous pacemaking activity in dopaminergic neurons (PMID: 30556810), spontaneous firing in auditory interneurons (PMID: 38197879), circadian regulation of SCN neuron excitability (PMID: 37339878), and intrinsic excitability of spinal projection neurons (PMID: 29746349). A gain-of-function missense mutation in mice identified by forward genetic screen reduced REM sleep, consistent with increased neuronal excitability (PMID: 27806374).

- *C. elegans:* Loss-of-function mutations in the NALCN ortholog nca-1/nca-2 and its regulators unc-79/unc-80 cause defective reversal behavior, with command interneurons AVA, AVE, and AVG identified as key sites of channelosome function (PMID: 31690562).

**Consistency with human phenotype:**
The mouse and invertebrate data are highly consistent with the human neurodevelopmental phenotype: NALCN is essential for normal neuronal excitability, respiratory rhythm, and spontaneous firing across multiple neuron types. The perinatal lethality of full knockouts mirrors the severe respiratory symptoms and early death seen in some CLIFAHDD patients (PMID: 40048676). However, direct modeling of ASD-relevant behaviors (social interaction, repetitive behaviors) in heterozygous or conditional Nalcn mice has not been reported in the available literature.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG** (for neurological function broadly; moderate specifically for ASD-relevant behavioral phenotypes)

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Molecular function and pathways:**
NALCN encodes the pore-forming subunit of a ~1 MDa channelosome. Cryo-EM structures have resolved the architecture of the NALCN-FAM155A-UNC79-UNC80 complex, showing that UNC79 and UNC80 form an intertwined HEAT-repeat superhelical assembly docking onto the intracellular face of NALCN, with calmodulin bound to the NALCN C-terminal domain as a modulatory hub (PMID: 34929720). The channel pore is tightly closed at rest with a low open probability, and patient missense mutations causing GoF phenotypes cluster around the S6 gate and π-bulges (PMID: 32698188). NALCN is regulated by multiple GPCRs including substance P receptors, M3 muscarinic receptors, D2 dopamine receptors, α2-adrenergic receptors, and GABA-B receptors, positioning it as a convergence point for neuromodulatory signaling.

**Expression pattern:**
NALCN is broadly expressed in neurons throughout the central and peripheral nervous systems, including hippocampus, brainstem, spinal cord, dorsal cochlear nucleus, suprachiasmatic nucleus, and dopaminergic neurons of the midbrain. Expression has also been documented in pancreatic β-cells (PMID: 21099296) and myometrial smooth muscle cells, where it is regulated by progesterone and estrogen (PMID: 31935111). The broad neuronal expression pattern is highly consistent with a role in neurodevelopmental disease.

**Key experimental findings relevant to neurodevelopment:**
The structural studies establish that disease-causing missense variants in pore-forming and gating regions directly perturb channel function (PMID: 32698188; PMID: 34929720). The forward genetic screen in mice linking a GoF NALCN mutation to altered REM sleep (PMID: 27806374) provides in vivo evidence that NALCN gain-of-function affects higher-order brain function. NALCN's role in maintaining resting membrane potential and modulating neuronal firing across diverse neuron types provides a plausible mechanistic basis for the broad neurodevelopmental phenotypes observed in patients. Roles in cancer cell dissemination and metastasis (PMID: 36175792; PMID: 37278161) and immune modulation (PMID: 40013144) are noted but are not directly relevant to the ASD/neurodevelopmental disease context.

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG** (for neuronal function and disease mechanism broadly; the specific mechanistic link to ASD endophenotypes such as social cognition or repetitive behavior has not been experimentally addressed)

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level for NALCN as an ASD gene with heterozygous (autosomal dominant) pathogenic variants: LIMITED**

**Key strengths:**
- NALCN is robustly established as a dominant neurodevelopmental disease gene, with de novo heterozygous missense variants causing CLIFAHDD syndrome confirmed across multiple independent cohorts totaling >50 patients (PMID: 40048676; PMID: 26763878; ASHG 2014 – Shively et al.).
- Strong model organism and structural biology data validate the channel's essential role in neuronal excitability and confirm that disease-associated variants alter channel gating.
- The neurodevelopmental phenotypes associated with heterozygous NALCN variants (intellectual disability, developmental delay, hypotonia) overlap substantially with ASD comorbidities, making biological plausibility high.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- Direct evidence for ASD as a primary phenotype attributable to heterozygous NALCN variants is restricted to two patients across two reports, one of which involves a confounding dual molecular diagnosis (ASHG 2014 – Powis et al.; PMID: 40558542). Neither report provides sufficient variant-level detail or functional validation to establish causality for ASD specifically.
- No systematic ASD cohort study has identified NALCN as a recurrently mutated gene, and no heterozygous mouse model has been reported to display ASD-relevant behavioral phenotypes.
- The predominant disease mechanism for heterozygous NALCN variants appears to be gain-of-function rather than haploinsufficiency, and the relationship between GoF channel activity and ASD-specific neurobiology has not been explored.

**Conclusion:**
NALCN is a well-validated dominant neurodevelopmental disease gene whose heterozygous de novo variants cause a spectrum of disorders ranging from severe CLIFAHDD syndrome to milder intellectual disability and ataxia, supported by strong clinical, model organism, and structural biological evidence. However, the specific evidence supporting NALCN as an ASD gene is currently limited to incidental findings in two patients without robust variant-level or functional corroboration. NALCN should be considered a plausible candidate gene for ASD given its neurobiological role and phenotypic overlap, but dedicated ASD cohort studies and functional modeling of ASD-associated variants are needed before a definitive classification can be made.
