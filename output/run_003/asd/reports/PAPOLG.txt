# Literature Evidence Report: PAPOLG as an Autism Spectrum Disorder / Neurodevelopmental Disease Gene (Heterozygous, Autosomal Dominant)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

PAPOLG (Poly(A) Polymerase Gamma) encodes a non-canonical poly(A) polymerase located at chromosomal locus 2p16.1, which functions in the post-transcriptional regulation of RNA by catalyzing the addition of adenosine residues to the 3' end of mRNA transcripts, thereby influencing mRNA stability and gene expression. It is a paralog of the canonical poly(A) polymerase PAPOLA and has been implicated in alternative polyadenylation, inflammatory signaling, and transcriptional regulation across multiple tissue types.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports:** Five independent reports were identified, comprising four published studies and one conference abstract.

**Total approximate number of patients:** Approximately 6–7 patients across all reports in whom PAPOLG was explicitly included in the deleted genomic interval. The exact number is difficult to determine precisely because several reports describe overlapping or multi-gene deletions in the context of the broader 2p15-p16.1 microdeletion syndrome, and one report (PMID: 24810580) does not specify a patient count for the PAPOLG-inclusive deletion.

**Most common phenotypes observed:** All patients with deletions encompassing PAPOLG presented with neurodevelopmental phenotypes. The most consistently reported features include intellectual disability, developmental delay (motor and/or speech), autistic traits or stereotyped behaviors, and dysmorphic facial features. Brain structural abnormalities—specifically hypoplasia of the pons and cerebellum—were reported in three patients from one series (PMID: 28717667). Microcephaly and growth retardation were reported in some but not all cases; notably, two patients with deletions restricted to BCL11A and PAPOLG lacked microcephaly (PMID: 28573701; ASHG 2015 - Levy et al.), suggesting that REL or other genes in the broader region may contribute to that specific feature.

**Predominant variant type:** All reported variants are heterozygous chromosomal deletions (copy number variants), ranging in size from approximately 0.3 Mb to 5.04 Mb. No point mutations (loss-of-function or missense) in PAPOLG alone have been reported. All deletions for which parental origin was assessed were confirmed de novo.

**Cohort affiliation:** None of the reported patients were identified from named large-scale cohorts such as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL. Cases were ascertained through individual clinical genetics centers, primarily in France (PMID: 28573701; ASHG 2015 - Levy et al.) and unspecified centers.

**Critical interpretive caveat:** In every reported case, PAPOLG is deleted in conjunction with other genes—most prominently BCL11A, which has independently established haploinsufficiency as a cause of neurodevelopmental delay and speech disorder (PMID: 24810580; PMID: 28717667). Multiple authors explicitly propose BCL11A as the primary driver of the neurodevelopmental phenotype, with PAPOLG considered a possible modifier or co-contributor. No patient has been reported with an isolated heterozygous deletion or loss-of-function variant affecting PAPOLG alone. The smallest deletion reported to include PAPOLG alongside BCL11A was approximately 0.3 Mb (PMID: 24810580), but even this does not isolate PAPOLG's contribution. One conference report (ASHG 2011 - Lozano & Wilcox) explicitly refined the critical region for the neurodevelopmental phenotype in a 2p15-16.1 deletion patient to exclude PAPOLG, implicating other genes such as KIAA1841 instead.

**Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED**
The clinical evidence is limited by the exclusive occurrence of PAPOLG deletions in the context of multi-gene copy number variants, the absence of any isolated PAPOLG variant, the small total number of patients, and the explicit attribution of the primary phenotype to co-deleted genes (particularly BCL11A) in most reports.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** No model organism studies of PAPOLG were identified in the evidence provided.

**Key phenotypes:** None available.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** Cannot be assessed.

**Strength of model organism evidence: NONE**
There are no published knockout, knockdown, or transgenic animal studies of PAPOLG relevant to neurodevelopmental or autism spectrum disorder phenotypes in the evidence base reviewed.

---

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:** PAPOLG encodes a poly(A) polymerase involved in post-transcriptional mRNA regulation via alternative polyadenylation. Functional cell culture studies demonstrate that PAPOLG stabilizes NF-κB mRNA, thereby promoting downstream inflammatory cytokine production (TNF-α, IL-6) in macrophages (PMID: 40607946). This places PAPOLG within the NF-κB/inflammatory signaling axis, which is biologically relevant given that its genomic neighbor REL encodes the c-Rel subunit of NF-κB. PAPOLG has also been identified as a predicted target of multiple miRNAs in the context of MAPK signaling and inflammatory pathways (PMID: 35997417).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:** No direct evidence of brain-specific or neurodevelopmentally enriched expression of PAPOLG was identified in the reviewed literature. Expression studies have focused on immune cells (macrophages, leukocytes), lymphoma tissue, hepatocellular carcinoma, oocytes, and mammary epithelial cells. PAPOLG was identified as part of a leukocyte gene expression signature in epithelial ovarian cancer (PMID: 23551967) and was differentially expressed in rabbit oocytes in relation to reproductive history and ovarian stimulation (PMID: 35724452). A GWAS-level association between PAPOLG genotype and plasma metabolic profiles in response to dietary intervention was reported (PMID: 23964055), suggesting a role in mitochondrial metabolic regulation, though this is not directly relevant to neurodevelopment.

**Oncological relevance:** The most mechanistically detailed biological evidence pertains to cancer biology. PAPOLG is significantly overexpressed in follicular lymphoma and transformed follicular lymphoma relative to normal B-cells, and amplification of the 2p15-p16.1 locus correlates with increased PAPOLG transcription, leading to its proposal as a proto-oncogene in lymphoma transformation (PMID: 31410080). PAPOLG is also highly expressed and associated with poor prognosis in hepatocellular carcinoma (PMID: 35482720). These findings establish a gain-of-function oncogenic role that is mechanistically opposite to the haploinsufficiency model implied by the deletion syndrome context.

**Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED**
While PAPOLG has a defined molecular function in mRNA polyadenylation and has been experimentally linked to NF-κB mRNA stabilization and inflammatory signaling, there is no direct biological evidence connecting PAPOLG function to neuronal development, synaptic biology, or autism spectrum disorder-relevant pathways. The available functional data are predominantly from immune and cancer cell contexts.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
- PAPOLG resides within a genomic region (2p15-p16.1) where de novo heterozygous deletions consistently produce a neurodevelopmental syndrome including intellectual disability, developmental delay, and autistic features across at least five independent reports totaling approximately 6–7 patients (PMID: 23495096; PMID: 28573701; PMID: 28717667; PMID: 24810580; ASHG 2015 - Levy et al.).
- All deletions encompassing PAPOLG were de novo, supporting a dominant mechanism of pathogenicity at the locus level.
- PAPOLG has a defined biochemical function (mRNA polyadenylation/stabilization) that could plausibly influence gene expression programs relevant to neurodevelopment.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- No patient has been reported with an isolated heterozygous loss-of-function variant in PAPOLG alone; all cases involve multi-gene deletions in which BCL11A is the most consistently implicated driver gene.
- Multiple authors and one conference report (ASHG 2011 - Lozano & Wilcox) explicitly argue against PAPOLG as the critical gene for the neurodevelopmental phenotype, attributing causality primarily to BCL11A haploinsufficiency.
- There are no model organism studies, no functional neurobiological data, and no brain expression data supporting a specific role for PAPOLG in autism spectrum disorder or neurodevelopment.

**Conclusion:** The current evidence is insufficient to classify PAPOLG as an autism spectrum disorder gene with heterozygous autosomal dominant pathogenic variants. While PAPOLG is consistently co-deleted in patients with the 2p15-p16.1 microdeletion syndrome, the neurodevelopmental phenotype is most parsimoniously attributed to co-deleted BCL11A, and no independent genetic or functional evidence isolates a causative role for PAPOLG. Establishing PAPOLG as a bona fide ASD gene would require identification of patients with isolated PAPOLG loss-of-function variants, functional neurobiological studies, and/or model organism data demonstrating relevant neurodevelopmental phenotypes.
