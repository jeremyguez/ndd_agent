# SOX11 as an Autism Spectrum Disorder Gene: Literature Evidence Report

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

SOX11 (SRY-box transcription factor 11) is a member of the SoxC family of transcription factors that plays a critical role in embryonic neurogenesis, neuronal differentiation, and survival, with particularly high expression during fetal brain development. It functions as a transcriptional regulator of chromatin-remodeling complexes, including the SWI/SNF complex, and is essential for the specification and maturation of neurons across multiple regions of the central and peripheral nervous systems.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports/series:** Ten independent publications or conference reports were identified, spanning case reports, case series, cohort studies, and registry analyses.

**Total approximate number of patients:** Across studies with specified patient counts, at least 33 patients with SOX11 variants are explicitly enumerated (2 from ASHG 2014 – Matsumoto et al.; 5 from PMID: 39290158; 21 from PMID: 37924570; 4 from ASHG 2019 – Sekiguchi et al.; 1 from ASHG 2015 – Pagnamenta et al.). Additional patients are described in larger registry-based studies (PMID: 34205270; PMID: 38117302; PMID: 30123105) without precise SOX11-specific counts, suggesting the true total is considerably higher.

**Most common phenotypes observed:** The predominant clinical presentation is Coffin-Siris syndrome (CSS) or a CSS-like syndrome, characterized by intellectual disability (ranging from mild to moderate/severe), developmental delay, hypotonia, coarse facial features, and hypoplasia of the fifth fingernails/distal phalanges. Neurodevelopmental features are universal. Notably, a behavioral characterization study of 21 individuals (PMID: 37924570) found that 90% exhibited clinically relevant autistic traits, with 62% showing severe autistic traits, alongside communication and daily living difficulties; a relative strength in social motivation was observed, suggesting a distinctive autistic profile. Additional phenotypes include microcephaly (ASHG 2015 – Pagnamenta et al.), malformations of cortical development (ASHG 2019 – Kini et al., DDD cohort), cochlear nerve deficiency/hearing impairment (PMID: 35642566), and endocrine abnormalities including idiopathic hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (PMID: 39290158).

**Predominant variant type:** Loss-of-function (LoF) variants — including nonsense mutations (e.g., p.S303X, p.C29*), frameshift variants (e.g., p.S345Afs*13), and whole-gene deletions — are the most frequently reported and best-supported pathogenic variant class, consistent with a haploinsufficiency mechanism. Missense variants affecting the HMG domain (e.g., p.G84D, p.P114S, p.R100Q) have also been reported as pathogenic or likely pathogenic (PMID: 39290158). The majority of reported variants are de novo.

**Known cohort representation:** One study explicitly identifies patients from the DDD (Deciphering Developmental Disorders) cohort (ASHG 2019 – Kini et al., UK). Additional patients were ascertained through the CSS/BAF complex registry (PMID: 34205270) and through clinical exome sequencing programs at Massachusetts General Hospital, USA (PMID: 39290158) and in Japan (ASHG 2019 – Sekiguchi et al.).

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**

While the number of patients with explicitly documented SOX11 variants remains modest in absolute terms, the consistent de novo occurrence, the recurrence across independent international cohorts, the application of ACMG pathogenicity criteria (PMID: 39290158), and the statistically significant enrichment of protein-truncating and HMG-domain missense variants in disease cohorts versus gnomAD (p=0.0005 and p=0.003, respectively; PMID: 39290158) collectively support a moderate-to-strong clinical signal. The specific documentation of autistic traits in 90% of a well-characterized cohort of 21 individuals (PMID: 37924570) is particularly relevant to the ASD gene classification question, though formal ASD diagnoses using standardized instruments are not consistently reported across studies.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*), zebrafish (*Danio rerio*), and *Xenopus laevis* have all been used to model SOX11 loss of function.

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

- *Neurological:* Conditional knockout of Sox11 in mice prevents neuronal differentiation of adult neural stem cells in the hippocampus and impairs reprogramming of astroglia into neurons (PMID: 22378879). Conditional deletion in dentate gyrus granule cells reduces excitability and upregulates potassium channel subunits (PMID: 32080705). Postnatal manipulation of SOX11 expression in cortical neurons impairs sensorimotor connectivity and disrupts selective exploratory behaviors (PMID: 34754107). Overexpression of SOX11 in adult-born hippocampal granule cells causes them to remain immature, demonstrating that precise dosage control is required for neuronal maturation (PMID: 39028813).

- *Inner ear/hearing:* Heterozygous Sox11 knockout mice exhibit mild high-frequency hearing loss and reduced vestibular sensory evoked potentials, with homozygous knockouts showing semicircular canal dysplasia and reduced cochlear sensory neuron survival (ASHG 2016 – Sperry et al.; PMID: 35642566). This directly parallels cochlear nerve deficiency reported in human CSS patients.

- *Enteric nervous system:* Zebrafish sox11 knockout results in defective neuronal subtype specification in the developing enteric nervous system, with reduced inhibitory motor neuron populations (PMID: 40789027).

- *Other organ systems:* Full knockout mice show pulmonary malformations (PMID: 40481144) and impaired wound re-epithelialization (PMID: 31492871). Sox11 full knockout mice are viable under normal conditions (PMID: 38991010), indicating that homozygous loss is not embryonic lethal in mice, though heterozygous effects on neurological function are documented.

- *Xenopus:* Sox11 knockdown causes reduced eye size, disrupted retinal lamination, and increased apoptosis in the developing eye (PMID: 23874955).

- *Zebrafish (CSS model):* Morpholino-mediated knockdown of sox11a/b in zebrafish produces brain abnormalities, providing direct in vivo support for the neurodevelopmental role (ASHG 2014 – Matsumoto et al.).

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The model organism data are highly consistent with the human phenotype. Neurological deficits (impaired neuronal differentiation, hippocampal dysfunction, cortical connectivity disruption), hearing impairment, and brain structural abnormalities observed in animal models mirror the intellectual disability, autistic traits, and neurodevelopmental features seen in patients. The haploinsufficiency mechanism inferred from human genetics is supported by phenotypes observed in heterozygous knockout mice.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**

Multiple independent laboratories across three species have demonstrated convergent neurological phenotypes upon SOX11 loss of function, with direct mechanistic relevance to the human neurodevelopmental disorder.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:** SOX11 functions as a transcriptional activator and chromatin-remodeling regulator. It directly regulates at least 10 core SWI/SNF complex components, including SMARCC1, SMARCA4/BRG1, and ARID1A (PMID: 36882421) — notably, pathogenic variants in multiple SWI/SNF subunits are themselves established causes of CSS and related neurodevelopmental disorders. SOX11 physically interacts with SMARCA4 at open chromatin and active promoters (PMID: 40739087), and its HMG box domain mediates direct protein-protein interactions (PMID: 38237141). SOX11 also regulates IGF2 signaling during organ development (PMID: 40481144) and modulates immune microenvironments via CD70 targeting (PMID: 34189576).

**Expression pattern:** SOX11 is highly expressed during embryonic and fetal brain development. It is a transcriptional determinant in transitional cerebellar progenitor populations connecting neural stem cells to neuronal lineages (PMID: 36450980), is expressed in adult hippocampal neurogenic niches (PMID: 22378879; PMID: 32080705), and shows temporally regulated expression in cortical projection neurons that governs circuit connectivity (PMID: 34754107). Its expression is activity-regulated in the dentate gyrus (PMID: 32080705), indicating a role in experience-dependent plasticity. Downregulation of SOX11 is required for the transition from immature to mature adult-born neurons (PMID: 39028813), underscoring the importance of precise dosage regulation throughout neuronal development.

**Key experimental findings:** A human iPSC-based model of CSS-like syndrome demonstrated that SOX11 haploinsufficiency directly impairs neuronal differentiation and survival, providing the most direct mechanistic link between heterozygous SOX11 loss and the human neurodevelopmental phenotype (PMID: 31035284). The regulation of SWI/SNF complex components by SOX11 provides a molecular explanation for the phenotypic overlap between SOX11-related CSS and CSS caused by direct SWI/SNF subunit mutations (PMID: 36882421).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

SOX11 has a well-characterized role as a dosage-sensitive transcriptional regulator of neuronal differentiation, with direct experimental evidence from human stem cell models, multiple animal systems, and molecular interaction studies converging on its neurodevelopmental function.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths:**
- Multiple independent international cohorts have identified de novo, predominantly loss-of-function SOX11 variants in patients with neurodevelopmental disorders, with statistically significant variant enrichment versus population controls (PMID: 39290158).
- Autistic traits are documented in 90% of a well-characterized patient cohort (PMID: 37924570), with a distinctive behavioral profile that supports SOX11 as a contributor to ASD phenomenology within the broader CSS/neurodevelopmental disorder spectrum.
- Convergent evidence from mouse, zebrafish, and Xenopus models, as well as human iPSC studies (PMID: 31035284), robustly supports the haploinsufficiency mechanism and neurological relevance of SOX11 loss.
- The molecular function of SOX11 as a regulator of SWI/SNF chromatin remodeling directly connects it to an established neurodevelopmental disease pathway.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- Formal ASD diagnoses using standardized diagnostic instruments (e.g., ADOS, ADI-R) are not consistently reported; most studies describe "autistic traits" rather than confirmed ASD diagnoses, limiting the precision of ASD-specific classification.
- The total number of patients with molecularly confirmed SOX11 variants and detailed phenotypic characterization remains relatively small, and many studies do not provide individual variant-level data.
- Genotype-phenotype correlations are incompletely defined, and it is unclear whether specific variant classes (LoF vs. HMG-domain missense) confer differential ASD risk within the broader CSS phenotypic spectrum.

**Conclusion:** SOX11 is a well-supported autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene in which heterozygous loss-of-function variants cause Coffin-Siris syndrome with a high prevalence of autistic traits, placing it as a credible ASD-associated gene within the broader category of chromatin-remodeling neurodevelopmental disorders. The evidence base is strengthened by convergent clinical, model organism, and molecular data, but would benefit from larger prospective cohorts with standardized ASD assessments to formally establish SOX11 as a definitive ASD gene rather than a gene causing a syndrome with prominent autistic features.
