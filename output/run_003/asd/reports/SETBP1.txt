# SETBP1 Evidence Report: Autism Spectrum Disorder and Heterozygous Autosomal Dominant Pathogenic Variants

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

SETBP1 (SET-binding protein 1) encodes a nuclear protein that binds the oncoprotein SET and regulates transcription, DNA binding, and self-renewal pathways across multiple tissue types. It plays critical roles in hematopoietic stem cell biology, neuronal differentiation, and transcriptional regulation, and harbors functionally distinct mutation classes that cause either neurodevelopmental disorders (via haploinsufficiency or non-degron missense variants) or hematologic malignancies and Schinzel-Giedion syndrome (via gain-of-function degron mutations).

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent case reports/series:** Nine independent publications or conference reports were identified, spanning case series, cohort studies, and large-scale genomic analyses.

**Total approximate number of patients:** Across studies with reported patient counts, approximately 135 individuals carry pathogenic or likely pathogenic SETBP1 variants in a neurodevelopmental context (34 patients in PMID: 33867525; 27 in PMID: 38923504; 18 in PMID: 41073373; 1 in PMID: 37798664; 1 in PMID: 25356899; 2 in the ASHG 2012 Brazilian report; 1 in the ASHG 2012 Korean report; plus unspecified numbers from large CNV/SNV cohorts in PMID: 25217958 and ASHG 2016).

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
The predominant neurodevelopmental phenotypes across loss-of-function (haploinsufficiency) cohorts include:
- Intellectual disability / developmental delay (reported in virtually all cohorts)
- Speech and language delays (PMID: 33867525; PMID: 38923504)
- Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (PMID: 38923504)
- **Autism spectrum disorder (ASD)**, including restricted interests, repetitive behaviors, and decreased social interest (PMID: 38923504)
- Hypotonia (PMID: 33867525; PMID: 38923504)
- Seizures (PMID: 38923504)
- Sleep issues, vision issues, and gastrointestinal issues (PMID: 38923504)

ASD was explicitly reported in the SETBP1 haploinsufficiency disorder (SETBP1-HD) cohort (PMID: 38923504), where it co-occurred with ADHD, speech delays, and restricted/repetitive behaviors, establishing it as a recognized feature of the SETBP1-HD phenotypic spectrum. The SETBP1-related disorder (SETBP1-RD) cohort in the same study, comprising individuals with non-degron missense variants, also showed ASD features.

**Predominant variant type:** Loss-of-function (LoF) variants — including frameshift, nonsense, and splice-site mutations, as well as single-gene coding deletions — are the predominant class associated with the neurodevelopmental/ASD phenotype, consistent with a haploinsufficiency mechanism. Non-degron missense variants (PMID: 41073373) represent a second, emerging class with overlapping neurodevelopmental features. Gain-of-function degron missense variants cause the distinct and more severe Schinzel-Giedion syndrome (PMID: 28346496) and are not primarily associated with ASD.

**Known cohorts:** The largest clinical cohorts were drawn from Simons Searchlight (PMID: 38923504), a well-established registry for rare genetic neurodevelopmental conditions. Additional patients were identified through the DDD (Deciphering Developmental Disorders) study framework (PMID: 25217958) and through exome sequencing in clinical and research settings. No patients were explicitly attributed to UDN, GREGoR, RUMC, or GEL cohorts in the available evidence.

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**
Multiple independent cohorts consistently report ASD as part of the SETBP1-HD phenotype, with the largest study (PMID: 38923504, n=27) providing the most direct evidence. However, the total number of patients with explicitly confirmed ASD diagnoses remains modest, and ASD is one of several co-occurring neurodevelopmental features rather than the sole or defining phenotype.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** All model organism studies identified used the mouse (*Mus musculus*).

**Key phenotypes from relevant models:**
- A heterozygous Setbp1 knockout mouse model (PMID: 41640626) — the most directly relevant to SETBP1 haploinsufficiency disorder — demonstrated **hyperactivity**, **decreased social interest**, and **increased olfactory exploration**, recapitulating core behavioral features of ASD and ADHD observed in human patients. At the circuit level, these mice showed hypoexcitability of layer-5 extratelencephalic projection neurons in the medial prefrontal cortex and alterations in the apical dendritic arbor of prefrontal pyramidal neurons, providing a neurobiological substrate for the behavioral phenotypes. Importantly, heterozygous knockout mice were viable and fertile.
- A knock-in mouse model carrying a gain-of-function Setbp1 mutation (PMID: 41612697) showed hydrocephalus in over 50% of cases and reduced survival, consistent with the more severe SGS phenotype; antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) treatment targeting SETBP1 prevented hydrocephalus and improved survival, demonstrating therapeutic tractability.
- Additional knock-in models confirmed roles in myeloid leukemogenesis (PMID: 38194688; PMID: 30367089; PMID: 34002029), which are less relevant to the neurodevelopmental/ASD phenotype but confirm the biological significance of SETBP1 dosage and mutation type.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The heterozygous knockout mouse model (PMID: 41640626) shows strong behavioral and neuroanatomical consistency with the human SETBP1-HD phenotype, particularly the hyperactivity, social deficits, and prefrontal circuit dysfunction that parallel ADHD and ASD features in patients.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**
A single published heterozygous knockout mouse study directly models the haploinsufficiency condition and recapitulates relevant behavioral phenotypes. The evidence is promising but limited by the absence of replication across independent laboratories or additional model organisms (e.g., zebrafish, *Drosophila*).

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
SETBP1 functions as a transcriptional regulator and SET-binding protein with roles in multiple signaling pathways. Key mechanisms include:
- **Proteasomal degradation regulation:** A 12-bp degron in exon 4 targets SETBP1 for ubiquitin-mediated degradation; gain-of-function mutations in this region prevent degradation and cause protein accumulation (PMID: 28346496; ASHG 2016 - Acuna-Hidalgo et al.).
- **Transcription and DNA binding:** SETBP1 directly binds DNA and regulates transcription; non-degron missense variants impair DNA binding, transcription, and neuronal differentiation independently of protein abundance (PMID: 41073373).
- **Neuronal differentiation:** Cellular assays demonstrated that SETBP1 plays a direct role in neuronal differentiation capacity and morphology (PMID: 41073373), providing a mechanistic link to neurodevelopmental phenotypes.
- **Hematopoietic pathways:** SETBP1 regulates HOXA-protein activation, attenuates RUNX1, activates MYB, and cooperates with ASXL1 to repress TGF-β pathway genes via histone deacetylation (PMID: 28447248; PMID: 30367089). While primarily relevant to hematologic disease, these findings confirm SETBP1's broad role as a transcriptional regulator.
- **PI3K-AKT and MAPK signaling:** SETBP1 participates in PI3K-AKT activation (PMID: 39054337) and MAPK pathway modulation (PMID: 34002029; PMID: 39979691), pathways with established relevance to neurodevelopmental biology.

**Expression pattern:**
SETBP1 and its target genes are broadly expressed across 31 adult human tissues, with tissue-specific transcription factor activity patterns identified by GTEx-based analysis (PMID: 37873221). Expression in brain and neural tissues, combined with the demonstrated role in neuronal differentiation (PMID: 41073373) and prefrontal circuit function in mice (PMID: 41640626), supports neurodevelopmental relevance. The gene is also expressed in long-term hematopoietic stem cells, though it is dispensable for normal hematopoiesis (PMID: 37464069).

**Key experimental findings:**
- Functional studies confirmed that haploinsufficiency reduces SETBP1 protein to approximately 20% of normal levels in at least one patient (PMID: 37798664), consistent with a dosage-sensitive mechanism.
- Non-degron missense variants cause loss-of-function effects on neuronal differentiation that are independent of protein abundance, defining a third mechanistic class (PMID: 41073373).
- Large-scale CNV/SNV analysis in 29,085 children with developmental delay statistically enriched SETBP1 loss-of-function variants in cases versus controls, establishing haploinsufficiency as a cause of a new neurodevelopmental disease subtype (PMID: 25217958).

**Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE**
There is solid mechanistic evidence for SETBP1's role in neuronal differentiation and transcriptional regulation, and expression data support brain relevance. However, direct mechanistic studies linking SETBP1 loss-of-function specifically to ASD-related molecular pathways (e.g., synaptic development, social behavior circuits) remain limited.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths:**
- Multiple independent clinical cohorts, including the Simons Searchlight registry (PMID: 38923504), consistently identify ASD as a recognized feature of SETBP1 haploinsufficiency disorder alongside intellectual disability, ADHD, and speech delays.
- A heterozygous knockout mouse model (PMID: 41640626) directly recapitulates social deficits and hyperactivity with prefrontal circuit abnormalities, providing convergent neurobiological support.
- The haploinsufficiency mechanism is well-established through large-scale genomic studies (PMID: 25217958), functional protein quantification (PMID: 37798664), and consistent de novo LoF variant identification across independent studies.
- All pathogenic variants associated with the neurodevelopmental/ASD phenotype follow an autosomal dominant heterozygous inheritance pattern, with the majority arising de novo.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- ASD is one of several co-occurring neurodevelopmental features in SETBP1-HD; it has not been studied as an isolated or primary phenotype, and the proportion of SETBP1-HD patients meeting formal ASD diagnostic criteria is not precisely quantified across all cohorts.
- The total number of patients with molecularly confirmed SETBP1-HD and explicitly documented ASD diagnosis remains modest (fewer than 100 with detailed phenotyping), limiting statistical power for genotype-phenotype correlations.
- Direct mechanistic studies linking SETBP1 haploinsufficiency to ASD-specific molecular pathways (e.g., synaptic function, social neural circuits) are absent; the mouse model behavioral data, while consistent, derive from a single published study.

**Conclusion:**
SETBP1 haploinsufficiency, caused by heterozygous loss-of-function variants inherited in an autosomal dominant manner, is an established neurodevelopmental disorder in which ASD is a recognized and recurrently reported feature alongside intellectual disability, ADHD, and speech delays. Convergent clinical, model organism, and biological evidence supports a moderate level of confidence for SETBP1 as an ASD-associated gene, with the primary limitation being the relatively small number of deeply phenotyped patients and the absence of mechanistic studies specifically addressing ASD-related biology. Expansion of patient registries and targeted neurobiological studies in the existing mouse model would substantially strengthen the evidence base.
