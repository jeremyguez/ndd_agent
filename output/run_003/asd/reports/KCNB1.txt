# Evidence Report: KCNB1 as an Autism Spectrum Disorder Gene (Autosomal Dominant / Heterozygous Variants)

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

KCNB1 encodes Kv2.1, a delayed-rectifier voltage-gated potassium channel that is highly expressed in the central nervous system, where it plays a critical role in regulating neuronal excitability, action potential repolarization, and membrane potential homeostasis. Beyond its canonical ion-conducting function, Kv2.1 also serves non-conducting structural roles, including formation of endoplasmic reticulum–plasma membrane junctions and protein–protein complexes with integrins and the leptin receptor, implicating it in neuronal development, migration, and intracellular signaling.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports/series:** Ten independent publications were identified, ranging from single case reports to international multi-center cohort studies.

**Total approximate number of patients:** Across all studies, approximately 144 patients with pathogenic or likely pathogenic KCNB1 variants are represented (36 in PMID: 32954514; 27 in PMID: 31513310; 25 in PMID: 34915430; 31 in PMID: 34181986; 10 in PMID: 35071126; 6 in PMID: 38951973; 3 in PMID: 36457583; 3 in PMID: 39135902; 2 in PMID: 39792727; 1 in PMID: 29264390). There is some potential overlap between cohorts, particularly among the larger international series.

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
The predominant clinical presentation is a **developmental and epileptic encephalopathy (DEE)** characterized by:
- Early-onset seizures (infantile or childhood onset), often refractory
- Global developmental delay and intellectual disability (ranging from mild to severe/profound)
- **Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) features and autistic behaviors**, reported across multiple cohorts
- Behavioral disturbances including attention problems, hyperactivity, and compulsive behaviors
- Language difficulties

ASD features are consistently reported as a prominent comorbidity. The largest natural history study (PMID: 32954514, n=36) documented autistic features in a substantial proportion of patients, and a dedicated behavioral study (PMID: 34915430, n=25) confirmed ASD risk, attention problems, and pathological adaptive behavior profiles, with adaptive behavior composite scores declining with age. A single-patient study (PMID: 29264390) reported a variant (p.Ile199Phe) associated specifically with autism, intellectual disability, and epilepsy with centrotemporal spikes. A familial case series (PMID: 39135902, n=3) described a p.Ser114Arg variant segregating with developmental delay, absence seizures, and ASD in a sibling pair, with autistic features in the obligate carrier father.

A notable **genotype–phenotype correlation** has emerged across studies: missense variants, particularly those with dominant-negative (DN) effects on Kv2.1 channel function, are associated with more severe epilepsy, earlier seizure onset, and more severe neurodevelopmental outcomes including ASD features, whereas truncating/loss-of-function (LoF) variants tend to produce milder epilepsy or no epilepsy, with neurodevelopmental disorder (intellectual disability, developmental delay) as the primary manifestation (PMID: 32954514; PMID: 31513310; PMID: 35071126). The severity of the epilepsy phenotype predicted the severity of the behavioral/ASD profile with 70% sensitivity and 90.9% specificity (PMID: 34915430).

**Predominant variant type:** The majority of reported pathogenic variants are **de novo heterozygous missense variants**, predominantly affecting the voltage-sensor domain (S4 segment) and pore domain of Kv2.1. Truncating (LoF) variants, including frameshift and nonsense variants, are less common but have been reported in both de novo and inherited contexts. The first inherited variant (p.Arg583*) was reported in PMID: 31513310, and a familial frameshift (c.522delA, p.Lys174Asnfs*20) was described in a Turkish family with intrafamilial phenotypic variability (PMID: 36457583). A familial missense variant (p.Ser114Arg) with incomplete penetrance was reported in PMID: 39135902.

**Known cohort membership:** None of the patients in the identified studies are explicitly reported as belonging to established research cohorts such as UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL. The studies represent independent clinical and research ascertainment from international centers (France, China, Turkey, South Korea, and multinational collaborations).

**Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG**
The evidence base includes multiple independent cohorts totaling over 140 patients, consistent de novo occurrence of heterozygous variants in the majority of cases, replication across diverse ethnic and geographic populations, clear genotype–phenotype correlations, ACMG-based pathogenicity classification, and functional validation in a subset of variants. ASD features are a recurrent and well-documented component of the phenotypic spectrum.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Both **zebrafish (Danio rerio)** and **mouse (Mus musculus)** models have been developed and characterized.

**Key findings:**

*Zebrafish:*
- Full knockout (kcnb1−/−) zebrafish exhibit impaired swimming behavior, spontaneous and provoked epileptiform-like electrographic activity, elevated locomotor activity upon PTZ exposure, and GABA dysregulation (PMID: 40332468). Critically, these phenotypes were **rescued by injection of human wild-type Kv2.1 DNA**, confirming specificity. Heterozygous fish also showed increased PTZ-induced activity (ASHG 2018 abstract). A separate zebrafish knockout study demonstrated defective inner ear development (PMID: 33316259), suggesting broader developmental roles.

*Mouse:*
- A knock-in mouse carrying the dominant-negative p.G379R variant (Kcnb1G379R) recapitulated core human phenotypes: **spontaneous seizures, hyperactivity, repetitive behaviors, impulsivity, and reduced anxiety** (PMID: 33132203). Heterozygous mice showed increased susceptibility to proconvulsant-induced seizures and abnormal interictal EEG activity. Immunohistochemistry confirmed reduced KV2.1, KV2.2, and AMIGO-1 protein expression, consistent with a dominant-negative mechanism.
- Two independent knock-in mouse lines carrying the recurrent p.R306C variant demonstrated **pronounced hyperactivity, altered seizure susceptibility, and frequent spike-wave discharges on EEG** reminiscent of Lennox-Gastaut syndrome in both heterozygous and homozygous animals (PMID: 38485094; PMID: 37034689).
- A knock-in mouse carrying p.R312H showed **severe cognitive deficits, compulsive behavior, neuronal damage, disrupted corticocortical and corticothalamic connectivity, and aberrant glutamatergic vesicular transport**, despite normal intrinsic electrical properties of pyramidal neurons, highlighting non-conducting pathogenic mechanisms (PMID: 39738805).
- Full knockout mice showed **impaired neocortical neuronal migration, disrupted synaptic connectivity, seizures, anxiety, and compulsive behavior** (PMID: 36207442).

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The model organism data are highly consistent with the human phenotype. Seizures, hyperactivity, repetitive/compulsive behaviors (analogous to ASD-related behaviors), cognitive deficits, and abnormal EEG activity are recapitulated across multiple independent mouse models and zebrafish lines. The behavioral phenotypes in mice (hyperactivity, repetitive behaviors, reduced anxiety, compulsivity) are well-recognized endophenotypes of ASD in rodent models.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent animal models (at least 5 distinct mouse lines and 3 zebrafish studies) across different variant types (full knockout, dominant-negative knock-in, haploinsufficiency-mimicking knock-in) consistently reproduce neurological, behavioral, and electrographic phenotypes relevant to the human condition.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
Kv2.1 is a principal delayed-rectifier K+ channel in central neurons. Its pathogenic variants cause dysfunction through multiple distinct mechanisms, including: altered current density, shifts in voltage dependence of activation/inactivation, reduced total or cell-surface protein expression, and dominant-negative suppression of co-expressed wild-type subunits (PMID: 31600826). High-throughput functional screening of 17 KCNB1 variants confirmed this mechanistic diversity.

Beyond ion conduction, KCNB1 participates in:
- **Integrin-K+ channel complexes (IKCs):** KCNB1 forms complexes with α5β5 integrins in neocortical neurons, regulating neuronal migration, morphology, and synaptic connectivity via FAK/Ras-MAPK signaling. Disruption of IKC signaling was demonstrated in R312H knock-in brains (PMID: 36207442).
- **Apoptotic signaling:** IKCs activate pro-apoptotic Ras-MAPK/ERK signaling under oxidative stress, while wild-type KCNB1 sequesters Akt to promote apoptosis; pathogenic variants disrupt this balance (PMID: 35357039).
- **Leptin receptor coupling:** KCNB1 forms stable complexes with the leptin receptor in hypothalamic POMC neurons, linking neuronal excitability to endocrine function (PMID: 39436109).
- **Cardiac electrophysiology:** A gain-of-function non-coding enhancer variant near KCNB1 was identified in familial ST-depression syndrome, implicating KCNB1 in cardiac arrhythmogenesis (PMID: 40208226).

**Expression pattern:**
In zebrafish, kcnb1 is expressed broadly in the CNS, including neurons, oligodendrocytes, and microglial cells (PMID: 40332468). In mammals, Kv2.1 is highly expressed in cortical and hippocampal pyramidal neurons, consistent with its role in regulating neuronal excitability and its implication in epilepsy and neurodevelopmental disorders.

**Key experimental findings relevant to ASD:**
- Functional studies of the p.Ile199Phe variant (PMID: 29264390) demonstrated partial LoF in both homotetrameric and heterotetrameric channels with normal protein expression and trafficking, establishing a direct link between channel dysfunction and ASD/epilepsy.
- The p.Ser114Arg variant (PMID: 39135902) dramatically slowed channel activation, deactivation, and inactivation with increased net current, representing a gain-of-function mechanism in an ASD-associated variant.
- Electrophysiological and protein expression studies in a Chinese cohort (PMID: 35071126) demonstrated that dominant-negative variants were associated with more severe ASD-related behavioral phenotypes than non-dominant-negative variants.
- GABA dysregulation in kcnb1−/− zebrafish (PMID: 40332468) provides a mechanistic link to the GABAergic imbalance hypothesis of ASD.

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**
Multiple independent functional studies across cell lines, primary neurons, and animal models have characterized the molecular consequences of KCNB1 variants. The gene's expression in relevant brain regions, its roles in neuronal excitability and neurodevelopment, and the mechanistic diversity of pathogenic variants are all well-established.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths:**
1. **Large, replicated clinical evidence base:** Over 140 patients with heterozygous KCNB1 variants have been reported across at least 10 independent publications from multiple countries, with ASD features consistently documented as a core component of the phenotypic spectrum alongside epilepsy and intellectual disability.
2. **Robust model organism validation:** Multiple independent mouse knock-in models and zebrafish knockouts recapitulate the human neurodevelopmental and behavioral phenotype, including ASD-relevant behaviors (repetitive behaviors, compulsivity, hyperactivity), with rescue experiments confirming specificity.
3. **Mechanistic depth:** Functional studies have characterized diverse loss-of-function and dominant-negative mechanisms, and non-conducting roles in neuronal migration and synaptic connectivity provide additional pathogenic mechanisms relevant to ASD.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. **ASD as a primary diagnosis is underrepresented:** In the majority of published cases, ASD features are reported as a comorbidity within a broader DEE or neurodevelopmental disorder phenotype rather than as the primary presenting diagnosis. Dedicated ASD cohort studies specifically ascertaining KCNB1 variants in ASD probands without epilepsy are limited.
2. **Inherited and familial variants:** Most pathogenic variants are de novo, and the evidence for autosomal dominant inheritance with reduced penetrance (as seen in PMID: 39135902 and PMID: 36457583) is based on small families. The contribution of inherited KCNB1 variants to ASD without epilepsy requires further characterization.
3. **Cohort overlap:** Potential patient overlap between the large international cohort studies cannot be excluded, which may inflate the apparent total number of independent observations.

**Conclusion:** KCNB1 is a well-established neurodevelopmental disorder gene in which heterozygous pathogenic variants—predominantly de novo missense variants—cause a spectrum of phenotypes ranging from severe DEE with prominent ASD features to milder neurodevelopmental disorder with intellectual disability. The evidence supporting KCNB1 as an ASD-associated gene is strong, supported by consistent clinical documentation of autistic features across large cohorts, multiple animal models recapitulating ASD-relevant behaviors, and mechanistic studies demonstrating diverse channel dysfunction and non-conducting neurodevelopmental roles. The primary caveat is that ASD in KCNB1 patients typically co-occurs with epilepsy and intellectual disability rather than presenting as isolated ASD, and further studies in ASD-specific cohorts would strengthen the evidence for KCNB1 as a primary ASD gene.
