# SPTAN1 Evidence Report: Autism Spectrum Disorder with Heterozygous (Autosomal Dominant) Pathogenic Variants

---

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

SPTAN1 encodes αII-spectrin, a ubiquitously expressed cytoskeletal scaffolding protein that is particularly abundant in the nervous system, where it plays essential roles in maintaining neuronal membrane integrity, axon initial segment (AIS) organization, voltage-gated sodium channel clustering, and myelination (PMID: 36697767). It forms obligate heterodimers with β-spectrins and interacts with ankyrin-G to establish and maintain the specialized membrane domains critical for neuronal excitability and signal propagation.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports/series:** Ten independent publications or conference reports were identified, spanning single case reports, small case series, and large international cohort studies.

**Total approximate number of patients:** Approximately 92 patients across all studies, including 31 (PMID: 36331550), 22 (PMID: 35150594), 20 (PMID: 39371122), 7 (PMID: 25631096), 3 (PMID: 34590414), 2 each from PMID: 39988451, PMID: 30548380, and the ASHG 2011 report, and 1 (PMID: 35219564).

**Most common phenotypes observed:** The predominant phenotypes across studies are neurodevelopmental in nature and include:
- Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy (DEE), including West syndrome with hypsarrhythmia, spastic quadriplegia, hypomyelination, and severe intellectual disability (PMID: 25631096; PMID: 35219564; ASHG 2010)
- Hereditary spastic paraplegia (HSP) and cerebellar ataxia, often overlapping (PMID: 35150594; PMID: 36331550)
- Intellectual disability and developmental delay, ranging from mild to severe (PMID: 30548380; ASHG 2011; PMID: 39988451)
- Seizures of variable type and severity (PMID: 36331550; PMID: 34590414)
- Early childhood-onset distal myopathy with neurogenic features (PMID: 39371122)
- Brain structural anomalies including pontocerebellar atrophy, hypomyelination, thin corpus callosum, and subependymal heterotopias (PMID: 25631096; PMID: 34590414; PMID: 35219564)

Notably, **autism spectrum disorder (ASD) as a primary or explicitly reported phenotype is not prominently featured** in the identified literature. Neurodevelopmental features such as intellectual disability, language delay, and developmental delay are consistently reported and may co-occur with ASD traits, but no study in this dataset specifically identifies ASD as a primary outcome or uses standardized ASD diagnostic criteria (e.g., DSM-5, ADOS) to characterize patients.

**Predominant variant type:** Three mechanistic classes are represented:
- *In-frame deletions/duplications* at the C-terminal αII/βII spectrin heterodimer nucleation site, acting via dominant-negative mechanisms, are associated with the most severe DEE phenotypes (PMID: 25631096; ASHG 2010).
- *Missense variants* (e.g., p.Arg19Trp, p.Arg1098Cys, p.Arg1624Cys) are associated with HSP, ataxia, and milder neurodevelopmental phenotypes (PMID: 35150594; PMID: 36331550).
- *Heterozygous loss-of-function (LoF) variants* (frameshift, nonsense, splice-site) are associated with distal myopathy (PMID: 39371122) and, in some cases, intellectual disability without epilepsy (PMID: 30548380).

**Known cohort representation:** Patients from the 100,000 Genomes Project (Genomics England/GEL) and DECIPHER are represented (PMID: 36331550). GeneMatcher facilitated international case aggregation in multiple studies. No patients are explicitly identified from UDN, GREGoR, or RUMC cohorts in the available data.

**Strength of clinical evidence for ASD specifically:** **LIMITED**. While SPTAN1 is robustly associated with a broad spectrum of autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorders—including DEE, HSP, ataxia, intellectual disability, and distal myopathy—the available clinical literature does not specifically establish ASD as a primary or well-characterized phenotype. The neurodevelopmental overlap is plausible but not directly demonstrated in this dataset.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (*Mus musculus*) and zebrafish (*Danio rerio*) have both been used to model SPTAN1 dysfunction.

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

- **Mouse conditional knockout (neuronal):** Loss of αII-spectrin in neurons caused death before one month of age, profound reductions in all β-spectrins, disrupted AIS, seizures, cortical lamination defects, and widespread neurodegeneration (PMID: 29038240). This demonstrates that SPTAN1 is essential for nervous system viability and function.
- **Mouse knock-in (calpain cleavage model):** Increased calpain-dependent degradation of αII-spectrin produced abnormal EEG activity, heightened neuronal excitability, AIS shortening and thinning, and increased sensitivity to chemoconvulsants (PMID: 41370269), directly linking αII-spectrin AIS integrity to seizure susceptibility.
- **Mouse conditional knockout (cochlear hair cells):** Hair cell-specific loss of Sptan1 caused rapid deafness and abnormal stereocilia formation via disrupted focal adhesion signaling (PMID: 34708331), demonstrating tissue-specific roles beyond the CNS.
- **Mouse conditional knockout (cardiac):** Cardiac-specific loss produced arrhythmia, fibrosis, and increased stress-induced mortality with altered ion channel expression (PMID: 31064843).
- **Zebrafish knockdown:** Morpholino-mediated knockdown of sptan1 impaired myelination (PMID: 20493457). Expression of patient-derived LoF variants (p.Gln1448Pro, p.Asn1839del) in sptan1-null zebrafish confirmed reduced protein abundance, impaired axonal localization, failure to restore voltage-gated sodium channel clustering, and impaired motility (PMID: 39988451).
- **Dominant-negative mouse cortical neuron model:** Transient expression of in-frame SPTAN1 mutants caused spectrin heterodimer aggregation, disrupted ankyrin-G and sodium channel clustering at the AIS, and elevated action potential threshold (PMID: 20493457).

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The model organism data are highly consistent with human phenotypes. Seizures, neurodegeneration, AIS disruption, hypomyelination, and motor impairment in mice and zebrafish mirror the epileptic encephalopathy, hypomyelination, and motor dysfunction observed in patients. The zebrafish functional rescue experiments directly validate specific patient variants as loss-of-function (PMID: 39988451).

**Strength of model organism evidence:** **STRONG**. Multiple independent model systems across two species, using diverse genetic approaches (conditional knockout, knock-in, knockdown, dominant-negative expression), consistently demonstrate that SPTAN1 dysfunction causes neurological phenotypes directly relevant to human disease.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**
- **AIS organization and neuronal excitability:** SPTAN1 is a core structural component of the AIS, where it scaffolds ankyrin-G and voltage-gated sodium channels to establish neuronal polarity and action potential initiation (PMID: 29038240; PMID: 20493457).
- **Hippo/YAP signaling:** SPTAN1 phosphorylation at high cell density recruits NUMB1/2 to the plasma membrane, enabling MST1/2 activation and downstream YAP inhibition, linking SPTAN1 to cell growth control and oncogenesis (PMID: 37843276).
- **Focal adhesion and integrin signaling:** In cochlear hair cells, SPTAN1 regulates focal adhesion formation and integrin signaling, critical for structural integrity (PMID: 34708331).
- **Inflammatory signaling:** A CRISPR screen identified SPTAN1 as an effector of hemodynamic shear stress in monocytes, activating store-operated calcium entry and IL-8 expression via a SPTAN1/RAF1/SOCE pathway (PMID: 41579376).
- **Nuclear signaling in cancer:** Lactylation of SPTAN1 at K1952/K1957 drives nuclear translocation and NOTCH1/HES1 activation in hepatocellular carcinoma (PMID: 41243220), indicating context-dependent non-cytoskeletal roles.
- **Cellular motility:** SPTAN1 is required for normal cell migration, as demonstrated in colorectal cancer cell lines where SPTAN1 knockdown reduced motility (PMID: 24456667).

**Expression pattern:** SPTAN1 is expressed ubiquitously in the mammalian nervous system, with subcellular specialization across neuronal subtypes (PMID: 36697767). It is particularly enriched at the AIS and nodes of Ranvier, structures critical for neuronal function. Expression is also documented in cochlear hair cells, cardiomyocytes, and monocytes.

**Key experimental findings relevant to neurodevelopmental disease:**
- Dominant-negative in-frame mutations produce thermolabile αII/β spectrin heterodimers that aggregate in neurons, disrupting AIS integrity and elevating action potential thresholds (PMID: 20493457).
- LoF variants cause nonsense-mediated decay of SPTAN1 mRNA, confirmed in patient muscle tissue (PMID: 39371122), and reduced protein abundance with impaired axonal localization in zebrafish (PMID: 39988451).
- ASO-mediated allele-specific knockdown of a dominant-negative SPTAN1 allele (p.D2303_L2305dup) in patient-derived iPSCs and iPSC-derived neurons achieved significant mutant mRNA reduction, establishing a therapeutic proof-of-concept for DEE5 (ASHG 2023).

**Strength of biological evidence:** **STRONG**. The molecular mechanisms underlying SPTAN1-related neurological disease are well-characterized across multiple experimental platforms, with direct mechanistic links between variant classes (dominant-negative vs. LoF), protein dysfunction (heterodimer instability, NMD, impaired AIS scaffolding), and neurological phenotypes.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level for SPTAN1 as an ASD gene with heterozygous autosomal dominant variants: LIMITED**

**Key strengths of the evidence:**
- SPTAN1 is robustly established as a dominant neurodevelopmental disease gene, with strong clinical, model organism, and biological evidence across multiple phenotypic categories (DEE, HSP, ataxia, intellectual disability, distal myopathy).
- The gene's role in AIS integrity, neuronal excitability, and myelination provides a compelling biological rationale for neurodevelopmental involvement broadly.
- Multiple independent international cohorts, functional studies, and animal models converge on SPTAN1 as a critical neurological gene, with over 90 patients reported across 10 publications.

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- **ASD is not explicitly reported as a primary phenotype** in any of the identified studies. No study applied standardized ASD diagnostic instruments, and ASD is not listed among the phenotypic outcomes in any cohort.
- The phenotypic spectrum of SPTAN1 is broad (DEE, HSP, ataxia, myopathy, intellectual disability), but ASD-specific features (social communication deficits, restricted/repetitive behaviors) are not systematically characterized, making it unclear whether ASD co-occurrence is incidental, underreported, or genuinely associated.
- Most severely affected patients (DEE5) have profound intellectual disability and epilepsy that may preclude ASD diagnosis; milder phenotype patients (HSP, ataxia) are not reported to have ASD features.

**Conclusion:** SPTAN1 is a well-validated autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disease gene with strong evidence across clinical, model organism, and biological domains, but the available literature does not specifically support ASD as a primary or well-characterized associated phenotype. Heterozygous pathogenic variants in SPTAN1 cause a clinically heterogeneous spectrum of neurological disorders—most prominently DEE, spastic paraplegia, cerebellar ataxia, and intellectual disability—that may share neurodevelopmental underpinnings with ASD but are phenotypically distinct. Prospective studies using standardized ASD assessments in SPTAN1 variant carriers, particularly those with milder phenotypes, would be required to formally evaluate ASD as part of the SPTAN1 phenotypic spectrum.
