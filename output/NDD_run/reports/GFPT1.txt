# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: GFPT1 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

GFPT1 (glutamine-fructose-6-phosphate transaminase 1), also known as GFAT, is the rate-limiting enzyme of the de novo hexosamine biosynthesis pathway (HBP) that produces UDP-GlcNAc, a critical substrate for N- and O-linked protein glycosylation. GFPT1 is essential for proper protein glycosylation at the neuromuscular junction and is regulated by nutrient availability and multiple signaling pathways including mTOR and AMPK (PMID: 37107691).

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of Studies and Patients:**
Two independent case reports/case series with detailed variant information were identified, totaling 6 patients with GFPT1 pathogenic variants. An additional three studies mentioned GFPT1 in CMS cohorts but without specific variant or individual patient details (PMID: 35661384, PMID: 28024842, PMID: 21310273).

**Clinical Phenotypes Observed:**
The most common phenotypes across reported cases include:
- Early-onset proximal and distal muscle weakness (PMID: 41323224)
- Fatigable weakness with marked decrements on repetitive nerve stimulation (RNS) ranging from 36.5% to >20% amplitude drop (PMID: 41323224, PMID: 38235042)
- Myopathic features on electromyography and muscle ultrasound (PMID: 41323224, PMID: 38235042)
- Late-onset progressive course in some patients (PMID: 38235042)
- Elevated muscle enzymes (PMID: 38235042)
- Atypical features in one patient including neonatal-onset epilepsy and cognitive impairment (PMID: 41323224)
- Structural muscle abnormalities including agenesis of pectoralis major muscle (Poland syndrome) (PMID: 38235042)

**Variant Types:**
The clinical evidence includes both missense variants (c.41G>A, p.Arg14Gln; c.22C>A) and loss-of-function variants (c.7+2T>G, a novel splice site mutation). All reported cases were autosomal recessive (homozygous or compound heterozygous), with no heterozygous dominant cases identified.

**Cohort Affiliation:**
No patients were reported from known research cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL). Cases were from Brazil and unspecified international centers.

**Diagnostic Confirmation:**
All cases showed favorable response to acetylcholinesterase inhibitors (pyridostigmine), supporting the congenital myasthenic syndrome diagnosis (PMID: 41323224, PMID: 38235042).

**Strength of Clinical Evidence: LIMITED**

The clinical evidence is limited to autosomal recessive cases only. No heterozygous dominant pathogenic variants in GFPT1 have been reported in the literature provided. The total patient number is small (6 patients with detailed phenotyping), and there is no evidence of de novo heterozygous variants or autosomal dominant inheritance patterns. The clinical evidence does not support GFPT1 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous pathogenic variants.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms Studied:**
Mouse (6 studies with conditional or full knockouts) and zebrafish (1 knockdown study).

**Key Phenotypes from Knockouts:**
Mouse studies consistently demonstrate neuromuscular junction dysfunction:
- Impaired neuromuscular transmission and fatigable muscle weakness (PMID: 29905857)
- Abnormal NMJ morphology and postsynaptic changes (PMID: 29905857, PMID: 40879313)
- Impaired protein O-GlcNAcylation and glycosylation, particularly of acetylcholine receptor delta subunit (AChRδ) (PMID: 40879313, PMID: 39456185)
- Progressive myopathic phenotype with tubular aggregates (PMID: 29905857)
- Muscle weakness and fatigue, especially in aged mice (PMID: 37744035, PMID: 38903011)
- Enhanced endoplasmic reticulum stress and increased apoptosis (PMID: 38903011)
- Impaired glucose metabolism and insulin-mediated glucose uptake (PMID: 37744035)

**Lethality and Mendelian Ratios:**
All mouse knockouts were viable with no reported lethality or Mendelian ratio deviations, indicating GFPT1 is not essential for embryonic development. Zebrafish knockdown was also viable (PMID: 17542649).

**Consistency with Human Phenotype:**
The mouse neuromuscular phenotypes (weakness, NMJ dysfunction, impaired glycosylation) are highly consistent with human GFPT1-related congenital myasthenic syndrome. Notably, galactose treatment rescued neuromuscular deficits in GFPT1-deficient mice (PMID: 40879313), suggesting potential therapeutic approaches.

**Strength of Model Organism Evidence: STRONG**

Multiple independent mouse models consistently demonstrate neuromuscular junction dysfunction and myopathic phenotypes that directly parallel human CMS presentations. The mechanistic findings regarding glycosylation defects and NMJ protein modifications provide strong biological support for the human phenotype.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known Pathways and Molecular Function:**
GFPT1 is the rate-limiting enzyme of the hexosamine biosynthesis pathway (HBP), which produces UDP-GlcNAc for protein glycosylation. GFPT1 is regulated by nutrient availability (glutamine, glucose, acetyl-CoA, UTP) and signaling molecules including mTOR, AMPK, PKA, and stress-responsive transcription factors (PMID: 37107691, PMID: 33897873). GFPT1 is a direct transcriptional target of Tisp40, a cardiomyocyte-enriched UPR-associated transcription factor (PMID: 37291168).

**Expression Pattern and Neurodevelopmental Relevance:**
GFPT1 is expressed in skeletal muscle and is critical for neuromuscular junction development and maintenance. The gene has a muscle-specific isoform with distinct regulatory properties (PMID: 37744035). GFPT1 is identified as one of the six most common causative genes in congenital myasthenic syndromes (PMID: 30808424) and was the fourth most commonly affected gene in a large Indian CMS cohort (7.6% of 156 genetically diagnosed patients) (PMID: 37721175).

**Key Experimental Findings:**
- GFPT1 mutations reduce GFPT1 levels and decrease protein glycosylation, leading to altered neuromuscular phenotype and compromised intracellular signaling at the NMJ (ASHG 2014 - Congenital Myasthenia Syndrome)
- GFPT1 deficiency specifically impairs glycosylation of AChRδ, a critical component of the acetylcholine receptor (PMID: 39456185)
- GFPT1 expression is significantly reduced in osteoarthritis cartilage, and GFPT1 overexpression protects chondrocytes (PMID: 39426230)
- GFPT1 is identified as a hub protein in pan-cancer proteomics across 22 cancer types (PMID: 40446800)
- GFPT1 regulates monocyte subset differentiation through metabolic pathways (PMID: 40777406)

**Strength of Biological Evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is robust, with well-characterized enzymatic function, clear pathway involvement in neuromuscular junction biology, and consistent mechanistic findings across multiple experimental systems. The role of GFPT1 in protein glycosylation and NMJ maintenance is well-established.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall Evidence Level for Heterozygous Dominant Pathogenic Variants: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key Strengths:**
- Strong biological evidence for GFPT1's critical role in hexosamine biosynthesis and neuromuscular junction function
- Consistent and robust mouse model phenotypes demonstrating neuromuscular dysfunction
- Well-characterized molecular mechanisms involving protein glycosylation defects
- GFPT1 is established as a common cause of autosomal recessive congenital myasthenic syndrome

**Key Gaps and Limitations:**
- **No heterozygous dominant cases identified**: All reported clinical cases are autosomal recessive (homozygous or compound heterozygous)
- **No de novo variants reported**: No spontaneous heterozygous variants have been documented
- **Limited clinical data**: Only 6 patients with detailed phenotyping across all studies
- **No neurodevelopmental phenotypes in heterozygotes**: The clinical evidence does not describe neurodevelopmental disorders, cognitive impairment, or developmental delay in heterozygous carriers
- **Mouse models are knockouts, not heterozygotes**: The animal evidence uses complete loss-of-function models, not haploinsufficiency models that would test dominant effects
- **Lack of segregation data**: No family studies demonstrating dominant inheritance patterns

**Conclusion:**

Based on the available literature, GFPT1 is a well-established gene for autosomal recessive congenital myasthenic syndrome with strong biological and model organism support. However, there is currently insufficient evidence to classify GFPT1 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous (autosomal dominant) pathogenic variants. All reported human cases follow autosomal recessive inheritance, and no heterozygous dominant pathogenic variants have been identified or characterized in the literature. To establish GFPT1 as a dominant neurodevelopmental gene would require identification of de novo or segregating heterozygous variants in affected individuals with neurodevelopmental phenotypes, along with functional studies demonstrating haploinsufficiency effects.
