# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: MARK2 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

MARK2 (Microtubule Affinity-Regulating Kinase 2) is a serine/threonine protein kinase that phosphorylates microtubule-associated proteins and regulates multiple signaling pathways critical for neuronal development and function, including WNT/β-catenin, PI3K/AKT/mTOR, and NF-κB pathways (PMID: 38302729). MARK2 plays essential roles in cell polarity, cytoskeletal organization, and synaptic transmission, making it a key regulator of neurodevelopmental processes.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports:** Three independent clinical studies were identified.

**Total patient count:** Approximately 33 patients across all studies (31 from large cohort study + 1 de novo case + retrotransposon insertion case).

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Autism spectrum disorder (present in 100% of reported cases)
- Neurodevelopmental delay/developmental delay (present in multiple cases)
- Distinctive facial features (reported in cohort study)
- Epilepsy (reported in 1 case with abnormal EEG findings)

**Predominant variant type:** Loss-of-function variants predominate, accounting for 81% of the 31-patient cohort (PMID: 39419027), with missense variants comprising 19%. Additional evidence includes a de novo frameshift insertion (c.645_646insA, p.(Ala216Serfs*12)) causing premature termination (PMID: 40542891) and a de novo retrotransposon insertion (ASHG 2013).

**Cohort source:** No patients were identified as originating from established diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL). The large cohort study (PMID: 39419027) appears to be from a large-scale sequencing initiative but specific cohort affiliation is not specified.

**Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) a substantial cohort of 31 patients with detailed phenotypic characterization and computational/functional validation (PMID: 39419027); (2) independent de novo variants meeting ACMG pathogenicity criteria (PMID: 40542891); (3) consistent phenotype across independent cases; and (4) functional validation through CRISPR-engineered iPSCs and transgenic mice demonstrating loss-of-function mechanisms. The predominance of loss-of-function variants and de novo mutations supports autosomal dominant inheritance with high penetrance.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (Mus musculus) exclusively; seven independent studies utilizing full knockout or conditional knockout models.

**Key neurological phenotypes from MARK2 knockout mice:**
- Dendritic spine morphology abnormalities and decreased excitatory synaptic transmission (PMID: 39436150)
- Impairments in learning and memory (PMID: 39436150)
- Defective social behavior (PMID: 39436150)
- Heightened seizure susceptibility and neuronal hyperexcitability in hippocampal slices (PMID: 39436150)
- Altered polarized vesicle transport affecting dendritic targeting (PMID: 38709923)
- Increased microglia density with primed phenotype and enhanced neuroinflammatory response (PMID: 30654821)
- Transcriptional changes in genes regulating synaptic transmission and ion homeostasis (PMID: 39436150)

**Lethality and viability:** All MARK2 knockout mice are viable, indicating that complete loss of MARK2 is not embryonic lethal. This is consistent with heterozygous pathogenic variants in humans causing neurodevelopmental phenotypes without embryonic lethality.

**Mendelian ratio deviations:** No Mendelian ratio deviations reported, suggesting normal Mendelian inheritance patterns.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The mouse knockout phenotypes show strong consistency with human clinical presentations. The social behavior deficits, learning/memory impairments, seizure susceptibility, and synaptic transmission abnormalities directly parallel autism spectrum disorder and neurodevelopmental delay observed in MARK2-variant patients. The neuroinflammatory priming phenotype (PMID: 30654821) may contribute to the broader neurodevelopmental phenotype.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**

Multiple independent mouse studies consistently demonstrate that MARK2 loss causes neurological phenotypes highly relevant to autism and neurodevelopmental disorders. The comprehensive characterization of synaptic, behavioral, and transcriptional changes provides mechanistic insight into how MARK2 variants cause human disease.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

MARK2 functions as a critical node in multiple neurodevelopmentally relevant pathways:

1. **WNT/β-catenin signaling:** MARK2 loss-of-function variants downregulate WNT/β-catenin signaling, leading to anomalous neural progenitor polarity, disorganized neural rosettes, and imbalanced proliferation/differentiation in iPSC-derived neurons (PMID: 39419027). This pathway is essential for cortical development and neurogenesis.

2. **Cytoskeletal regulation:** MARK2 phosphorylates KIF13A and regulates microtubule stability and polarized vesicular transport (PMID: 38709923). MARK2 also regulates tight junction dynamics and actin/microtubule organization in epithelial cells (PMID: 35971301).

3. **Cell polarity:** MARK2 (Par1b) associates with polarity regulators including PARD3 and is required for asymmetric cell division in muscle stem cells (PMID: 26569381). Loss of MARK2 impairs polarity establishment in neural progenitors.

4. **Hippo/YAP-TAZ pathway:** MARK2 and MARK3 directly phosphorylate NF2 and YAP/TAZ, functioning as catalytic codependencies for YAP/TAZ function (PMID: 39058094).

5. **Inflammatory regulation:** MARK2 phosphorylates UBAC2 to promote selective ER autophagy, restraining inflammatory responses (PMID: 39284914).

**Expression pattern:** MARK2 is expressed in the developing and adult nervous system, with particular relevance to neuronal progenitors, neurons, and glia. The gene's involvement in synaptic transmission, dendritic morphology, and microglial function indicates broad CNS expression.

**Key experimental findings:**

- Large-scale GWAS identified MARK2 as a new moderate-risk gene for autism through analysis of 42,607 autism cases, with loss-of-function variants associated with less cognitive impairment than highly penetrant autism genes (PMID: 35982159; P < 2.5 × 10⁻⁶).

- Integrated analysis of de novo SNVs and CNVs in 41,165 individuals with neurodevelopmental disorders identified MARK2 as a high-confidence (>90% true-positive probability) NDD gene (PMID: 35468861).

- iPSC-derived neurons from MARK2-deficient individuals show anomalous polarity and disorganized neural rosettes, with Mark2+/- mice exhibiting abnormal cortical formation and ASD-like behavior (PMID: 39419027).

- MARK2 regulates C9orf72 repeat-associated non-AUG translation, providing neuroprotection under proteotoxic conditions (PMID: 41231952).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is comprehensive, with MARK2's role in WNT/β-catenin signaling, cell polarity, cytoskeletal organization, and synaptic function directly explaining the neurodevelopmental phenotypes observed in patients. Multiple independent GWAS studies confirm MARK2 as a statistically significant neurodevelopmental disorder gene, and functional studies demonstrate that loss-of-function variants impair critical developmental pathways.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths of the evidence:**

1. **Convergent clinical and genetic evidence:** Multiple independent studies (large cohort, de novo cases, retrotransposon insertions) consistently identify MARK2 loss-of-function variants in autism spectrum disorder and neurodevelopmental delay patients.

2. **Robust statistical support:** Two large-scale GWAS studies (42,607 autism cases and 41,165 NDD cases) independently identified MARK2 as a statistically significant moderate-to-high-confidence neurodevelopmental disorder gene.

3. **Functional validation:** CRISPR-engineered iPSCs and transgenic mice provide mechanistic evidence that MARK2 loss impairs WNT/β-catenin signaling and causes ASD-relevant phenotypes including social behavior deficits, learning impairments, and seizure susceptibility.

4. **Pathway clarity:** MARK2's roles in WNT/β-catenin signaling, cell polarity, and synaptic transmission are well-characterized and directly relevant to neurodevelopmental processes.

5. **Consistent inheritance pattern:** Predominance of de novo and loss-of-function variants, combined with viable knockout mice, supports autosomal dominant inheritance with high penetrance.

**Key gaps or limitations:**

1. **Limited patient cohort diversity:** The 31-patient cohort represents the largest single study, but patients are not from established diagnostic networks (UDN, GREGoR, DDD), limiting generalizability assessment.

2. **Incomplete phenotypic characterization:** While autism and developmental delay are consistently reported, detailed cognitive, behavioral, and neuroimaging data are limited. The relationship between MARK2 variants and the reported epilepsy phenotype requires further investigation.

3. **Heterozygous vs. homozygous effects:** While clinical evidence focuses on heterozygous variants, the biological evidence derives primarily from complete knockout models, which may not fully recapitulate haploinsufficiency mechanisms.

4. **Missense variant characterization:** Although 19% of the cohort carries missense variants, detailed functional characterization of specific missense mutations is limited.

**Conclusion:**

MARK2 is a well-supported neurodevelopmental disorder gene with strong evidence for autosomal dominant pathogenic variants causing autism spectrum disorder and developmental delay. The convergence of large-scale genetic studies, consistent clinical phenotypes across independent cases, and comprehensive functional validation through iPSC and mouse models provides compelling evidence for MARK2's role in neurodevelopmental pathology. Future studies should focus on expanding patient cohorts through established diagnostic networks, characterizing the full phenotypic spectrum including neuroimaging and detailed cognitive assessments, and elucidating the specific mechanisms by which heterozygous MARK2 variants impair WNT/β-catenin signaling and synaptic development.
