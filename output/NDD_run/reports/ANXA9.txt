# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: ANXA9

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

ANXA9 (Annexin A9) is a member of the annexin family of calcium-dependent phospholipid-binding proteins. The gene is located at chromosome 1q21.3 and encodes a protein involved in membrane organization and cellular signaling, though its specific role in neurodevelopmental processes remains incompletely characterized.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of case reports/series:** 0

**Total patients:** 0

**Most common phenotypes:** Not applicable

**Predominant variant type:** Not applicable

**Known cohort sources:** Not applicable

**Strength of clinical evidence: NONE**

No clinical case reports or case series describing patients with ANXA9 pathogenic variants and neurodevelopmental phenotypes were identified in the literature. There is currently no clinical evidence linking ANXA9 to neurodevelopmental disorders in humans.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Zebrafish

**Key phenotypes:** A zebrafish knockdown study (PMID: 21931561) demonstrated abnormal kidney development and impaired glomerular filtration capacity. No neurological phenotypes, lethality data, or Mendelian ratio deviations were reported.

**Consistency with human neurodevelopmental phenotype:** The available model organism evidence does not address neurodevelopmental function. The renal phenotypes observed in zebrafish knockdown are unrelated to the proposed neurodevelopmental disorder indication.

**Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED**

The single zebrafish study provides evidence for a role in renal development but offers no support for neurodevelopmental pathology. Additional model organism studies specifically examining neurological function would be needed.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:** ANXA9 is located within a melanoma susceptibility locus at 1q21.3. According to GWAS data (ASHG 2011 - MacGregor et al.), the SNP rs7412746 in this region acts as an expression quantitative trait locus (eQTL) that modulates expression of ARNT and CTSS, with secondary effects on CTSK and SETDB1 expression.

**Expression pattern:** The biological evidence focuses on ANXA9's genomic location and regulatory context rather than its direct expression pattern or brain-specific function. No data on ANXA9 expression in neural tissues or during neurodevelopment were identified.

**Key experimental findings:** The melanoma GWAS (Discovery: 2,168 cases/4,387 controls; Replication cohorts totaling 5,193 cases/15,144 controls) identified rs7412746 with highly significant association (P=2.5×10⁻⁷ discovery, P=1.5×10⁻⁵ replication, P=9.0×10⁻¹¹ meta-analysis). However, this evidence pertains to melanoma susceptibility, not neurodevelopmental function.

**Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED**

The biological evidence establishes ANXA9's location in a regulatory region associated with melanoma risk but provides no mechanistic insight into neurodevelopmental pathways or evidence that ANXA9 itself is the causal gene at this locus.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
- ANXA9 is located in a well-characterized genomic region with strong GWAS support for disease association
- Model organism studies confirm the gene has developmental relevance

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- Complete absence of clinical evidence: no reported patients with ANXA9 variants and neurodevelopmental phenotypes
- No functional studies demonstrating ANXA9's role in neural development or function
- Available model organism data address only renal development, not neurological phenotypes
- Biological evidence links the genomic region to melanoma, not neurodevelopmental disorders
- No evidence that ANXA9 is the causal gene at the 1q21.3 locus versus other nearby genes

**Conclusion:**

There is currently insufficient evidence to support ANXA9 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous pathogenic variants. While ANXA9 is located in a genomic region with established disease associations and shows developmental relevance in model organisms, the available evidence does not address neurodevelopmental function. Establishing ANXA9's role in neurodevelopmental disorders would require identification of affected patients with ANXA9 variants, functional studies demonstrating neural relevance, and mechanistic evidence linking ANXA9 dysfunction to neurodevelopmental pathology.
