# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: UPF1 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

UPF1 is a core ATP-dependent RNA helicase that functions as a master regulator of nonsense-mediated mRNA decay (NMD), a translation-coupled quality control mechanism that eliminates mRNAs with premature termination codons (PMID: 37605642). Beyond its canonical NMD role, UPF1 is involved in virtually all aspects of eukaryotic RNA metabolism including transcription regulation, pre-mRNA splicing, ribosome assembly, translation, and structure-dependent mRNA degradation pathways (PMID: 37474727).

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports:** Three independent case reports/case series were identified.

**Total patient count:** Four patients with de novo UPF1 variants associated with neurodevelopmental phenotypes (1 patient in PMID: 39993774; 2 patients in PMID: 39571789; 1 patient in ASHG 2023 presentation).

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Intellectual disability (present in all 4 patients)
- Developmental delay (motor and/or verbal; 3 of 4 patients)
- Autism spectrum features (3 of 4 patients)
- Dysmorphic facial features including frontal bossing, hypertelorism, and midface hypoplasia (3 of 4 patients)
- Infantile hypotonia (1 patient, but part of broader neurodevelopmental presentation)
- Myoclonic epilepsy (1 patient)
- Hearing loss (1 patient)

**Predominant variant type:** Mixed. Two missense variants (p.Ala526Thr in PMID: 39993774; p.Asp662Asn in PMID: 39571789; p.Thr616Ala in ASHG 2023) and one loss-of-function deletion (p.Asp317del in PMID: 39571789). All variants were de novo and located in functionally critical domains (helicase motifs or conserved regions).

**Cohort source:** No patients were identified as part of established diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL). All cases appear to be from individual clinical centers.

**Diagnostic methodology:** All cases were identified through trio exome sequencing with de novo variant confirmation. The ASHG 2023 case included functional validation through ACMG classification as Likely Pathogenic. One study (PMID: 39571789) included functional validation using Drosophila models, demonstrating that both variants caused developmental lethality at different stages, supporting pathogenicity.

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) consistent de novo inheritance pattern across all cases; (2) location of variants in highly conserved functional domains; (3) consistent neurodevelopmental phenotype across patients; and (4) functional validation in model organisms for at least one variant. However, the evidence is limited by: (1) very small sample size (4 patients total); (2) lack of independent replication in large cohorts; (3) no population frequency data to exclude benign variants; and (4) limited functional characterization of individual variants in human cells.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (4 studies) and zebrafish (1 study).

**Key neurological phenotypes from knockouts:**

- **Brain development:** Conditional knockout of UPF1 in mouse neural progenitors causes perinatal microcephaly with prolonged cell cycle in radial glia progenitor cells and reduced upper-layer neurons, demonstrating UPF1's critical role in controlling progenitor cell maintenance and brain size (PMID: 38697111).

- **Developmental lethality:** UPF1 conditional knockout in mouse is perinatal lethal in the context of brain development (PMID: 38697111), indicating essential developmental function.

- **B cell development:** Conditional knockout impairs early B cell development with blocked VH-DHJH recombination and failure to develop immature B cells (PMID: 38982067), though this is not directly neurological.

- **NMD dysregulation in neurological disease models:** In the Fmr1-knockout mouse model of fragile X syndrome, NMD is hyperactivated during cerebral cortex, hippocampus, and cerebellum development, leading to abnormal regulation of neuronal mRNAs important for brain development (PMID: 34784943).

- **Transcript regulation:** Zebrafish UPF1 knockdown alters endogenous transcript levels of APP family members, demonstrating involvement in degradation of mutant mRNAs with premature termination codons (PMID: 38789273).

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The mouse brain development data showing microcephaly and developmental delay are consistent with the intellectual disability and developmental delays observed in human patients. However, the human cases show overgrowth features (particularly in the ASHG 2023 case) that are not directly recapitulated in the knockout models. The perinatal lethality of complete UPF1 knockout contrasts with the viability of heterozygous human carriers, suggesting that heterozygous missense variants may cause haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative effects rather than complete loss of function.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) consistent demonstration of UPF1's essential role in brain development; (2) specific neurological phenotypes (microcephaly, developmental delay) that parallel human disease; and (3) demonstration of NMD dysregulation in relevant neurological disease contexts. However, the evidence is limited by: (1) reliance on complete or conditional knockouts rather than heterozygous missense variants matching human cases; (2) lack of direct modeling of the specific human variants identified; and (3) incomplete phenotypic overlap (overgrowth features not observed in models).

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

UPF1 functions as a core component of multiple RNA decay pathways:

1. **Nonsense-mediated mRNA decay (NMD):** UPF1 is the central trans-acting factor in NMD, a translation-coupled quality control mechanism that eliminates mRNAs with premature termination codons (PMID: 37605642). This pathway is critical for preventing synthesis of C-terminally truncated proteins that could be pathogenic.

2. **Structure-dependent mRNA decay:** UPF1 is part of a genome-wide RNA decay pathway that reduces mRNA half-lives based on 3' UTR secondary structure, independent of sequence specificity (PMID: 32017897). Depletion of UPF1 increases steady-state levels of mRNAs with highly structured 3' UTRs.

3. **Staufen-mediated mRNA decay (SMD):** UPF1 directly interacts with STAU1 and STAU2 double-stranded RNA-binding proteins to enhance helicase activity and promote mRNA decay (PMID: 23681777). SMD and NMD are competitive pathways that both employ UPF1.

4. **lncRNA regulation:** UPF1 targets long intergenic non-coding RNAs (lincRNAs) for NMD-mediated degradation, with approximately 11% of lincRNAs affected by NMD (ASHG 2017 presentation). UPF1 also interacts with other lncRNAs to regulate gene expression and control RNA decay (PMID: 33732285).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**

While specific brain expression data are not detailed in the provided evidence, the model organism studies demonstrate that UPF1 is essential during brain development, particularly in neural progenitor cells (PMID: 38697111). The involvement of UPF1 in regulating neuronal mRNAs important for brain development is demonstrated in the fragile X syndrome model (PMID: 34784943).

**Key experimental findings:**

- **Regulation by kinase signaling:** Oncogenic MET signaling induces phosphorylation of UPF1, which then downregulates STING expression by modulating 3'-UTR length through NMD, demonstrating that UPF1 activity is regulated by kinase signaling pathways (PMID: 34099454).

- **Protein stability regulation:** TRIM34 directly interacts with UPF1 to promote its ubiquitination and degradation, demonstrating that UPF1 protein levels are tightly regulated by E3 ligase-mediated mechanisms (PMID: 38704136).

- **Disease relevance of NMD dysregulation:** UPF1 knockdown rescues mRNA and protein levels of disease-causing mutations in SMN1 (spinal muscular atrophy; up to 22-fold rescue with puromycin) and DHTKD1 (Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease), demonstrating that NMD-mediated degradation of mutant transcripts contributes to disease pathogenesis in inherited neurological disorders (ASHG 2016 and 2012 presentations).

- **Non-canonical functions:** UPF1 deficiency in pancreatic cancer cells increases mitochondrial ROS production and inflammatory cytokine secretion, revealing a non-canonical role for UPF1 in regulating mitochondrial metabolism (PMID: 40591563).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) extensive characterization of UPF1's multiple molecular functions across different RNA decay pathways; (2) demonstration of UPF1's essential role in brain development and neuronal mRNA regulation; (3) clear mechanistic links between NMD dysregulation and inherited neurological diseases; (4) evidence that UPF1 activity is regulated by multiple signaling pathways and protein-protein interactions; and (5) multiple independent studies confirming UPF1's central role in mRNA quality control. The evidence is comprehensive and well-supported across multiple experimental systems.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths of the evidence:**

1. **Consistent clinical phenotype:** All four reported patients with de novo UPF1 variants present with intellectual disability and developmental delay, with high concordance for autism spectrum features and dysmorphic facial features.

2. **De novo inheritance pattern:** All variants are de novo, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance and suggesting that heterozygous variants are sufficient to cause disease.

3. **Functional domain localization:** All variants are located in highly conserved functional domains (helicase motifs), supporting pathogenicity.

4. **Mechanistic plausibility:** The biological evidence strongly supports a mechanism whereby disruption of UPF1 function impairs NMD and other RNA decay pathways, leading to dysregulation of neuronal mRNAs critical for brain development.

5. **Model organism validation:** Mouse studies demonstrate that UPF1 is essential for normal brain development, with knockout causing microcephaly and developmental delay phenotypes consistent with human disease.

6. **Disease relevance of NMD dysregulation:** Multiple studies demonstrate that NMD dysregulation contributes to pathogenesis of inherited neurological disorders (SMA, CMT2), supporting the biological plausibility of UPF1 variants causing neurodevelopmental disease.

**Key gaps and limitations:**

1. **Very small sample size:** Only four patients have been reported, limiting statistical power and phenotypic characterization.

2. **Lack of large cohort validation:** No cases identified in major diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD), limiting population frequency estimates and phenotypic spectrum definition.

3. **Limited functional characterization:** Only one variant (p.Asp317del) has been functionally validated in model organisms. The other three variants lack direct functional studies in human cells or model organisms.

4. **Incomplete phenotypic overlap with models:** The overgrowth features observed in the ASHG 2023 case are not recapitulated in UPF1 knockout models, suggesting either incomplete understanding of UPF1's role or potential involvement of additional genetic factors.

5. **Heterozygous vs. homozygous effects:** All human cases are heterozygous, but most model organism studies use complete or conditional knockouts, making direct comparison difficult.

6. **Lack of segregation data:** No multi-generational families with UPF1 variants have been reported, limiting assessment of penetrance and expressivity.

**Conclusion:**

UPF1 is emerging as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene associated with de novo heterozygous missense and loss-of-function variants. The clinical evidence, while limited in sample size, shows consistent presentation with intellectual disability, developmental delay, autism spectrum features, and dysmorphic facial features. The biological evidence strongly supports a mechanistic link between UPF1 dysfunction and neurodevelopmental disease through impaired NMD and dysregulation of neuronal mRNAs. However, the evidence remains at a MODERATE level due to the very small number of reported cases and limited functional characterization of individual variants. Larger cohort studies and functional validation of additional variants are needed to establish UPF1 as a definitively pathogenic neurodevelopmental disorder gene and to fully characterize the phenotypic spectrum.
