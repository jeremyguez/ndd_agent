# CAMK4 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE: EVIDENCE REPORT

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

CAMK4 (Calcium/Calmodulin-Dependent Protein Kinase IV) is a serine/threonine kinase that regulates calcium-activated signaling pathways critical for T-cell differentiation, podocyte function, and neurodevelopment. CaMK4 controls transcriptional programs affecting cell proliferation, apoptosis, and synaptic plasticity through phosphorylation of downstream effectors including CREB, CREM-α, and BCL6.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports:** 2 case reports/series with detailed clinical data (PMID: 30262571, PMID: 39442785); 1 additional case report title identified but without detailed variant information (PMID: 37070062).

**Total patients:** Approximately 4 patients with documented CAMK4 variants associated with neurodevelopmental phenotypes (1 patient with de novo splice-site variant; 3 patients with unspecified variants).

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Hyperkinetic movement disorder (present in all reported cases)
- Autism spectrum disorder (present in all reported cases)
- Intellectual disability (1 case)
- Ataxia (1 case)
- Severe generalized dystonia (1 case)
- Milestone delay (1 case)
- Comorbid neurological symptoms (3 cases)

**Predominant variant type:** The single well-characterized variant is a loss-of-function (LoF) splice-site variant (c.981+1G>A) causing out-of-frame deletion and premature termination codon. However, biochemical studies demonstrated a gain-of-function mechanism with constitutive CaMKIV activity (PMID: 30262571). The three variants in the second series are described as "likely pathogenic" but specific variant types are not detailed in the available abstract (PMID: 39442785).

**Cohort source:** No indication that patients are from established diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL).

**Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED**

The clinical evidence is limited by the small number of patients (4 total) and incomplete variant characterization in 75% of cases. However, the single well-characterized case demonstrates compelling evidence: a de novo variant unobserved in human variation catalogs, in silico and cDNA validation of pathogenic mechanism, and biochemical confirmation of functional consequence. The convergence of hyperkinetic movement disorder and autism spectrum disorder across all reported cases suggests a consistent phenotypic signature. The apparent gain-of-function mechanism in the characterized case is notable and requires clarification regarding whether heterozygous variants are pathogenic through haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative/gain-of-function mechanisms.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (Mus musculus) exclusively; 10 studies identified with various knockout and knockdown approaches.

**Neurological phenotypes from mouse models:**
- Cognitive defects associated with iron dyshomeostasis in cerebellum and liver (PMID: 32460794)
- Altered intracellular calcium homeostasis and abnormal calcium signaling (PMID: 32460794)
- Reduced seizure-induced apoptosis and inflammation in hippocampal neurons when CAMK4 is knocked down (PMID: 39140685)
- Ameliorated experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis (EAE) in Camk4-/- mice (PMID: 24667640)

**Lethality and Mendelian ratios:** No reports of embryonic lethality or deviation from expected Mendelian ratios in Camk4-/- mice, suggesting the gene is not essential for viability.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The mouse evidence demonstrates CaMK4's role in calcium signaling, neuronal function, and immune regulation. However, the mouse knockout studies primarily focus on inflammatory and autoimmune phenotypes rather than neurodevelopmental outcomes. The cognitive defects observed in the iron homeostasis study (PMID: 32460794) provide some neurological relevance, but no mouse model has been reported with hyperkinetic movement disorder or autism-like phenotypes. The lack of neurodevelopmental phenotypes in Camk4-/- mice may reflect differences between complete knockout and heterozygous gain-of-function variants, or species-specific developmental compensation.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**

While extensive mouse studies demonstrate CaMK4's involvement in calcium signaling, immune regulation, and some cognitive function, the evidence is not strongly aligned with the human neurodevelopmental phenotypes. The absence of hyperkinetic movement disorder or autism-like behaviors in mouse models limits direct phenotypic correlation. However, the demonstrated role in calcium homeostasis, neuronal signaling, and the cognitive defects in iron-dysregulated mice provide biological plausibility for neurodevelopmental involvement.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

CaMK4 functions as a central hub in calcium-activated signaling with multiple neurodevelopmentally relevant pathways:

- **Transcriptional regulation:** CaMK4 phosphorylates CREB at Ser129 and Ser133, driving expression of genes involved in cell survival and stemness (PMID: 39076120). CaMK4 also regulates CREM-α and BCL6 expression, controlling T-cell differentiation and follicular helper T cell function (PMID: 38287012).

- **Metabolic regulation:** CaMK4 phosphorylates PFKP to promote aerobic glycolysis in T cells, affecting cell function and stability (PMID: 36449620).

- **Calcium homeostasis:** CaMK4 regulates transferrin trafficking and iron homeostasis through CAMKK2-CAMK4 signaling, with direct relevance to cerebellar and hepatic function (PMID: 32460794).

- **Synaptic plasticity and neuroinflammation:** CaMK4 regulates neuron apoptosis and inflammation through miR-206-3p-dependent mechanisms (PMID: 39140685).

**Expression pattern:** CaMK4 is expressed in neurons and immune cells. The gene is upregulated in psoriatic lesional skin (PMID: 35869084) and advanced atherosclerotic plaques (PMID: 40336480), indicating context-dependent expression. Specific brain region expression patterns are not detailed in the available evidence, but the involvement in calcium signaling, synaptic plasticity, and the cognitive defects in knockout mice indicate neuronal expression.

**Key experimental findings:**

- CaMK4 is identified as an emerging autism risk gene with three pathogenic variants found in individuals with hyperkinetic movement disorder and autism spectrum disorder (PMID: 39442785).

- Twelve autism-associated genes impact CaMK4 signaling pathways, suggesting CaMK4 is a convergence point for autism-related molecular mechanisms (PMID: 39442785).

- CaMK4 controls follicular helper T cell expansion through BCL6 regulation, with CAMK4 mRNA levels in Tfh cells correlating with BCL6 expression in SLE patients (PMID: 38287012).

- GWAS evidence identifies CAMK4 as associated with lung cancer risk in African ancestry populations (p < 5 × 10⁻⁸) (ASHG 2023), elevated testosterone in PCOS (P = 5.98 × 10⁻⁸), and systolic blood pressure (p = 0.02) (ASHG 2010, ASHG 2016), indicating broader biological relevance.

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is strong, demonstrating CaMK4's central role in calcium-activated signaling pathways with direct relevance to neurodevelopment, synaptic plasticity, and neuroinflammation. The identification of CaMK4 as a convergence point for autism-associated signaling pathways (PMID: 39442785) and the multiple validated downstream targets (CREB, CREM-α, BCL6, PFKP) provide robust mechanistic support. The gene's involvement in calcium homeostasis, transferrin trafficking, and iron metabolism—all critical for cerebellar and neuronal function—strengthens the biological plausibility for neurodevelopmental phenotypes.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths:**

1. **Convergent clinical phenotype:** All reported patients (n=4) present with hyperkinetic movement disorder and autism spectrum disorder, suggesting a consistent and recognizable phenotypic signature.

2. **Mechanistic validation:** The single well-characterized de novo variant (c.981+1G>A) has been validated through in silico analysis, cDNA studies, and biochemical confirmation of functional consequence, establishing pathogenicity.

3. **Biological plausibility:** CaMK4 is identified as a key modulator of autism-associated signaling pathways (PMID: 39442785) with demonstrated roles in calcium homeostasis, synaptic plasticity, and neuroinflammation—all critical for neurodevelopment.

4. **Pathway convergence:** Multiple autism-associated genes converge on CaMK4 signaling, suggesting the gene is a central node in neurodevelopmental regulation.

**Key gaps and limitations:**

1. **Small patient cohort:** Only 4 patients with CAMK4 variants have been reported, limiting statistical power and phenotypic characterization.

2. **Incomplete variant characterization:** Three of four patients lack detailed variant information, making it impossible to assess whether pathogenic variants are consistently loss-of-function, gain-of-function, or mixed mechanisms.

3. **Mechanism uncertainty:** The characterized variant shows a LoF mutation but gain-of-function biochemical activity, creating ambiguity about the pathogenic mechanism for heterozygous variants.

4. **Limited mouse model correlation:** Camk4-/- mice do not recapitulate the hyperkinetic movement disorder or autism-like phenotypes observed in humans, limiting translational validation.

5. **Lack of functional studies in neurons:** Most biological evidence derives from immune cells and non-neuronal tissues; direct functional studies in neurons or neuronal models are limited.

**Conclusion:**

CAMK4 shows MODERATE evidence as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene associated with heterozygous pathogenic variants. The convergent clinical phenotype of hyperkinetic movement disorder and autism spectrum disorder across all reported cases, combined with strong biological evidence identifying CaMK4 as a key modulator of autism-associated signaling pathways, supports pathogenic involvement. However, the small number of patients (n=4), incomplete variant characterization in 75% of cases, and lack of consistent neurodevelopmental phenotypes in mouse models limit the strength of evidence. Additional case reports with complete variant characterization, functional studies in neuronal systems, and investigation of whether pathogenic variants act through haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative mechanisms would substantially strengthen the evidence base.
