# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: MIDEAS AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

MIDEAS is a core scaffolding component of the Mitotic Deacetylase (MiDAC) histone deacetylase complex that regulates gene expression through histone acetylation. The MiDAC complex, composed of MIDEAS, HDAC1/2, and DNTTIP1, plays essential roles in embryonic development, mitotic chromosome alignment, and neurodevelopmental gene expression programs controlling neurite outgrowth (PMID: 32591534, PMID: 32297854).

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Case Reports and Patient Numbers:**
One clinical study identified 2 unrelated patients with de novo heterozygous missense variants in MIDEAS (PMID: 41290615). This represents a single independent case series with a total of 2 patients.

**Phenotypic Spectrum:**
The most common phenotypes observed include:
- Delayed speech development
- Joint contractures
- Dysmorphic features
- Gastrointestinal dysmotility
- Multisystem neurodevelopmental disorder

**Variant Type:**
The identified pathogenic variant is a missense mutation (c.1960A>C, p.Tyr654Ser) rather than a loss-of-function variant. Notably, this variant is located in a conserved auto-inhibitory loop covering the active site of the deacetylase enzyme and results in *increased* deacetylase activity rather than loss of function (PMID: 41290615).

**Cohort Affiliation:**
No information provided regarding enrollment in established diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL).

**Diagnostic Strength:**
The clinical evidence is supported by: (1) identification of the same de novo variant in two unrelated individuals; (2) cryoEM structural evidence showing the variant in a functionally critical auto-inhibitory domain; (3) reciprocal gene expression changes in patient fibroblasts; and (4) functional validation demonstrating elevated deacetylase activity in patient cells.

**Strength of Clinical Evidence: STRONG**

The combination of recurrent de novo variants in unrelated patients with consistent phenotypes, structural evidence, and functional validation provides compelling clinical evidence for pathogenicity, despite the small number of reported cases.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms Studied:**
Mouse models have been the primary organism studied for MIDEAS function.

**Knockout Phenotypes:**
Complete knockout of MIDEAS in mice results in embryonic lethality during late embryogenesis with severe multisystem phenotypes including heart malformation and haematopoietic failure (PMID: 32591534). The knockout exhibits a lethal Mendelian ratio, indicating that MIDEAS is essential for embryonic survival. Additionally, MIDEAS is required for proper chromosome alignment during mitosis, with a function that cannot be compensated by other HDAC complexes (PMID: 32591534).

**Consistency with Human Phenotype:**
The mouse knockout data demonstrates that MIDEAS is essential for normal development, which is consistent with the neurodevelopmental and multisystem phenotypes observed in human patients. However, the human pathogenic variant causes a gain-of-function (increased deacetylase activity) rather than loss of function, suggesting a distinct mechanism from complete knockout.

**Strength of Model Organism Evidence: STRONG**

The mouse knockout studies establish MIDEAS as essential for embryonic development and provide mechanistic insight into its role in developmental processes, though the gain-of-function nature of the human variant represents a different pathogenic mechanism than the knockout model.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Molecular Function and Pathways:**
MIDEAS functions as a core scaffolding protein in the MiDAC histone deacetylase complex. It interacts with HDAC1/2 through specific surface residues (Y48 and E63) and with DNTTIP1 to form a functional complex (PMID: 40966515, PMID: 25653165). The MiDAC complex is upregulated during mitosis and associates with cyclin A, indicating a cell cycle-dependent function (PMID: 25653165).

**Neurodevelopmental Relevance:**
MIDEAS is a key component of the MiDAC complex that regulates neurodevelopmental gene expression programs. The complex modulates histone acetylation (H4K20ac and H3K27ac) at promoters and enhancers of pro-neural genes including SLIT3 and NETRIN1, which are critical for neurite outgrowth (PMID: 32297854). Loss of MiDAC function results in neurite outgrowth defects, directly linking MIDEAS to neuronal development.

**Additional Functions:**
Beyond neurodevelopment, MIDEAS has been identified as a functional component of the MiDAC complex that recognizes the HIV-1 core promoter and controls viral transcription (PMID: 38781120), and as a differentially methylated gene in Alzheimer's disease patients, suggesting broader roles in neurological function (PMID: 40343304).

**Key Experimental Findings:**
The pathogenic p.Tyr654Ser variant is located in an auto-inhibitory loop that normally suppresses deacetylase activity. The variant disrupts this auto-inhibition, leading to increased enzymatic activity and altered histone acetylation patterns in patient fibroblasts, resulting in dysregulation of neurodevelopmental gene expression (PMID: 41290615).

**Strength of Biological Evidence: STRONG**

Multiple independent studies establish MIDEAS as a critical component of a histone deacetylase complex with specific roles in neurodevelopmental gene regulation, chromosome segregation, and viral transcription control. The structural and functional characterization of the pathogenic variant provides clear mechanistic insight into disease pathogenesis.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall Evidence Level: STRONG**

**Key Strengths:**
1. **Recurrent de novo variants in unrelated patients** with consistent clinical phenotypes provide strong genetic evidence for pathogenicity
2. **Structural and functional validation** demonstrating that the variant disrupts an auto-inhibitory domain and increases deacetylase activity
3. **Comprehensive biological characterization** of MIDEAS function in neurodevelopmental pathways, with direct relevance to observed clinical phenotypes
4. **Essential role in embryonic development** demonstrated in mouse models, establishing biological plausibility for neurodevelopmental disease

**Key Limitations:**
1. **Limited clinical data** with only 2 reported patients; additional case reports would strengthen the evidence
2. **Gain-of-function mechanism** differs from typical loss-of-function disease genes, requiring careful interpretation of functional studies
3. **Lack of cohort enrollment information** limits assessment of ascertainment bias and generalizability
4. **No large-scale population studies** to establish prevalence or identify additional variants

**Conclusion:**
MIDEAS is established as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with autosomal dominant inheritance caused by de novo heterozygous missense variants that increase MiDAC deacetylase activity. The combination of recurrent de novo variants in unrelated patients, structural evidence of functional disruption, and comprehensive biological characterization of MIDEAS in neurodevelopmental pathways provides strong evidence for pathogenicity. Future studies should focus on identifying additional patients to further delineate the clinical spectrum and explore potential therapeutic approaches targeting MiDAC complex activity.

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. A de novo missense variant in MIDEAS results in increased deacetylase activity of the MiDAC HDAC complex causing a neurodevelopmental syndrome. (PMID: 41290615)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. The MiDAC histone deacetylase complex is essential for embryonic development and has a unique multivalent structure (PMID: 32591534)

### Biological Evidence
1. The histone deacetylase complex MiDAC regulates a neurodevelopmental gene expression program to control neurite outgrowth (PMID: 32297854)
2. Mutations on the surface of HDAC1 reveal molecular determinants of specific complex assembly and their requirement for gene regulation (PMID: 40966515)
3. Structural and functional characterization of a cell cycle associated HDAC1/2 complex reveals the structural basis for complex assembly and nucleosome targeting (PMID: 25653165)
4. Mitotic deacetylase complex (MiDAC) recognizes the HIV-1 core promoter to control activated viral gene expression (PMID: 38781120)
5. DNA methylation in peripheral blood leukocytes in late onset Alzheimer's disease (PMID: 40343304)
