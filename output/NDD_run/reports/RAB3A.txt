# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: RAB3A AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

RAB3A encodes a small GTP-binding protein abundant in brain synaptic vesicles that is crucial for neurotransmitter release, synaptic plasticity, and regulated exocytosis. The protein functions as a molecular switch controlling the trafficking and release of synaptic vesicles and dense-core vesicles containing neuromodulators and neuropeptides.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent studies:** 3 case reports/case series

**Total patients:** Approximately 20 patients across studies (18 from PMID: 40166812; 2 from PMID: 41492970; additional cases from PMID: 37553802 involving mouse ortholog)

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Cerebellar ataxia (progressive, adult-onset in most cases)
- Cerebellar atrophy with vermis accentuation
- Cerebellar cortical degeneration
- Motor learning deficits and developmental coordination disorder-like features
- Tremors and mild gait spasticity
- Neurodevelopmental disorder features

**Predominant variant type:** Missense variants in conserved functional domains, particularly the recurrent p.Arg83Trp (c.247C>T) variant identified in 9 families. Loss-of-function variants also reported.

**Cohort source:** Patients identified through the Genomics England (GEL) 100,000 Genomes Project and computational genetic association; de novo occurrences documented.

**Inheritance pattern:** Autosomal dominant with heterozygous variants

**Mechanistic basis:** The p.Arg83Trp and p.Tyr91Cys variants lie in the conserved switch II region critical for effector protein interaction. These variants reduce GAP-dependent GTP hydrolysis and fail to bind key effector proteins RIM1 and Rabphilin-3A, resulting in diffuse cytoplasmic mislocalization rather than proper vesicle localization. Functional studies demonstrated failure to rescue synaptic phenotypes in Drosophila Rab3 loss-of-function models (PMID: 40166812, PMID: 41492970).

**Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) identification of the same recurrent variant (p.Arg83Trp) in multiple independent families; (2) de novo occurrence supporting pathogenicity; (3) detailed functional characterization demonstrating partial loss-of-function mechanism; (4) consistent phenotype across patients; and (5) identification through large-scale genomic projects with computational validation.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (primary model)

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**
- Full RAB3A knockout mice are viable with Mendelian inheritance ratios, indicating the gene is not essential for embryonic development (PMID: 16734774, PMID: 33982832)
- Neurological/behavioral phenotypes: deficit in cued fear conditioning, reduced anxiety-like behavior, altered stress response (PMID: 16734774)
- Synaptic dysfunction: reduced GTP and GDP binding capacity, reduced protein levels of Rab3A and rabphilin3A, subtle gene expression changes in cortex and hippocampus (PMID: 16734774)
- Vesicle trafficking defects: inactivation of all four RAB3 genes nearly ablated dense-core vesicle exocytosis; re-expression of RAB3A restored this deficit, demonstrating essential role in neuropeptide and neurotrophin release (PMID: 31679900)
- Podocyte cytoskeletal changes and altered structure in high glucose conditions (PMID: 34299299)

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The mouse knockout studies demonstrate that RAB3A loss impairs synaptic function and neurotransmitter/neuromodulator release, which is consistent with the cerebellar ataxia and motor learning deficits observed in human patients. However, complete knockout mice do not develop progressive cerebellar ataxia, suggesting that heterozygous missense variants may cause disease through a dominant-negative mechanism or specific loss of effector binding rather than simple haploinsufficiency.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**

While mouse knockouts demonstrate the gene's importance for synaptic function and vesicle trafficking, they do not fully recapitulate the human cerebellar ataxia phenotype. The viability of complete knockouts suggests that heterozygous missense variants in humans may act through dominant-negative effects rather than simple loss-of-function, which is not fully captured by knockout models.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

RAB3A functions as a central regulator of regulated exocytosis through multiple interconnected pathways:

1. **Synaptic vesicle trafficking and neurotransmitter release:** RAB3A localizes to synaptic vesicles and regulates their docking and fusion through interaction with effector proteins RIM1 and Rabphilin-3A (PMID: 41492970). The RAB3-RIM pathway is essential for dense-core vesicle exocytosis and neuromodulator release (PMID: 31679900).

2. **Insulin secretion and glucose homeostasis:** RAB3A forms a complex with MADD and PTPRN proteins that links to insulin-containing dense-core secretory vesicles. Gene-gene interactions among MADD, PTPRN, and RAB3A were identified in association with type 2 diabetes across eight independent multi-ethnic studies (15,502 participants; p=3.1×10^-5 to p=1.9×10^-8) (ASHG 2016 - Identification of interactions in the MADD PTPRN RAB3 complex with type 2 diabetes in multiple populations).

3. **Regulated exocytosis in secretory cells:** RAB3A activity is regulated by palmitoylation of its GAP protein (Rab3gap1), which controls the spatial segregation of RAB3A and elevation of Rab3a-GTP levels, affecting exocytosis and secretion (PMID: 37325411).

4. **Protein-protein interactions:** Alpha-synuclein interacts with GTP-bound Rab3a, inhibiting its GTP hydrolysis; phosphorylation of alpha-synuclein at Ser129 (associated with Parkinson's disease) enhances this interaction (PMID: 35809645). KIF1A motor protein transports presynaptic vesicle precursors containing RAB3A (PMID: 36284339).

**Expression pattern:** RAB3A is expressed almost exclusively in neurons and secretory cells, with particularly high abundance in brain synaptic vesicles, making it highly relevant to neurodevelopmental and neurological function.

**Key experimental findings:**
- Missense variants in the switch II region impair effector protein binding while maintaining GTP-binding affinity, resulting in cytoplasmic mislocalization (PMID: 41492970)
- Reduced GAP-dependent GTP hydrolysis for specific missense variants (PMID: 40166812)
- Failure to rescue synaptic phenotypes in Drosophila models (PMID: 40166812)
- Essential role in neuropeptide and neurotrophin release from dense-core vesicles (PMID: 31679900)

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is comprehensive, demonstrating RAB3A's critical role in synaptic vesicle trafficking, neurotransmitter/neuromodulator release, and regulated exocytosis across multiple tissues. The molecular mechanisms by which specific variants impair function are well-characterized, and the gene's expression pattern in neurons strongly supports its relevance to neurodevelopmental disorders.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths of the evidence:**

1. **Recurrent pathogenic variant:** The p.Arg83Trp variant identified in 9 independent families provides strong evidence for pathogenicity through independent ascertainment.

2. **Mechanistic clarity:** Detailed functional studies demonstrate that pathogenic variants impair GAP-dependent GTP hydrolysis and effector protein binding, resulting in cytoplasmic mislocalization and failure to rescue synaptic function in model organisms.

3. **Consistent phenotype:** All reported patients present with cerebellar ataxia and motor dysfunction, with consistent neuroimaging findings of cerebellar atrophy.

4. **Large-scale genomic validation:** Identification through the 100,000 Genomes Project with computational genetic association provides population-level support.

5. **Biological plausibility:** RAB3A's well-established role in synaptic vesicle trafficking and neurotransmitter release directly supports its involvement in cerebellar function and motor coordination.

**Key gaps or limitations:**

1. **Limited patient numbers:** Only ~20 patients have been formally reported across studies, which is relatively small for establishing a new disease gene.

2. **Incomplete genotype-phenotype correlation:** The relationship between specific variants (missense vs. loss-of-function) and phenotypic severity or age of onset requires further characterization.

3. **Lack of heterozygous mouse models:** Available mouse models are complete knockouts, which do not fully recapitulate the dominant-negative mechanism suggested by human missense variants.

4. **Limited functional validation in human neurons:** Most functional studies use cell culture systems or Drosophila models; validation in human neuronal systems would strengthen the evidence.

**Conclusion:**

RAB3A is a well-supported neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous autosomal dominant pathogenic variants causing progressive cerebellar ataxia through a partial loss-of-function mechanism. The recurrent p.Arg83Trp variant identified in multiple families, combined with detailed mechanistic characterization and identification through large-scale genomic projects, provides strong evidence for pathogenicity. Future studies should focus on expanding the patient cohort, characterizing additional variants, and developing heterozygous mouse models to better understand the dominant-negative effects of missense variants.
