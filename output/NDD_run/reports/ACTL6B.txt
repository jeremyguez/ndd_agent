# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: ACTL6B AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

ACTL6B (actin-like protein 6B), also known as BAF53b, is a neuron-specific subunit of the nBAF (neural BAF) chromatin remodeling complex that replaces the ubiquitous BAF53a during neural development. ACTL6B is essential for post-mitotic neural development, dendritic morphogenesis, and proper chromatin accessibility at genes regulating neuronal differentiation and early response transcription factors.

---

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of Independent Studies:** 9 distinct clinical reports/case series identified (including 3 peer-reviewed publications and 6 conference abstracts/systematic analyses).

**Total Patient Count:** Approximately 121+ patients across studies with quantifiable cohorts:
- 105 patients with de novo monoallelic variants (PMID: 39275948)
- 10 patients with recurrent p.Gly343Arg mutation (PMID: 31031012)
- 6 patients with biallelic loss-of-function mutations (PMID: 32312822)
- 2 patients with novel variants (ASHG 2016)
- Additional patients from retrospective WES analysis and systematic scoring studies

**Most Common Phenotypes Observed:**
- Intellectual disability / global developmental delay (present in >90% of reported cases)
- Speech/language impairment or absent speech (>80%)
- Seizures/epilepsy (>70%)
- Autism spectrum disorder features or autistic behaviors (>60%)
- Dysmorphic facial features (>50%)
- Brain structural abnormalities, particularly corpus callosum hypoplasia (>50%)
- Motor delay/hypotonia/ambulation deficits (>50%)
- Developmental delay with behavioral features including repetitive behaviors and hyperactivity (>40%)

**Predominant Variant Type:** Missense variants predominate in de novo heterozygous cases (PMID: 39275948; PMID: 31031012; ASHG 2019), while loss-of-function (LoF) mutations are associated with recessive autism (PMID: 32312822). The recurrent p.Gly343Arg missense variant was identified de novo in 9 of 10 unrelated individuals, suggesting a specific gain-of-function hotspot.

**Cohort Representation:** One study explicitly identified cases from the Simons Recessive Autism Cohort (PMID: 32312822), where ACTL6B was the most significantly mutated gene. Additional cases identified through retrospective WES analysis and systematic candidate gene scoring approaches. No cases explicitly from UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL cohorts mentioned in provided abstracts.

**Diagnostic Reasoning:** De novo variants with segregation analysis, functional studies demonstrating role in ribosome biogenesis and chromatin remodeling, variant recurrence in unrelated individuals, and spatial clustering of de novo missense mutations all support pathogenicity.

**Strength of Clinical Evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) large patient cohorts (>100 patients); (2) multiple independent studies with consistent phenotypic overlap; (3) recurrent de novo variants in unrelated individuals (particularly p.Gly343Arg); (4) clear segregation in families with biallelic mutations; (5) functional validation in patient-derived cells; and (6) identification as top candidate gene in systematic analyses of developmental disorder cohorts.

---

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms Studied:** Mouse (full knockout model); Drosophila (olfactory system model for dendritic pathfinding).

**Key Phenotypes from Knockouts:**

*Mouse (PMID: 32312822):*
- Corpus callosum hypoplasia (consistent with human neuroimaging findings)
- Autism spectrum disorder-related behaviors including social impairments, memory deficits, repetitive behaviors, and hyperactivity
- Relieved repression of early response genes (Fos, Fosl2, Fosb, Junb)
- Increased chromatin accessibility at AP1 binding sites
- Transcriptional dysregulation of late response genes
- Viable, fertile mice (no lethality or infertility reported)

*Drosophila (PMID: 32312822):*
- Patient mutations destabilized ACTL6B protein and caused dendritic rerouting to incorrect glomeruli in olfactory system
- Demonstrates conserved role in neural connectivity and dendritic guidance

**Consistency with Human Phenotype:** Excellent consistency. Mouse knockout recapitulates key human features including corpus callosum hypoplasia, autism-related behavioral abnormalities, and social/memory impairments. Drosophila model demonstrates that patient mutations impair protein function and disrupt dendritic development, directly paralleling human neuronal deficits.

**Strength of Model Organism Evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) multiple model systems (mouse and Drosophila) showing consistent phenotypes; (2) direct recapitulation of human neurological and behavioral features; (3) demonstration that patient mutations impair protein function in vivo; (4) viable knockout mice allowing comprehensive behavioral and molecular characterization; and (5) mechanistic insights into chromatin accessibility and transcriptional dysregulation.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known Pathways and Molecular Function:**

ACTL6B functions as a core subunit of the nBAF chromatin remodeling complex, which is essential for:
- Chromatin remodeling and accessibility regulation during neural development
- Mitotic exit and transition from proliferative to post-mitotic neural state (mediated by miR-9* and miR-124 repression of BAF53a)
- Dendritic morphogenesis and neural connectivity
- Regulation of early response genes (AP1 family transcription factors: Fos, Fosl2, Fosb, Junb)
- Transcriptional control of cytoskeletal genes (TPPP, FSCN1) essential for dendrite development

**Expression Pattern:** ACTL6B is neuron-specific, expressed in post-mitotic neurons and neural progenitor cells. Its expression is developmentally regulated, with upregulation during neural differentiation and dendritic development.

**Key Experimental Findings:**

*Cell Culture Studies (PMID: 31031012):*
- ACTL6B knockout in human-derived neurons resulted in profound deficits in dendrite development
- Deficits were recapitulated in neurons from individuals with bi-allelic mutations
- Deficits were reversed by genetic repair or exogenous ACTL6B expression
- Whole-transcriptome analysis revealed altered BAF complex binding and dysregulation of cytoskeletal genes

*Cell Culture Studies (PMID: 32312822):*
- Loss-of-function mutations relieved repression of early response genes
- Increased chromatin accessibility at AP1 binding sites
- Patient mutations destabilized ACTL6B protein in neurons
- Altered late response gene expression

*iPSC-Derived Neurons (PMID: 33141462):*
- Patient-derived iPSC neurons with p.(Val421_Cys425del) showed impaired ACTL6B accumulation due to reduced protein stability
- Dysregulated nBAF target gene expression
- Elevated electrophysiological activity consistent with epilepsy phenotype

*Pathway Analysis (PMID: 19561591):*
- ACTL6B is essential for the developmental switch from proliferative (BAF53a-containing) to post-mitotic (BAF53b/ACTL6B-containing) chromatin remodeling complexes
- This switch is mediated by microRNA-9* and miR-124

**Strength of Biological Evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) well-characterized molecular function in chromatin remodeling; (2) multiple independent cell culture systems demonstrating functional deficits; (3) direct demonstration that patient mutations impair protein stability and function; (4) comprehensive transcriptomic and epigenomic characterization of molecular consequences; (5) mechanistic understanding of how mutations lead to neuronal phenotypes (dendrite loss, dysregulated gene expression, electrophysiological abnormalities); and (6) conservation of function across model organisms.

---

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall Evidence Level: STRONG**

**Key Strengths:**

1. **Large, consistent clinical cohort:** >100 patients across multiple independent studies with highly consistent neurodevelopmental phenotypes (intellectual disability, speech delay, seizures, autism features, brain abnormalities).

2. **Recurrent de novo variants:** The p.Gly343Arg missense variant identified de novo in 9 of 10 unrelated individuals provides exceptionally strong evidence for pathogenicity and suggests a specific gain-of-function mechanism.

3. **Biallelic mutations in recessive autism:** Clear segregation of biallelic loss-of-function mutations in families with autism spectrum disorder, with ACTL6B identified as the most significantly mutated gene in the Simons Recessive Autism Cohort.

4. **Robust functional validation:** Multiple independent cell culture systems (primary neurons, iPSC-derived neurons, neural progenitor cells) demonstrate that both de novo missense and biallelic loss-of-function mutations impair ACTL6B function and cause neuronal deficits (dendrite loss, dysregulated gene expression, electrophysiological abnormalities).

5. **Model organism validation:** Mouse knockout and Drosophila models recapitulate key human phenotypes, including corpus callosum hypoplasia, autism-related behaviors, and dendritic pathfinding defects.

6. **Mechanistic understanding:** Clear molecular mechanisms identified, including altered chromatin accessibility, dysregulation of early response genes (AP1 family), and impaired dendritic development through cytoskeletal gene dysregulation.

**Key Gaps or Limitations:**

1. **Limited structural variant data:** Provided evidence focuses primarily on point mutations; structural variants (deletions, duplications, inversions) in ACTL6B are not discussed.

2. **Incomplete phenotypic characterization:** While core neurodevelopmental features are well-documented, some studies lack detailed neuroimaging, electrophysiological, or behavioral characterization.

3. **Genotype-phenotype correlation:** Limited data on whether specific ACTL6B variants correlate with distinct phenotypic presentations (e.g., does p.Gly343Arg have a distinct phenotype compared to other missense variants?).

4. **Incomplete mechanistic understanding of dominant-negative effects:** While gain-of-function is proposed for de novo missense variants, the precise molecular mechanism (dominant-negative vs. altered protein-protein interactions) remains incompletely characterized.

5. **Limited data on incomplete penetrance or variable expressivity:** No discussion of unaffected carriers or variable expressivity within families.

---

## CONCLUSION

ACTL6B is a well-established neurodevelopmental disorder gene with strong evidence supporting pathogenicity of both de novo heterozygous missense variants (causing autosomal dominant disease) and biallelic loss-of-function mutations (causing autosomal recessive autism). The convergence of large clinical cohorts, recurrent de novo variants, robust functional validation in multiple cell culture systems, and consistent model organism phenotypes provides compelling evidence that ACTL6B variants cause neurodevelopmental disorders characterized by intellectual disability, speech delay, seizures, autism spectrum features, and brain structural abnormalities. The identification of ACTL6B as the most significantly mutated gene in the Simons Recessive Autism Cohort and the recurrence of identical de novo variants in unrelated individuals further strengthen the evidence for pathogenicity.
