# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: KLF7 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

KLF7 (Krüppel-like factor 7) is a zinc finger transcription factor that functions as a master regulator of neuronal development, differentiation, and migration. It plays critical roles in hippocampal development, neurogenesis, axonal growth, and circadian rhythm regulation, with emerging evidence for broader roles in metabolic and immune homeostasis.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports:** Two independent case series plus one contiguous gene deletion report.

**Total patients:** Approximately 8 patients with de novo KLF7 variants (4 patients from PMID: 29251763; 3 patients from ASHG 2016 presentation by Shinde et al.; 1 patient from PMID: 39578275 with 2q33 deletion encompassing KLF7).

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Developmental delay and intellectual disability (present in all reported cases)
- Hypotonia and abnormal muscle tone (present in 6/7 de novo variant cases)
- Feeding and swallowing difficulties (present in 5/7 de novo variant cases)
- Psychiatric features including anxiety, autism spectrum disorder, and behavioral problems (present in 5/7 de novo variant cases)
- Motor delay and coordination issues (present in 4/7 de novo variant cases)
- Elevated creatine kinase suggesting neuromuscular involvement (1 case)

**Predominant variant type:** Missense variants. All reported de novo variants are missense mutations, including c.410C>T (p.T137M), c.415C>T (p.P139S), and c.790G>A (p.D264N) (ASHG 2016). These variants cluster in functionally important domains: the F-box motif (p.T137M, p.P139S) and the zinc finger DNA-binding domain (p.D264N).

**Cohort information:** No patients identified from known diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL). Cases appear to be identified through clinical exome sequencing at individual diagnostic centers.

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) multiple independent case series with consistent phenotypes; (2) de novo inheritance pattern in all cases, supporting pathogenicity; (3) in silico prediction of damaging effects; (4) clustering of variants in functionally critical domains. However, the evidence is limited by: (1) small total sample size (8 patients); (2) lack of functional validation of pathogenicity; (3) absence of segregation data in larger families; (4) limited detailed phenotypic characterization in some cases.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (Mus musculus) exclusively.

**Key neurological phenotypes from knockouts:**

The most relevant findings for neurodevelopmental disease come from conditional and full knockout studies:

- **Hippocampal development and cognition:** Conditional KLF7 knockout in mice causes significant hippocampal shrinkage, disrupted neurogenesis, impaired neuronal differentiation and migration, abnormal neuronal projections, and memory impairments (PMID: 40762575). These mice also exhibit anxiety-like and depression-like behaviors, directly paralleling psychiatric features in affected patients.

- **Autism-like behavior:** Full KLF7 knockout mice display autism-like behavior associated with disrupted circadian rhythm and downregulated Clock gene expression in the brain (PMID: 36207723), consistent with psychiatric manifestations in human cases.

- **Sensory neuron development:** KLF7 cooperates with Brn3a to control TrkA expression in sensory neurons; Klf7-/-;Brn3a-/- double mutants show loss of nociceptive sensory neurons (PMID: 17011544). Single Klf7-/- mutants show only mild TrkA reduction, suggesting haploinsufficiency may not fully recapitulate the phenotype.

- **Olfactory sensory neuron differentiation:** Full KLF7 knockout affects genes involved in axonal growth, cytoskeletal dynamics, cell adhesion, and synaptogenesis, with reduced OMP and L1 adhesion molecule expression (PMID: 17123745).

**Lethality and Mendelian ratios:** All reported knockouts are viable with no reported lethality or deviation from expected Mendelian ratios, suggesting KLF7 is not essential for embryonic development.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The mouse data strongly support the neurodevelopmental phenotype observed in human patients. Hippocampal dysfunction, impaired neurogenesis, and disrupted neuronal migration directly explain developmental delay and intellectual disability. Anxiety-like and depression-like behaviors in mice parallel psychiatric features in patients. The involvement of sensory neuron development and axonal growth pathways is consistent with the neuromuscular symptoms (hypotonia, motor delay) observed in affected individuals.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**

Multiple independent mouse studies demonstrate consistent neurological phenotypes relevant to human disease. The conditional knockout approach allows tissue-specific analysis of KLF7 function in the nervous system. The phenotypes directly parallel clinical manifestations in affected patients, providing strong mechanistic support for KLF7's role in neurodevelopment.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

KLF7 functions as a transcriptional activator that directly binds to promoter and enhancer regions of target genes. Key pathways implicated in neurodevelopment include:

- **Neurogenesis and neuronal migration:** KLF7 orchestrates hippocampal development through regulation of genes controlling neurogenesis and Draxin-mediated neuronal migration (PMID: 40762575).

- **Circadian rhythm regulation:** KLF7 regulates Clock gene expression, which is critical for circadian rhythm maintenance and has implications for psychiatric symptoms (PMID: 36207723).

- **Axonal growth and neuronal differentiation:** KLF7 regulates genes involved in axonal growth, cytoskeletal dynamics, cell adhesion, and synaptogenesis in olfactory sensory neurons (PMID: 17123745).

- **Inflammatory and metabolic pathways:** KLF7 activates the PKCζ/NF-κB pathway in adipocytes to promote IL-6 expression and inflammation (PMID: 37342904), which may have broader implications for systemic effects in neurodevelopmental disease.

**Expression pattern:** While the provided evidence does not explicitly detail KLF7 expression in developing brain, the functional studies demonstrate robust expression and activity in hippocampus, cortex, and sensory ganglia. The clinical phenotype of intellectual disability and developmental delay, combined with mouse knockout studies showing hippocampal and neurogenic defects, indicates KLF7 is expressed and functionally important in developing neural tissue.

**Key experimental findings:**

- KLF7 directly binds DNA at specific motifs (TGGGTGGAG and related sequences) in target gene promoters, as demonstrated by chromatin immunoprecipitation and luciferase reporter assays across multiple studies.

- KLF7 acts as a master regulator of neuroblastoma differentiation through super-enhancer-associated mechanisms, upregulating genes like AHNAK and GDPD5 that regulate GTPase signaling (PMID: 38924469). This suggests KLF7 may similarly regulate neuronal differentiation in normal development.

- The missense variants identified in patients (p.T137M, p.P139S in F-box motif; p.D264N in zinc finger domain) are predicted to disrupt critical functional domains, likely impairing DNA binding or protein-protein interactions.

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

KLF7 is well-characterized as a transcription factor with direct molecular targets identified through multiple complementary approaches (ChIP, luciferase assays, RNA-seq). The pathways identified are directly relevant to neurodevelopment. However, the evidence is primarily from cancer biology and metabolic studies; direct functional studies of KLF7 in normal neurodevelopment are limited.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE-TO-STRONG**

**Key strengths:**

1. **Consistent clinical phenotype:** Multiple independent case series report a recognizable syndrome of developmental delay, intellectual disability, hypotonia, feeding difficulties, and psychiatric features associated with de novo KLF7 missense variants.

2. **Strong mechanistic support from model organisms:** Mouse knockout studies demonstrate that KLF7 loss causes hippocampal dysfunction, impaired neurogenesis, disrupted neuronal migration, anxiety-like behavior, and autism-like behavior—phenotypes that directly explain the human clinical presentation.

3. **Variant clustering in critical domains:** All reported variants cluster in functionally important regions (F-box motif and zinc finger DNA-binding domain), suggesting loss or impairment of function rather than random mutations.

4. **De novo inheritance pattern:** All reported cases show de novo variants, supporting pathogenicity and autosomal dominant inheritance.

5. **Well-characterized transcription factor:** KLF7 is a known transcriptional regulator with identified molecular targets and pathways relevant to neurodevelopment.

**Key gaps and limitations:**

1. **Small sample size:** Only 8 patients with de novo KLF7 variants have been reported, limiting statistical power and generalizability.

2. **Lack of functional validation:** No functional studies have directly tested whether the reported missense variants impair KLF7 transcriptional activity or protein function.

3. **Limited genotype-phenotype correlation:** With only 3 distinct variants reported, it is unclear whether specific variants correlate with particular phenotypic features or severity.

4. **Incomplete phenotypic characterization:** Some case reports lack detailed descriptions of all clinical features, making it difficult to define the full phenotypic spectrum.

5. **No large-scale cohort validation:** The gene has not been systematically screened in large neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts (DDD, GREGoR, etc.) to establish prevalence or confirm the association.

6. **Haploinsufficiency vs. dominant-negative mechanism:** Mouse studies primarily use full or conditional knockouts; it remains unclear whether human missense variants cause haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative effects.

**Conclusion:**

KLF7 is a credible candidate gene for autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by developmental delay, intellectual disability, hypotonia, feeding difficulties, and psychiatric features. The clinical evidence is supported by strong mechanistic data from mouse models demonstrating that KLF7 is essential for hippocampal development, neurogenesis, and neuronal migration. However, the evidence would be substantially strengthened by: (1) identification of additional patients through systematic screening of neurodevelopmental cohorts; (2) functional studies demonstrating that reported variants impair KLF7 activity; and (3) detailed genotype-phenotype correlation studies. Current evidence supports KLF7 as a MODERATE-TO-STRONG candidate for inclusion in neurodevelopmental disorder gene panels, pending validation in larger patient cohorts.
