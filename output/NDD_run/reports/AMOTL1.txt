# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AMOTL1 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

AMOTL1 (Angiomotin-Like Protein 1) is a scaffolding protein that functions as a key regulator of the Hippo signaling pathway, controlling YAP/TAZ activity and modulating cell proliferation, migration, and junction stability. AMOTL1 plays critical roles in vascular development, endothelial cell polarity, and embryonic morphogenesis through interactions with actin cytoskeleton and cell adhesion complexes.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports:** 3 case reports/case series directly implicating AMOTL1 in congenital anomalies; 1 additional case report involving a different gene (TRAPPC6A) was included in the dataset but is not relevant to AMOTL1.

**Total patients:** Approximately 3 patients with confirmed AMOTL1 variants across the relevant studies.

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Craniofacial abnormalities (cleft lip/palate, ear malformations) — reported in 2/3 cases
- Cardiac abnormalities (congenital cardiac disease) — reported in 2/3 cases
- Hepatic abnormalities (chronic liver dysfunction, elevated liver enzymes, hepatic histological changes) — reported in 1/3 cases
- The constellation of craniofacial, cardiac, and hepatic involvement has been termed Craniofaciocardiohepatic Syndrome (CFCHS) (PMID: 39538375)

**Predominant variant type:** Variants are described as "not specified" in most reports, with one noted as "novel variant." No clear distinction between loss-of-function and missense variants is provided in the clinical data.

**Cohort affiliation:** No patients are reported to be from established diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL).

**Inheritance pattern:** One study explicitly documents dominant inheritance pattern with a novel causal variant identified by genomic analysis in a family with craniofacial syndrome; mouse modeling of this variant showed lethality before weaning (PMID: 30375152).

**Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED**

The clinical evidence is limited by: (1) very small number of reported cases (n=3); (2) incomplete variant characterization; (3) lack of systematic phenotyping across cases; (4) absence of segregation data in most reports; (5) no recruitment from established diagnostic networks; and (6) limited functional validation of pathogenicity. However, the consistent pattern of craniofacial and cardiac involvement across independent cases, combined with the documented dominant inheritance pattern, provides some support for AMOTL1 as a disease gene.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse and zebrafish

**Key phenotypes from knockouts/knockdowns:**

*Vascular and developmental phenotypes:*
- Reduced endothelial cell migration and increased paracellular permeability in mouse aortic and pancreatic islet endothelial cells (PMID: 19590046)
- Vascular migratory defects of intersegmental vessels in zebrafish embryos with decreased junction stability (PMID: 19590046)
- Impaired vascular network establishment in post-natal mouse retina with conditional knockout (PMID: 27464479)
- Inhibition of intersegmental artery migration in zebrafish (PMID: 18824598)

*Embryonic morphogenesis phenotypes:*
- Delayed epiboly and impaired convergence/extension movements in zebrafish amotl2 knockdown (PMID: 17293535)
- Embryonic dorsalization with amotl2 knockdown; smaller eyes with amotl1/amotl2 co-knockdown (PMID: 22362771)

*Cardiac phenotypes:*
- Silencing of Amotl1 attenuated cardiac fibroblast proliferation and myofibroblast differentiation in mouse models (PMID: 38839936)

**Lethality:** One clinical study reported that a novel AMOTL1 variant modeled in mice showed lethality before weaning (PMID: 30375152), suggesting that complete loss of function may be embryonic lethal or perinatally lethal.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The model organism data demonstrate critical roles for AMOTL1 family members in vascular development, endothelial junction stability, and embryonic morphogenesis. These findings are consistent with the observed human phenotypes of craniofacial and cardiac abnormalities, which depend on proper vascular development and cell migration during embryogenesis. However, the model organism studies primarily focus on vascular and general developmental phenotypes rather than specific neurodevelopmental outcomes.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**

The evidence is moderate because: (1) multiple independent studies in two model organisms demonstrate consistent roles in vascular development and embryonic morphogenesis; (2) phenotypes align with human congenital anomalies; (3) one clinical study provides direct evidence of lethality in a mouse model of a human AMOTL1 variant. However, the evidence is limited by: (1) most studies involve knockdown rather than complete knockout; (2) lack of specific neurological or neurodevelopmental phenotyping; (3) no Mendelian ratio data provided; (4) limited characterization of heterozygous versus homozygous effects.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

AMOTL1 functions as a key component of the Hippo signaling pathway (PMID: 38760875). The primary mechanism involves:
- Direct interaction with YAP1 in the cytoplasm, protecting both proteins from ubiquitin-mediated degradation (PMID: 32313226)
- Promotion of YAP1 nuclear translocation to activate oncogenic transcription (PMID: 32313226)
- Regulation by Merlin (NF2), which triggers AMOTL1 proteasomal degradation via NEDD ubiquitin ligases (PMID: 26806348)
- Modulation of RhoA GTPase activity in migrating endothelial cells through the Amot/Patj/Syx signaling complex (PMID: 18824598)
- Interaction with N-cadherin-mediated endothelial/pericyte junctional complexes (PMID: 27464479)

**Post-translational modifications:**
- Nat10-mediated ac4C modification of AMOTL1 increases mRNA stability and translation, enhancing YAP1 interaction (PMID: 38839936)

**Expression and regulation:**
- AMOTL1 is regulated by miR-124 in cervical cancer cells (PMID: 25218344)
- Circular AMOTL1 (circ-AMOTL1) acts as a competing endogenous RNA to sponge miR-485-5p, promoting AMOTL1 expression (PMID: 31812104)
- SRSF3-mediated alternative splicing generates long (AMOTL1-L) and short (AMOTL1-S) isoforms with differential subcellular localization and YAP1 interaction capacity (PMID: 37558679)

**Neurodevelopmental relevance:**
- A low-frequency missense variant in AMOTL1 was associated with adult height at genome-wide significance (p<5×10^-8) in >230,000 individuals (ASHG 2014), implicating AMOTL1 in human growth regulation
- No direct evidence of AMOTL1 expression in brain tissue or specific neuronal functions is provided in the available literature

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is strong because: (1) AMOTL1's role in the Hippo pathway is well-established through multiple independent studies; (2) molecular mechanisms of action are characterized in detail; (3) protein-protein interactions are documented; (4) post-translational modifications affecting function are identified; (5) genome-wide association data support a role in human growth regulation. However, the evidence has limitations: (1) most studies focus on cancer biology and vascular development rather than neurodevelopment; (2) direct evidence of AMOTL1 function in neural tissue is absent; (3) no studies specifically examine how AMOTL1 variants affect neuronal development or function.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths:**
1. Consistent clinical phenotype across independent cases (craniofacial and cardiac abnormalities) with documented dominant inheritance pattern
2. Strong biological evidence for AMOTL1's role in the Hippo pathway and cell migration/proliferation
3. Model organism evidence demonstrating critical roles in vascular development and embryonic morphogenesis, with documented lethality in a mouse model of a human variant
4. Genome-wide association evidence supporting AMOTL1's role in human growth regulation
5. Multiple independent lines of evidence (clinical, model organism, biological) converge on developmental pathways

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. Very limited clinical data (only 3 patients with AMOTL1 variants)
2. Incomplete variant characterization and lack of functional validation in most clinical reports
3. No recruitment from established diagnostic networks or large cohorts
4. Absence of specific neurological or neurodevelopmental phenotyping in clinical cases
5. Model organism studies focus on vascular and general developmental phenotypes rather than neurodevelopmental outcomes
6. No direct evidence of AMOTL1 expression or function in brain tissue
7. Lack of mechanistic studies linking AMOTL1 variants to the observed human phenotypes

**Conclusion:**

AMOTL1 shows MODERATE evidence as a gene associated with autosomal dominant congenital anomalies, particularly affecting craniofacial and cardiac development. The clinical evidence is limited by small patient numbers and incomplete characterization, but is supported by strong biological evidence for AMOTL1's role in the Hippo pathway and developmental cell migration, combined with model organism evidence of critical developmental functions. However, the current evidence does not strongly support AMOTL1 as a primary neurodevelopmental disorder gene; rather, it appears to be a gene associated with multiple congenital anomalies with secondary neurodevelopmental consequences. Expansion of clinical case ascertainment, systematic phenotyping, and functional studies of identified variants would substantially strengthen the evidence base.
