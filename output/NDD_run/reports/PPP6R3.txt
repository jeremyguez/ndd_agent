# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: PPP6R3 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

PPP6R3 is a regulatory subunit of protein phosphatase 6 (PP6), a serine/threonine phosphatase that regulates multiple cellular processes including mRNA translation, cell death pathways, and skeletal development. The gene is located on chromosome 11q13 and functions as part of the PP6 holoenzyme complex with redundant roles alongside PPP6R1 and PPP6R2 in controlling phosphorylation-dependent signaling.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of case reports/series:** 0

**Total patients:** 0

**Most common phenotypes:** Not applicable

**Predominant variant type:** Not applicable

**Known cohort sources:** Not applicable

**Strength of clinical evidence: NONE**

No clinical case reports, case series, or patient cohorts with PPP6R3 variants associated with neurodevelopmental or other human disease phenotypes were identified in the literature. The absence of clinical evidence represents a significant gap in establishing PPP6R3 as a disease gene in humans.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse and zebrafish

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

Mouse studies (conditional knockout) demonstrated male infertility with abnormal spermatogonial differentiation, translation inhibition of differentiation-associated mRNAs, and increased phosphorylation of translation initiation factors EIF3C and EIF4G1 (PMID: 40721635). The mice were viable, indicating PPP6R3 is not essential for embryonic development.

Zebrafish full knockout studies revealed male-only development with blocked transition of spermatocytes to mature sperm and inability to fertilize wild-type eggs, indicating disrupted gonadal differentiation and gametogenesis (PMID: 40962474). A separate zebrafish study examining microexon removal in ppp6r3 showed only mild neural phenotypes with reduced activity in the telencephalon compared to other microexons studied (PMID: 41252186).

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The model organism evidence does not demonstrate neurodevelopmental phenotypes. The primary findings relate to spermatogenesis and gonadal differentiation, with only minimal effects on brain morphology and function. No seizures, intellectual disability, developmental delay, or other neurodevelopmental features were reported in any knockout model.

**Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED**

While the model organism studies are well-designed and reproducible across species, they do not support a neurodevelopmental disorder phenotype. The evidence is strongest for reproductive/gametogenic functions rather than neurological roles.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

PPP6R3 functions as a regulatory subunit of the PP6 phosphatase complex with multiple documented roles:

- **Translation regulation:** PPP6R3-mediated dephosphorylation of EIF3C and EIF4G1 regulates mRNA translation during spermatogonial differentiation (PMID: 40721635)

- **Cell death regulation:** PPP6R3 has redundant roles with PPP6R1 and PPP6R2 in regulating RIPK1-dependent PANoptosis, promoting pro-death S166 auto-phosphorylation while reducing pro-survival S321 phosphorylation (PMID: 38807188)

- **Skeletal development:** PPP6R3 is a putatively causal gene for bone mineral density based on TWAS and eQTL colocalization analysis in the largest BMD GWAS to date (PMID: 36416764), with SNP rs7102273 near PPP6R3 associated with hip geometry measures at genome-wide significance (p≤2.6×10⁻⁸) and any-type fracture (p=7.5×10⁻⁵) (PMID: 30888730)

- **Muscle-bone interactions:** PPP6R3/LRP5 locus variants show pleiotropic effects on both lean mass and bone mineral density in children with 43% shared genetic component (PMID: 28743860), and genome-wide significant association with lean mass in children (p=2.9×10⁻⁸) (ASHG 2012)

- **Skeletal aging:** PPP6R3 was highlighted as a key gene in transcriptome-wide analyses of skeletal aging with associations across integrated GWAS data from osteoporosis, osteoarthritis, lumbar spinal stenosis, telomere length, and low back pain (p<5×10⁻¹²) (PMID: 41185046)

- **Viral replication:** PPP6R3 copurifies with influenza A virus RNA-dependent RNA polymerase, suggesting PP6 positively regulates viral replication (PMID: 25187537)

**Expression pattern:** No brain-specific or neurodevelopmental expression data were identified. The evidence suggests broader tissue expression with particular relevance to bone, muscle, and reproductive tissues.

**Cancer-associated rearrangements:** PPP6R3 has been identified in chromosomal rearrangements producing oncogenic fusion transcripts, including a PPP6R3-TENM4-NRG1 triple fusion in breast cancer (PMID: 33413557) and a DPP9-PPP6R3 fusion in high-grade serous ovarian carcinoma (PMID: 28893231), suggesting potential involvement in cancer biology.

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is robust, with multiple independent lines demonstrating PPP6R3's role in phosphatase-mediated signaling, skeletal development, and cellular processes. However, the evidence is predominantly focused on skeletal, reproductive, and systemic functions rather than neurodevelopmental pathways.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
- Strong biological evidence for PPP6R3 function in phosphatase signaling and skeletal development
- Consistent model organism evidence across mouse and zebrafish demonstrating reproductive phenotypes
- Robust GWAS evidence for bone mineral density and skeletal aging associations
- Clear molecular mechanism for PP6-mediated dephosphorylation of translation factors

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- Complete absence of clinical evidence: no human case reports, case series, or patient cohorts with PPP6R3 variants
- Model organism knockouts do not demonstrate neurodevelopmental phenotypes; only mild neural effects observed with microexon removal
- No evidence of haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative effects from heterozygous variants
- No brain-specific expression data or neurodevelopmental pathway involvement documented
- The primary phenotypes in model organisms relate to spermatogenesis and skeletal development, not neurological function

**Conclusion:**

While PPP6R3 is a well-characterized gene with strong biological evidence for roles in phosphatase signaling and skeletal development, there is currently insufficient evidence to classify it as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene. The complete absence of clinical case reports in humans, combined with the lack of neurodevelopmental phenotypes in model organisms, indicates that PPP6R3 variants have not been established as a cause of human neurodevelopmental disease. Future studies would require identification of patients with PPP6R3 variants presenting with neurodevelopmental phenotypes and functional validation of pathogenic mechanisms in neuronal systems to support this classification.
