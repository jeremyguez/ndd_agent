# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: HLA-F AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

HLA-F is a non-classical HLA class Ib molecule expressed at the maternal-fetal interface and other immune-privileged sites. Its primary known functions include immune modulation through interactions with killer immunoglobulin-like receptors (KIRs) on natural killer cells and innate lymphoid cells, and regulation of trophoblast proliferation and invasion during pregnancy.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (Mus musculus)

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

Two independent mouse knockout studies were identified, neither of which demonstrated neurodevelopmental phenotypes:

- A full knockout of FAT10 (which is distinct from HLA-F but was included in the evidence) showed metabolic and longevity phenotypes including increased lifespan (20%), elevated metabolic rate, and reduced adiposity (PMID: 24706839), with viable offspring and no reported neurological effects.

- A second FAT10 knockout study demonstrated that the gene is not essential for MHC-I antigen presentation, with no effects on MHC-I surface expression, epitope generation, or cytotoxic T cell responses (PMID: 40821782).

**Critical limitation:** The model organism evidence provided appears to reference FAT10 (ubiquitin-like modifier FAT10), not HLA-F itself. No mouse knockout or knockdown studies of HLA-F with neurodevelopmental readouts were identified.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** Not assessable due to absence of HLA-F-specific model organism data.

**Strength of model organism evidence: NONE**

No relevant model organism evidence for HLA-F and neurodevelopmental phenotypes was found.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

HLA-F functions as a non-classical HLA molecule with multiple immune-regulatory roles:

- **Maternal-fetal immune tolerance:** HLA-F is expressed on extravillous trophoblasts at the maternal-fetal interface and mediates immune tolerance through KIR-HLA interactions with decidual NK cells (PMID: 35610960, PMID: 16487601). HLA-F can exist in both open conformers (binding KIRs) and peptide-bound forms (binding ILT2 and ILT4 receptors) (PMID: 31558330).

- **Trophoblast proliferation and metabolism:** HLA-F regulates trophoblast proliferation through PKM2-dependent glycolysis. HLA-F binds the PKM2 promoter to enhance transcription and suppresses lactylation of PKM2 K305 to increase enzymatic activity (PMID: 40251569).

- **Innate lymphoid cell activation:** HLA-F interacts with innate lymphoid cell receptors on NK cells and ILC2 cells, contributing to immune responses in hepatocellular carcinoma (PMID: 37371767).

**Expression pattern:**

HLA-F is expressed at the maternal-fetal interface on extravillous trophoblasts (PMID: 16487601) and in immune tissues. No evidence of significant HLA-F expression in brain or neurodevelopmental tissues was identified in the provided literature.

**Key experimental findings:**

- Single-cell RNA-seq analysis of 101,250 cells revealed that HLA-F overexpression in Jar trophoblast cells promoted cell proliferation, invasion, and migration, while HLA-F overexpression in NK-92MI cells increased secretion of immunoregulatory cytokines (CSF1, CCL22) and promoted adaptive NKG2C+ NK cell transformation (PMID: 37854606).

- Genome-wide association studies identified significant associations between HLA-F SNPs (rs3129055 and rs9258122) and nasopharyngeal carcinoma risk (p = 7.36 × 10⁻¹¹ and 3.33 × 10⁻¹⁰, respectively) in a Taiwanese population (ASHG 2010 - Identification of multiple genetic variants related to nasopharyngeal carcinoma predisposition).

- A polymorphic 2.6 kbp deletion upstream of HLA-F was identified and correlated with HLA-F expression level and exon usage, demonstrating regulatory variation at the locus (ASHG 2018 - Illuminating the dark genome).

- Multi-dataset GWAS analysis identified HLA-F as a shared candidate gene in rheumatoid arthritis across multiple tissues and cell types with highly significant gene-based association (p = 1.03 × 10⁻³¹) (ASHG 2015 - Integrative analysis for identification of shared markers).

**Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE**

While HLA-F has well-characterized immune and metabolic functions with strong experimental support, the biological evidence is primarily focused on reproductive immunology, cancer immunology, and autoimmune disease. No evidence directly implicates HLA-F in neurodevelopmental pathways or brain function.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
- HLA-F has well-characterized molecular functions with strong experimental support in cell culture and GWAS studies
- The gene is involved in immune regulation and cellular proliferation, pathways potentially relevant to development
- Regulatory variants at the HLA-F locus have been identified and functionally characterized

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- **Complete absence of clinical evidence:** No case reports, case series, or patient cohorts with HLA-F variants and neurodevelopmental phenotypes have been published
- **No neurodevelopmental expression data:** HLA-F expression is documented at the maternal-fetal interface and in immune tissues, but not in brain or neurodevelopmental tissues
- **No relevant model organism data:** Available mouse studies focus on FAT10 (a different gene) or metabolic phenotypes, not neurodevelopmental outcomes
- **Disease associations are non-neurological:** Published GWAS and functional studies link HLA-F to nasopharyngeal carcinoma, rheumatoid arthritis, and pregnancy complications—not neurodevelopmental disorders

**Conclusion:**

There is currently insufficient evidence to support HLA-F as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene. While HLA-F is a functionally important immune molecule with documented roles in reproduction and cancer immunology, no clinical cases, neurodevelopmental expression data, or relevant model organism evidence support a role in neurodevelopmental pathology. Before HLA-F can be considered a candidate neurodevelopmental gene, clinical case reports with detailed neurodevelopmental phenotyping, brain expression studies, and functional validation in neurodevelopmental contexts would be required.
