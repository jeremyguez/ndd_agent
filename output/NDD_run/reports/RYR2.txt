# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: RYR2 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

RYR2 (ryanodine receptor type 2) is the cardiac sarcoplasmic reticulum calcium release channel essential for excitation-contraction coupling and intracellular calcium homeostasis. Beyond its well-established cardiac function, RYR2 is increasingly recognized as a regulator of neuronal calcium signaling and brain development, with emerging evidence linking RYR2 dysfunction to multiple neurodevelopmental phenotypes.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of Studies and Patient Count:**
Eight independent clinical studies were identified, encompassing approximately 42 patients with neurodevelopmental phenotypes associated with RYR2 variants. The largest cohort consisted of 19 pediatric patients with catecholaminergic polymorphic ventricular tachycardia (CPVT) and concurrent intellectual/neurodevelopmental delay (PMID: 41000018).

**Most Common Phenotypes Observed:**
- Intellectual and neurodevelopmental delay (reported in 4 studies; 19 patients in largest series)
- Benign epilepsy of childhood with centrotemporal spikes (BECTS) (5 patients; PMID: 33897349)
- Autism spectrum disorder (2 patients across 2 studies)
- Catecholaminergic polymorphic ventricular tachycardia with neurodevelopmental features (19 patients)
- Epilepsy (2 patients in pediatric cohorts)

**Predominant Variant Types:**
The evidence includes diverse variant types: loss-of-function variants (frameshift; PMID: 33897349), missense variants (multiple studies), and de novo mutations (PMID: 25356899). Specific variant details were often not provided in abstracts, limiting detailed classification.

**Cohort Sources:**
- International Pediatric CPVT Registry (PMID: 41000018)
- Autism Genetics Resource Exchange (AGRE) (ASHG 2011)
- Japanese pediatric DCM cohort (PMID: 39681577)
- Polish pediatric epilepsy cohort (ASHG 2022)
- Middle Eastern (Qatar) ASD cohort (PMID: 38572415)
- General ID screening cohorts (PMID: 25356899)

**Diagnostic Reasoning:**
Clinical evidence is strengthened by: (1) de novo mutations identified in disease-specific screens; (2) protein network analysis supporting involvement in neurodevelopmental pathways; (3) statistical enrichment in disease cohorts versus controls; (4) ClinGen Clinical Validity Framework support for BECTS association (PMID: 33897349); and (5) variants absent or at low frequency in gnomAD.

**Strength of Clinical Evidence: MODERATE**

The evidence demonstrates consistent association between RYR2 variants and multiple neurodevelopmental phenotypes, particularly BECTS and intellectual delay. However, the total patient numbers remain modest (42 patients across 8 studies), specific variant details are often lacking, and functional validation is limited. The strongest evidence comes from the BECTS study (PMID: 33897349) with 5 patients, detailed variant information, and statistical validation. The CPVT-neurodevelopmental phenotype (PMID: 41000018) provides the largest single cohort but conflates cardiac and neurological manifestations.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms Studied:**
Mouse models exclusively; no Drosophila, C. elegans, or zebrafish studies identified.

**Key Phenotypes from Knockouts/Knockins:**

*Cardiac-focused models:*
- RYR2 loss-of-function knock-in mice: viable, no catecholamine-provoked arrhythmias but LBLPS pacing-induced arrhythmias; responsive to antiarrhythmic drugs (PMID: 33536282)
- RYR2-I4855M+/- mice: viable, left ventricular noncompaction with hypertrabeculation, increased susceptibility to electrical stimulation-induced arrhythmias (PMID: 37325910)
- RYR2 full knockout: viable, ER stress pathway activation, impaired cardiomyocyte maturation (PMID: 35576474)

*Neurocognitive-relevant models:*
- RYR2-S2808A phosphorylation site knock-in: prevented chemotherapy-induced cognitive dysfunction, improved neurocognitive function, normalized brain glucose metabolism, reduced inflammatory signaling (TNF-α, TGF-β) (PMID: 37756377)
- Conditional RYR2 knockout in sinoatrial node: dysregulated calcium handling, increased fibrosis, decreased Ryr2 expression (PMID: 36317529)

**Mendelian Ratio and Lethality:**
All RYR2 knockout/knockin models were viable with no reported lethality or Mendelian ratio deviations, suggesting RYR2 mutations are not embryonic lethal in mice.

**Consistency with Human Phenotype:**
Model organism evidence shows strong consistency with human cardiac phenotypes (arrhythmias, structural abnormalities, calcium handling defects). The chemotherapy-induced cognitive dysfunction prevention (PMID: 37756377) provides direct evidence that RYR2 dysfunction affects brain function and that RYR2 stabilization improves neurocognitive outcomes. However, no models specifically recapitulate primary neurodevelopmental disorders (autism, intellectual disability, epilepsy) without concurrent cardiac involvement.

**Strength of Model Organism Evidence: MODERATE**

Mouse models convincingly demonstrate that RYR2 dysfunction causes calcium handling abnormalities and can impair neurocognitive function. The viability of all models supports heterozygous pathogenic variants as disease-causing. However, the lack of models specifically engineered to study neurodevelopmental phenotypes (autism, intellectual disability, BECTS) limits direct mechanistic insight into RYR2's role in primary neurodevelopmental disorders.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known Pathways and Molecular Function:**

RYR2 functions as the primary sarcoplasmic reticulum calcium release channel mediating calcium-induced calcium release (CICR) during excitation-contraction coupling. Beyond cardiac function, RYR2 regulates:

1. **Neuronal calcium signaling:** RYR2 undergoes post-translational modifications (PKA phosphorylation, oxidation, nitrosylation, calstabin2 depletion) in hippocampal neurons, with pathological calcium leak contributing to heart failure-induced cognitive dysfunction (PMID: 37429912). RYR2 stabilizer drug S107 protects against HF-induced cognitive dysfunction.

2. **Seizure pathogenesis:** SerpinA3N interacts with RYR2 and promotes RYR2 phosphorylation in astrocytes during temporal lobe epilepsy, contributing to seizure-induced neuroinflammation through NF-κB pathway activation (PMID: 37422673). This represents a novel mechanism linking RYR2 to seizure pathogenesis beyond cardiac function.

3. **Immune regulation:** Foxp3 transcription factor blocks RYR2 transcription in regulatory T cells, reducing basal calcium oscillations required for T cell suppression (PMID: 38099494). RYR2 deficiency renders CD4+ T cells immune suppressive, revealing RYR2-mediated calcium signaling as a central effector of immune regulation.

4. **Cardiomyocyte maturation:** RYR2 depletion triggers ER stress pathway activation through ATF4, impairing cardiomyocyte maturation (PMID: 35576474). RYR2 regulates ER homeostasis essential for proper development.

5. **Redox regulation:** RYR2 forms a redox-sensitive intraluminal complex with ERp44 mediated by cysteine 4806. Ero1α-mediated oxidation causes ERp44 dissociation from RYR2, increasing channel activity and spontaneous calcium release (PMID: 35086342).

**Expression Pattern:**
RYR2 is expressed in cardiac muscle (highest), skeletal muscle, and brain tissue. The biological evidence indicates RYR2 expression in hippocampal neurons, astrocytes, regulatory T cells, and sinoatrial node pacemaker cells, supporting neurodevelopmental and neurological relevance.

**Key Experimental Findings:**
- RYR2 loss-of-function mutations cause calcium release deficiency syndrome (CRDS) with LOD score 11.479 for linkage (PMID: 33536282)
- RYR2 gain-of-function mutations cause CPVT through excessive spontaneous SR calcium release; loss-of-function variants cause CRDS—distinct ryanodinopathies with different pathophysiological mechanisms (PMID: 37387319)
- RYR2 is a direct transcriptional target of Yap in sinoatrial node pacemaker cells, linking Hippo-Yap pathway to RYR2-mediated calcium regulation (PMID: 36317529)
- RYR2 stabilization via S107 Rycal drug ameliorates chemotherapy-induced cognitive dysfunction and normalizes brain glucose metabolism

**Strength of Biological Evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is comprehensive and robust, demonstrating RYR2's critical role in calcium homeostasis across multiple tissues including brain. Multiple independent mechanisms link RYR2 dysfunction to neurodevelopmental and neurological phenotypes: neuronal calcium leak, seizure-associated neuroinflammation, immune dysregulation, and ER stress-mediated developmental impairment. The evidence for RYR2 stabilization as a therapeutic approach (S107 drug) provides functional validation.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall Evidence Level: MODERATE**

**Key Strengths:**
1. **Consistent clinical phenotypes:** Multiple independent studies identify RYR2 variants in neurodevelopmental disorders (intellectual disability, autism, epilepsy, BECTS), with strongest evidence for BECTS (5 patients, statistical validation, ClinGen support; PMID: 33897349)
2. **Robust biological mechanisms:** Comprehensive evidence for RYR2's role in neuronal calcium signaling, seizure pathogenesis, and neurodevelopment through multiple independent pathways
3. **Therapeutic validation:** RYR2 stabilization (S107 drug) prevents chemotherapy-induced cognitive dysfunction in mice, providing functional proof-of-concept
4. **Diverse variant types:** Both loss-of-function and missense variants identified, with de novo mutations supporting pathogenicity
5. **Model organism viability:** RYR2 knockout/knockin mice are viable, consistent with heterozygous pathogenic variants in humans

**Key Gaps and Limitations:**
1. **Limited patient numbers:** Only 42 patients across 8 studies; largest neurodevelopmental cohort (19 patients) conflates cardiac and neurological phenotypes
2. **Incomplete variant characterization:** Many studies lack specific variant details, limiting genotype-phenotype correlation
3. **Lack of primary neurodevelopmental models:** No mouse models specifically engineered to study autism, intellectual disability, or BECTS without concurrent cardiac involvement
4. **Incomplete functional validation:** Limited functional studies demonstrating pathogenicity of specific variants identified in neurodevelopmental cohorts
5. **Phenotypic heterogeneity:** RYR2 variants associated with diverse phenotypes (BECTS, autism, intellectual disability, CPVT with neurodevelopmental features), complicating disease specificity
6. **Ascertainment bias:** Most neurodevelopmental cases identified through cardiac screening or multi-gene panels rather than primary neurodevelopmental cohorts

**Conclusion:**

RYR2 demonstrates MODERATE evidence as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous pathogenic variants, particularly for benign epilepsy of childhood with centrotemporal spikes. The convergence of clinical case reports, robust biological mechanisms linking RYR2 dysfunction to neuronal calcium signaling and seizure pathogenesis, and therapeutic validation through RYR2 stabilization supports a genuine role in neurodevelopment. However, the modest patient numbers, phenotypic heterogeneity, and lack of primary neurodevelopmental animal models prevent classification as STRONG evidence. Future studies should include larger neurodevelopmental cohorts with detailed variant characterization, functional validation of identified variants, and development of neurodevelopmental-specific RYR2 animal models.
