# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: TMEM63B AND NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDERS

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

TMEM63B is a mechanosensitive and osmosensitive ion channel that functions as a monomeric cation channel with high activation thresholds (PMID: 37543036). Beyond its canonical ion channel activity, TMEM63B also functions as a membrane structure-responsive lipid scramblase that regulates plasma membrane phospholipid asymmetry and can form functional complexes with other transporters to modulate phospholipid scrambling (PMID: 39424995, PMID: 39217145).

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of Studies and Patient Count:**
Four clinical evidence sources were identified. The primary case series (PMID: 37421948) reported 17 unrelated individuals with TMEM63B variants. Two additional studies (PMID: 40480214, PMID: 39424995) referenced the same variants in the context of functional studies without independent patient cohorts. One abstract from ASHG 2019 identified TMEM63B variants in unsolved developmental disorder cases through essentiality screens. Total approximate patient count: 17 individuals with detailed phenotypic characterization.

**Most Common Phenotypes:**
The clinical presentation is remarkably consistent across patients:
- Severe early-onset developmental and epileptic encephalopathy (DEE)
- Intellectual disability (universal)
- Severe motor impairment (universal)
- Cortical visual impairment (universal)
- Progressive neurodegenerative brain changes (universal)
- Additional features in some patients: macrocytosis and hemolysis (PMID: 37421948)

**Predominant Variant Type:**
All reported variants are missense mutations affecting conserved residues in transmembrane regions: p.Val44Met, p.Arg433His, p.Thr481Asn, p.Gly580Ser, p.Gly580Cys, p.Arg660Thr, and p.Phe697Leu. No loss-of-function variants (frameshift, nonsense, or splice site mutations) have been reported in association with this phenotype.

**Inheritance Pattern and De Novo Status:**
16 of 17 individuals (94%) carried de novo variants, strongly supporting autosomal dominant inheritance with high penetrance. This de novo enrichment is a key diagnostic feature (PMID: 37421948).

**Cohort Information:**
The primary study does not specify recruitment from named cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL), though the identification of 17 unrelated individuals suggests systematic ascertainment.

**Functional Validation:**
Cell culture studies in Neuro2a cells demonstrated that heterozygous TMEM63B variants cause inward leak cation currents in isotonic conditions and impaired response to hypo-osmotic challenge with reduced Ca2+ transients (PMID: 37421948). Ectopic expression of p.Val44Met and p.Gly580Cys in Drosophila resulted in early death, suggesting gain-of-function toxicity (PMID: 37421948). Structural and functional studies revealed that p.Val44Met is a gain-of-function mutation causing constitutive lipid scramblase activity through major rearrangements of pore-lining helices and lateral cleft formation (PMID: 40480214).

**Strength of Clinical Evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) consistent phenotypic presentation across 17 unrelated individuals; (2) high de novo variant frequency (94%); (3) multiple independent missense variants at different positions all causing the same severe phenotype; (4) functional validation demonstrating gain-of-function mechanisms; (5) variants affecting conserved residues in critical protein domains. The main limitation is the relatively small absolute number of patients, though this is offset by the consistency and severity of the phenotype.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms Studied:**
Mouse models have been extensively characterized. One study mentions Drosophila as a heterologous expression system.

**Key Phenotypes from Knockouts:**

*Full Knockout Mice:*
- Profound deficits in thirst and impaired osmolarity sensing (PMID: 40107268)
- Progressive hearing loss due to necroptosis of outer hair cells and impaired hypo-osmolarity-induced Ca2+ influx (PMID: 32375046)
- Altered Ca2+ permeability and osmosensitivity in brain tissue (PMID: 33100268)
- Embryonic lethality with sub-Mendelian ratios in one essentiality screen study (ASHG 2019)

*Conditional Knockout Mice:*
- Decelerated intestinal motility, impaired digestion, and attenuated intestinal stem cell proliferation (PMID: 39513891)
- No significant impact on voiding behavior even under urothelial stress (PMID: 41348674)

**Lethality and Mendelian Ratios:**
Most full knockout studies report viable mice, but one essentiality screen identified TMEM63B as a "developmental lethal category gene" with embryonic lethality and sub-Mendelian ratios (ASHG 2019). This discrepancy may reflect differences in genetic background or screening methodology.

**Consistency with Human Phenotype:**
The mouse knockout phenotypes reveal TMEM63B's role in osmosensing and mechanosensing across multiple tissues (brain, inner ear, intestine, kidney). However, the human disease phenotype is dominated by severe neurological manifestations (DEE, intellectual disability, progressive neurodegeneration), which are only partially modeled by mouse knockouts. The mouse studies demonstrate loss-of-function effects, whereas human disease appears to result from gain-of-function missense variants—a critical mechanistic distinction.

**Strength of Model Organism Evidence: MODERATE**

Strengths include: (1) multiple independent knockout studies demonstrating tissue-specific functions; (2) clear demonstration of osmosensing and mechanosensing roles; (3) identification as a developmental lethal gene in essentiality screens. Limitations include: (1) loss-of-function models do not recapitulate the gain-of-function mechanism of human disease variants; (2) neurological phenotypes are not prominently featured in most mouse studies; (3) lack of transgenic models expressing human disease variants.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Molecular Function and Pathways:**

TMEM63B functions through multiple complementary mechanisms:

1. **Mechanosensitive Ion Channel:** TMEM63B is a monomeric mechanosensitive cation channel with small conductance and high activation thresholds, with a single highly restricted pore (PMID: 37543036). It is activated by cell stretch and mechanical stimuli.

2. **Osmosensitive Ion Channel:** TMEM63B functions as a mammalian hyperosmolar sensor for thirst (PMID: 40107268) and responds to hypo-osmotic challenge in inner ear sensory cells (PMID: 32375046).

3. **Lipid Scramblase:** TMEM63B functions as a membrane structure-responsive lipid scramblase that bidirectionally translocates phospholipids upon changes in membrane curvature and thickness (PMID: 39424995). Disease-related variants, particularly p.Val44Met, confer constitutive scramblase activity independent of mechanical stimulation (PMID: 40480214, PMID: 39424995).

4. **Protein Complex Formation:** TMEM63B forms a heterodimer with the thiamine transporter SLC19A2 that induces phospholipid scrambling upon Ca2+ stimulation, functioning with the Ca2+-activated K+ channel KCNN4 to regulate phospholipid scrambling activity (PMID: 39217145).

**Expression Pattern:**

TMEM63B is ubiquitously expressed across tissues with particular relevance to:
- Brain (altered Ca2+ permeability and osmosensitivity documented) (PMID: 33100268)
- Inner ear sensory cells (PMID: 32375046)
- Alveolar type 2 cells in lung (PMID: 38127458)
- Intestinal epithelium and stem cells (PMID: 39513891)
- Kidney tubule and collecting duct epithelium (PMID: 38724885)
- Urinary tract epithelium (PMID: 38724885)

**Key Experimental Findings:**

*Gain-of-Function Mechanism in Disease:*
The p.Val44Met variant, identified in multiple patients, causes constitutive lipid scramblase activity without enhancing ion channel activity (PMID: 40480214). Cryo-EM structures reveal that this mutation causes major rearrangements of pore-lining helices, creating a lateral cleft and disrupting a surface-proximal hydrophobic latch (putative force-sensing module) that normally gates lipid scrambling (PMID: 40480214).

*Cellular Consequences:*
Heterozygous TMEM63B variants in cell culture demonstrate inward leak cation currents in isotonic conditions and impaired response to hypo-osmotic challenge (PMID: 37421948). The p.Val44Met mutation disrupts phosphatidylcholine and sphingomyelin asymmetry in the plasma membrane (PMID: 39424995), which could impair neuronal function through altered membrane properties and signaling.

*Surfactant Secretion:*
TMEM63B loss-of-function in mice results in atelectasis and respiratory failure due to deficient surfactant secretion, with TMEM63B localizing to lamellar body limiting membranes in alveolar type 2 cells (PMID: 38127458). This demonstrates the critical role of mechanosensitive activation in exocytic processes.

**Genetic Association Evidence:**
Rare non-coding variants in gene regulatory elements interacting with TMEM63B were identified in a VEGFA-TMEM63B locus cluster significantly associated with Alzheimer's disease risk in microglial cells (ASHG 2023, P=1.6×10^-7), suggesting TMEM63B expression modulation may contribute to neurodegeneration.

**Strength of Biological Evidence: STRONG**

Strengths include: (1) detailed structural and functional characterization of disease variants; (2) clear mechanistic understanding of gain-of-function through constitutive lipid scramblase activity; (3) multiple independent functional assays confirming the same mechanism; (4) tissue-specific expression patterns consistent with clinical phenotypes; (5) identification of protein-protein interactions relevant to disease pathophysiology; (6) genetic association with neurodegenerative disease. Limitations include: (1) incomplete understanding of how constitutive lipid scrambling specifically causes developmental encephalopathy and progressive neurodegeneration; (2) limited in vivo studies of disease variant effects in brain tissue.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall Evidence Level: STRONG**

**Key Strengths:**

1. **Consistent Clinical Phenotype:** 17 unrelated individuals with severe, uniform presentation of early-onset DEE, intellectual disability, motor impairment, cortical visual impairment, and progressive neurodegeneration.

2. **High De Novo Frequency:** 94% de novo variants strongly support autosomal dominant inheritance with high penetrance and reduced reproductive fitness.

3. **Convergent Functional Evidence:** Multiple independent missense variants at different positions all cause the same severe phenotype through a shared gain-of-function mechanism (constitutive lipid scramblase activity).

4. **Detailed Mechanistic Understanding:** Cryo-EM structures and functional studies have elucidated how disease variants disrupt normal channel gating to cause constitutive lipid scrambling, with clear cellular consequences for membrane organization.

5. **Tissue-Specific Expression:** TMEM63B expression in brain and other neurologically relevant tissues is consistent with the clinical phenotype.

**Key Gaps and Limitations:**

1. **Limited Patient Numbers:** Only 17 individuals with detailed characterization, though this is partially offset by phenotypic consistency.

2. **Lack of In Vivo Disease Models:** No transgenic mouse models expressing human disease variants have been reported; existing mouse knockouts model loss-of-function rather than gain-of-function.

3. **Incomplete Mechanistic Link:** While constitutive lipid scrambling is clearly demonstrated, the precise pathophysiological mechanism linking this to developmental encephalopathy and progressive neurodegeneration remains incompletely understood.

4. **Limited Genotype-Phenotype Correlation:** All reported variants produce essentially identical severe phenotypes; no milder variants or phenotypic heterogeneity has been documented.

5. **Absence from Major Cohorts:** The primary clinical series does not specify recruitment from large diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD), limiting assessment of ascertainment bias.

**Conclusion:**

TMEM63B is a well-established gene for autosomal dominant developmental and epileptic encephalopathy with progressive neurodegeneration, caused by heterozygous gain-of-function missense variants. The evidence is strengthened by consistent clinical presentation across 17 unrelated individuals, high de novo frequency, convergent functional evidence from multiple independent variants, and detailed structural and mechanistic characterization. The primary limitation is the relatively small absolute number of patients and the lack of in vivo disease models, though the phenotypic consistency and functional validation provide strong support for pathogenicity. TMEM63B should be considered a high-priority gene for diagnostic testing in individuals presenting with early-onset developmental and epileptic encephalopathy, particularly when accompanied by progressive neurodegeneration and cortical visual impairment.
