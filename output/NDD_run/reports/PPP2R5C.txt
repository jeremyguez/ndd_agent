# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: PPP2R5C AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

PPP2R5C encodes the B56γ regulatory subunit of protein phosphatase 2A (PP2A), a serine/threonine phosphatase complex that regulates multiple cellular signaling pathways including cell proliferation, differentiation, and developmental processes. The PPP2R5C protein functions as a regulatory component that directs PP2A substrate specificity and catalytic activity through interactions with the catalytic C subunit and structural A subunit of the PP2A holoenzyme.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent studies:** Five independent case reports/case series were identified.

**Total patient count:** Approximately 41 patients with PPP2R5C-related neurodevelopmental disorder across all studies (26 patients from PMID: 39978342; 2 patients from PMID: 39696819; 5 patients from PMID: 25972378; 1 patient from DDD-Africa cohort; 7 patients from ASHG 2019 presentation).

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Developmental delay / intellectual disability (reported in nearly all cases)
- Macrocephaly or increased head circumference (reported in majority of cases)
- Hypotonia / congenital hypotonia (reported in most cases)
- Seizures / epilepsy (reported in multiple cases, including refractory epilepsy)
- Behavioral problems and mildly dysmorphic facial features (reported in subset)
- Overgrowth / increased height (reported in some cases)

**Predominant variant type:** Missense variants predominate across all studies. The largest series (PMID: 39978342) identified 19 de novo missense variants in 26 patients, with variants clustering in substrate binding regions (2/19), C-subunit binding regions (2/19), or both (15/19). Five recurrent variants were identified, including p.Glu153Lys (PMID: 39696819) and p.Glu122Lys (ASHG 2019), both affecting highly conserved acidic residues critical for PP2A catalytic subunit interaction. Loss-of-function variants were also reported but appear less common; notably, some LoF variants were inherited from non-symptomatic parents, suggesting a dominant-negative mechanism rather than simple haploinsufficiency.

**Cohort representation:** Patients were identified through international collaborations and specialized cohorts including Matchmaker Exchange (ASHG 2019) and the DDD-Africa cohort (ASHG 2023). The largest series (PMID: 39978342) represents an international multi-center collaboration.

**Diagnostic criteria met:** All reported variants are de novo (except some inherited LoF variants), absent from population databases, affect highly conserved residues, and demonstrate variable effects on PP2A catalytic activity. Recurrent variants in unrelated individuals (p.Glu153Lys, p.Glu122Lys) provide strong evidence for pathogenicity.

**Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG**

The clinical evidence is strong, supported by: (1) multiple independent case series totaling 41 patients; (2) consistent phenotypic features across studies; (3) predominantly de novo missense variants affecting conserved functional domains; (4) identification of recurrent variants in unrelated individuals; (5) clear segregation of variants with disease in families; and (6) absence of variants in population databases.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse models exclusively.

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

Single PPP2R5C knockout mice are viable and fertile, with phenotypes dependent on tissue-specific context:
- Full knockout mice show viable phenotypes with enhanced PP2A activity in specific tissues, resulting in reduced inflammatory responses (PMID: 39018689, 39155740, 39287443)
- Tissue-specific effects include ameliorated emphysema, reduced airway inflammation, suppressed Th2 cell differentiation, and enhanced fungicidal immunity

Double knockout of PPP2R5C and PPP2R5D (B56γ and B56δ) results in embryonic lethality with severe developmental defects:
- Fetal growth arrest around embryonic day E12 (PMID: 35415460)
- Cardiac developmental defect: single outflow vessel instead of separate aorta and pulmonary artery
- Demonstrates genetic interaction and conserved function between B56 family members

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The embryonic lethality and cardiac developmental defects in double knockout mice are consistent with the severe neurodevelopmental phenotype observed in humans with heterozygous PPP2R5C variants. However, single PPP2R5C knockout mice do not recapitulate the neurodevelopmental disorder phenotype, suggesting that heterozygous missense variants causing dominant-negative effects may have different consequences than complete loss of function. The viable single knockouts with enhanced PP2A activity suggest that loss of PPP2R5C function alone may not be the primary pathogenic mechanism in human disease.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**

The model organism evidence is moderate in strength. While the double knockout data demonstrates the developmental importance of PPP2R5C family members and the embryonic lethality supports a critical developmental role, the lack of a neurodevelopmental phenotype in single heterozygous or homozygous knockouts limits direct translation to human disease. The human variants are predominantly missense mutations affecting specific functional domains rather than complete loss-of-function, suggesting a dominant-negative mechanism that may not be fully captured by knockout models.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

PPP2R5C functions as a regulatory subunit of the PP2A holoenzyme, which dephosphorylates multiple substrates involved in:
- Cell proliferation and p53 regulation (PMID: 19840435)
- Th2 cell differentiation and immune responses (PMID: 38262392)
- IL-1β ubiquitination and inflammatory signaling (PMID: 39018689)
- Epithelial-mesenchymal transition and TGF-β1 signaling (PMID: 39155740)
- PI3K/AKT signaling in cancer cells (PMID: 37272097)

**Expression pattern:** PPP2R5C is expressed across multiple tissues with elevated expression in leukemia and malignant cells (PMID: 21548944). Five transcript variants are identified with similar frequencies across tissues. While specific brain/neurodevelopmental expression data are not detailed in the provided evidence, the clinical phenotype of neurodevelopmental disorder and the identification of variants affecting conserved residues critical for PP2A function suggest important roles in neural development.

**Key experimental findings:**

Pathogenic mechanism: De novo missense variants in PPP2R5C impair binding of PP2A catalytic C and structural A subunits and reduce PP2A catalytic activity (ASHG 2019). The recurrent p.Glu122Lys variant affects a residue that directly interacts with the catalytic subunit, showing significantly impaired subunit binding and phosphatase activity compared to wildtype. Variants cluster in substrate binding regions or C-subunit binding regions, with variable effects on catalytic activity (PMID: 39978342).

Protein interactions: PPP2R5C directly interacts with the PP2A catalytic C subunit through conserved acidic residues (Glu122, Glu153), which are frequently affected by pathogenic variants. Loss-of-function variants can be inherited from asymptomatic parents, suggesting dominant-negative effects where mutant protein interferes with wildtype PP2A complex assembly or function.

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is strong, supported by: (1) well-characterized molecular function as a PP2A regulatory subunit; (2) clear identification of specific residues critical for PP2A subunit interactions; (3) demonstration that pathogenic variants impair these critical interactions and reduce phosphatase activity; (4) evidence for dominant-negative mechanisms; and (5) multiple pathway analyses showing PPP2R5C involvement in developmental and neurological processes.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths of the evidence:**

1. **Robust clinical evidence:** 41 patients across five independent studies with consistent neurodevelopmental phenotypes (developmental delay, hypotonia, seizures, macrocephaly) and predominantly de novo missense variants absent from population databases.

2. **Recurrent variants in unrelated individuals:** Multiple recurrent variants (p.Glu153Lys, p.Glu122Lys) identified in unrelated patients provide strong evidence for pathogenicity and establish a clear genotype-phenotype correlation.

3. **Mechanistic understanding:** Clear demonstration that pathogenic variants affect conserved residues critical for PP2A subunit interactions, impair catalytic activity, and likely function through dominant-negative mechanisms.

4. **Functional conservation:** Evidence from model organisms (particularly double knockout lethality and cardiac developmental defects) demonstrates the developmental importance of PPP2R5C family members.

5. **Biological plausibility:** PPP2R5C's known roles in cell proliferation, differentiation, and signaling pathways are consistent with a neurodevelopmental disorder phenotype.

**Key gaps or limitations:**

1. **Limited model organism correlation:** Single PPP2R5C knockout mice do not recapitulate the human neurodevelopmental phenotype, limiting direct experimental validation of the dominant-negative mechanism in vivo.

2. **Incomplete functional characterization:** While some variants have been functionally characterized, not all reported variants have detailed biochemical analysis of their effects on PP2A activity.

3. **Brain-specific expression data:** Limited information on PPP2R5C expression patterns specifically in developing brain or neural tissues.

4. **Genotype-phenotype correlation:** While phenotypes are generally consistent, there is variability in severity and specific features across patients, and the relationship between specific variant locations and phenotypic severity is not fully characterized.

**Conclusion:**

PPP2R5C is a well-established gene for autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder caused by heterozygous de novo missense variants. The evidence is strong across clinical, biological, and model organism domains, with 41 patients identified across multiple independent studies, recurrent pathogenic variants in unrelated individuals, and clear mechanistic understanding of how variants impair PP2A function through dominant-negative effects. The consistent phenotype of developmental delay, hypotonia, seizures, and macrocephaly, combined with the clustering of variants in functionally critical domains and their absence from population databases, provides compelling evidence for PPP2R5C as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene. Future studies should focus on functional characterization of additional variants and development of animal models that better recapitulate the dominant-negative mechanism observed in human disease.

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. Pathogenic de novo variants in PPP2R5C cause a neurodevelopmental disorder within the Houge-Janssens syndrome spectrum (PMID: 39978342)
2. A recurrent variant in PPP2R5C identified in individuals with macrocephaly, intellectual disability, and seizures (PMID: 39696819)
3. Mutations in the PP2A regulatory subunit B family genes PPP2R5B, PPP2R5C and PPP2R5D cause human overgrowth (PMID: 25972378)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. LincR-PPP2R5C regulates IL-1β ubiquitination in macrophages and promotes airway inflammation and emphysema in a murine model of COPD (PMID: 39018689)
2. LincR-PPP2R5C Deficiency Alleviates Airway Remodeling by Inhibiting Epithelial-Mesenchymal Transition Through the PP2A/TGF-β1 Signaling Pathway in Chronic Experimental Allergic Asthma (PMID: 39155740)
3. LincR-PPP2R5C deficiency enhancing the fungicidal activity of neutrophils in pulmonary cryptococcosis is linked to the upregulation of IL-4 (PMID: 39287443)
4. Growth arrest of PPP2R5C and PPP2R5D double knockout mice indicates a genetic interaction and conserved function for these PP2A B subunits (PMID: 35415460)
5. LincR-PPP2R5C Promotes Th2 Cell Differentiation Through PPP2R5C/PP2A by Forming an RNA-DNA Triplex in Allergic Asthma (PMID: 38262392)

### Biological Evidence
