# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: RAB5C AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

RAB5C is a small GTPase that functions as a master regulator of early endocytic trafficking and vesicular membrane dynamics. It plays critical roles in endosomal maturation, receptor internalization, and signaling pathway regulation across multiple cellular contexts including neuronal development, angiogenesis, and immune cell function.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent studies:** 2 case reports/series

**Total patients:** Approximately 13 patients (12 from primary RAB5C study + 1 from duplication case)

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Developmental delay (present in all reported cases)
- Macrocephaly (9 patients with missense variants)
- Intellectual disability (2 patients with loss-of-function variants)
- Refractory epilepsy (2 patients with loss-of-function variants)
- Dysmorphic features (1 patient with duplication)

**Predominant variant type:**
The primary clinical evidence distinguishes two pathogenic mechanisms:
- Heterozygous de novo missense variants (9 variants in 9 patients) causing macrocephaly with mild-to-moderate developmental delay
- Loss-of-function variants (2 variants in 2 patients) causing refractory epilepsy and intellectual disability with normal head circumference

**Cohort source:** No indication that patients were from established diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL)

**Diagnostic evidence quality:** The primary study (PMID: 37552066) provides strong mechanistic support: de novo variants were identified in affected individuals, in vitro biochemical studies demonstrated that all four tested missense variants were damaging with increased nucleotide exchange rates and attenuated responsivity to guanine exchange factors, and both C. elegans and zebrafish models confirmed in vivo pathogenicity with dominant negative mechanisms demonstrated for two variants.

The secondary study (PMID: 23337768) reports a de novo 103 kb duplication at 17q21.2 including RAB5C and 7 other genes in a patient with severe developmental delay and microcephaly. However, this evidence is complicated by the presence of multiple genes in the duplicated region, with three other genes (KAT2A, KCNH4, STAT5B) also implicated in intellectual disability, making RAB5C's specific contribution unclear.

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**

The evidence for heterozygous missense variants causing neurodevelopmental phenotypes is reasonably strong with functional validation, but the total number of patients is small (12 with point variants), and the duplication case provides only indirect evidence. The distinction between missense variants (macrocephaly-predominant) and loss-of-function variants (epilepsy-predominant) suggests possible genotype-phenotype correlation but requires larger case series for confirmation.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Zebrafish (4 studies), Xenopus laevis (1 study), C. elegans (1 study, referenced in clinical paper), mammalian cell culture (HeLa cells)

**Key neurological and developmental phenotypes from knockdown/knockout:**

- **Zebrafish hematopoietic development (PMID: 32275659):** Rab5c knockdown caused defects in hematopoietic stem and progenitor cell (HSPC) production and hemogenic endothelium specification, with reduced survival of hemogenic endothelium in the ventral wall of the dorsal aorta. Excessive endolysosomal trafficking upon Rab5c overactivation also led to HSPC production defects, suggesting a dose-sensitive requirement.

- **Zebrafish gastrulation (PMID: 16198297):** Rab5c knockdown caused defects in gastrulation morphogenesis and loss of mesendodermal cell cohesion through disruption of E-cadherin-mediated cell adhesion. The phenotype mimicked slb/wnt11 mutants when Rab5c activity was blocked, and was rescued when Rab5c activity was enhanced.

- **Zebrafish angiogenesis (PMID: 33983539):** Rab5c knockdown and CRISPR/Cas9-mediated gene editing caused disrupted VEGF signaling through ERK and PI3-K pathways, reduced expression of immediate VEGF target genes, defects in specification of angiogenic tip and stalk cell phenotypes, defects in cell sprouting, and defects in developmental angiogenesis in vivo. The mechanism involved premature degradation of internalized VEGFR2.

- **Xenopus laevis visual system development (PMID: 41507541):** Rab5c knockdown accelerated developmental decline of glutamatergic synaptic transmission with reduced miniature excitatory postsynaptic current (mEPSC) amplitudes and reduced visually evoked responses. The mechanism involved Rab5c-dependent endocytosis of AMPA receptor subunits GluA1 and GluA2, with altered dendritic architecture observed.

- **Mammalian cell culture (PMID: 34817057):** Conditional Rab5 knockout in HeLa cells resulted in cell death 48 hours after protein depletion, loss of early and late endosomes, and dramatic reduction in intracellular signals of endocytic cargos via both receptor-mediated and fluid-phase endocytosis, demonstrating essential cellular function.

**Consistency with human phenotype:**
The model organism evidence demonstrates that Rab5c is essential for multiple developmental processes including cell cohesion, vascular development, neuronal synaptic plasticity, and hematopoietic development. The neurological phenotypes (synaptic dysfunction, altered neuronal excitability, defective dendritic architecture) are consistent with the developmental delay and intellectual disability observed in human patients. The dose-sensitivity observed in zebrafish (both loss and overactivation causing defects) aligns with the dominant negative mechanism proposed for human missense variants.

**Lethality and Mendelian ratios:** No complete knockout lethality data reported in the provided studies, though conditional knockout in mammalian cells shows 48-hour lethality upon protein depletion, suggesting essential function.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**

Multiple independent model systems (zebrafish, Xenopus, C. elegans, mammalian cells) consistently demonstrate that Rab5c is essential for neuronal development, synaptic function, and multiple developmental processes. The phenotypes are mechanistically detailed and show dose-sensitivity consistent with dominant negative effects.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

RAB5C functions as a central hub in multiple cellular pathways:

1. **Endocytic trafficking and receptor signaling:** RAB5C regulates early endosome formation and maturation, controlling internalization and trafficking of growth factor receptors (VEGFR2, EGFR) and neurotransmitter receptors (AMPA receptors). It interacts with SNAP29 to regulate vesicle fusion and exocytosis (PMID: 41537263).

2. **Autophagy and selective autophagy:** RAB5C directs cargo to lysosomes via P62-dependent selective autophagy (PMID: 39779666) and controls LC3-associated phagocytosis (LAP) by regulating phagosome maturation and recruitment of autophagy machinery (PMID: 40196584).

3. **Cell adhesion and morphogenesis:** RAB5C regulates E-cadherin-mediated cell cohesion through endocytic trafficking, essential for gastrulation and tissue morphogenesis (PMID: 16198297).

4. **Signaling pathway regulation:** RAB5C modulates Notch signaling (PMID: 32275659), VEGF/ERK/PI3K signaling (PMID: 33983539), and MAPK/ERK signaling (PMID: 37213158).

5. **Phosphatidylcholine metabolism:** RAB5C was identified in a genome-scale CRISPR screen as involved in vesicular membrane trafficking and phosphatidylcholine metabolism (PMID: 36917984).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**

While the provided biological evidence does not include comprehensive brain expression data, the functional studies demonstrate RAB5C activity in:
- Neuronal synaptic transmission and AMPA receptor trafficking (PMID: 41507541)
- Dendritic architecture development (PMID: 41507541)
- Hematopoietic stem cell development with implications for neural crest-derived tissues (PMID: 32275659)

The involvement in synaptic plasticity, receptor trafficking, and developmental morphogenesis directly supports relevance to neurodevelopmental disorders.

**Key experimental findings:**

- Missense variants in RAB5C increase nucleotide exchange rate and attenuate responsivity to guanine exchange factors, suggesting gain-of-function or altered regulation (PMID: 37552066)
- Dominant negative mechanisms demonstrated for at least two missense variants (PMID: 37552066)
- Dose-sensitivity: both loss and overactivation of Rab5c cause developmental defects in zebrafish (PMID: 32275659)
- RAB5C is essential for cell survival in mammalian cells (PMID: 34817057)
- RAB5C regulates multiple signaling pathways critical for development (VEGF, Notch, MAPK/ERK)

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence comprehensively demonstrates RAB5C's essential role in endocytic trafficking, autophagy, cell adhesion, and multiple developmental signaling pathways. The mechanistic studies showing altered nucleotide exchange kinetics and dominant negative effects provide clear molecular explanations for how missense variants could cause disease. The dose-sensitivity and multiple pathway involvement explain the range of phenotypes observed.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE-TO-STRONG**

**Key strengths:**

1. **Functional validation:** The primary clinical study (PMID: 37552066) provides robust mechanistic evidence with in vitro biochemical characterization, C. elegans and zebrafish validation, and demonstration of dominant negative mechanisms for missense variants.

2. **Consistent model organism evidence:** Multiple independent model systems (zebrafish, Xenopus, C. elegans) demonstrate that Rab5c is essential for neuronal development, synaptic function, and developmental morphogenesis, with phenotypes consistent with human neurodevelopmental delay.

3. **Molecular mechanism clarity:** The distinction between missense variants (altered nucleotide exchange kinetics, dominant negative effects) and loss-of-function variants provides a mechanistic framework for understanding genotype-phenotype correlation.

4. **Pathway relevance:** RAB5C's roles in endocytic trafficking, autophagy, and multiple developmental signaling pathways (VEGF, Notch, MAPK/ERK) are well-established and directly relevant to neurodevelopment.

**Key limitations and gaps:**

1. **Limited patient numbers:** Only 12 patients with point variants and 1 with a duplication. Larger case series are needed to establish robust genotype-phenotype correlations and confirm the distinction between missense and loss-of-function phenotypes.

2. **Incomplete clinical characterization:** The primary study abstract does not list individual variants or provide detailed phenotypic descriptions for each patient, limiting assessment of phenotypic heterogeneity.

3. **Lack of cohort data:** No indication that patients were ascertained through established diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD), which would strengthen evidence for pathogenicity.

4. **Brain-specific expression data:** The provided evidence does not include comprehensive RAB5C expression data in developing human brain or specific neuronal populations.

5. **Incomplete penetrance and variable expressivity:** No data on incomplete penetrance, variable expressivity, or familial cases to assess inheritance patterns.

**Conclusion:**

RAB5C is a credible neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous de novo missense variants causing macrocephaly and developmental delay through dominant negative mechanisms affecting endocytic trafficking and developmental signaling pathways. The evidence is supported by robust functional validation in multiple model organisms and clear molecular mechanisms. However, the evidence remains moderate rather than strong due to the limited number of reported patients and lack of data from established diagnostic cohorts. Additional case reports and functional studies of individual variants would strengthen the evidence base and clarify genotype-phenotype correlations, particularly regarding the apparent distinction between missense variants (macrocephaly-predominant) and loss-of-function variants (epilepsy-predominant).

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. Macrocephaly and developmental delay caused by missense variants in RAB5C (PMID: 37552066)
2. A de novo 17q21.2 duplication in a boy with developmental delay and dysmorphic features (PMID: 23337768)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. Rab5c-mediated endocytic trafficking regulates hematopoietic stem and progenitor cell development via Notch and AKT signaling (PMID: 32275659)
2. Wnt11 functions in gastrulation by controlling cell cohesion through Rab5c and E-cadherin (PMID: 16198297)
3. The endosomal RIN2/Rab5C machinery prevents VEGFR2 degradation to control gene expression and tip cell identity during angiogenesis (PMID: 33983539)
4. Prolonged visual experience accelerates developmental synaptic downscaling via epigenetic regulation and Rab5c mediated AMPA receptor trafficking (PMID: 41507541)
5. Establishment and analysis of conditional Rab1- and Rab5-knockout cells using the auxin-inducible degron system (PMID: 34817057)

### Biological Evidence
1. RAB5C Increases Endothelial Release of VWF by Regulating Vesicle Trafficking (PMID: 41537263)
2. S100P is a ferroptosis suppressor to facilitate hepatocellular carcinoma development by rewiring lipid metabolism (PMID: 39779666)
3. RAB5c controls the assembly of non-canonical autophagy machinery to promote phagosome maturation and microbicidal function of macrophages (PMID: 40196584)
4. Effects of miR-145 targeting rab5c and regulating MAPK/ERK signaling pathway on proliferation and invasion of thyroid papillary carcinoma cells (PMID: 37213158)
5. Organelle-selective click labeling coupled with flow cytometry allows pooled CRISPR screening of genes involved in phosphatidylcholine metabolism (PMID: 36917984)
