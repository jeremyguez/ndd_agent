# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: PCBP2 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

PCBP2 (poly(rC)-binding protein 2) is an RNA-binding protein that functions as a post-transcriptional regulator of mRNA stability and translation. PCBP2 plays critical roles in cellular homeostasis through iron chaperone activity, mRNA stabilization, and regulation of translation initiation, with emerging evidence for involvement in neuroprotection and cellular stress responses.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of studies and patients:**
One study identified 3 patients with de novo PCBP2 variants through burden re-analysis of neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts (PMID: 38965372).

**Phenotypes observed:**
The three patients presented with a consistent neurodevelopmental phenotype including:
- Neurodevelopmental disorder (core diagnosis)
- Motor delay
- Language delay

**Variant type:**
De novo variants were identified, but specific variant types (loss-of-function vs. missense) were not detailed in the available abstract.

**Cohort information:**
The variants were identified through burden analysis of unsolved neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts, suggesting participation in research cohorts focused on rare genetic causes of NDD, though specific cohort names (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, etc.) were not specified.

**Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED**

The clinical evidence is limited by: (1) small sample size (n=3 patients), (2) lack of detailed variant characterization, (3) single study source, and (4) absence of functional validation or segregation data. However, the identification of three independent de novo variants in the same gene through unbiased burden analysis provides some support for pathogenicity.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:**
Mouse models have been extensively studied, including conditional knockouts, full knockouts, and knockdown approaches.

**Key neurological and developmental phenotypes:**
While the model organism studies do not directly address neurodevelopmental phenotypes, several findings are potentially relevant to neurodevelopment:

- **Oxidative stress and apoptosis:** PCBP2-knockout mice showed increased apoptosis under oxidative conditions with elevated caspase-3 and PARP1 cleavage (PMID: 32647012), suggesting PCBP2 is critical for cellular survival under stress—a mechanism potentially relevant to neuronal development and maintenance.

- **Antioxidant defense:** PCBP2-deficient mice exhibited decreased p73 expression and increased reactive oxygen species (ROS) production in primary embryonic fibroblasts, with accelerated cellular senescence (PMID: 26907686). Elevated ROS is known to impair neuronal development and function.

- **Translation regulation:** Pcbp1/2 co-deletion resulted in severe diabetes with compromised β cell viability and downregulation of core translation initiation factors and ribosomal mRNAs (PMID: 40451383). Since translation initiation is fundamental to all cell types including neurons, this suggests PCBP2 is essential for maintaining translational capacity.

- **No reported lethality or Mendelian ratio deviations:** The available studies do not report embryonic lethality or Mendelian ratio deviations, suggesting that complete loss of PCBP2 may not be embryonic lethal in mice, though conditional knockout approaches were used in some studies.

**Consistency with human phenotype:**
The model organism evidence does not directly demonstrate neurodevelopmental phenotypes. However, the critical roles in oxidative stress response, translation regulation, and cellular viability are biologically plausible mechanisms that could underlie neurodevelopmental dysfunction if disrupted during critical developmental windows.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**

While multiple mouse studies demonstrate essential functions of PCBP2 in cellular homeostasis and stress response, none directly model neurodevelopmental phenotypes. The evidence supports PCBP2 as functionally important for cell survival and translation, but does not provide direct validation of a neurodevelopmental disorder mechanism.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

PCBP2 functions in multiple critical cellular pathways:

1. **mRNA stabilization and translation regulation:** PCBP2 binds and stabilizes specific mRNAs, including those encoding translation initiation factors, ribosomal proteins, and metabolic enzymes (PMID: 40451383). This post-transcriptional regulation is essential for maintaining translational capacity across cell types.

2. **Iron homeostasis:** PCBP2 functions as a cytosolic iron chaperone, facilitating safe transport of ferrous iron and preventing oxidative damage (PMID: 30055235). Iron is critical for neuronal development, myelin formation, and mitochondrial function.

3. **Ferroptosis regulation:** PCBP2 stabilizes SLC7A11 mRNA, a key component of the cystine/glutamate antiporter system Xc−, thereby inhibiting ferroptosis—a form of iron-dependent cell death (PMID: 40486855, PMID: 39731111). Ferroptosis dysregulation has been implicated in neurodegeneration.

4. **Oxidative stress response:** PCBP2 suppresses apoptosis under oxidative conditions and regulates p73-dependent antioxidant defense (PMID: 32647012, PMID: 26907686), protecting cells from ROS-induced damage.

5. **miRNA pathway regulation:** PCBP2 associates with Dicer and promotes miRNA precursor processing in an iron-dependent manner, linking iron metabolism to post-transcriptional gene regulation (ASHG 2011 - Iron homeostasis modulates the activity of microRNA pathway through PCBP2).

6. **Neuroprotection:** PCBP2 has been identified as a key mediator of neuroprotection against ferroptosis in neural cells exposed to heat stress (PMID: 39731111), and PCBP2 elevation in Alzheimer's disease brain correlates with pathological biomolecular condensate formation (PMID: 41298370).

**Expression pattern:**
While comprehensive brain expression data are not provided in the available evidence, PCBP2 is expressed in multiple tissues including pancreatic β cells, granulosa cells, hepatocytes, and neural cells (PC12 cells), suggesting broad expression including in the nervous system.

**Key experimental findings:**
- PCBP2 is essential for maintaining global mRNA translation rates in metabolically active cells (PMID: 40451383)
- PCBP2 stabilizes multiple mRNAs critical for cellular function through direct RNA binding
- PCBP2 levels are dynamically regulated and can be modulated by small molecules (PMID: 41298370)
- PCBP2 dysfunction correlates with ferroptosis, oxidative stress, and cellular dysfunction in multiple disease contexts

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is strong, demonstrating that PCBP2 is a multifunctional RNA-binding protein with critical roles in translation, iron homeostasis, oxidative stress response, and neuroprotection. These functions are fundamental to neuronal development and maintenance, providing plausible mechanistic links to neurodevelopmental dysfunction.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths:**
1. **Functional relevance:** PCBP2 has well-characterized roles in translation regulation, oxidative stress response, and iron homeostasis—all critical for neurodevelopment
2. **De novo variants in affected individuals:** Three independent de novo variants identified through unbiased burden analysis in patients with consistent NDD phenotype (motor and language delay)
3. **Cellular stress sensitivity:** Model organisms demonstrate that PCBP2 loss impairs cellular survival under oxidative stress and compromises translation capacity
4. **Neuroprotective function:** Direct evidence that PCBP2 mediates neuroprotection against ferroptosis in neural cells

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. **Very limited clinical data:** Only 3 patients from a single study; no functional validation of variants, no segregation data, and no detailed variant characterization (LoF vs. missense)
2. **No direct neurodevelopmental models:** Mouse studies do not directly model neurodevelopmental phenotypes; evidence is indirect through cellular stress and translation mechanisms
3. **Lack of expression data:** No detailed information on PCBP2 expression during critical windows of neurodevelopment
4. **Incomplete variant information:** Specific variants and their predicted effects are not detailed in available abstracts
5. **No replication:** Findings have not been independently replicated in other cohorts

**Conclusion:**

PCBP2 is a biologically plausible candidate gene for autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder based on its critical roles in translation regulation, oxidative stress response, and neuroprotection. However, the clinical evidence remains limited to three de novo variants in a single study without detailed characterization or functional validation. Additional clinical cases, functional studies of identified variants, and mechanistic investigation of how PCBP2 disruption impairs neurodevelopment during critical developmental windows would substantially strengthen the evidence for PCBP2 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene.

### Biological Evidence
1. DMT1 and iron transport (PMID: 30055235)
2. Acteoside ameliorates hepatocyte ferroptosis and hepatic ischemia-reperfusion injury via targeting PCBP2 (PMID: 40486855)
3. Role of PCBP2 in regulating nanovesicles loaded with curcumin to mitigate neuroferroptosis in neural damage caused by heat stroke (PMID: 39731111)
4. Pharmacologic inhibition of PCBP2 biomolecular condensates relieves Alzheimer's disease (PMID: 41298370)
