# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: NLGN2 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

NLGN2 (Neuroligin 2) is a postsynaptic cell adhesion molecule that selectively functions at inhibitory GABAergic synapses, where it recruits and organizes postsynaptic GABA(A) receptors and the gephyrin scaffold. NLGN2 plays critical roles in inhibitory synapse formation, plasticity, and the regulation of neural circuits controlling anxiety, mood, cognition, and sleep.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent case reports/series:** Four distinct clinical reports were identified (one published case report, two ASHG conference abstracts, and one additional case series reference).

**Total patients:** Approximately 3-4 independent patients with NLGN2 variants were documented across the clinical evidence.

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Autism spectrum disorder (present in 3/3 cases with detailed phenotyping)
- Anxiety and anxiety-related behaviors (3/3 cases)
- Developmental delay/intellectual disability (3/3 cases)
- Behavioral problems (3/3 cases)
- Language impairment/delays (2/3 cases)
- Hyperphagia and obesity (2/3 cases)
- Macrocephaly (2/3 cases)
- Catatonia (1 case, noted as emerging feature during disease progression)
- Motor delays and hypotonia (1 case)
- Dysmorphic features (2/3 cases)

**Predominant variant type:** Loss-of-function variants predominate. One nonsense variant (c.441C>A, p.Tyr147Ter) was explicitly characterized as LoF (PMID: 27865048). Two additional cases involved de novo missense or unspecified de novo variants, with one case report specifically documenting catatonia as a progressive feature (PMID: 32405903).

**Cohort source:** No patients were identified as originating from known diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL). All cases appear to be individual case reports or small case series from clinical centers.

**Inheritance pattern:** All reported variants were de novo heterozygous, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance with complete penetrance.

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**

The clinical evidence is strengthened by the presence of de novo variants in unaffected parents, consistent phenotypic features across cases (particularly autism, anxiety, and developmental delay), and the first-ever reported NLGN2 nonsense variant in humans. However, the evidence is limited by the small number of patients (n=3-4), lack of systematic cohort ascertainment, and incomplete molecular characterization of some variants. The emergence of catatonia as a progressive feature in one case is noteworthy but requires confirmation in additional patients.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (Mus musculus) exclusively; eight independent studies utilizing various knockout and knock-in approaches.

**Knockout types and viability:** All NLGN2 knockout and knock-in mouse models are viable and fertile, with no reported lethality or Mendelian ratio deviations. This indicates that NLGN2 is not essential for embryonic development or postnatal survival in mice.

**Key phenotypes from mouse models:**

*Anxiety and mood-related phenotypes:*
- Robust anxiety phenotype upon anxiogenic stimulus exposure (PMID: 26142252)
- Exacerbated anxiety-related behavior with abnormal gephyrin aggregation (PMID: 39284869)
- Pronounced anxiety phenotype with altered oscillatory synchrony in ventral hippocampus-medial prefrontal cortex-basolateral amygdala circuit (PMID: 33960026)
- Resistance to chronic stress-induced depressive behaviors when NLGN2 is engineered to resist MDGA1-mediated suppression (PMID: 39897557)

*Cognitive and behavioral phenotypes:*
- Cognition deficits and impaired pre-pulse inhibition in R215H knock-in model (PMID: 29859117)
- Abnormal stress responses (PMID: 29859117)

*Sleep and neurophysiological phenotypes:*
- Increased wakefulness with decreased NREM and REM sleep (PMID: 30231918)
- Impaired state consolidation and abnormal hypersynchronized EEG events (PMID: 30231918)
- Altered theta and beta oscillatory synchrony across anxiety-related neural circuits (PMID: 33960026)

*Synaptic phenotypes:*
- Impaired inhibitory synaptic transmission across multiple brain regions (PMID: 26142252, PMID: 39284869, PMID: 29859117)
- Affected postsynaptic structures at perisomatic sites in basal amygdala (PMID: 26142252)
- Abnormal cytosolic gephyrin aggregation (PMID: 39284869)
- Altered GABAergic synapse organization in sex-specific fashion (PMID: 38571813)

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The mouse models show strong consistency with human clinical features. The prominent anxiety phenotype in mice directly parallels the anxiety and anxiety-related behaviors (including obsessive-compulsive behaviors) observed in human patients. The cognitive deficits in mice align with developmental delay and intellectual disability in humans. The sleep abnormalities in mice may relate to the behavioral dysregulation and developmental regression observed clinically. The underlying mechanism—impaired GABAergic inhibitory transmission—provides a plausible biological basis for the neurodevelopmental and psychiatric features in human patients.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) multiple independent mouse models with consistent phenotypic findings; (2) detailed characterization of synaptic mechanisms underlying behavioral phenotypes; (3) demonstration that NLGN2 function is critical for normal anxiety regulation, cognition, and sleep; (4) viability of knockouts ruling out essential developmental functions; and (5) direct mechanistic insights into GABAergic synapse dysfunction. The evidence is limited only by the use of a single organism model (mice) and the lack of studies in other model organisms.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

NLGN2 functions as a key organizer of inhibitory GABAergic synapses through multiple mechanisms:

1. **Postsynaptic receptor and scaffold recruitment:** NLGN2 recruits and organizes GABA(A) receptors at the postsynaptic membrane by promoting assembly of the gephyrin scaffold (PMID: 39284869, PMID: 39747663). The extracellular domains of NLGN2 confer inhibitory synapse specificity, while its cytoplasmic sequences, including the gephyrin-binding motif, are essential for synaptic function (PMID: 39747663).

2. **MDGA1-mediated regulation:** NLGN2 selectively interacts with MDGA1 (myelin-associated glycoprotein domain-containing protein 1) in the lateral habenula and other brain regions. MDGA1-mediated suppression of NLGN2 limits GABAergic synapse formation and facilitates depression onset. Conversely, NLGN2 variants incapable of binding MDGA1 enhance inhibitory transmission and increase GABAergic synapse density, conferring resistance to stress-induced depression (PMID: 39897557). Loss of MDGA1 ameliorates abnormal gephyrin aggregation and reduced inhibitory transmission in NLGN2 knockout mice (PMID: 39284869).

3. **Circuit-specific regulation:** NLGN2 differentially regulates dendritic versus perisomatic GABAergic presynaptic differentiation in a sex-specific manner (PMID: 38571813). NLGN2 in the prefrontal cortex regulates memory formation and modulation of memory strength through changes in expression in inhibitory neuronal subpopulations (PMID: 29278843).

4. **Distinct mechanisms from NLGN1:** NLGN2 has distinct mechanisms from NLGN1, with NLGN2 selectively restoring inhibitory synaptic transmission in neuroligin-deficient neurons (PMID: 39747663).

**Expression pattern:**

NLGN2 is expressed throughout the brain with particular enrichment in regions critical for anxiety regulation (amygdala, hippocampus, prefrontal cortex), mood regulation (lateral habenula), and sleep-wake control. NLGN2 mRNA expression is dynamically regulated: it is significantly downregulated in lipopolysaccharide-induced neuroinflammation models and associated with memory impairment and depressive-like behavior (PMID: 39930360). NLGN2 is downregulated in parvalbumin interneurons in schizophrenia-relevant models (PMID: 37004850).

**Key experimental findings:**

1. **Structural insights:** Cryo-EM structures of human NLGN2 reveal relative rotations of NLGN2 homodimers that may affect interactions with MDGAs, suggesting distinct assembly mechanisms that could affect roles in autism spectrum disorder development (PMID: 36479216).

2. **Rare variant association with schizophrenia:** A rare missense mutation (p.R309Q) was identified in schizophrenia patients but absent in 1,517 healthy controls and the ExAC database. Immunoblot analysis showed this mutation decreased NLGN2 protein expression in HEK293 cells, suggesting loss-of-function contribution to schizophrenia (ASHG 2018 - Targeted sequencing of postsynaptic protein genes).

3. **Genome-wide association findings:** NLGN2 was identified as a high-confidence (>90% true-positive probability) candidate gene for neurodevelopmental disorders through integrated analysis of de novo SNVs and CNVs in 41,165 individuals with NDDs, based on functional similarity to known NDD genes (PMID: 35468861). NLGN2 was also identified as one of 21 druggable genes significantly associated with migraine through genome-wide Mendelian randomization analysis (PMID: 38867170).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is exceptionally strong, supported by: (1) detailed mechanistic understanding of NLGN2 function at GABAergic synapses; (2) identification of key protein-protein interactions (MDGA1, gephyrin, GABA(A) receptors); (3) structural characterization of NLGN2 homodimers; (4) demonstration of dynamic regulation of NLGN2 expression in disease-relevant contexts; (5) genome-wide association studies identifying NLGN2 as a high-confidence NDD candidate gene; and (6) consistent findings across multiple experimental approaches (cell biology, mouse genetics, structural biology, population genetics).

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths of the evidence:**

1. **Convergent evidence from multiple sources:** Clinical case reports of de novo NLGN2 variants causing neurodevelopmental phenotypes are supported by extensive mouse genetic evidence demonstrating that NLGN2 loss-of-function causes anxiety, cognitive deficits, and sleep abnormalities—phenotypes directly observed in human patients.

2. **Clear mechanistic basis:** The biological evidence provides a coherent mechanistic explanation for the human phenotypes through impaired GABAergic inhibitory synapse function, with specific molecular pathways (MDGA1 interaction, gephyrin recruitment, GABA(A) receptor organization) identified and validated.

3. **De novo inheritance pattern:** All reported human cases involve de novo heterozygous variants in unaffected parents, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance and indicating high penetrance.

4. **Genome-wide validation:** Large-scale genomic studies (41,165 NDD cases) identify NLGN2 as a high-confidence candidate gene for neurodevelopmental disorders based on functional similarity to known NDD genes.

5. **Consistent phenotypic features:** Across the limited number of human cases, autism spectrum disorder, anxiety, developmental delay, and behavioral problems are consistently observed, suggesting a recognizable clinical phenotype.

**Key gaps and limitations:**

1. **Small number of human cases:** Only 3-4 independent patients with NLGN2 variants have been reported, limiting the ability to define a comprehensive clinical phenotype or assess phenotypic variability.

2. **Incomplete molecular characterization:** Some clinical cases lack detailed variant information (specific nucleotide changes, predicted functional consequences), limiting assessment of genotype-phenotype correlations.

3. **Lack of systematic cohort ascertainment:** Cases appear to be sporadic clinical reports rather than systematic screening of diagnostic cohorts, potentially introducing ascertainment bias toward more severe phenotypes.

4. **Limited functional validation of human variants:** Only one human variant (p.R309Q in schizophrenia) has been functionally validated in vitro; the nonsense variant (p.Tyr147Ter) and missense variants lack direct functional studies.

5. **Absence of other model organisms:** Evidence is limited to mouse models; studies in other organisms (zebrafish, Drosophila, C. elegans) would strengthen the evidence base.

6. **Limited data on variable expressivity and incomplete penetrance:** With only a few cases, it is unclear whether NLGN2 variants show variable expressivity or whether penetrance is truly complete.

**Conclusion:**

NLGN2 is a strong candidate gene for autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder, supported by de novo heterozygous variants in human patients with autism spectrum disorder, anxiety, developmental delay, and behavioral problems; extensive mouse genetic evidence demonstrating that NLGN2 loss-of-function causes anxiety, cognitive deficits, and sleep abnormalities; and detailed biological evidence establishing NLGN2's critical role in GABAergic inhibitory synapse organization and function. While the clinical evidence is currently limited to a small number of cases, the convergence of human genetic, model organism, and mechanistic biological evidence provides strong support for NLGN2 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene. Systematic screening of neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts and functional validation of additional human variants would further strengthen this evidence and enable definition of the full clinical spectrum associated with NLGN2 pathogenic variants.
