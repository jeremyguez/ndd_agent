# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: SF3B1 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

SF3B1 (Splicing Factor 3B Subunit 1) is a core component of the spliceosome that recognizes intronic branch sites and 3' splice sites during pre-mRNA splicing (PMID: 39146933). Mutations in SF3B1 alter splice site recognition and disrupt normal splicing patterns, with established roles in both cancer pathogenesis and, more recently, neurodevelopmental disorders.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of Studies and Patient Count:**
One primary clinical study was identified (PMID: 41577671) reporting 26 patients with de novo SF3B1 variants associated with neurodevelopmental disorders. A secondary study (PMID: 30953002) provided supporting evidence through functional analysis of SF3B1 as an interactor of AUTS2, a known neurodevelopmental disorder gene.

**Most Common Phenotypes Observed:**
The predominant clinical features across patients include:
- Global developmental delay (present in all 26 patients)
- Neurological traits (present in all 26 patients)
- Facial dysmorphic traits (present in all 26 patients)
- Heart anomalies (primarily in missense variant carriers)
- Gastrointestinal anomalies (primarily in missense variant carriers)

**Predominant Variant Type:**
The cohort included both loss-of-function (LoF) variants (n=9) and missense variants (n=17). Notably, missense variants were associated with a more severe and syndromic phenotype compared to LoF variants, suggesting potential gain-of-function or dominant-negative mechanisms. This distinction is clinically important and contrasts with typical neurodevelopmental disorder genes where LoF variants predominate.

**Cohort Information:**
The study does not explicitly identify patients as part of established research cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL), though the de novo variant discovery methodology and functional validation approach are consistent with modern genomic medicine standards.

**Diagnostic Rationale:**
The clinical evidence is strengthened by: (1) de novo variant status in all cases, distinguishing these from inherited or cancer-associated variants; (2) functional complementation assays demonstrating altered splicing patterns; and (3) consistent phenotypic presentation across unrelated individuals.

**Strength of Clinical Evidence: MODERATE**

The evidence is supported by a reasonably sized cohort (26 patients) with consistent phenotypes and de novo variants, but represents a single primary publication. The distinction between LoF and missense variant phenotypes requires validation in larger cohorts. The functional studies provide mechanistic support but are limited to in vitro assays.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms Studied:**
Mouse models were the primary organisms examined, with four relevant studies identified.

**Key Phenotypes from Model Organisms:**

The model organism evidence primarily focuses on cancer-related phenotypes rather than neurodevelopmental outcomes:

- **Transcriptional and chromatin effects** (PMID: 38521065): SF3B1 knock-in mice showed reduced RNA polymerase II elongation rate, decreased RNAPII density at promoters, reduced chromatin accessibility, and disrupted pre-spliceosome assembly. These molecular phenotypes could theoretically impact neurodevelopmental processes but were not explicitly assessed.

- **Pancreatic cancer progression** (PMID: 37823551): SF3B1 knock-in mice with concurrent KRAS and p53 mutations showed increased tumor aggressiveness with aberrant splicing of Map3k7 and altered EMT gene expression.

- **HIF1 signaling in cancer** (PMID: 36001976): Heterozygous SF3B1 knockout mice showed impeded tumor formation and reduced HIF1 pathway activity, demonstrating that SF3B1 haploinsufficiency can suppress certain cancer phenotypes.

- **Lymphoma progression** (PMID: 39083585): While primarily focused on MGA deletion, this study examined SF3B1 in the context of Richter's transformation, showing mitochondrial aberrations and elevated OXPHOS.

**Consistency with Human Phenotype:**
The model organism evidence shows limited direct consistency with the neurodevelopmental phenotypes observed in human patients. No studies explicitly examined neurological development, learning, behavior, or developmental delay in SF3B1-mutant mice. The transcriptional and chromatin accessibility changes observed could theoretically impact neurodevelopment, but this connection remains speculative.

**Strength of Model Organism Evidence: LIMITED**

While mouse models demonstrate that SF3B1 mutations have functional consequences on splicing and transcription, the evidence lacks direct assessment of neurodevelopmental phenotypes. The cancer-focused studies do not address whether heterozygous SF3B1 variants cause developmental abnormalities, lethality, or Mendelian ratio deviations. No data on embryonic lethality, postnatal survival, or neurological function in heterozygous mice were reported.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known Pathways and Molecular Function:**

SF3B1 functions as a critical spliceosome component with multiple well-characterized roles:

1. **Core splicing function** (PMID: 39146933): SF3B1 recognizes intronic branch sites and 3' splice sites, making it essential for pre-mRNA splicing.

2. **Post-translational regulation** (PMID: 36104565): SF3B1 is phosphorylated by cyclin-dependent kinase 11 (CDK11) at threonine residues in its N-terminus during spliceosome activation. This phosphorylation is critical for SF3B1 association with U5 and U6 snRNAs in the activated Bact complex, and CDK11 inhibition prevents spliceosomal transition and causes intron retention.

3. **Pan-cancer splicing alterations** (ASHG 2023 - SoMAS): SF3B1 was identified as a pan-cancer gene whose mutations are significantly associated with differential isoform expression across 33 cancer types (9,738 tumor samples), with both cis- and trans-regulatory effects on splicing and enrichment in cancer cell growth and metastasis pathways.

4. **Protein-protein interactions** (PMID: 30953002): SF3B1 interacts with AUTS2 isoforms, which control neuronal differentiation. Heterozygous mutations in Auts2 in mouse embryonic stem cells showed increased cell death during in vitro corticogenesis and impaired neuronal differentiation, suggesting a functional link between SF3B1 and neurodevelopmental processes.

**Expression Pattern:**
While explicit brain-specific expression data are not provided in the evidence, the functional interaction with AUTS2 (a known neurodevelopmental disorder gene) and the clinical presentation of neurodevelopmental phenotypes in SF3B1-mutant patients suggest relevant expression in developing neural tissues.

**Key Experimental Findings:**

- **Splicing alterations in multiple myeloma** (ASHG 2018): SF3B1 mutations (1.7% of 1,273 MM samples) caused differential splicing in genes including DYNLL1, TMEM14C, CRNDE, BRD4, and BCL2L1, with samples showing increased alternative splicing enriched for high-risk clinical outcomes.

- **APOBEC3B splicing regulation** (ASHG 2019): SF3B1 inhibition by pladienolide B reduced exon 5 skipping of APOBEC3B, demonstrating SF3B1's role in controlling alternative splicing at weak branch point sites.

- **Structural and drug targeting studies** (ASHG 2022): Molecular docking identified 23 small molecule compounds with strong binding affinity to the SF3B1 K700E hotspot variant (binding energies -59.4 to -88.2 kcal/mol), suggesting potential for selective targeting of SF3B1-mutant cells.

- **Genome-wide splicing analysis** (ASHG 2020): SF3B1 mutations result in frequent selective aberrant mRNA splicing events including alternative 3' splice site usage.

**Strength of Biological Evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is comprehensive, demonstrating SF3B1's essential role in spliceosome function, its regulation by CDK11, its interaction with neurodevelopmental genes (AUTS2), and its widespread effects on alternative splicing across multiple cell types and tissues. The molecular mechanisms are well-characterized, and multiple experimental approaches (functional assays, structural studies, genome-wide analyses) converge on SF3B1's critical role in splicing regulation.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall Evidence Level: MODERATE**

**Key Strengths:**

1. **Clinical coherence**: A well-characterized cohort of 26 unrelated patients with de novo SF3B1 variants and consistent neurodevelopmental phenotypes (global developmental delay, neurological traits, facial dysmorphism) provides solid clinical evidence.

2. **Functional validation**: The primary clinical study included functional complementation assays demonstrating altered splicing patterns, strengthening the causal link between variants and phenotype.

3. **Mechanistic plausibility**: Strong biological evidence for SF3B1's role in spliceosome function, combined with demonstrated interaction with AUTS2 (a known neurodevelopmental disorder gene), provides mechanistic support for a neurodevelopmental role.

4. **Variant specificity**: The distinction between LoF and missense variants, with missense variants showing more severe phenotypes, suggests specific molecular mechanisms rather than simple haploinsufficiency.

**Key Gaps and Limitations:**

1. **Limited clinical data**: Only one primary clinical study (26 patients) has been published. Replication in independent cohorts and larger sample sizes are needed to establish SF3B1 as a definitively established neurodevelopmental disorder gene.

2. **Lack of model organism validation**: No mouse studies have directly examined neurodevelopmental phenotypes in SF3B1-mutant animals. The available mouse models focus on cancer biology and do not assess developmental delay, learning, behavior, or neurological function.

3. **Incomplete phenotypic characterization**: The clinical study does not provide detailed information on cognitive testing, neuroimaging findings, or long-term developmental outcomes that would strengthen the neurodevelopmental disorder classification.

4. **Mechanistic gap between cancer and neurodevelopment**: While SF3B1 mutations are well-characterized in cancer, the specific splicing alterations driving neurodevelopmental phenotypes remain unclear. It is unknown whether the same splicing targets (e.g., Map3k7, DYNLL1) are relevant to neurodevelopment.

5. **Genotype-phenotype correlation**: The observation that missense variants cause more severe phenotypes than LoF variants is intriguing but requires mechanistic explanation and validation.

**Conclusion:**

SF3B1 emerges as a promising candidate neurodevelopmental disorder gene based on a coherent clinical presentation in 26 de novo variant carriers and strong biological evidence for its role in spliceosome function and interaction with known neurodevelopmental genes. However, the evidence remains at a MODERATE level due to limited clinical replication, absence of direct neurodevelopmental phenotyping in model organisms, and incomplete mechanistic understanding of how SF3B1 mutations specifically disrupt neural development. Additional studies including independent patient cohorts, detailed neuroimaging and cognitive assessments, and targeted mouse models examining neurodevelopmental outcomes are needed to establish SF3B1 as a definitively established neurodevelopmental disorder gene.
