# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: AP2S1 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

AP2S1 encodes the adaptor protein-2 sigma subunit, a critical component of the heterotetrameric AP2 complex that mediates clathrin-dependent endocytosis. The AP2 complex is essential for cellular internalization of membrane proteins and has roles in calcium homeostasis, synaptic function, and developmental patterning.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports:** 2 case reports

**Total patients:** 2 patients (both with the same p.Arg15Leu variant)

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Hypercalcemia and hypocalciuria (100% of cases)
- Elevated parathyroid hormone (1/2 cases reported)
- Reduced/low bone mineral density (2/2 cases)
- Neurodevelopmental difficulties/developmental disorder (2/2 cases)
- Familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia type 3 (FHH3) diagnosis (2/2 cases)

**Predominant variant type:** Missense mutation (p.Arg15Leu, c.44G>T)

**Cohort source:** Neither case was from a known research cohort (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL). One case was from Japan; the other center/country was not specified.

**Diagnostic context:** Both cases were diagnosed through genetic testing after exclusion of CASR mutations and primary hyperparathyroidism. One case was noted as de novo; the other's inheritance pattern was not explicitly stated. The p.Arg15 residue appears to be a recurrent mutation site, as all affected FHH3 patients in the literature harbor heterozygous mutations at this position (PMID: 39949382, PMID: 41501259).

**Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED**

The clinical evidence is limited to two case reports of the same recurrent variant. While both cases demonstrate consistent phenotypes including neurodevelopmental difficulties, the sample size is very small and lacks systematic characterization of the neurodevelopmental phenotype. The broader FHH3 literature (PMID: 26963950, PMID: 27913609) identifies AP2S1 mutations in approximately 6% of CASR-negative FHH patients across 33 cases and 19 families, but these studies do not systematically characterize neurodevelopmental outcomes.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (2 studies) and zebrafish (3 studies)

**Mouse knockout phenotypes:**

In heterozygous mice carrying an Ap2s1 knock-in mutation, one study (PMID: 29479578) reported normal appearance, growth, and normal plasma calcium, phosphate, magnesium, and parathyroid hormone levels—findings that do not recapitulate the human hypercalcemia phenotype. However, a second mouse study (PMID: 33729479) reported that heterozygous Ap2s1 knock-in mice exhibited marked hypercalcemia, hypermagnesemia, hypophosphatemia, and increased fibroblast growth factor-23, with impaired AP2σ-calcium-sensing receptor (CaSR) protein-protein interaction. Homozygous Ap2s1 mutations are perinatal lethal (PMID: 33729479) or embryonic lethal at E3.5-E9.5 (PMID: 29479578), with the latter study documenting defects in cardiac, thyroid, hepatic, gastrointestinal, pulmonary, pancreatic, and neural development.

**Zebrafish knockdown/knockout phenotypes:**

Three zebrafish studies identified roles for AP2 complex components in behavioral plasticity and sensorimotor decision-making. Knockdown of AP2 components impaired acoustically evoked habituation learning and altered escape behavior choice (PMID: 38550987, PMID: 36972301). A CaSR knockout in zebrafish (PMID: 29681477) showed deficits in sensorimotor choice modulation and altered clathrin-mediated endocytosis signaling, suggesting AP2 involvement in CaSR-dependent behavioral processes.

**Consistency with human phenotype:**

The mouse models show inconsistent results regarding calcium homeostasis, with one study showing normal calcium levels in heterozygotes and another showing hypercalcemia. Neither mouse study explicitly characterized neurodevelopmental or behavioral phenotypes in heterozygotes. The zebrafish studies demonstrate that AP2 complex dysfunction impairs learning and behavioral plasticity, which could be relevant to the neurodevelopmental difficulties observed in human patients, but direct evidence linking AP2S1 mutations to neurodevelopmental phenotypes in model organisms is lacking.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**

The mouse models establish that homozygous Ap2s1 mutations are embryonic lethal with widespread developmental defects, supporting a critical developmental role. However, heterozygous mouse phenotypes are inconsistently characterized across studies, and neurodevelopmental phenotypes have not been systematically evaluated in heterozygous mice. Zebrafish studies implicate AP2 in learning and behavioral processes but do not directly test AP2S1-specific mutations.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

AP2S1 is a core component of the clathrin-mediated endocytosis machinery. The AP2 complex (comprising AP2α, AP2β, AP2μ, and AP2σ subunits) is essential for clathrin-coated pit formation and internalization of membrane receptors (PMID: 29479578). In the context of FHH3, AP2S1 mutations impair the interaction between the calcium-sensing receptor (CaSR) and the AP2 complex, disrupting normal calcium homeostasis (PMID: 33729479). AP2S1 also interacts with GPN3 to regulate EGFR endocytosis (PMID: 39893205) and with RNF41 to modulate leptin and LIF receptor signaling (PMID: 29560723).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**

AP2S1 is expressed in synaptosomes and is identified as a hub protein in the synaptic vesicle cycle pathway. In a rat model of vascular dementia, AP2S1 expression was significantly downregulated in synaptosomes compared to controls (PMID: 38990251), suggesting a role in synaptic function and cognitive processes. The embryonic lethality and widespread developmental defects in homozygous Ap2s1 knockout mice (PMID: 29479578) indicate critical roles in neural development and organogenesis.

**Key experimental findings:**

- AP2S1 mutations at codon 15 (p.Arg15Leu and p.Arg15His) are recurrent in FHH3 families (PMID: 27913609)
- AP2S1 mutations affect calcium homeostasis through impaired CaSR-AP2 interaction (PMID: 33729479)
- AP2S1 is involved in clathrin-mediated endocytosis of multiple receptor types (PMID: 39893205, PMID: 29560723)
- AP2S1 is a component of synaptic vesicle machinery with potential relevance to cognitive function (PMID: 38990251)
- AP2S1 cooperates with HAVCR1 in viral entry pathways (PMID: 40156834)

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence clearly establishes AP2S1 as a critical component of clathrin-mediated endocytosis with multiple protein interactions and roles in calcium homeostasis, synaptic function, and developmental processes. The molecular mechanism linking AP2S1 mutations to FHH3 is well-characterized. However, the specific molecular mechanisms linking AP2S1 mutations to neurodevelopmental phenotypes remain incompletely understood.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths:**
1. Recurrent heterozygous missense mutations (p.Arg15Leu) identified in multiple independent FHH3 patients with consistent phenotypes including neurodevelopmental difficulties
2. Well-characterized molecular function in clathrin-mediated endocytosis and calcium homeostasis with clear mechanistic links to disease
3. Homozygous lethality in model organisms with widespread developmental defects, supporting a critical developmental role
4. AP2S1 expression in synaptic vesicle machinery and involvement in behavioral plasticity in zebrafish models
5. AP2S1 included in first-line genetic screening panels for familial hyperparathyroidism (PMID: 39818301)

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. Only 2 case reports of AP2S1-associated neurodevelopmental disorder; limited characterization of the neurodevelopmental phenotype
2. Inconsistent heterozygous mouse phenotypes across studies; lack of systematic neurodevelopmental assessment in heterozygous mice
3. No direct evidence that AP2S1 mutations cause neurodevelopmental disorder independent of calcium homeostasis abnormalities
4. Zebrafish studies implicate AP2 in learning but do not test AP2S1-specific mutations
5. Unclear whether neurodevelopmental difficulties are primary effects of AP2S1 mutations or secondary to chronic hypercalcemia

**Conclusion:**

AP2S1 heterozygous missense mutations, particularly p.Arg15Leu, are established as a cause of familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia type 3 with consistent clinical presentation including neurodevelopmental difficulties. However, the evidence that AP2S1 mutations cause neurodevelopmental disorder as a primary phenotype is limited to two case reports. The strong biological evidence for AP2S1's role in synaptic function and developmental processes, combined with embryonic lethality in homozygous mice, suggests plausible mechanisms for neurodevelopmental effects, but systematic characterization of the neurodevelopmental phenotype and its relationship to calcium homeostasis abnormalities is needed to fully establish AP2S1 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene.
