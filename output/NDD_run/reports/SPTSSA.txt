# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: SPTSSA AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

SPTSSA (serine palmitoyltransferase small subunit A) is a component of the human serine palmitoyltransferase (SPT) complex that catalyzes the first committed step of sphingolipid biosynthesis. SPTSSA participates in acyl-CoA coordination within the SPT complex and is subject to negative feedback regulation by ORMDL proteins, which modulate enzyme activity and substrate selectivity (PMID: 33558762).

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Case Reports and Patient Numbers:**
One independent case report was identified describing a single patient with a heterozygous SPTSSA variant (PMID: 40533086). This report explicitly notes it is the "fourth patient with spastic paraplegia 90," indicating that at least 4 patients total have been reported in the literature, though only 1 is detailed in the extracted evidence. Additional patients (at least 3 unrelated individuals) are referenced in the biological evidence literature (PMID: 36718090, ASHG 2022).

**Most Common Phenotypes Observed:**
The clinical presentation centers on hereditary spastic paraplegia with complex multi-system involvement:
- Neurological: global developmental delay, inability to walk, axial hypotonia, extremity spasticity, dystonia
- Renal/urological: distal renal tubular acidosis, recurrent urinary tract infections, nephrolithiasis, neurogenic bladder, primary polydipsia

**Predominant Variant Type:**
Missense variants predominate in reported cases. The recurrent variant p.Thr51Ile has been identified in at least two patients (PMID: 40533086), with structural evidence showing that Thr51 resides near the ORMDL3 binding interface (ASHG 2022 - SPTSSA variants alter sphingolipid synthesis). A homozygous frameshift variant (p.Gln58AlafsTer10) has also been reported (ASHG 2022), indicating that loss-of-function variants can cause disease, though the heterozygous missense variants appear more common in reported cases.

**Cohort Information:**
No patients are identified as being from established diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL). Cases appear to be identified through exome sequencing in clinical diagnostic settings.

**Strength of Clinical Evidence: LIMITED**

While the clinical phenotype is well-characterized and the variant segregation pattern is consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance, the total number of reported patients is small (approximately 4 documented cases across all literature). The recurrence of the p.Thr51Ile variant in multiple unrelated patients strengthens the evidence for pathogenicity, but the limited patient numbers restrict the ability to define the full phenotypic spectrum or establish robust genotype-phenotype correlations.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms Studied:**
Drosophila melanogaster has been used to model SPTSSA pathogenic variants.

**Key Phenotypes from Model Studies:**
In Drosophila, overexpression of wild-type human SPT caused severe motor defects and shortened lifespan. Critically, co-expression of ORMDL3 rescued the phenotype of wild-type SPT but failed to rescue mutant SPT, demonstrating that pathogenic SPTSSA variants impair the normal negative feedback regulation by ORMDL proteins (PMID: 36718090, ASHG 2022). This finding directly links the molecular defect (impaired ORMDL-mediated feedback inhibition) to the neurological phenotype (motor dysfunction).

**Consistency with Human Phenotype:**
The motor defects observed in Drosophila expressing mutant human SPT are consistent with the human spastic paraplegia phenotype, supporting a causal relationship between impaired sphingolipid regulation and neurological dysfunction.

**Strength of Model Organism Evidence: MODERATE**

Drosophila studies provide functional validation that pathogenic variants impair ORMDL regulation and cause neurological phenotypes. However, only one model organism has been systematically studied, and no knockout or knockdown studies in mammalian systems (mice) have been reported in the extracted evidence. The Drosophila data strongly support the pathogenic mechanism but do not fully recapitulate the complex multi-system phenotype (particularly the renal manifestations) observed in human patients.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known Pathways and Molecular Function:**
SPTSSA functions as a core component of the serine palmitoyltransferase complex, catalyzing the condensation of serine and palmitoyl-CoA to form 3-ketosphinganine, the first committed step in sphingolipid biosynthesis. The complex is subject to negative feedback inhibition by ORMDL proteins, which bind near the Thr51 residue and suppress SPT activity when sphingolipid levels are elevated (PMID: 33558762, ASHG 2022).

**Pathogenic Mechanism:**
Pathogenic SPTSSA variants (both missense and frameshift) impair the ability of ORMDL proteins to suppress SPT activity, leading to excessive and unregulated sphingolipid synthesis (PMID: 36718090). This mechanism has been confirmed in patient fibroblasts and validated in Drosophila models.

**Expression Pattern:**
SPTSSA is broadly expressed, with documented expression in:
- Respiratory epithelial cells (decreased during human rhinovirus infection) (PMID: 34851798)
- Nervous system tissues (implied by neurological phenotype)
- Multiple cancer types (gastric cancer, glioblastoma) where it is often upregulated

The gene is regulated by the aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR), which binds the SPTSSA promoter and activates transcription (PMID: 32029474).

**Key Experimental Findings:**
- Cryo-EM structures reveal SPTSSA's precise role in acyl-CoA coordination and ORMDL3 interaction (PMID: 33558762)
- Patient fibroblasts carrying pathogenic variants show altered sphingolipid profiles consistent with impaired ORMDL regulation (PMID: 36718090)
- Drosophila models demonstrate that mutant SPT causes motor dysfunction that cannot be rescued by ORMDL3 co-expression (PMID: 36718090, ASHG 2022)
- SPTSSA is a component of sphingolipid metabolism networks implicated in inflammatory and immune responses (PMID: 31512789)

**Strength of Biological Evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is robust, with structural data, biochemical validation, and functional studies in both patient cells and model organisms. The molecular mechanism linking SPTSSA variants to excessive sphingolipid synthesis is well-established. However, the specific mechanisms by which dysregulated sphingolipid synthesis causes the observed neurological and renal phenotypes remain incompletely understood.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall Evidence Level: MODERATE**

**Key Strengths:**
1. **Recurrent pathogenic variant**: The p.Thr51Ile variant has been identified in at least two unrelated patients, providing strong evidence for pathogenicity independent of segregation analysis.
2. **Robust molecular mechanism**: Structural and biochemical evidence clearly demonstrates how pathogenic variants impair ORMDL-mediated feedback inhibition of SPT, leading to excessive sphingolipid synthesis.
3. **Functional validation**: Drosophila models confirm that pathogenic variants cause neurological dysfunction through impaired ORMDL regulation.
4. **Consistent clinical phenotype**: All reported patients present with spastic paraplegia and related neurological features, suggesting a coherent disease entity.

**Key Limitations:**
1. **Small patient cohort**: Only approximately 4 patients have been reported, limiting statistical power and phenotypic characterization.
2. **Limited model organism diversity**: Only Drosophila has been systematically studied; mammalian knockout or knockdown models are absent from the extracted evidence.
3. **Incomplete mechanistic understanding**: While the molecular defect is clear, the pathophysiological mechanisms linking excessive sphingolipid synthesis to the specific neurological and renal manifestations remain unclear.
4. **Lack of cohort-based studies**: No large-scale screening studies in established diagnostic cohorts have been reported, limiting assessment of prevalence and phenotypic spectrum.

**Conclusion:**
SPTSSA is established as a gene causing autosomal dominant hereditary spastic paraplegia through heterozygous missense variants that impair ORMDL-mediated negative feedback regulation of serine palmitoyltransferase. The molecular mechanism is well-characterized through structural and functional studies, and the clinical phenotype is consistent across reported cases. However, the limited number of patients and absence of mammalian model systems restrict the evidence to a MODERATE level. Additional patient reports and expanded functional studies would strengthen the evidence base and clarify the full phenotypic spectrum and genotype-phenotype correlations.
