# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: REM1 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

REM1 is a member of the RGK (Rad-Gem/Kir-related) GTPase family, an atypical branch of the Ras superfamily with distinct biochemical properties including micromolar-range nucleotide binding affinities and higher intrinsic GTPase activity compared to canonical Ras proteins (PMID: 17107948). REM1 has been characterized with multiple distinct functions across different biological contexts: as a meiosis-specific cyclin in fission yeast, as a cardiac-enriched regulator of voltage-gated calcium channel trafficking, and as a phosphoprotein involved in plant cell wall stress responses.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of case reports/series:** 0

**Total patients:** 0

**Most common phenotypes:** Not applicable

**Predominant variant type:** Not applicable

**Patient cohort origin:** Not applicable

**Strength of clinical evidence: NONE**

No clinical case reports, case series, or patient cohorts with REM1 pathogenic variants were identified in the literature. There is no evidence of REM1 involvement in human neurodevelopmental disorders or any other human disease phenotype in the available evidence base.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Saccharomyces cerevisiae (baker's yeast) and Schizosaccharomyces pombe (fission yeast)

**Key phenotypes from knockouts/mutations:**

In S. cerevisiae, rem1 mutations (identified as alleles of RAD3) confer high levels of spontaneous mitotic gene conversion, crossing over, and mutation, with distinct genetic interactions in recombination-repair pathways (PMID: 2840336). Double mutants of rem1 with recombination-repair defects are inviable, suggesting rem1 functions in a recombination-repair pathway.

In S. pombe, rem1 deletion causes decreased intragenic meiotic recombination and delayed onset of meiosis I (PMID: 18815595). Rem1-deficient cells show impaired meiotic recombination and require Rem1 for premeiotic DNA synthesis when Cig2 is absent (PMID: 16024772). Notably, ectopic expression of Rem1 in mitotic cells induces G1 arrest and mitotic catastrophes (PMID: 18815595), indicating that dysregulation of Rem1 levels has severe cellular consequences.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** Not assessable—no human phenotype has been documented.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**

While yeast studies demonstrate that REM1 has important functions in cell cycle regulation and meiotic recombination, these findings are limited to lower eukaryotes and do not directly inform potential neurodevelopmental functions in mammals. The severe cellular consequences of Rem1 dysregulation (mitotic catastrophe, meiotic defects) suggest the protein is functionally important, but no neurological phenotypes have been characterized in model organisms.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

REM1 functions in multiple distinct biological contexts:

1. **Meiotic cell cycle regulation (yeast):** REM1 is a B-type cyclin required for proper meiotic progression, with splicing regulation controlled by forkhead transcription factors Mei4 (positive regulator) and Fkh2 (negative regulator) (PMID: 18815595, PMID: 16024772). Splicing regulation occurs via inhibition in mitotic cells rather than positive activation (PMID: 15893732).

2. **Cardiac calcium channel trafficking (mammals):** REM1 is strongly and specifically expressed in cardiac tissue and functions as an inhibitor of voltage-gated L-type calcium channels. Phosphorylation of Rem1 at Ser18 by protein kinase D1 (downstream of α1-adrenergic signaling) relieves this inhibition, increasing channel activity and plasma membrane expression in cardiomyocytes (PMID: 22076634).

3. **Plant cell wall stress response:** REM1.3 in Arabidopsis is a phosphoprotein with rapid oligo-galacturonide-induced phosphorylation changes, and null mutations enhance a dominant WAK allele, indicating a role in cell wall stress response pathways (PMID: 26811356).

**Expression pattern:**

REM1 expression is highly tissue-specific and context-dependent. In mammals, REM1 is strongly expressed in cardiac tissue (PMID: 22076634). In plants, REM1 represents a cis-acting regulatory element associated with root-specific gene expression (PMID: 40004183). No data on REM1 expression in the developing or adult mammalian brain were identified.

**Key experimental findings:**

- REM1 is a member of an atypical GTPase family with distinct biochemical properties (micromolar Kd, high intrinsic GTPase activity) (PMID: 17107948)
- REM1 splicing is tightly regulated in a cell-cycle-dependent manner in yeast
- REM1 function is dosage-sensitive, with both loss and ectopic expression causing cellular dysfunction
- REM1 is subject to post-translational modification (phosphorylation) that modulates its inhibitory function on ion channels

**Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE**

While REM1 has well-characterized molecular functions in yeast and cardiac tissue, there is no evidence of expression or function in the nervous system. The biological evidence demonstrates that REM1 is a functionally important, dosage-sensitive protein, but this does not establish relevance to neurodevelopmental disorders.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
- REM1 is a functionally important, dosage-sensitive protein with well-characterized roles in cell cycle regulation and ion channel trafficking
- Model organism studies demonstrate that REM1 dysregulation causes severe cellular phenotypes
- REM1 is subject to post-translational regulation, consistent with a protein that could be affected by missense variants

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- Complete absence of clinical evidence: no human cases, no disease associations, no variant reports
- No evidence of REM1 expression or function in the mammalian nervous system
- No neurological phenotypes characterized in any model organism
- The characterized functions (meiotic cyclins in yeast, cardiac calcium channel regulation) do not obviously connect to neurodevelopmental pathways
- No functional studies of potential pathogenic variants

**Conclusion:**

There is currently insufficient evidence to support REM1 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene. While REM1 is a functionally important protein with well-characterized roles in cell cycle regulation and cardiac physiology, there are no clinical cases, no documented brain expression, and no neurological phenotypes in model organisms. To establish REM1 as a disease gene, clinical evidence from affected individuals with REM1 variants would be required, along with functional studies demonstrating how specific variants impair REM1 function and how this dysfunction leads to neurodevelopmental pathology.
