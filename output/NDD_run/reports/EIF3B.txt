# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: EIF3B AND NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

EIF3B (eukaryotic initiation factor 3 subunit B) is a core subunit of the eIF3 complex, which plays a critical role in mRNA binding to the 40S ribosomal subunit during translation initiation. The gene is highly constrained against loss of function, indicating its essential role in cellular protein synthesis and development.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of Studies and Patients:**
Two independent clinical studies were identified. The primary evidence comes from an international matchmaking effort describing 14 patients with de novo loss-of-function variants in EIF3B (PMID: 41033306). A secondary study reports 1 additional patient with a de novo interstitial deletion on chromosome 7p22 that may encompass EIF3B regulatory elements (PMID: 21671376), bringing the total to approximately 15 patients across studies.

**Most Common Phenotypes:**
The cardinal features of EIF3B loss-of-function disorder include:
- Cardiac defects (tetralogy of Fallot, thin heart tubes in models)
- Craniofacial dysmorphisms and abnormalities (bilateral branchial arch remnants, lack of craniofacial cartilage in models)
- Neurodevelopmental delays (mild developmental delays reported)
- Behavioral abnormalities
- Embryonic lethality in animal models

**Variant Type:**
Predominantly loss-of-function variants (de novo deletions and likely truncating mutations). The specific variants are not detailed in available abstracts, but the diagnostic rationale emphasizes loss-of-function annotation and the genes' high constraint against LoF variation.

**Cohort Source:**
Patients were identified through international matchmaking efforts across multiple centers; no indication of enrollment in formal cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL).

**Strength of Clinical Evidence: MODERATE**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) multiple independent de novo variants in unrelated patients, (2) consistent phenotypic pattern across cases, (3) functional validation in zebrafish models, and (4) localization to a minimally deleted region previously implicated in cardiac malformations. However, the evidence is limited by: (1) small sample size (14-15 patients), (2) lack of detailed variant information in available abstracts, and (3) absence of formal cohort enrollment or systematic ascertainment.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms Studied:**
Zebrafish and mouse models have been employed, though with different experimental contexts.

**Key Phenotypes from Loss-of-Function Models:**

*Zebrafish (developmental context):*
Knockdown/mutation of eif3b in zebrafish results in:
- Thin heart tubes
- Lack of craniofacial cartilage
- Developmental abnormalities
- Embryonic lethality (PMID: 41033306)

These phenotypes directly parallel the human clinical presentation of cardiac and craniofacial defects, providing strong functional validation of the human disease mechanism.

*Mouse (cancer-focused context):*
Multiple mouse knockdown studies demonstrate EIF3B's role in tumor progression across pancreatic, breast, prostate, and hematologic malignancies (PMID: 33459066, 37293180, 32556998, 32236758, 40660438). While these studies confirm EIF3B's biological importance, they do not directly address neurodevelopmental or cardiac phenotypes relevant to the human disorder.

**Consistency with Human Phenotype:**
The zebrafish model shows excellent concordance with human clinical features (cardiac and craniofacial defects). The embryonic lethality in zebrafish is consistent with the severe developmental impact observed in human patients. Mouse cancer models are not directly relevant to the neurodevelopmental disorder phenotype.

**Strength of Model Organism Evidence: MODERATE**

Zebrafish data provide strong functional support for the human phenotype with clear developmental relevance. However, the evidence is limited by: (1) lack of complete knockout mouse data addressing developmental phenotypes, (2) absence of detailed Mendelian ratio analysis or sample size reporting in zebrafish studies, and (3) reliance on knockdown rather than complete knockout approaches.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known Pathways and Molecular Function:**

EIF3B functions as a core component of the eIF3 translation initiation complex and also participates in multiple cancer-associated signaling pathways:

- **Translation initiation:** EIF3B is essential for mRNA binding to the 40S ribosomal subunit (PMID: 41033306)
- **Protein stabilization:** EIF3B stabilizes multiple oncogenic proteins including MAP2K2 (via inhibition of VHL-mediated ubiquitination), PTGS2 (via counteracting MDM2-mediated ubiquitination), and FOXA1/PUS1 axis components (PMID: 40691141, 36050601, 39247811)
- **Translation enhancement:** EIF3B is recruited by YTHDF2 to facilitate translation of ETV5 mRNA in hepatocellular carcinoma (PMID: 38247171)
- **Signaling pathway activation:** EIF3B promotes ERK and AKT signaling cascades (PMID: 40691141, 31481019)
- **Autophagy regulation:** eIF3b acts as an upstream regulator of autophagy in drug-resistant breast cancer (PMID: 41089706)

**Expression Pattern:**

EIF3B shows broad expression with particular relevance to:
- Developmental tissues (cardiac and craniofacial primordia, based on zebrafish phenotypes)
- Inflammatory tissues: EIF3B is differentially expressed in rheumatoid arthritis and osteoarthritis synovial membranes, with higher expression in RA (p<0.01) and diagnostic AUC of 0.795-0.958 (PMID: 40592131)
- Multiple cancer types (pancreatic, breast, prostate, cervical, laryngeal, esophageal, melanoma, hepatocellular carcinoma, colorectal cancer)

**Key Experimental Findings:**

The biological evidence demonstrates that EIF3B loss impairs fundamental cellular processes:
- Zebrafish eif3b mutations cause developmental arrest with specific defects in cardiac and craniofacial development (PMID: 41033306)
- EIF3B is essential for proper translation initiation, a process critical during rapid cell proliferation in development
- EIF3B participates in protein stabilization networks that regulate developmental signaling pathways
- Downregulation of EIF3B in inflammatory disease models normalizes cellular proliferation and reduces proinflammatory responses (PMID: 37865179)

**Strength of Biological Evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is comprehensive, demonstrating: (1) EIF3B's essential role in translation initiation, (2) multiple protein-protein interactions with developmental and signaling relevance, (3) consistent functional effects across diverse cellular contexts, and (4) direct correlation between EIF3B dysfunction and developmental abnormalities in model organisms. The evidence is limited by: (1) most functional studies focus on cancer contexts rather than developmental biology, and (2) limited direct investigation of EIF3B's role in cardiac and craniofacial development outside of zebrafish models.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall Evidence Level: MODERATE**

**Key Strengths:**
1. **Functional validation in developmental model:** Zebrafish eif3b loss-of-function directly recapitulates human cardiac and craniofacial phenotypes, providing strong mechanistic support
2. **Consistent clinical presentation:** Multiple independent patients with de novo LoF variants show a recognizable, reproducible phenotypic pattern
3. **Gene constraint:** EIF3B is highly constrained against loss of function, consistent with its essential developmental role
4. **Biological plausibility:** EIF3B's role in translation initiation and protein stabilization provides clear mechanistic explanation for developmental defects
5. **Chromosomal context:** Localization to a minimally deleted region previously associated with cardiac malformations (7p22.3) provides independent supporting evidence

**Key Gaps and Limitations:**
1. **Small sample size:** Only 14-15 patients identified; larger cohorts would strengthen evidence
2. **Limited variant detail:** Specific mutations not fully characterized in available abstracts
3. **Incomplete mouse developmental data:** No complete knockout mouse studies addressing neurodevelopmental phenotypes; most mouse work focuses on cancer biology
4. **Lack of cohort enrollment:** Patients identified through ad hoc international matching rather than systematic ascertainment
5. **Limited mechanistic detail:** Specific developmental pathways disrupted by EIF3B loss remain incompletely characterized

**Conclusion:**

EIF3B loss-of-function variants cause a distinct autosomal-dominant neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by cardiac defects, craniofacial dysmorphisms, developmental delays, and behavioral abnormalities. The evidence is supported by multiple independent de novo variants in unrelated patients, direct functional validation in zebrafish developmental models, and strong biological plausibility based on EIF3B's essential role in translation initiation. While the clinical sample size is modest and additional functional studies in mammalian developmental models would strengthen the evidence, the convergence of clinical, model organism, and biological data provides moderate-to-strong support for EIF3B as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene.

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. A cardiovascular, craniofacial, and neurodevelopmental disorder caused by loss-of-function variants in the eIF3 complex component genes EIF3A and EIF3B. (PMID: 41033306)
2. Delineation of a less than 200 kb minimal deleted region for cardiac malformations on chromosome 7p22. (PMID: 21671376)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. EIF3B promotes cancer progression in pancreatic cancer (PMID: 33459066)
2. Eukaryotic initiation factor 3b regulates the development and progression of breast cancer (PMID: 37293180)
3. Eukaryotic translation initiation factor 3 subunit b is a novel oncogenic factor in prostate cancer (PMID: 32556998)
4. eIF3b regulates the cell proliferation and apoptosis processes in chronic myelogenous leukemia cell lines via regulating the expression of C3G (PMID: 32236758)
5. CD133(+)-Derived Exosomes Carrying EIF3B Mediate Cell Metastasis and Stemness in Colorectal Cancer (PMID: 40660438)
6. Eukaryotic Initiation Factor 4E (eIF4E) as a Target of Anti-Psoriatic Treatment (PMID: 37865179)

### Biological Evidence
1. EIF3B stabilizes MAP2K2 to activate the ERK pathway and promote the progression of laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma (PMID: 40691141)
2. EIF3B stabilizes PTGS2 expression by counteracting MDM2-mediated ubiquitination to promote the development and progression of malignant melanoma (PMID: 36050601)
3. FOXA1-dependent PUS1 regulates EIF3b stability in a non-enzymatic pathway mediating prostate cancer bone metastasis (PMID: 39247811)
4. YTHDF2 Is a Therapeutic Target for HCC by Suppressing Immune Evasion and Angiogenesis Through ETV5/PD-L1/VEGFA Axis (PMID: 38247171)
5. TEX9 and eIF3b functionally synergize to promote the progression of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (PMID: 31481019)
6. eIF3b-driven autophagy and Wnt/β-catenin crosstalk: a novel regulatory axis in adriamycin resistance of breast cancer (PMID: 41089706)
7. Shared molecular biomarkers and therapeutic targets in rheumatoid arthritis and osteoarthritis: Focus on EIF3B, KHSRP, NCL, PDCD1LG2, and SLC25A37 (PMID: 40592131)
