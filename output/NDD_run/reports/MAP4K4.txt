# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: MAP4K4 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

MAP4K4 (Mitogen-Activated Protein Kinase Kinase Kinase Kinase 4) is a serine/threonine kinase of the Ste20p family that functions as a key integrator of extracellular signaling cues. MAP4K4 plays critical roles in multiple pathways including JNK/MAPK signaling, Hippo pathway regulation, and RAS signaling control, with particular importance in early embryonic development and neurodevelopment.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent studies:** Four independent case reports/case series were identified.

**Total patient count:** Approximately 31 patients across all studies (21 patients from PMID: 37126546; 2 patients from PMID: 36469137; 1 patient from PMID: 38679877; 7 patients from ASHG 2018 abstract).

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Neurodevelopmental disorders (present in all four studies)
- Developmental delay/neurologic dysfunction (multiple studies)
- Congenital heart defects (cardiac anomalies, septal defects, valve insufficiency)
- Craniofacial dysmorphism and craniosynostosis
- Multiple congenital anomalies

**Predominant variant type:** Loss-of-function variants predominate (frameshift variants, truncating variants, and variants predicted to cause nonsense-mediated decay). However, missense variants in functionally critical domains (protein kinase domain hotspot, highly conserved regions) are also reported as pathogenic (PMID: 38679877; ASHG 2018).

**Cohort source:** No patients were explicitly identified as from established research cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL). The Korean case (PMID: 38679877) was identified through trio exome sequencing at a single center.

**Variant characteristics:** All reported variants are de novo, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. MAP4K4 shows extreme intolerance to missense variation (Z score 4.01) and maximum pLI score of 1.0, indicating strong constraint against loss-of-function variation in the general population (ASHG 2018).

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) multiple independent case series with consistent phenotypes; (2) de novo variants in all cases; (3) functional validation in model organisms; (4) extreme gene constraint metrics. However, the evidence is limited by: (1) relatively small total patient numbers; (2) lack of large cohort studies; (3) limited detailed genotype-phenotype correlation; (4) absence of replication in established disease cohorts.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Zebrafish and mouse models have been extensively characterized.

**Key neurological and developmental phenotypes:**

*Zebrafish:* Knockdown of Map4k4 causes developmental defects, congenital anomalies, and neurodevelopmental differences, with impaired RAS signaling regulation (PMID: 37126546). These findings directly support the human neurodevelopmental phenotype.

*Mouse:* Multiple knockout studies reveal critical developmental roles:
- Embryonic lethality with impaired somite development (PMID: 37223681)
- Neural tube malformation and homozygous lethality (PMID: 37290629)
- Vulnerability of photoreceptor neurites and imbalance in neurogenesis-related factors (PMID: 37290629)
- Impaired retinal neuron regeneration from Müller glia with loss of YAP-dependent reprogramming (PMID: 37443319)
- Neutropenia with impeded neutrophil differentiation (PMID: 40096134)

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The zebrafish knockdown phenotype (developmental defects, neurodevelopmental differences, RAS signaling dysregulation) directly parallels the human clinical presentation. Mouse embryonic lethality with neural tube malformation is consistent with the severe neurodevelopmental phenotype observed in human patients. The finding that MAP4K4 negatively regulates RAS signaling in early embryos (PMID: 37126546) aligns with the RASopathy-like features (cardiac anomalies, craniofacial dysmorphism) observed in some patients (PMID: 38679877; ASHG 2018).

**Mendelian ratio deviations:** Mouse full knockouts show embryonic lethality (PMID: 37223681; PMID: 37290629), indicating that complete loss of MAP4K4 is incompatible with development. This is consistent with the observation that heterozygous variants cause disease in humans, suggesting haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative effects.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) consistent phenotypes across multiple model systems; (2) direct demonstration of developmental and neurological defects; (3) mechanistic insights into RAS and Hippo pathway regulation; (4) embryonic lethality in homozygous knockouts supporting the severity of complete loss. Limitations include: (1) lack of heterozygous mouse models specifically designed to recapitulate human variants; (2) limited characterization of specific neurodevelopmental outcomes beyond embryonic lethality.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

MAP4K4 functions as a critical hub in multiple signaling pathways:

1. **RAS/MAPK pathway:** MAP4K4 negatively regulates RAS signaling in early embryos (PMID: 37126546), which is essential for proper embryonic development. Dysregulation of this pathway explains the RASopathy-like features (cardiac anomalies, craniofacial dysmorphism) observed in patients.

2. **Hippo pathway:** MAP4K4 is a direct effector of RAP2 GTPase in mechanotransduction, stimulating LATS1/LATS2 and inhibiting YAP/TAZ in response to extracellular matrix stiffness (PMID: 30135582). Loss of MAP4K4 impairs Hippo pathway responses and reduces LATS1/2 activation (PMID: 38574891).

3. **JNK/MAPK signaling:** MAP4K4 activates JNK and MLK3 pathways, controlling proliferation, embryonic development, and apoptosis (ASHG 2018; PMID: 37223681). This pathway is critical for neuronal development and cell fate decisions.

4. **Immune and inflammatory pathways:** MAP4K4 regulates T cell activation, neutrophil differentiation, and IL-6 production, with roles in both pro-inflammatory and regulatory immune responses (PMID: 26791862; PMID: 32220977; PMID: 40096134).

**Expression pattern:** MAP4K4 is broadly expressed with particular relevance to neurodevelopment:
- Expressed in retinal endothelial cells and photoreceptor neurons (PMID: 37290629; PMID: 40072537)
- Upregulated in neural tissues during development
- Critical for proper neural tube formation and neurogenesis

**Key experimental findings:**

- Loss-of-function and dominant-negative variants impair RAS signaling regulation in early embryos, causing developmental defects (PMID: 37126546)
- De novo frameshift variants cause nonsense-mediated decay and deleterious effects on mRNA splicing and stability (PMID: 36469137)
- Missense variants in the protein kinase domain hotspot (e.g., p.Gly190Val) are de novo and located in functionally critical regions (PMID: 38679877)
- MAP4K4 inhibition via integrin α5β1 pathway may be therapeutically targetable (ASHG 2018)
- MAP4K4 regulates cytoskeleton dynamics and actin remodeling, critical for neuronal morphogenesis and development

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) well-characterized molecular pathways with clear relevance to neurodevelopment; (2) mechanistic understanding of how loss-of-function variants cause disease; (3) identification of specific functional domains critical for pathogenicity; (4) demonstration of pathway dysregulation in both model organisms and human cells. Limitations include: (1) limited direct evidence of pathway dysregulation in patient-derived cells; (2) incomplete understanding of how specific variants (particularly missense variants) alter protein function; (3) need for more detailed characterization of MAP4K4 expression during critical windows of human neurodevelopment.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE-TO-STRONG**

**Key strengths of the evidence:**

1. **Consistent clinical phenotype:** Multiple independent case series (PMID: 37126546, 36469137, 38679877; ASHG 2018) report neurodevelopmental disorders, developmental delay, and congenital anomalies with de novo variants in MAP4K4.

2. **Strong genetic constraint:** MAP4K4 shows extreme intolerance to missense variation (Z score 4.01, pLI = 1.0), indicating that the gene is under strong purifying selection and that heterozygous variants are likely to be pathogenic.

3. **Functional validation in model organisms:** Both zebrafish and mouse models demonstrate that loss of MAP4K4 causes developmental defects and neurodevelopmental abnormalities, directly supporting the human phenotype.

4. **Mechanistic understanding:** Well-characterized pathways (RAS signaling, Hippo pathway, JNK/MAPK signaling) provide clear biological mechanisms by which MAP4K4 variants could cause neurodevelopmental disease.

5. **De novo inheritance pattern:** All reported variants are de novo, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance and supporting pathogenicity.

**Key gaps and limitations:**

1. **Limited patient numbers:** Approximately 31 total patients across four studies is relatively small for establishing a novel disease gene. Larger cohort studies would strengthen the evidence.

2. **Lack of cohort-based discovery:** Patients were not identified through large-scale sequencing cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD), which would provide independent replication and phenotypic standardization.

3. **Incomplete genotype-phenotype correlation:** Limited detailed characterization of how specific variant types (LoF vs. missense) correlate with phenotypic severity or specific clinical features.

4. **Heterozygous mouse models lacking:** While homozygous knockouts show embryonic lethality, heterozygous mouse models specifically designed to recapitulate human variants would provide stronger functional validation.

5. **Limited patient-derived cellular studies:** Direct evidence of pathway dysregulation in patient-derived cells (fibroblasts, iPSC-derived neurons) would strengthen the mechanistic evidence.

**Conclusion:**

MAP4K4 is a credible neurodevelopmental disorder gene with moderate-to-strong evidence for pathogenicity of heterozygous loss-of-function and missense variants. The consistent clinical phenotype across multiple independent case series, combined with extreme gene constraint metrics, de novo inheritance pattern, and functional validation in model organisms demonstrating developmental and neurological defects, provides compelling evidence for MAP4K4 as a novel autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder gene. However, the evidence would be substantially strengthened by larger cohort-based studies, detailed genotype-phenotype correlation, and functional studies in patient-derived cells. The identification of potential therapeutic targets (integrin α5β1 inhibition) suggests clinical utility of MAP4K4 variant identification.
