# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: LRP1 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

LRP1 (Low-Density Lipoprotein Receptor-Related Protein 1) is a multifunctional endocytic receptor that mediates cellular uptake and clearance of various ligands including amyloid-β, apolipoprotein E, and pathogenic proteins. LRP1 functions as a master regulator of protein homeostasis in the brain and plays critical roles in cell signaling, autophagy, mitochondrial function, and developmental processes including chondrocyte differentiation.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent studies:** 3 studies identified

**Total patient count:** Approximately 10 patients with confirmed LRP1 variants (9 patients with developmental dysplasia of the hip; 1 patient with autism spectrum disorder)

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Developmental dysplasia of the hip (DDH) with acetabular and femoral head malformations (9 patients; PMID: 36067312)
- Autism spectrum disorder (1 patient; ASHG 2014 - Banks et al.)
- Schizophrenia (mentioned in association with de novo variants; ASHG 2014 - Favé et al.)

**Predominant variant type:** Primarily missense variants and other likely pathogenic variants. The DDH study identified multiple likely pathogenic variants in LRP1 including the missense variant R1783W, which was functionally validated in a heterozygous knockout mouse model (PMID: 36067312). De novo variants were identified in autism and schizophrenia cases.

**Cohort source:** The DDH study identified patients via whole-exome sequencing in two families and seven unrelated sporadic cases. The autism case was identified through the Central Ohio Registry for Autism (CORA) in the United States. No patients appear to be from established rare disease cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL).

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) identification of likely pathogenic variants in multiple unrelated families and sporadic cases; (2) functional validation in mouse models recapitulating human phenotypes; (3) confirmation of de novo variants via Sanger sequencing with parental testing. However, the evidence is limited by: (1) small total patient numbers (10 patients across all neurodevelopmental phenotypes); (2) lack of systematic screening in large cohorts; (3) limited phenotypic diversity (DDH dominates the clinical evidence); (4) incomplete variant characterization in some studies.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (8 studies) and zebrafish (1 study)

**Key neurological phenotypes from knockouts:**

*Developmental/Structural:*
- Heterozygous Lrp1 knockout and Lrp1R1783W knockin mice recapitulated human DDH phenotypes with impaired triradiate chondrocyte differentiation (PMID: 36067312)
- Zebrafish expressing LRP1 mutations showed developmental defects comparable to Noonan syndrome genes (ASHG 2012)

*Neurodegenerative/Protein Homeostasis:*
- Conditional neuronal LRP1 knockout significantly reduced α-synuclein spread between neurons and in brain (PMID: 36056345)
- Conditional neuronal LRP1 knockout significantly reduced tau propagation between neurons and in vivo (PMID: 32296178)
- Astrocytic LRP1 knockout reduced mitochondria transfer to neurons and worsened ischemia-reperfusion injury in stroke models (PMID: 38906140)

*Metabolic/Cognitive:*
- GABAergic neuron-specific LRP1 knockout (Vgat-Cre; LRP1loxP/loxP mice) resulted in severe obesity and age-dependent impairment in spatial memory, learning, and fear conditioning at 32 weeks (PMID: 38636794)

*Lethality and Mendelian ratios:* All conditional knockout mice were viable with no reported lethality or infertility, suggesting that complete LRP1 loss is tolerated in specific cell types but heterozygous deficiency causes phenotypes.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The mouse models show strong consistency with human clinical findings. The heterozygous Lrp1 knockout and R1783W knockin mice directly recapitulated the DDH phenotype observed in human patients (PMID: 36067312). The neurological phenotypes (impaired protein clearance, cognitive deficits) align with the neurodevelopmental and neuropsychiatric phenotypes observed in human cases.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) multiple independent mouse models demonstrating consistent phenotypes; (2) direct validation of human variants (R1783W) in knockin mice; (3) cell-type specific conditional knockouts revealing tissue-specific functions; (4) demonstration of viability with heterozygous deficiency, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance; (5) mechanistic insights into LRP1 function in autophagy, protein clearance, and mitochondrial transfer.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

LRP1 operates in multiple critical pathways relevant to neurodevelopment and neurological function:

1. *Autophagy and protein homeostasis:* LRP1 regulates autophagy in chondrocytes; heterozygous LRP1 deficiency decreases autophagy and increases β-catenin, suppressing chondrocyte differentiation (PMID: 36067312)

2. *Amyloid-β clearance:* LRP1 on the blood-brain barrier mediates transcytosis and clearance of amyloid-β; LRP1-targeted polymersomes upregulated LRP1 expression and reduced brain Aβ levels by 45% with 8-fold increase in plasma Aβ in AD model mice (PMID: 41052971)

3. *Pathogenic protein uptake and spread:* LRP1 mediates neuronal uptake of α-synuclein monomers, oligomers, and pre-formed fibrils through lysine residues and N-terminus interactions (PMID: 36056345); LRP1 is a master regulator of tau uptake and spread (PMID: 32296178)

4. *Mitochondrial function:* Astrocytic LRP1 suppresses glucose uptake, glycolysis, and lactate production, promoting astrocyte-to-neuron mitochondria transfer and mitigating ischemic stroke injury (PMID: 38906140)

5. *Neuroprotection signaling:* LRP1 activates the PI3K/AKT pathway to protect against cisplatin-induced ototoxicity and apoptosis (PMID: 35530135)

6. *Metabolic homeostasis:* LRP1 in GABAergic neurons regulates energy homeostasis and memory function; loss results in obesity and cognitive impairment (PMID: 38636794)

7. *Tumor suppression:* LRP1 suppresses hepatocellular carcinoma progression by stabilizing OGA protein and suppressing NF-κB signaling (PMID: 39405202)

8. *Viral entry:* LRP1 serves as a host entry receptor for SFTSV and OROV; LRP1 neutralizing antibodies and antagonists effectively attenuate infection (PMID: 40301361; PMID: 35939689)

**Expression pattern:** LRP1 is widely expressed in the brain including in neurons, astrocytes, microglia, and oligodendrocyte progenitor cells. The evidence demonstrates cell-type specific functions, with particular relevance to GABAergic neurons, astrocytes, and developing chondrocytes.

**Key experimental findings:**
- Genome-wide association study identified LRP1 locus at 12q13.3 as a genome-wide significant risk locus for pain susceptibility (p-value 6.8e-72) with pleiotropic relationships to psychiatric, metabolic, and immunological traits (ASHG 2022 - Mocci et al.)
- LRP1 knockdown in iPSC-derived neurons significantly reduced α-synuclein uptake
- DNMT inhibitor RG108 increased LRP1 expression and decreased LRP1 promoter methylation, triggering PI3K/AKT pathway activation
- Conditional knockout studies demonstrate cell-type specific functions without global lethality

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) extensive characterization of LRP1 function across multiple biological pathways; (2) demonstration of cell-type specific functions through conditional knockouts; (3) mechanistic studies showing how LRP1 variants impair specific cellular processes (autophagy, protein clearance, mitochondrial transfer); (4) genome-wide association evidence linking LRP1 to pain susceptibility and psychiatric traits; (5) consistent findings across multiple experimental systems (cell culture, mouse models, human tissues).

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths of the evidence:**

1. *Functional validation:* Human LRP1 variants have been functionally validated in mouse models that recapitulate human phenotypes, particularly for DDH (PMID: 36067312)

2. *Mechanistic understanding:* Extensive biological evidence demonstrates how LRP1 deficiency impairs critical cellular processes including autophagy, protein homeostasis, mitochondrial function, and developmental differentiation

3. *Genetic evidence:* De novo variants identified in autism and schizophrenia cases; multiple likely pathogenic variants identified in DDH families and sporadic cases; GWAS evidence linking LRP1 to pain susceptibility and psychiatric traits

4. *Model organism consistency:* Multiple independent mouse models demonstrate consistent phenotypes with human disease, including direct validation of human variants

5. *Cell-type specificity:* Conditional knockout studies reveal tissue-specific functions relevant to neurodevelopment and neurological disease

**Key gaps and limitations:**

1. *Limited patient numbers:* Only 10 patients with confirmed LRP1 variants across all neurodevelopmental phenotypes; larger cohort studies needed

2. *Phenotypic heterogeneity:* Clinical phenotypes are diverse (DDH, autism, schizophrenia, pain susceptibility) without clear genotype-phenotype correlation; unclear whether these represent distinct conditions or variable expressivity

3. *Incomplete variant characterization:* Several studies do not provide specific variant notation or detailed functional characterization; limited segregation data in families

4. *Lack of systematic screening:* No large-scale screening in established rare disease cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD); ascertainment bias likely

5. *Incomplete penetrance and expressivity:* Limited data on incomplete penetrance, variable expressivity, or modifier genes; unclear why some heterozygous carriers are affected while others may not be

6. *Dosage sensitivity unclear:* While heterozygous deficiency causes phenotypes in mice, the specific mechanisms of haploinsufficiency versus dominant-negative effects remain incompletely characterized

**Conclusion:**

LRP1 harbors likely pathogenic heterozygous variants associated with developmental dysplasia of the hip, autism spectrum disorder, and schizophrenia, with supporting evidence from functional studies demonstrating impaired autophagy, protein homeostasis, and developmental differentiation. While biological and model organism evidence is strong, clinical evidence remains moderate due to limited patient numbers and lack of systematic cohort screening. Additional studies in large rare disease cohorts and functional characterization of identified variants would strengthen the evidence for LRP1 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with autosomal dominant inheritance.

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. Heterozygous LRP1 deficiency causes developmental dysplasia of the hip by impairing triradiate chondrocytes differentiation due to inhibition of autophagy (PMID: 36067312)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. LRP1 is a neuronal receptor for α-synuclein uptake and spread (PMID: 36056345)
2. LRP1 is a master regulator of tau uptake and spread (PMID: 32296178)
3. Astrocytic LRP1 enables mitochondria transfer to neurons and mitigates brain ischemic stroke by suppressing ARF1 lactylation (PMID: 38906140)
4. Genome-wide CRISPR screening identifies LRP1 as an entry factor for SFTSV (PMID: 40301361)

### Biological Evidence
1. LRP1 in GABAergic neurons is a key link between obesity and memory function (PMID: 38636794)
2. Rapid amyloid-β clearance and cognitive recovery through multivalent modulation of blood-brain barrier transport (PMID: 41052971)
3. Inhibiting DNA methylation alleviates cisplatin-induced hearing loss by decreasing oxidative stress-induced mitochondria-dependent apoptosis via the LRP1-PI3K/AKT pathway (PMID: 35530135)
4. Loss of LRP1 Promotes Hepatocellular Carcinoma Progression via UFL1-Mediated Activation of NF-κB Signaling (PMID: 39405202)
5. Oropouche orthobunyavirus infection is mediated by the cellular host factor Lrp1 (PMID: 35939689)
