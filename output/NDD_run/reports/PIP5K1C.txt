# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: PIP5K1C AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

PIP5K1C (phosphatidylinositol-4-phosphate 5-kinase type-1 gamma) is a lipid kinase that catalyzes the conversion of phosphatidylinositol 4-phosphate (PtdIns4P) to phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate [PtdIns(4,5)P2], a critical signaling lipid regulating actin dynamics, membrane trafficking, and cell polarization. The gene is essential for proper endosomal trafficking, cytoskeletal organization, and multiple signaling pathways relevant to neurodevelopment.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent case reports/series:** One case series (PMID: 37451268)

**Total patients:** 9 unrelated children with de novo PIP5K1C variants

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Intellectual disability (core feature)
- Developmental delay
- Seizures
- Acquired microcephaly
- Visual abnormalities
- Dysmorphic features
- Forebrain developmental defects
- Craniofacial abnormalities
- Eye abnormalities

**Predominant variant type:** Missense variants (heterozygous de novo)

**Cohort information:** No indication that patients were from established diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL); appears to be a discovery series from clinical genetics centers.

**Diagnostic evidence strength:** The clinical evidence is supported by functional validation—zebrafish knock-in models carrying the patient variants recapitulated forebrain and craniofacial defects, and mechanistic studies demonstrated that the variants increase the endosomal PI(4,5)P2 pool, causing ectopic filamentous actin recruitment at early endosomes and dysfunction in endosomal trafficking (PMID: 37451268). This functional recapitulation in model organisms substantially strengthens the causal inference.

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**

The evidence is based on a single case series with 9 patients and de novo variants in all cases, which is consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance. However, the clinical evidence would be strengthened by additional independent case reports and larger cohort studies. The functional validation in zebrafish is a significant strength.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Zebrafish and mouse

**Zebrafish evidence (PMID: 37451268):**
- Knock-in models carrying patient variants showed developmental defects affecting forebrain, eye abnormalities, and craniofacial abnormalities
- Phenotypes directly recapitulate human clinical features
- Demonstrates that patient variants are sufficient to cause neurodevelopmental defects

**Mouse evidence (multiple studies):**
- Conditional knockout in sensory ganglia (Brn3a-Cre) showed recovery of thermal and mechanical hypersensitivity, indicating a role in pain signaling (PMID: 29020859)
- Heterozygous knockout mice showed reduced pronociceptive receptor signaling and reduced TRPV1 sensitization (PMID: 24853942)
- Full knockout is viable but causes subfertility/infertility (PMID: 22321832)
- Conditional deletion in osteocytes causes low bone mass and impaired bone remodeling (PMID: 38495744)
- Conditional deletion in chondrocytes causes spontaneous osteoarthritic lesions in aged mice (PMID: 37008048)
- Conditional deletion in mesenchymal stem cells causes osteopenia (PMID: 35090892)
- Knock-in models show altered PIP5K1C expression in brain regions (thalamus, basolateral amygdala) after acute alcohol exposure (PMID: 29667742)

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The zebrafish knock-in models directly recapitulate the neurodevelopmental phenotypes (forebrain defects, craniofacial abnormalities, eye abnormalities) observed in human patients. Mouse models primarily demonstrate roles in pain signaling, bone metabolism, and alcohol response rather than neurodevelopmental phenotypes, though they confirm the gene's importance in nervous system function.

**Lethality and Mendelian ratios:** Full knockout mice are viable, and conditional knockouts follow Mendelian ratios, indicating that complete loss of PIP5K1C is not embryonic lethal. This is consistent with the observation that human patients carry heterozygous missense variants rather than null variants.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**

The zebrafish knock-in models provide direct functional validation of patient variants and recapitulate key neurodevelopmental phenotypes. Mouse models, while not specifically addressing neurodevelopmental outcomes, confirm the gene's viability and demonstrate its importance in multiple physiological systems.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

PIP5K1C functions as a critical hub in phosphoinositide signaling. Key pathways include:

1. **Endosomal trafficking and actin dynamics:** Patient variants increase the endosomal PI(4,5)P2 pool, causing ectopic filamentous actin recruitment at early endosomes and impairing endosomal trafficking (PMID: 37451268). This suggests that proper regulation of PI(4,5)P2 levels is essential for normal endosomal function during neurodevelopment.

2. **PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling:** A somatic Pip5k1c mutation in a breast cancer model stabilizes the Pip5k1c protein and activates PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling, promoting metastatic breast cancer development (PMID: 36322759). This indicates that PIP5K1C activity directly influences growth signaling pathways.

3. **Focal adhesion and cell-matrix interactions:** Pip5k1c deletion in osteocytes, chondrocytes, and mesenchymal stem cells impairs focal adhesion proteins (integrin β1, talin, vinculin), reducing cell adhesion and spreading (PMID: 37008048, PMID: 38495744, PMID: 35090892). These findings suggest that PIP5K1C is essential for proper cell-matrix interactions during development.

4. **Pain signaling:** PIP5K1C regulates TRPV1 sensitization and pronociceptive receptor signaling through PLCβ/PI3Kγ-GSK3 pathways (PMID: 24853942, PMID: 22172670).

5. **Neutrophil polarization and chemotaxis:** PIP5K1C regulates integrin-induced polarization and chemotaxis (PMID: 22172670).

**Expression pattern:** While the provided evidence does not detail comprehensive brain expression mapping, the functional studies in zebrafish and the mouse studies showing altered PIP5K1C expression in brain regions (thalamus, basolateral amygdala) after acute alcohol exposure (PMID: 29667742) indicate CNS expression. The clinical phenotype of forebrain developmental defects and intellectual disability strongly suggests neurodevelopmental relevance.

**Key experimental findings:**

- Patient variants increase endosomal PI(4,5)P2 pools and cause ectopic actin recruitment, disrupting endosomal trafficking (PMID: 37451268)
- PIP5K1C catalyzes one of two independent pathways for PtdIns4P to PtdIns(4,5)P2 conversion; cancer cells with low PIP5K1C depend on the alternative PIKFYVE-PIP4K2C pathway (PMID: 36803256)
- SNP rs11666894 in PIP5K1C is associated with cutaneous melanoma disease-specific survival (adjusted HR=1.47, 95% CI=1.15-1.89) and with PIP5K1C mRNA expression in lymphoblastoid cell lines (P<0.0001) (PMID: 33163277)
- Eight SNPs in PIP5K1C are significantly associated with alcohol use disorder in African ancestry populations (PMID: 29667742)
- High-throughput screening identified potent PIP5K1C inhibitors (e.g., UNC3230) that reduce nociceptive sensitization in chronic pain models (PMID: 25534829)

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence demonstrates clear mechanistic pathways by which PIP5K1C dysfunction could cause neurodevelopmental disease. The identification of specific molecular consequences of patient variants (increased endosomal PI(4,5)P2, ectopic actin recruitment, impaired trafficking) provides a direct link between genotype and phenotype. The gene's involvement in multiple critical cellular processes (endosomal trafficking, focal adhesion, actin dynamics) relevant to neurodevelopment is well-established.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE-TO-STRONG**

**Key strengths:**

1. **Functional validation of patient variants:** Zebrafish knock-in models carrying patient variants directly recapitulate human neurodevelopmental phenotypes (forebrain defects, craniofacial abnormalities), providing strong functional evidence for pathogenicity.

2. **Clear mechanistic pathway:** The identification of increased endosomal PI(4,5)P2 and ectopic actin recruitment provides a specific molecular mechanism linking patient variants to cellular dysfunction.

3. **De novo variants in unrelated patients:** All 9 patients carry de novo heterozygous missense variants, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance and supporting a causal relationship.

4. **Biological plausibility:** PIP5K1C's established roles in endosomal trafficking, focal adhesion, and actin dynamics are all relevant to neurodevelopment and consistent with the observed phenotypes.

5. **Gene viability:** Mouse models confirm that heterozygous loss or missense variants are compatible with viability, consistent with the human presentation.

**Key gaps and limitations:**

1. **Limited clinical data:** Only one case series with 9 patients has been published. Additional independent case reports or larger cohort studies would strengthen the evidence.

2. **Incomplete phenotypic characterization:** The abstract of the primary clinical study does not provide detailed phenotypic descriptions for individual patients, limiting assessment of phenotypic heterogeneity.

3. **Lack of mouse neurodevelopmental models:** While zebrafish models are informative, mouse models specifically examining neurodevelopmental outcomes (e.g., forebrain development, cortical organization) would provide additional support.

4. **Limited genotype-phenotype correlation:** With only 9 patients and three variants, it is not yet possible to establish clear genotype-phenotype correlations or identify variant-specific effects.

5. **Mechanism of gain-of-function vs. dominant-negative:** While the zebrafish studies suggest that patient variants increase endosomal PI(4,5)P2 levels, the precise mechanism (gain-of-function, dominant-negative, or haploinsufficiency) remains incompletely characterized.

**Conclusion:**

PIP5K1C is a credible neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous missense variants causing a syndrome characterized by intellectual disability, developmental delay, seizures, microcephaly, and craniofacial abnormalities. The evidence is supported by functional validation in zebrafish models that recapitulate human phenotypes and by clear mechanistic pathways involving altered phosphoinositide signaling and endosomal trafficking. However, the evidence base remains limited to a single case series of 9 patients, and additional clinical and experimental studies are needed to fully establish the gene's role in neurodevelopmental disease and to characterize the full spectrum of associated phenotypes.
