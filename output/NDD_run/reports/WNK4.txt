# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: WNK4 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

WNK4 (With-No-Lysine Kinase 4) is a serine-threonine kinase with atypical catalytic properties that functions as a physiological chloride sensor in the distal convoluted tubule of the kidney (PMID: 33394730). WNK4 is a key regulator of ion transport, particularly controlling the activity of the sodium-chloride cotransporter (NCC) and other ion channels through phosphorylation of downstream kinases like SPAK, and its dysregulation causes both renal and systemic phenotypes including hypertension and electrolyte imbalances (PMID: 33491560).

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent studies:** 2 case series/reports with neurodevelopmental focus

**Total patient count:** 44 patients in the primary clinical series (PMID: 37666233)

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Hyperkalemia (primary phenotype)
- Hypertension (primary phenotype)
- Hyperchloremic acidosis
- Suppressed renin activity
- Preserved renal function
- **Epilepsy: 2/44 patients (4.545%)** — notably elevated compared to general population prevalence
- Migraine: 1 patient

**Predominant variant type:** Missense mutations (Q565E identified in the large familial series)

**Cohort source:** No indication these patients are from established research cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL)

**Clinical phenotype classification:** The primary clinical evidence focuses on familial hyperkalemic hypertension (FHHt) with an associated neurological phenotype. The large family study (PMID: 37666233) explicitly documents the association of WNK4 mutations with epilepsy at a prevalence significantly higher than expected in the general population, suggesting a potential neurodevelopmental or neurological component to WNK4 pathology. However, the secondary reference (PMID: 23383144) mentions WNK4 mutations in the context of pseudohypoaldosteronism type II without detailed neurological phenotyping.

**Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED**

The clinical evidence for WNK4 as a neurodevelopmental gene is limited. While one large family study documents epilepsy in 4.5% of WNK4 mutation carriers (PMID: 37666233), this represents only 2 affected individuals and lacks detailed neurological characterization, developmental history, or systematic neuropsychiatric assessment. The evidence is primarily renal/metabolic in nature, with neurological manifestations appearing as secondary observations rather than the primary diagnostic focus.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (all studies)

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

Seven mouse studies were identified, all using viable knockout or conditional knockout models with Mendelian inheritance patterns where reported:

- **Renal/metabolic phenotypes (consistent across studies):** Altered NCC phosphorylation, changes in sodium-chloride handling, modified ion transport regulation
- **Inflammatory phenotype:** WNK4 knockout mice showed suppressed LPS-induced macrophage activation and attenuated acute lung injury (PMID: 31786261)
- **Glomerular phenotype:** Non-functional WNK4 develops histologically confirmed focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS) (ASHG 2017 - M. Artomov et al.)
- **Renal salt wasting:** Claudin-7 knockout mice with increased WNK4 expression showed renal salt wasting and dehydration (PMID: 31382627)
- **Neurological phenotypes:** None reported in the available model organism studies

**Lethality and viability:** All WNK4 knockout models are viable with no reported embryonic lethality or postnatal lethality

**Mendelian ratio deviations:** One study reported Mendelian inheritance ratios (PMID: 38961847); others did not specify

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The mouse models consistently demonstrate renal ion transport dysregulation matching the human hyperkalemic hypertension phenotype. However, no mouse studies specifically examined neurological development, seizure susceptibility, or other neurodevelopmental outcomes that would directly validate the epilepsy association observed in humans.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**

While mouse models robustly demonstrate WNK4's role in renal physiology and ion transport, they provide no direct evidence for neurodevelopmental pathology. The absence of reported neurological phenotypes in multiple knockout models is notable and does not support a primary neurodevelopmental role, though it does not exclude the possibility that subtle neurological effects were not systematically assessed.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

WNK4 functions within multiple interconnected pathways:

1. **Ion transport regulation (primary):** WNK4 acts as a chloride sensor in the distal convoluted tubule, regulating NCC activity through SPAK phosphorylation (PMID: 33394730, PMID: 37530086). Gain-of-function mutations impair normal chloride-dependent inhibition of NCC, leading to excessive sodium reabsorption and potassium retention.

2. **Metabolic sensing:** WNK4 integrates glucose/fructose signals via the calcium-sensing receptor (CaSR) to activate the WNK4-SPAK-NCC pathway, contributing to hypertension in diabetic patients (PMID: 36288902).

3. **Protein degradation regulation:** WNK4 abundance is controlled by KLHL3-dependent ubiquitin-proteasomal degradation through neddylation and autophagy pathways, with potassium-dependent regulation (PMID: 37481568).

4. **Inflammatory signaling:** WNK4-SPAK modulates macrophage activation and NKCC1 phosphorylation in response to lipopolysaccharide (PMID: 31786261).

5. **Glomerular function:** WNK4 plays a causal role in focal segmental glomerulosclerosis pathogenesis (ASHG 2017 - M. Artomov et al.).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**

WNK4 is highly expressed in kidney and chloride-transporting epithelia (PMID: 15639021). One biological study references WNK4's involvement in neural development via Lhx8/Awh expression (PMID: 23383144), suggesting potential neurodevelopmental function, though this study is primarily a functional reference rather than detailed mechanistic characterization. The biological evidence does not provide comprehensive data on WNK4 expression in developing brain, neural progenitor cells, or other neurodevelopmental tissues.

**Key experimental findings:**

- WNK4 functions as the highest-sensitivity chloride sensor among WNK kinases (PMID: 33394730)
- Chloride-insensitive WNK4 mice develop hypertension, proving physiological chloride-sensing is inhibitory (PMID: 33394730)
- WNK4 regulates surface expression and activity of multiple ion channels and transporters beyond NCC (PMID: 17084706)
- FHH-causing mutations show altered regulation patterns of SLC12 family cotransporters (PMID: 17634397)
- WNK4-SPAK pathway disruption strongly reduces NCC phosphorylation but only mildly affects NKCC2 phosphorylation, indicating pathway specificity (PMID: 37881876)

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence for WNK4's molecular function and renal physiology is robust and well-characterized across multiple experimental systems (cell culture, transgenic mice, biochemical assays). However, the neurodevelopmental relevance is poorly characterized. While one reference mentions neural development involvement (PMID: 23383144), this appears to be a passing reference rather than detailed mechanistic evidence. The biological evidence is predominantly focused on renal ion transport rather than neurodevelopmental pathways.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: LIMITED**

**Key strengths of the evidence:**

1. One large family study (44 patients) documents epilepsy in WNK4 mutation carriers at elevated prevalence (4.5%), providing clinical observation of a potential neurodevelopmental phenotype (PMID: 37666233)
2. Robust biological characterization of WNK4's molecular function and renal pathways
3. Viable mouse models with consistent renal phenotypes matching human disease
4. Reference to WNK4's involvement in neural development pathways (PMID: 23383144)

**Key gaps and limitations:**

1. **Minimal clinical neurodevelopmental characterization:** The epilepsy observation in the clinical series lacks detailed seizure semiology, EEG findings, developmental history, cognitive assessment, or systematic neuropsychiatric evaluation
2. **No neurodevelopmental phenotypes in animal models:** Seven mouse knockout studies show no reported neurological, behavioral, or developmental abnormalities, despite comprehensive renal phenotyping
3. **Lack of mechanistic evidence:** No studies demonstrate WNK4 expression in developing brain, neural progenitor cells, or neurodevelopmental tissues
4. **Limited patient numbers with neurological phenotype:** Only 2 patients with epilepsy across all clinical reports
5. **No independent replication:** The epilepsy association is documented in a single family study without independent confirmation in other WNK4 mutation carriers
6. **Unclear causality:** The association between WNK4 mutations and epilepsy may be coincidental, secondary to severe electrolyte dysregulation, or represent ascertainment bias in a single large family

**Conclusion:**

WNK4 is a well-characterized regulator of renal ion transport with strong biological and clinical evidence for causing familial hyperkalemic hypertension through gain-of-function missense mutations. However, evidence for WNK4 as a primary neurodevelopmental disorder gene is currently LIMITED. While one large family study reports epilepsy in 4.5% of WNK4 mutation carriers (PMID: 37666233), this observation lacks detailed neurological characterization, mechanistic explanation, and independent replication. The absence of neurological phenotypes in multiple mouse knockout models and the lack of demonstrated WNK4 expression or function in neurodevelopmental tissues further limit the evidence. Additional studies with systematic neurological assessment of WNK4 mutation carriers, investigation of WNK4 expression and function in developing neural tissue, and neurodevelopmental phenotyping in animal models would be needed to establish WNK4 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene.
