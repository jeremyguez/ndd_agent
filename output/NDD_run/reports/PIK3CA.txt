# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: PIK3CA AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

PIK3CA encodes the catalytic subunit alpha (p110α) of phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K), a key regulator of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway that controls cell growth, proliferation, and survival. Gain-of-function mutations in PIK3CA cause constitutive pathway activation and are associated with a spectrum of developmental overgrowth and vascular malformation syndromes, including several with significant neurodevelopmental manifestations.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of Studies and Patient Population:**
Ten independent clinical studies or case series were identified, encompassing approximately 161 patients with documented PIK3CA variants. Studies ranged from single case reports to large cohort analyses, with the largest series including 86 patients with vascular malformations (ASHG 2022 - Myers et al.).

**Most Common Phenotypes Observed:**
The clinical evidence reveals a spectrum of neurodevelopmental and vascular phenotypes:
- **Brain malformations:** Megalencephaly (including hemimegalencephaly), focal cortical dysplasia type II (FCD2), and cortical malformations (PMID: 22729224; PMID: 37149062)
- **Vascular malformations:** Capillary-venous malformations, venous malformations, arteriovenous malformations, and cerebral cavernous malformations (PMID: 38880808; PMID: 33910229; ASHG 2023 - Jensen et al.)
- **Overgrowth syndromes:** CLOVES syndrome, megalencephaly-capillary malformation (MCAP), and megalencephaly-polymicrogyria-polydactyly-hydrocephalus (MPPH) (PMID: 29899452; PMID: 22729224)
- **Neurological complications:** Drug-resistant epilepsy, seizures, and strokes (PMID: 37149062; PMID: 33910229)
- **Associated features:** Hemihypertrophy, scoliosis, polydactyly, hydrocephalus, and congestive heart failure (PMID: 29899452; PMID: 22729224)

**Predominant Variant Type:**
All identified variants were missense mutations, predominantly somatic and mosaic (post-zygotic) in nature. Notably, the clinical evidence includes both somatic mutations (affecting specific tissues like brain or vascular endothelium) and de novo germline or postzygotic mutations. Specific recurrent variants include H1047R and E545K, which are well-characterized gain-of-function mutations (PMID: 22729224; ASHG 2019 - Lai et al.).

**Cohort Information:**
The studies do not explicitly reference participation in major diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL), though several represent institutional case series and ASHG conference presentations suggesting multi-center collaboration.

**Diagnostic Methodology:**
Variants were identified through exome sequencing, targeted deep sequencing (≥2000X read depth), and specialized clinical sequencing assays (VANSeq). Notably, somatic variants were detected in tissue-based testing and, in some cases, in plasma cell-free DNA (44% detection rate in venous malformation cohort; ASHG 2023 - Jensen et al.). The low variant allele frequencies (<5% in 43% of samples) underscore the mosaic nature of these mutations (ASHG 2022 - Myers et al.).

**Strength of Clinical Evidence: STRONG**

The clinical evidence is strong, supported by: (1) multiple independent case series and cohort studies totaling >160 patients; (2) consistent phenotypic associations across studies; (3) robust diagnostic methodology with molecular confirmation; (4) detailed characterization of neurodevelopmental manifestations (megalencephaly, FCD, epilepsy); and (5) demonstration of therapeutic responsiveness to PI3K pathway inhibitors (alpelisib, miransertib, rapamycin), providing functional validation of pathogenicity (PMID: 29899452; PMID: 38880808).

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms Studied:**
Eight model organism studies were identified, predominantly using mice (7 studies) with one zebrafish study. All employed gain-of-function approaches (knock-in or overexpression models), consistent with the human somatic gain-of-function mutations.

**Key Neurological and Developmental Phenotypes:**
- **Brain malformations and overgrowth:** Glioblastoma formation with brain hyperexcitability and increased neuronal activity (PMID: 31996845); vascular malformations in retinal vasculature with increased endothelial cell proliferation (ASHG 2019 - Kobialka et al.)
- **Vascular phenotypes:** Capillary-venous malformations, lymphatic malformations, venous malformations, and complex vascular anomalies (PMID: 38880808; ASHG 2015 - Boon et al.; PMID: 36688917)
- **Systemic manifestations:** Overgrowth phenotype, organ dysfunction, congestive heart failure, hemihypertrophy, and scoliosis (PMID: 29899452)
- **Angiogenesis defects:** Perturbed angiogenesis and abnormal vascular development in zebrafish (PMID: 33729480)

**Lethality and Mendelian Ratios:**
No data on Mendelian ratios or embryonic lethality were reported in the available studies, limiting assessment of whether heterozygous mutations are viable in the germline state.

**Consistency with Human Phenotype:**
The model organism phenotypes show strong concordance with human clinical presentations. Mouse knock-in models recapitulate the overgrowth, vascular malformation, and organ dysfunction phenotypes observed in patients with PIK3CA-related overgrowth syndrome (PMID: 29899452). Brain hyperexcitability in mice parallels the seizure phenotype in human patients with PIK3CA-associated focal cortical dysplasia (PMID: 31996845). Importantly, therapeutic interventions effective in mice (rapamycin, alpelisib, miransertib) have been translated to clinical use with documented benefit (PMID: 29899452; PMID: 38880808).

**Strength of Model Organism Evidence: STRONG**

The model organism evidence is strong, supported by: (1) multiple independent mouse models with consistent phenotypes; (2) direct phenotypic overlap with human disease (overgrowth, vascular malformations, brain hyperexcitability); (3) demonstration of pathway-specific effects (PI3K/AKT/mTOR activation); and (4) successful therapeutic validation in vivo, confirming that PIK3CA mutations are sufficient to cause disease phenotypes.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known Pathways and Molecular Function:**
PIK3CA encodes the catalytic p110α subunit of PI3K, which phosphorylates PIP2 to generate PIP3, activating downstream AKT and mTOR signaling. Gain-of-function mutations (particularly H1047R in the kinase domain and E545K in the helical domain) cause constitutive PI3K pathway activation independent of growth factor stimulation (PMID: 26627007; PMID: 39555098). The pathway regulates cell proliferation, survival, metabolism, and angiogenesis—all processes dysregulated in PIK3CA-associated developmental disorders.

**Expression Pattern and Neurodevelopmental Relevance:**
While specific brain expression data are not detailed in the provided evidence, the clinical and model organism data demonstrate that PIK3CA is functionally active in multiple neurodevelopmentally relevant cell types: (1) neural progenitors and neurons (evidenced by megalencephaly and cortical dysplasia phenotypes); (2) endothelial cells (vascular malformations); and (3) glial cells (glioblastoma formation in mice). Single-cell transcriptomics from a hemimegalencephaly patient revealed cell-type-specific effects, with mTOR pathway activation and CREB phosphorylation in neurons carrying the PIK3CA E545K variant (ASHG 2023 - Gade et al.).

**Key Experimental Findings:**

*Pathway Activation and Cellular Effects:*
- PIK3CA mutations activate PI3K signaling with variant-specific oncogenic activity correlating imperfectly with mutation frequency (PMID: 26627007)
- H1047R mutation causes endothelial cell hyperproliferation, reduced pericyte coverage, and decreased arteriovenous specification markers; rapamycin treatment normalized these effects (PMID: 27030595)
- PIK3CA gain-of-function mutations trigger metabolic shifts toward glycolytic activation and activate mTOR pathway signaling (PMID: 40553493; ASHG 2023 - Gade et al.)

*Signaling Specificity and Heterogeneity:*
- PIK3CA H1047R corrupts the fidelity of growth factor-induced PI3K/AKT signaling in a dose-dependent manner, preferentially amplifying EGFR signaling while enhancing signaling heterogeneity (PMID: 39706867)
- This corrupted signaling specificity may explain the variable penetrance and expressivity observed in patients with the same PIK3CA mutation

*Metabolic and Epigenetic Regulation:*
- PIK3CA mutations transcriptionally repress PPT1 via PI3K-AKT-c-JUN signaling, leading to enhanced P300 palmitoylation and altered chromatin accessibility (PMID: 41610323)
- Metabolic conditions (obesity, type 1 diabetes) enhance PIK3CA mutant cell competitive fitness, while metformin reduces it, demonstrating that the metabolic environment selectively influences PIK3CA mutant evolution (PMID: 39169259)

*Non-Cell-Autonomous Effects:*
- PIK3CA gain-of-function mutations in Schwann cells trigger metabolic shifts that affect neighboring healthy cells (adipocytes, hair follicles) through crosstalk mechanisms, with early alpelisib treatment significantly improving disease signs (PMID: 40553493)

*Therapeutic Vulnerability:*
- CRISPRi genome-wide screening identified neddylation pathway genes as essential co-dependencies with PIK3CA E545K in head and neck cancer, revealing novel regulatory mechanisms and therapeutic vulnerabilities (PMID: 40154000)

**Strength of Biological Evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is strong, supported by: (1) comprehensive characterization of PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway activation by PIK3CA mutations; (2) multiple independent cell culture and in vivo studies demonstrating variant-specific effects; (3) elucidation of downstream mechanisms including metabolic reprogramming, epigenetic regulation, and signaling fidelity corruption; (4) demonstration of non-cell-autonomous effects relevant to developmental pathology; and (5) identification of therapeutic vulnerabilities validated in clinical trials.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall Evidence Level: STRONG**

PIK3CA qualifies as a well-established neurodevelopmental disorder gene with strong evidence across all three domains of evidence.

**Key Strengths:**

1. **Robust Clinical Evidence:** Over 160 patients across multiple independent studies with consistent phenotypic associations (megalencephaly, focal cortical dysplasia, vascular malformations, drug-resistant epilepsy) and molecular confirmation via exome/targeted sequencing.

2. **Functional Validation:** Multiple mouse models recapitulate human phenotypes, and therapeutic interventions (PI3K inhibitors, mTOR inhibitors) show clinical efficacy, providing strong functional validation of pathogenicity.

3. **Mechanistic Understanding:** Comprehensive characterization of PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway activation, with elucidation of downstream effects on cell proliferation, metabolism, angiogenesis, and epigenetic regulation.

4. **Variant-Specific Effects:** Evidence that different PIK3CA mutations (H1047R, E545K, etc.) produce variant-specific activation patterns and phenotypic consequences, explaining clinical heterogeneity.

5. **Therapeutic Translation:** Successful translation of mechanistic insights into clinical therapeutics (alpelisib, miransertib, rapamycin) with documented patient benefit.

**Key Gaps and Limitations:**

1. **Germline vs. Somatic Distinction:** The clinical evidence predominantly describes somatic and mosaic mutations. While de novo germline mutations have been reported (PMID: 22729224), the relative contribution of germline versus somatic variants to neurodevelopmental disease is not fully characterized. No data on Mendelian ratios or embryonic lethality in heterozygous germline carriers.

2. **Incomplete Penetrance and Variable Expressivity:** The mechanisms underlying variable penetrance and expressivity of the same PIK3CA mutation across individuals remain incompletely understood, though metabolic and epigenetic factors appear relevant.

3. **Limited Longitudinal Data:** Most studies are cross-sectional case series; longitudinal natural history data and long-term outcomes following therapeutic intervention are limited.

4. **Brain Expression and Cell-Type Specificity:** While functional effects in neurons and endothelial cells are documented, comprehensive characterization of PIK3CA expression patterns across brain cell types and developmental stages is not provided.

5. **Genotype-Phenotype Correlations:** Systematic genotype-phenotype correlation studies are lacking; it remains unclear whether specific PIK3CA mutations consistently predict particular neurodevelopmental phenotypes.

**Conclusion:**

PIK3CA is a well-established gene for neurodevelopmental disorders caused by somatic and de novo gain-of-function mutations. The evidence strongly supports pathogenicity through multiple lines of evidence: consistent clinical phenotypes across >160 patients (megalencephaly, focal cortical dysplasia, vascular malformations, drug-resistant epilepsy), functional validation in multiple model organisms, and comprehensive mechanistic understanding of PI3K pathway dysregulation. Therapeutic responsiveness to PI3K and mTOR inhibitors provides additional functional validation. While the majority of disease-causing variants are somatic/mosaic rather than germline heterozygous, the clinical, biological, and model organism evidence collectively establish PIK3CA as a high-confidence neurodevelopmental disorder gene with clear pathogenic mechanisms and therapeutic implications.
