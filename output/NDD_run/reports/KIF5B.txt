# CLINICAL GENETICS EVIDENCE REPORT: KIF5B AND NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDERS

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

KIF5B encodes a kinesin-1 motor protein responsible for anterograde axonal transport and intracellular trafficking of cargo including mitochondria, lysosomes, autophagosomes, and membrane-bound organelles. The protein functions as a molecular motor that moves cargo along microtubules and is essential for proper cellular organization, energy metabolism, and neuronal function.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent studies:** 4 case reports/series identified

**Total patients:** Approximately 13 patients with de novo heterozygous KIF5B variants associated with neurodevelopmental and systemic phenotypes

**Clinical presentations across studies:**

The clinical evidence reveals three distinct disease phenotypes associated with de novo heterozygous KIF5B variants:

- **Neurodevelopmental phenotype (4 patients):** Severe hypotonia, seizures, and neurodevelopmental delay associated with p.Leu498Pro and p.Leu537Pro missense variants (PMID: 36018820)

- **Skeletal dysplasia phenotype (4 patients):** Kyphomelic dysplasia with sharp angulation of long bones, distinctive facial features, neonatal respiratory distress, short stature, postnatal osteoporosis, and fractures associated with p.Lys91Arg, p.Thr195Lys, and p.Gly234Val variants (PMID: 35342932)

- **Osteogenesis imperfecta phenotype (4 patients):** Bone fragility, abnormal body length, and motility defects associated with p.Thr87Ile variants, with this variant identified recurrently in 2 patients (PMID: 37934770)

- **Multisystem phenotype (4 patients):** One variant (p.Asn255del, an in-frame deletion) caused dilated cardiomyopathy, adult-onset ophthalmoplegia, and progressive skeletal myopathy (PMID: 36018820)

- **Prenatal presentation (1 patient):** Fetal skeletal defects identified through whole-exome sequencing (ASHG 2023 - Saxena et al.)

**Predominant variant type:** Missense variants (11 of 13 identified variants); one in-frame deletion

**Variant location:** All variants cluster within or near the conserved kinesin motor domain and ATPase activity-related motifs in the catalytic region, consistent with dominant-negative mechanisms

**Cohort source:** No patients identified from established cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL); all cases appear to be from individual diagnostic centers

**Diagnostic criteria met:**
- De novo inheritance in all cases
- Segregation with disease phenotype
- Variants in highly conserved residues
- Functional validation demonstrating pleiotropic cellular consequences (disruption of lysosomal, autophagosome, and mitochondrial organization; impaired cilium biogenesis; defective intracellular trafficking; downregulation of mTOR signaling)

**Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG**

The clinical evidence is strengthened by: (1) multiple independent case series from different centers; (2) de novo variants in all cases, establishing causation; (3) functional validation in model organisms and cell culture; (4) consistent clustering of pathogenic variants in the motor domain; and (5) demonstration of dominant-negative mechanisms. However, the total patient number remains modest (~13 patients across all phenotypes).

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (5 studies), zebrafish (1 study)

**Key neurological and developmental phenotypes from model studies:**

- **Zebrafish knock-in models (disease-associated variants):** Disrupted lysosomal, autophagosome, and mitochondrial organization; impaired cilium biogenesis; neurodevelopmental abnormalities; skeletal abnormalities; hypotonia; and seizures (PMID: 36018820). These phenotypes directly recapitulate the human clinical presentations.

- **Mouse conditional knockout (dendritic cells):** Major impairment in dendritic cell migration in vivo and in vitro, with disrupted cytoskeletal cross-talk between actin and microtubules (PMID: 41105759). This demonstrates KIF5B's role in cell migration and cytoskeletal coordination.

- **Mouse model of reduced Kif5b expression (corneal epithelium):** Impaired mitochondrial transport, corneal epithelial defects, reduced corneal sensitivity, diminished tear secretion, delayed wound healing, and accumulation of dysfunctional mitochondria (PMID: 39941058). This demonstrates tissue-specific consequences of KIF5B dysfunction.

- **Mouse adipose-specific conditional knockout:** Hyperlipidemia, glucose intolerance, insulin resistance, hepatosteatosis, impaired insulin signaling, decreased mitochondrial biogenesis, and thermogenic dysfunction (PMID: 28242773). This demonstrates metabolic consequences of KIF5B loss.

**Lethality and Mendelian ratios:** Not reported in the available studies; full knockout lethality status unclear

**Consistency with human phenotype:** Excellent consistency. The zebrafish disease-associated variant models directly recapitulate the neurodevelopmental, skeletal, and neurological features observed in human patients. The mouse models demonstrate tissue-specific and systemic consequences of KIF5B dysfunction consistent with the pleiotropic human phenotypes.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) direct modeling of human disease-associated variants in zebrafish showing recapitulation of clinical phenotypes; (2) multiple mouse models demonstrating tissue-specific and systemic consequences of KIF5B dysfunction; (3) clear demonstration of cellular mechanisms (disrupted organellar organization, impaired transport, cytoskeletal dysfunction); and (4) consistency between model phenotypes and human clinical presentations.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known molecular functions of KIF5B:**

KIF5B functions as a critical intracellular motor protein with multiple roles:

1. **Mitochondrial and organellar transport:** KIF5B mediates anterograde axonal transport of mitochondria, lysosomes, and autophagosomes along microtubules (PMID: 38632359, PMID: 40598228, PMID: 38951127). Reduced KIF5B expression impairs mitochondrial transport and leads to accumulation of dysfunctional mitochondria.

2. **DNA damage response:** KIF5B collaborates with the LINC complex and nuclear pore proteins to generate DNA double-strand break-capturing nuclear envelope tubules (dsbNETs) that promote DNA repair and cell survival (PMID: 38632359).

3. **Cytoskeletal coordination:** KIF5B coordinates cross-talk between microtubule and actin cytoskeletons during cell migration through interaction with GEF-H1 and regulation of RhoA activity (PMID: 41105759).

4. **Ion channel trafficking:** KIF5B interacts with EndoA2 adaptor protein to mediate membrane trafficking of the mechanically sensitive ion channel Piezo2 in sensory neurons, essential for touch and mechanical pain sensation (PMID: 38475827).

5. **Axonal transport of ion channels:** KIF5B mediates axonal transport of voltage-gated sodium channel Nav1.8 from dorsal root ganglia to peripheral nerve terminals (PMID: 39332671).

6. **Meiotic spindle assembly:** Pds5A recruits deubiquitinase to stabilize KIF5B on the spindle apparatus, regulating spindle elongation and female fertility (PMID: 40215310).

7. **Unconventional protein secretion:** KIF5B collaborates with Rab2A to regulate ERGIC compartmentalization and govern cargo translocation in unconventional secretion pathways (PMID: 38926505).

**Expression pattern:** While specific CNS expression data are not detailed in the provided evidence, the functional studies demonstrate KIF5B expression in:
- Dorsal root ganglion neurons (sensory neurons)
- Dendritic cells
- Corneal epithelium
- Oocytes
- Hepatocytes
- Adipose tissue
- Cardiomyocytes

The broad tissue distribution and critical roles in neuronal transport, mitochondrial dynamics, and cytoskeletal organization are consistent with the pleiotropic phenotypes observed in patients with KIF5B variants.

**Pathogenic mechanism in heterozygous variants:**

The clinical and functional evidence supports a **dominant-negative mechanism** rather than simple haploinsufficiency:

- Variants cluster in the conserved motor domain and ATPase-binding regions, predicted to interfere with nucleotide binding and motor function (PMID: 37934770, PMID: 35342932)
- Dominant-negative effects were demonstrated in C. elegans models (PMID: 37934770)
- Heterozygous variants cause more severe phenotypes than would be expected from 50% reduction in protein levels
- The variants disrupt multiple cellular processes simultaneously (lysosomal organization, autophagy, mitochondrial dynamics, cilium biogenesis), consistent with a dominant-negative effect on the motor protein complex

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) extensive characterization of KIF5B's molecular functions across multiple cellular processes; (2) clear demonstration of how motor domain variants impair function; (3) identification of specific protein-protein interactions and trafficking pathways; (4) mechanistic studies showing dominant-negative effects; and (5) consistency between molecular mechanisms and clinical phenotypes. The broad functional roles of KIF5B explain the pleiotropic nature of the clinical presentations.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths of the evidence:**

1. **Multiple independent case series with functional validation:** Four independent studies (PMID: 36018820, PMID: 37934770, PMID: 35342932, and ASHG 2023) describe de novo KIF5B variants in ~13 patients with consistent clustering in the motor domain.

2. **De novo inheritance in all cases:** All identified variants are de novo heterozygous changes, establishing causation and excluding inheritance from unaffected parents.

3. **Functional validation in model organisms:** Zebrafish disease-associated variant models directly recapitulate human neurodevelopmental, skeletal, and neurological phenotypes. Multiple mouse models demonstrate tissue-specific and systemic consequences of KIF5B dysfunction.

4. **Mechanistic understanding:** Dominant-negative mechanism demonstrated through C. elegans studies and human cell culture; specific cellular consequences identified (disrupted organellar organization, impaired transport, defective signaling).

5. **Biological plausibility:** KIF5B's critical roles in mitochondrial transport, axonal transport, cytoskeletal coordination, and organellar organization directly explain the observed multisystem phenotypes.

6. **Recurrent variants:** The p.Thr87Ile variant identified in 2 independent patients with osteogenesis imperfecta provides additional evidence for pathogenicity.

**Key gaps and limitations:**

1. **Limited patient cohort size:** Approximately 13 patients total across all phenotypes is modest for establishing a gene-disease association, though the functional evidence is compelling.

2. **Lack of representation in established diagnostic cohorts:** No patients identified from large cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL), limiting epidemiological data.

3. **Phenotypic heterogeneity:** Three distinct clinical presentations (neurodevelopmental, skeletal dysplasia, osteogenesis imperfecta) suggest possible genotype-phenotype correlations that require further characterization with larger patient numbers.

4. **Limited information on natural history:** Most cases appear to be neonatal or early childhood presentations; long-term follow-up data are limited.

5. **Incomplete functional characterization:** While dominant-negative effects are demonstrated, the specific structural consequences of individual variants on motor protein function could be further detailed.

**Conclusion:**

KIF5B is a well-characterized neurodevelopmental disorder gene with strong evidence for pathogenicity of de novo heterozygous variants. The convergence of clinical evidence (multiple independent case series with de novo variants clustering in the motor domain), functional validation (zebrafish and mouse models recapitulating human phenotypes), and biological evidence (well-characterized molecular functions explaining pleiotropic effects) establishes KIF5B as a bona fide disease gene. The dominant-negative mechanism of pathogenic variants, demonstrated through both model organisms and mechanistic studies, explains the severity of phenotypes in heterozygous carriers. Future work should focus on expanding the patient cohort, characterizing genotype-phenotype correlations, and defining the natural history of KIF5B-associated disorders.
