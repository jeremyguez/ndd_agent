# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: MAEA AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

MAEA (Macrophage Erythroblast Attacher) is a membrane-associated E3 ubiquitin ligase and core catalytic component of the CTLH E3 ubiquitin ligase complex. MAEA functions in multiple cellular processes including DNA repair and replication fork stability, autophagy regulation, hematopoietic stem cell maintenance, and developmental signaling pathways including Wnt/β-catenin regulation.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent studies:** Two independent clinical studies were identified.

**Total patient count:** Approximately 8+ patients with MAEA variants associated with neurodevelopmental phenotypes, though one study (PMID: 40880485) did not specify patient numbers.

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Microcephaly (reported in all variant carriers)
- Craniofacial abnormalities (reported in all variant carriers)
- Ocular defects (reported in all variant carriers)
- Heart malformations (reported in all variant carriers)
- Developmental delay and intellectual disability (DIADEM phenotype in 8 patients; PMID: 41420108)

**Predominant variant type:** Missense variants. Four disease-linked missense variants were identified (R34C, E349G, Y394D, M396R) in the first study (PMID: 40880485), all associated with the same constellation of developmental abnormalities. The second study (PMID: 41420108) identified 8 patients with MAEA variants causing neurodevelopmental disorder but did not specify variant types.

**Cohort information:** No indication that patients were from established diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL).

**Mechanistic support:** Patient-derived cell lines from MAEA variant carriers showed defects in homologous recombination-dependent DNA repair and replication fork restart (PMID: 41420108), directly linking the genetic variants to cellular dysfunction relevant to neurodevelopment.

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**

The evidence is supported by two independent studies with consistent phenotypic findings and functional validation in patient cells. However, the total patient number is modest (8+ patients), and detailed clinical characterization and segregation data are not fully described in the available abstracts.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (three independent studies) and Xenopus laevis (one study).

**Key phenotypes from mouse knockouts:**

*DNA repair and developmental function (PMID: 41420108):*
- Impaired homologous recombination
- Defective replication fork restart and protection
- Reduced RAD51 loading at DNA damage sites
- Developmental delay
- Compromised genome integrity
- Impaired cell division during development

*Hematopoietic function (PMID: 33947846):*
- Severely impaired HSC quiescence
- Lethal myeloproliferative syndrome (full knockout is lethal)
- Stabilized surface expression of hematopoietic cytokine receptors (MPL, FLT3)
- Impaired autophagy flux in HSCs
- Loss of lymphoid potential

*Erythropoiesis (PMID: 30674470):*
- Perinatal anemia (full knockout)
- Defective erythrocyte enucleation (full knockout)
- Perinatal lethality (full knockout)
- Severe reduction of bone marrow macrophages and erythroblasts (macrophage-specific knockout)
- Impaired bone marrow erythropoiesis (postnatal deletion)

*Developmental signaling (PMID: 36444483):*
- Xenopus laevis maea knockdown interferes with head formation
- Altered β-catenin protein stability and increased ubiquitination
- Reduced expression of Wnt target genes (nodal3.1, sia1)
- maea.S overexpression enlarges head structures

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The mouse knockout studies demonstrate that loss of MAEA causes developmental abnormalities, particularly affecting head/craniofacial development (consistent with human microcephaly and craniofacial abnormalities) and impairs DNA repair processes (consistent with the replication stress sensitivity observed in patient cells). The Xenopus data directly support a role in head formation through β-catenin regulation. However, the mouse full knockouts are embryonic/perinatal lethal, suggesting that heterozygous missense variants in humans may have hypomorphic rather than null effects.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**

Multiple independent model systems consistently demonstrate that MAEA loss impairs developmental processes, DNA repair, and cell division—all relevant to the human neurodevelopmental phenotype. The lethality of full knockouts in mice supports the likelihood that human pathogenic variants are hypomorphic missense mutations rather than null alleles.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

MAEA functions as the catalytic E3 ubiquitin ligase subunit of the CTLH complex with multiple critical roles:

1. **DNA Repair and Replication (PMID: 40880485):** MAEA mediates ubiquitylation of Ku80, enabling its removal from replication fork ends and facilitating RAD51 loading for homologous recombination. Disease-linked variants (R34C, E349G, Y394D, M396R) show defective replication stress response, increased sensitivity to replication stress, and excessive nascent DNA degradation.

2. **Autophagy and HSC Maintenance (PMID: 33947846):** MAEA restricts cytokine receptor signaling (MPL, FLT3) via autophagy-mediated degradation, essential for maintaining HSC quiescence.

3. **Developmental Signaling (PMID: 36444483):** MAEA promotes β-catenin ubiquitination and degradation, regulating canonical Wnt signaling during early embryogenesis and head formation.

4. **mTORC1 Regulation (PMID: 41315365):** MAEA ubiquitinates and degrades ZMYND19 and MKLN1, which negatively regulate mTORC1 signaling at the lysosomal membrane.

**Expression pattern:** While specific brain expression data are not detailed in the provided evidence, the consistent neurodevelopmental phenotypes in human patients and the critical role of MAEA in DNA repair, cell division, and developmental signaling pathways indicate relevance to neurodevelopment. The Xenopus studies demonstrate active expression during early embryogenesis.

**Key experimental findings:**
- Patient-derived cell lines show defective HR-dependent DNA repair and replication fork restart (PMID: 41420108)
- Disease-linked variants are defective in replication stress response (PMID: 40880485)
- MAEA knockout mice show impaired RAD51 recruitment and compromised genome integrity (PMID: 41420108)
- MAEA regulates multiple developmental pathways including Wnt/β-catenin signaling (PMID: 36444483)

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The molecular mechanisms are well-characterized, with clear links between MAEA function and processes critical for neurodevelopment (DNA repair, cell division, developmental signaling). Functional validation in patient cells directly demonstrates that disease-linked variants impair these processes.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE-TO-STRONG**

**Key strengths:**
1. Two independent clinical studies identifying patients with MAEA variants and consistent neurodevelopmental phenotypes (microcephaly, craniofacial abnormalities, ocular defects, heart malformations, developmental delay)
2. Functional validation in patient-derived cell lines demonstrating defective DNA repair and replication fork restart
3. Multiple model organisms (mouse, Xenopus) consistently showing that MAEA loss impairs developmental processes, DNA repair, and cell division
4. Well-characterized molecular mechanisms linking MAEA function to neurodevelopmentally relevant pathways (DNA repair, Wnt signaling, cell cycle regulation)
5. Specific disease-linked missense variants (R34C, E349G, Y394D, M396R) shown to be defective in replication stress response

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. Modest total patient number (8+ patients across studies) limits statistical power
2. Limited detailed clinical characterization and segregation data in available reports
3. No information on whether variants segregate with disease in families or occur de novo
4. Mouse full knockouts are embryonic/perinatal lethal, making it difficult to model heterozygous human missense variants directly
5. Incomplete specification of variant types in the larger patient cohort (PMID: 41420108)
6. Limited data on brain-specific MAEA expression patterns

**Conclusion:**

MAEA is a credible neurodevelopmental disorder gene with emerging evidence for autosomal dominant pathogenic variants. The clinical phenotype of microcephaly, craniofacial abnormalities, ocular defects, and developmental delay is supported by functional studies demonstrating that disease-linked variants impair DNA repair and replication fork stability—processes critical for normal neurodevelopment. Multiple model organisms confirm that MAEA loss disrupts developmental processes and genome integrity. While the current evidence is moderate-to-strong, additional studies characterizing more patients, confirming de novo or segregating variants, and determining the inheritance pattern would further establish MAEA as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene.

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. Pathogenic variants in MAEA disrupt DNA replication fork stability and are associated with developmental abnormalities in humans (PMID: 40880485)
2. Loss of CTLH component MAEA impairs DNA repair and replication and leads to developmental delay (PMID: 41420108)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. MAEA is an E3 ubiquitin ligase promoting autophagy and maintenance of haematopoietic stem cells (PMID: 33947846)
2. Maea expressed by macrophages, but not erythroblasts, maintains postnatal murine bone marrow erythroblastic islands (PMID: 30674470)
3. maea affects head formation through ß-catenin degradation during early Xenopus laevis development (PMID: 36444483)

### Biological Evidence
1. The CTLH ubiquitin ligase substrates ZMYND19 and MKLN1 negatively regulate mTORC1 at the lysosomal membrane (PMID: 41315365)
