# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: H2BC8 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

H2BC8 is a histone H2B variant gene that encodes a core histone protein involved in chromatin structure and gene regulation. Its primary known function relates to nucleosome assembly and chromatin remodeling, with emerging evidence suggesting roles in immune cell function and cellular stress responses.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

The available biological evidence for H2BC8 derives from two bioinformatics-based studies examining gene expression patterns in disease contexts:

- **Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA):** H2BC8 was identified as one of eleven hub genes in peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) from OSA patients, with expression levels correlating to continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) treatment response (PMID: 34529670). The gene was incorporated into a random forest predictive model for CPAP therapeutic efficacy that achieved an AUC of 0.92, and showed partial correlation with monocyte infiltration patterns associated with OSA remission.

- **Pyroptosis and Papillary Thyroid Cancer:** H2BC8 was identified as one of nine pyroptosis-related genes (PRGs) in a prognostic model for papillary thyroid cancer (PMID: 38287330). Dysregulated H2BC8 expression was associated with remodeling of the tumor immune microenvironment and patient prognosis, with high PyroScore patients exhibiting 'hot' tumor immunophenotypes. This model demonstrated superior predictive performance compared to four established clinical staging systems.

**Expression pattern:** The identified studies demonstrate H2BC8 expression in peripheral immune cells (monocytes, PBMCs) and tumor-associated immune microenvironments. No specific data on brain or neurodevelopmental tissue expression patterns were reported.

**Key experimental findings:** Both studies employed integrated bioinformatics approaches (gene expression profiling, network analysis, machine learning) rather than functional validation. The evidence suggests H2BC8 involvement in immune cell function and inflammatory responses, but lacks mechanistic characterization.

**Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED**

The biological evidence is limited to correlative bioinformatics findings in non-neurological disease contexts. There is no functional validation, no mechanistic characterization of H2BC8 protein function, and no evidence linking H2BC8 to neurodevelopmental processes.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
- H2BC8 has been identified in two independent bioinformatics studies as a gene with disease-relevant expression patterns
- The OSA study demonstrated strong predictive performance (AUC 0.92) in a clinical context

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- Complete absence of clinical evidence: no reported cases, no patient cohorts, no genetic diagnoses linking H2BC8 variants to any human disease
- No model organism studies to establish functional relevance
- Biological evidence is limited to correlative expression studies in non-neurological contexts (immune cells, cancer)
- No evidence of neurodevelopmental relevance or brain-specific function
- No functional studies demonstrating how H2BC8 variants might cause disease
- No data on variant types, inheritance patterns, or pathogenicity in humans

**Conclusion:**

There is currently insufficient evidence to support H2BC8 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene. While H2BC8 shows expression changes in immune-related disease contexts, there are no clinical cases, no model organism data, and no mechanistic evidence linking this gene to neurodevelopmental phenotypes or autosomal dominant inheritance. Establishing H2BC8 as a disease gene would require identification of patients with pathogenic variants and associated clinical phenotypes, functional validation studies, and characterization of the molecular mechanisms underlying any pathogenic effects.
