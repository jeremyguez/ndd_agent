# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: ATXN7L3 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

ATXN7L3 is a non-enzymatic scaffolding component of the SAGA deubiquitinase module that coordinates the activity of multiple histone H2B deubiquitinating enzymes (USP22, USP27X, USP51) to regulate H2B monoubiquitination (H2Bub1) levels (PMID: 27132940). The gene plays essential roles in transcriptional regulation, cellular proliferation, and embryonic development through epigenetic control of histone modifications.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent reports:** Two independent case series/reports were identified.

**Total patient count:** Approximately 10 patients with de novo ATXN7L3 variants associated with neurodevelopmental phenotypes (9 patients from PMID: 38753057 and 1 patient from PMID: 41760364).

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Global motor developmental delay (100% of reported cases)
- Language/speech developmental delay (100% of reported cases)
- Hypotonia (100% of reported cases)
- Distinctive facial dysmorphism including hypertelorism, epicanthal folds, blepharoptosis, small nose, small mouth, low-set ears, and posteriorly rotated ears (100% of reported cases)
- Pre-axial polydactyly (reported in 1 patient; PMID: 41760364)

**Predominant variant type:** Loss-of-function (LoF) variants exclusively. All identified variants were nonsense mutations or frameshift variants predicted to result in truncated proteins (e.g., c.340C>T p.(Arg114Ter), c.236G>A p.(Trp79*)).

**Inheritance pattern:** All variants were de novo heterozygous variants, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance with complete penetrance.

**Cohort source:** No indication that patients were from established diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL). Cases were identified through exome/whole-genome sequencing and GeneMatcher.

**Biochemical validation:** Both studies provided functional evidence supporting pathogenicity. Patient fibroblasts showed reduced ATXN7L3 protein levels and impaired deubiquitylation activity with increased histone H2Bub1 levels (PMID: 38753057). One study demonstrated marked reduction in ATXN7L3 transcript levels and 3D protein modeling showing substantial distortions in secondary structure and loss of C-terminal domain (PMID: 41760364).

**Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG**

The clinical evidence is strengthened by: (1) multiple independent case reports with consistent phenotypic features; (2) de novo variants in unaffected parents establishing causality; (3) biochemical characterization confirming loss of protein function; (4) perfect segregation with disease phenotype; and (5) consistency with predicted pathogenic mechanism based on biological function.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (Mus musculus)

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:** Atxn7l3-null mouse embryos exhibit developmental delay at embryonic day 7.5, consistent with the human neurodevelopmental phenotype (PMID: 33731875). The mouse model demonstrates that ATXN7L3 is essential for normal embryonic development.

**Molecular consequences in model:** Atxn7l3-null embryos show strongly increased global H2Bub1 levels and selective deregulation of gene subsets in embryonic stem cells and fibroblasts, indicating that loss of ATXN7L3 function leads to impaired H2B deubiquitylation (PMID: 33731875).

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The developmental delay observed in Atxn7l3-null mouse embryos directly parallels the global developmental delay seen in human patients with de novo ATXN7L3 loss-of-function variants. The biochemical mechanism (increased H2Bub1 levels) is consistent between mouse and human studies.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**

The mouse knockout data provides strong support by demonstrating that complete loss of ATXN7L3 is embryonic lethal or causes severe developmental impairment, establishing the gene's essential role in development. The biochemical findings in mouse tissues directly validate the pathogenic mechanism observed in human patient cells.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

ATXN7L3 functions as a core component of the SAGA (Spt-Ada-Gcn5-Acetyltransferase) deubiquitinase module. It acts as a non-enzymatic scaffolding protein that coordinates the activity of multiple deubiquitinating enzymes (USP22, USP27X, USP51) to regulate H2B monoubiquitination (H2Bub1) levels (PMID: 27132940). ATXN7L3 interacts with ENY2 to orchestrate deubiquitinase complex assembly and function.

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**

While specific brain expression data are not detailed in the provided evidence, the essential role of ATXN7L3 in embryonic development (PMID: 33731875) and its function in regulating transcription through histone modifications strongly suggest neurodevelopmental relevance. The gene's role in selective gene expression regulation in embryonic stem cells and fibroblasts indicates importance for developmental processes.

**Key experimental findings:**

1. **H2Bub1 regulation:** ATXN7L3 depletion results in increased global H2Bub1 levels, indicating impaired deubiquitylation activity (PMID: 27132940, PMID: 33731875). Patient fibroblasts from individuals with ATXN7L3 loss-of-function variants show the same biochemical signature (PMID: 38753057).

2. **Transcriptional regulation:** ATXN7L3 dynamically regulates H2Bub levels during transcription elongation and in response to cellular stress (PMID: 36659919). The gene functions as a coactivator for transcription factor-mediated gene expression (PMID: 33186807).

3. **Developmental essentiality:** Atxn7l3-null mouse embryos show developmental delay at E7.5, demonstrating the gene's essential role in early development (PMID: 33731875).

4. **Isoform-specific regulation:** A cytoplasmic ATXN7L3B isoform can compete with nuclear ATXN7L3 for binding partners, suggesting potential for dominant-negative effects (PMID: 27601583).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is strong because: (1) ATXN7L3's molecular function is well-characterized through multiple independent studies; (2) the biochemical mechanism (H2Bub1 dysregulation) is consistently demonstrated across different cell types and experimental systems; (3) the gene's essential role in development is established in model organisms; and (4) the pathogenic mechanism in patients (loss of deubiquitylation activity) directly correlates with biochemical findings in functional studies.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths of the evidence:**

1. **Consistent clinical phenotype:** All reported patients with de novo ATXN7L3 loss-of-function variants present with a highly consistent triad of global developmental delay, hypotonia, and distinctive facial dysmorphism, establishing a recognizable clinical syndrome (PMID: 38753057, PMID: 41760364).

2. **Mechanistic validation:** The pathogenic mechanism is well-established through biochemical studies showing that ATXN7L3 loss-of-function leads to impaired H2B deubiquitylation and increased H2Bub1 levels, with this mechanism validated in both patient cells and model organisms.

3. **De novo inheritance pattern:** All identified variants are de novo heterozygous mutations in unaffected parents, establishing causality and supporting autosomal dominant inheritance with complete penetrance.

4. **Functional studies:** Multiple independent lines of evidence (patient fibroblasts, mouse knockouts, cell culture studies) consistently demonstrate the same molecular consequence of ATXN7L3 loss.

5. **Model organism support:** Mouse knockout data demonstrates embryonic lethality/severe developmental impairment, providing strong biological plausibility for the human neurodevelopmental phenotype.

**Key gaps or limitations:**

1. **Limited patient cohort:** Only 10 patients have been reported to date, which is a relatively small number for establishing a novel neurodevelopmental disorder gene. Larger case series would strengthen the evidence.

2. **Incomplete phenotypic characterization:** While core features are consistent, detailed neuroimaging, developmental assessments, and long-term follow-up data are not provided in the available abstracts.

3. **Absence of model organism neurodevelopmental data:** While mouse embryonic lethality is documented, detailed characterization of neurodevelopmental phenotypes in conditional knockout models or heterozygous mice is not reported in the provided evidence.

4. **Limited mechanistic detail on neurodevelopmental pathogenesis:** While H2Bub1 dysregulation is established, the specific downstream gene expression changes that lead to developmental delay and hypotonia remain incompletely characterized.

5. **No population frequency data:** Carrier frequency in general populations is not provided, limiting assessment of whether ATXN7L3 variants are truly rare.

**Conclusion:**

ATXN7L3 is a strong candidate gene for autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder caused by heterozygous loss-of-function variants. The evidence is supported by consistent clinical phenotypes across independent case reports, de novo inheritance pattern, biochemical validation of pathogenic mechanism in patient cells, and strong biological plausibility from model organism and functional studies demonstrating the gene's essential role in development and transcriptional regulation. The identification of a recognizable clinical syndrome (developmental delay, hypotonia, and distinctive facial features) associated with ATXN7L3 variants, recently termed Harel-Tora Neurodevelopmental Syndrome (HATONS) when pre-axial polydactyly is present (PMID: 41760364), further supports the gene's role in neurodevelopmental disease.
