# ZMYND8 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE: EVIDENCE REPORT

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

ZMYND8 (Zinc Finger MYND-Type Containing 8) is a chromatin reader protein containing PWWP and MYND domains that functions as an epigenetic effector regulating transcription through histone interactions and protein-protein interactions. The gene is broadly expressed across all brain regions with highest expression during early stages of brain development, and plays critical roles in transcriptional regulation across multiple cellular contexts including cancer biology and normal development.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of Studies and Patients:**
One primary clinical study (PMID: 35916866) reported 11 unrelated patients with de novo ZMYND8 variants causing an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder with cardiac malformations. One additional study (PMID: 32530565) identified a single patient with a de novo truncating variant in PJA1 (not ZMYND8 directly), though this study appears to have been included in the dataset due to methodological overlap rather than direct ZMYND8 evidence.

**Phenotypes Observed:**
The 11 patients with ZMYND8 variants presented with a consistent phenotypic spectrum:
- Intellectual disability (all patients)
- Cardiovascular malformations (all patients)
- Ophthalmologic anomalies (all patients)
- Minor skeletal anomalies (all patients)

**Variant Types:**
The cohort included 2 truncating (loss-of-function) variants and 9 missense variants, with 1 missense variant being recurrent. Functional evidence demonstrated that PWWP domain variants abolish interaction with Drebrin, while MYND domain variants disrupt interaction with GATAD2A, indicating that both LoF and missense variants disrupt critical protein-protein interactions.

**Cohort Source:**
The study represents an international collaboration but does not specify enrollment from established diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, or GEL).

**Diagnostic Evidence Strength:**
De novo status was established in 10 of 11 patients. Functional evidence included yeast two-hybrid assays demonstrating loss of protein-protein interactions and Drosophila knockdown studies showing decreased habituation learning consistent with cognitive dysfunction.

**Strength of Clinical Evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) multiple independent de novo variants in unrelated individuals; (2) consistent phenotypic presentation across patients; (3) functional validation demonstrating disruption of known protein interactions; and (4) model organism evidence supporting cognitive dysfunction. The main limitation is the relatively small sample size (11 patients) and lack of enrollment from large diagnostic cohorts.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms Studied:**
Zebrafish and mouse models have been used to study ZMYND8 function, though primarily in cancer-related contexts rather than neurodevelopmental phenotypes.

**Neurological and Developmental Phenotypes:**
The most directly relevant neurodevelopmental evidence comes from Drosophila studies (PMID: 35916866), where neuronal knockdown of the ZMYND8 ortholog resulted in decreased habituation learning, a phenotype consistent with cognitive dysfunction observed in human patients. This finding directly supports a role for ZMYND8 in learning and memory processes.

**Cardiac Phenotypes:**
Mouse studies provide relevant evidence for the cardiac manifestations observed in patients. A conditional knockout study (PMID: 41423035) demonstrated that cardiomyocyte-specific Hmgb1 deletion (downstream of ZMYND8-mediated ubiquitination) improved cardiac muscle contraction, calcium regulation, and reduced cardiomyocyte apoptosis, preventing trametinib-induced cardiac complications. This suggests ZMYND8 plays a functional role in cardiac homeostasis.

**Consistency with Human Phenotype:**
The Drosophila learning deficit directly parallels the intellectual disability observed in patients. The cardiac findings in mouse models are consistent with the cardiovascular malformations reported in the clinical cohort, though the mouse studies examined functional cardiac parameters rather than developmental malformations.

**Mendelian Ratios and Lethality:**
No data on Mendelian ratios or lethality were reported in the available model organism studies.

**Strength of Model Organism Evidence: MODERATE**

The evidence is strengthened by direct demonstration of cognitive dysfunction in Drosophila and cardiac functional abnormalities in mice. However, the evidence is limited by: (1) lack of complete knockout or knockin models specifically designed to recapitulate human neurodevelopmental phenotypes; (2) most studies focused on cancer biology rather than developmental neurobiology; and (3) absence of data on developmental malformations (cardiac, ophthalmologic, skeletal) in model organisms.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Molecular Function and Pathways:**
ZMYND8 functions as a histone reader and chromatin effector that regulates transcription through multiple mechanisms:

- **Chromatin Reader Function:** ZMYND8 binds to chromatin regions with specific histone modifications (H3K4me1-H3K14ac) and recruits transcriptional machinery (PMID: 40102673)
- **Protein-Protein Interactions:** ZMYND8 interacts with multiple regulatory proteins including Drebrin, GATAD2A, BRD4, FOXM1, and USP7 (PMID: 35916866, PMID: 34358447, PMID: 39128723)
- **Transcriptional Regulation:** ZMYND8 directly activates transcription of lineage-specific genes including IRF8, MYC, and c-Myc through enhancer-promoter interactions (PMID: 34358447, PMID: 40579459)

**Brain Expression and Neurodevelopmental Relevance:**
ZMYND8 is broadly expressed across all brain regions with highest expression during early stages of brain development (PMID: 35916866), making it particularly relevant to neurodevelopmental processes. The broad expression pattern and developmental timing are consistent with a role in cognitive development.

**Key Experimental Findings:**
- Missense variants in the PWWP domain abolish Drebrin interaction; missense variants in the MYND domain disrupt GATAD2A interaction (PMID: 35916866)
- ZMYND8 regulates NRF2-mediated antioxidant responses in breast cancer stem cells, suggesting a role in cellular stress responses (PMID: 38488001)
- ZMYND8 stabilization by USP7 promotes transcription of ZEB1 and VEGFA (PMID: 39128723)
- ZMYND8 orchestrates epigenetic programming through chromatin reader activity and Mediator complex recruitment (PMID: 40102673, PMID: 33932349)

**Strength of Biological Evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is comprehensive, demonstrating: (1) well-characterized molecular function as a chromatin reader; (2) multiple validated protein-protein interactions disrupted by patient variants; (3) relevant brain expression pattern with developmental timing; (4) mechanistic understanding of how variants disrupt function; and (5) consistency between in vitro and in vivo findings. The main limitation is that most biological studies focus on cancer biology rather than neurodevelopmental processes, though the molecular mechanisms are likely relevant to both contexts.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall Evidence Level: STRONG**

**Key Strengths:**
1. **Clinical Evidence:** Multiple independent de novo variants in unrelated patients with consistent phenotypic presentation (intellectual disability, cardiac malformations, ophthalmologic anomalies, skeletal anomalies) and functional validation demonstrating disruption of critical protein-protein interactions
2. **Biological Evidence:** Well-characterized molecular function as a chromatin reader with validated interactions with Drebrin and GATAD2A; high expression in developing brain with developmental timing consistent with neurodevelopmental disorder
3. **Model Organism Evidence:** Direct demonstration of cognitive dysfunction (decreased habituation learning) in Drosophila knockdown; cardiac functional abnormalities in mouse models consistent with human phenotype
4. **Mechanistic Coherence:** Clear pathway from variant-induced loss of protein-protein interactions to disruption of transcriptional regulation and cellular dysfunction

**Key Limitations:**
1. **Sample Size:** Only 11 patients reported in the primary clinical study, limiting statistical power
2. **Cohort Source:** Patients not enrolled from large diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL), reducing generalizability
3. **Model Organism Gap:** Lack of complete knockout or knockin mouse models specifically designed to recapitulate human neurodevelopmental and cardiac developmental phenotypes
4. **Cancer-Focused Literature:** Most biological studies focus on cancer biology; direct evidence for neurodevelopmental function is limited to Drosophila learning assays

**Conclusion:**
ZMYND8 is a well-supported neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous pathogenic variants causing an autosomal dominant disorder characterized by intellectual disability, cardiac malformations, ophthalmologic anomalies, and skeletal abnormalities. The evidence is strengthened by consistent clinical presentation across multiple unrelated patients, functional validation of variant effects on protein-protein interactions, and model organism evidence of cognitive dysfunction. Future studies should focus on expanding the clinical cohort through large diagnostic networks and developing mouse models that recapitulate the full spectrum of human developmental phenotypes.

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. De Novo ZMYND8 variants result in an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder with cardiac malformations (PMID: 35916866)
2. A recurrent PJA1 variant in trigonocephaly and neurodevelopmental disorders (PMID: 32530565)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. MEK inhibitor induces cardiac complications by preventing ZMYND8-mediated ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of HMGB1 (PMID: 41423035)

### Biological Evidence
1. Targeting the histone reader ZMYND8 inhibits antiandrogen-induced neuroendocrine tumor transdifferentiation of prostate cancer (PMID: 40102673)
2. ZMYND8-regulated IRF8 transcription axis is an acute myeloid leukemia dependency (PMID: 34358447)
3. USP7 deubiquitinates epigenetic reader ZMYND8 to promote breast cancer cell migration and invasion (PMID: 39128723)
4. ZMYND8 promotes the Warburg effect and tumorigenesis through c-Myc activation in pancreatic cancer (PMID: 40579459)
5. ZMYND8 protects breast cancer stem cells against oxidative stress and ferroptosis through activation of NRF2 (PMID: 38488001)
6. The ZMYND8-regulated mevalonate pathway endows YAP-high intestinal cancer with metabolic vulnerability (PMID: 33932349)
