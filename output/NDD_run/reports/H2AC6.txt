# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: H2AC6 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

H2AC6 (H2A clustered histone 6) is a histone variant belonging to the H2A family of core histones involved in chromatin structure and gene regulation. While its primary characterized functions relate to nucleosome dynamics and chromatin remodeling, emerging evidence suggests roles in cellular stress responses, including PANoptosis regulation and lactylation-dependent processes.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

H2AC6 has been identified in several disease-relevant biological contexts, though none specifically related to neurodevelopmental pathways:

- **PANoptosis and intervertebral disc degeneration:** H2AC6 was identified as one of five core PANoptosis-related genes significantly upregulated in degenerated intervertebral discs. In IL-1β-induced nucleus pulposus cell models, baicalin treatment downregulated H2AC6 expression at both mRNA and protein levels, suggesting a role in degenerative processes (PMID: 40463856).

- **Lactylation and disc degeneration:** H2AC6 was identified as a lactylation-related gene positively correlated with increased risk of intervertebral disc degeneration through multi-omics summary-based Mendelian randomization analysis combining GWAS, eQTL, and mQTL data (PMID: 40988222).

- **miRNA regulation in renal protection:** H2AC6 was identified as a target of miR-155-5p in a protective regulatory network against angiotensin II-induced hypertensive renal injury, with miR-155-5p expression negatively correlated with H2AC6 expression (PMID: 34926670).

- **Cancer-related expression:** H2AC6 was identified as a novel differentially expressed gene critical for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma survival through comparative transcriptomic analysis across six global populations (PMID: 37641095).

- **Exercise response in long COVID:** H2AC6 was among the top 5 downregulated genes in serum extracellular vesicles at peak exercise following 10-week exercise training in long COVID patients (q < 0.05), suggesting modulation in response to physiological adaptation (PMID: 41588020).

**Expression pattern:** The available evidence does not specifically characterize H2AC6 expression in brain or neurodevelopmental tissues. Identified expression changes relate to intervertebral disc tissue, renal endothelial cells, esophageal cancer, and circulating extracellular vesicles.

**Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED**

While H2AC6 has been identified in multiple biological contexts, the evidence is limited to observational studies (GWAS, transcriptomics, pathway analysis) and cell culture models. There is no mechanistic evidence linking H2AC6 to neurodevelopmental processes, and no evidence of brain-specific expression or function.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
- H2AC6 has been identified in multiple independent biological studies, suggesting it participates in cellular regulatory networks
- Evidence spans multiple disease contexts (degenerative, inflammatory, neoplastic), indicating broad biological relevance
- Some studies employ rigorous methodologies (Mendelian randomization, multi-omics integration)

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- Complete absence of clinical evidence: no case reports, case series, or patient cohorts with H2AC6 variants
- No model organism studies to establish causality or characterize loss-of-function phenotypes
- No evidence of neurodevelopmental relevance; identified associations relate to degenerative, inflammatory, and neoplastic processes
- No characterization of H2AC6 expression in brain or neurodevelopmental tissues
- No functional studies demonstrating how H2AC6 variants might cause disease
- Identified associations are correlative rather than causative

**Conclusion:**

There is currently insufficient evidence to support H2AC6 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous autosomal dominant pathogenic variants. While H2AC6 participates in multiple biological pathways relevant to cellular stress and degeneration, no clinical cases, model organism evidence, or neurodevelopmental-specific functional data exist. Establishing H2AC6 as a disease gene would require identification of patients with pathogenic variants, characterization of associated clinical phenotypes, and functional validation in appropriate model systems.
