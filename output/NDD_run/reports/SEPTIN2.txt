# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: SEPTIN2 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

SEPTIN2 encodes a member of the septin family of GTPase-binding proteins that form filamentous cytoskeletal structures essential for cell division, morphogenesis, and membrane organization. SEPTIN2 functions as a critical component of septin complexes involved in axonal development, neuronal polarity, ciliogenesis, and myelin integrity.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of Studies:** 1 case series

**Total Patients:** 6 patients (5 unrelated individuals plus 1 mother-daughter duo)

**Most Common Phenotypes Observed:**
- Cognitive impairment (primary phenotype)
- Neuronal polarity defects
- Axon initial segment (AIS) dysfunction
- Axonal shortening
- Electrophysiological hypoexcitability

**Variant Type:** Missense mutations (specific variants not detailed in available abstract)

**Inheritance Pattern:** Dominant negative mechanism proposed; mutations predicted to cause dominant negative blockade on septin octamers by precluding SEPTIN2 homodimerization

**Cohort Information:** No indication of recruitment from known diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL)

**Molecular Pathology:** Loss of Ankyrin G in the AIS and aberrant MAP2 localization observed in mutant-expressing neurons, indicating disruption of critical AIS protein scaffolding (PMID: 41408595)

**Strength of Clinical Evidence: LIMITED**

The clinical evidence is limited to a single case series with only 6 patients total. While the phenotype is well-characterized and the molecular mechanism is plausible (dominant negative effect on septin octamer assembly), the small sample size and lack of detailed variant information restrict the strength of evidence. Confirmation in additional independent families and detailed variant characterization would strengthen the evidence base.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms Studied:** Mouse (multiple independent studies)

**Key Neurological Phenotypes:**

- **Neural tube defects and ciliogenesis:** Septin2 knockdown impairs neural tube closure and ciliogenesis through disruption of the septin2-6-7 complex and altered GTP binding activity (PMID: 28432198). This directly parallels the neurodevelopmental phenotype observed in human patients.

- **Myelin integrity:** Conditional knockout of septin-regulating proteins (CDC42-effector proteins) leads to myelin pathology with outfoldings and disorganized septin filaments, including reduced SEPTIN2 abundance (PMID: 41616085). This indicates SEPTIN2 is essential for CNS myelin structural integrity.

- **Spermatogenesis and fertility:** Septin2 knockdown causes impaired spermatogenesis, spermatogonial proliferation defects, and G1-S phase arrest (PMID: 40487848). While not directly neurodevelopmental, this demonstrates SEPTIN2's role in cell cycle regulation and proliferation.

**Lethality and Viability:** All studies report viable animals, indicating that complete loss of Septin2 function is not embryonic lethal in mice, though this does not exclude the possibility of neurodevelopmental impairment.

**Mendelian Ratios:** Not reported in available studies

**Consistency with Human Phenotype:** The neural tube defect and ciliogenesis findings are highly consistent with the cognitive impairment and neuronal polarity defects observed in human patients. Both point to disruption of neuronal development and axonal organization. The myelin pathology is also consistent with potential neurological dysfunction.

**Strength of Model Organism Evidence: MODERATE**

Multiple mouse studies demonstrate critical roles for SEPTIN2 in neural development, ciliogenesis, and myelin integrity. However, the available studies primarily use knockdown or conditional knockout approaches rather than heterozygous missense mutations that would directly model the human dominant negative variants. The neural tube defect and ciliogenesis findings provide strong mechanistic support for the human neurodevelopmental phenotype.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known Pathways and Molecular Functions:**

SEPTIN2 participates in multiple cellular processes relevant to neurodevelopment:

1. **Axon Initial Segment (AIS) Organization:** SEPTIN2 is required for maintenance of the AIS through interaction with Ankyrin G and proper localization of MAP2, critical for neuronal polarity and axonal function (PMID: 41408595).

2. **Ciliogenesis and Neural Tube Closure:** Folate-dependent methylation of SEPTIN2 governs its GTP binding activity and complex formation with SEPTIN6 and SEPTIN7, essential for ciliogenesis during neural tube closure (PMID: 28432198).

3. **Myelin Structural Integrity:** SEPTIN2 is a component of septin filaments in myelin that maintain structural organization; disruption leads to myelin pathology (PMID: 41616085).

4. **Cytoskeletal Organization:** SEPTIN2 regulates actin cytoskeletal dynamics through interaction with ARHGAP25 (a Rac1 GAP), controlling lamellipodia formation and cell spreading (PMID: 40205813).

5. **Cell Cycle Regulation:** SEPTIN2 expression is cell-cycle regulated (highest in G2/M phase) and interacts with CDK2 to stabilize septin complexes during proliferation (PMID: 30444001).

6. **Stress Response:** SEPTIN2 expression is induced by ER stress and acts as a negative regulator of macrophage autoactivation through HSP5 modification (PMID: 37978190).

**Expression Pattern:** While the available biological evidence does not explicitly detail SEPTIN2 brain expression patterns, the clinical and model organism evidence strongly indicates expression in developing neurons, the AIS, myelin-forming cells, and ciliated cells during neural tube development.

**Key Experimental Findings:**

- Dominant negative mutations disrupt septin octamer assembly and prevent SEPTIN2 homodimerization (PMID: 41408595)
- Loss of Ankyrin G localization in the AIS and aberrant MAP2 distribution in mutant neurons (PMID: 41408595)
- Septin2 phosphoinositide-binding residues are functionally important for cellular processes (PMID: 31865373)
- S-nitrosylation of SEPTIN2 at cysteine 111 alters protein-protein interactions (PMID: 38357802)

**Strength of Biological Evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is strong, with multiple independent studies demonstrating SEPTIN2's critical roles in AIS organization, ciliogenesis, myelin integrity, and cytoskeletal regulation. The mechanistic pathways are well-characterized, and the molecular consequences of SEPTIN2 dysfunction are clearly documented. The evidence directly supports how mutations could impair neurodevelopment.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall Evidence Level: MODERATE**

**Key Strengths:**

1. **Mechanistic coherence:** The clinical phenotype (cognitive impairment, neuronal polarity defects, AIS dysfunction) is directly explained by SEPTIN2's known molecular functions in AIS organization and neuronal development.

2. **Multiple biological pathways:** SEPTIN2 dysfunction affects multiple neurodevelopmentally relevant processes (AIS maintenance, ciliogenesis, myelin integrity, cell cycle regulation), providing multiple mechanisms by which mutations could cause disease.

3. **Model organism support:** Mouse studies demonstrate that SEPTIN2 disruption causes neural tube defects and ciliogenesis impairment, directly paralleling the human neurodevelopmental phenotype.

4. **Dominant negative mechanism:** The proposed dominant negative mechanism (disruption of septin octamer assembly) is plausible and consistent with missense mutations in structural proteins.

**Key Limitations:**

1. **Very limited clinical data:** Only 6 patients from a single case series; no confirmation in independent cohorts or large diagnostic databases (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, etc.).

2. **Lack of variant detail:** Specific SEPTIN2 variants are not detailed in the available abstract, limiting assessment of variant pathogenicity and segregation patterns.

3. **Model organism-human mismatch:** Available mouse studies use knockdown or conditional knockout approaches rather than heterozygous missense mutations that would directly model the human dominant negative variants.

4. **No functional validation of specific variants:** The clinical report does not include functional studies (e.g., septin octamer assembly assays, AIS organization assays) to confirm that the identified variants cause the predicted dominant negative effect.

5. **Limited phenotypic detail:** The clinical report lacks detailed neuropsychological assessment, imaging findings, or longitudinal follow-up data.

**Conclusion:**

SEPTIN2 shows MODERATE evidence as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous dominant negative variants. The biological and model organism evidence strongly supports a role in neuronal development, AIS organization, and ciliogenesis. However, the clinical evidence is limited to a single small case series (6 patients) without detailed variant characterization or confirmation in independent cohorts. Additional clinical cases, detailed variant analysis, functional validation of specific mutations, and recruitment into large diagnostic cohorts would substantially strengthen the evidence for SEPTIN2 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene.
