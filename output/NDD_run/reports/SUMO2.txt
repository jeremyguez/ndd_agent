# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: SUMO2 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

SUMO2 is a small ubiquitin-like modifier protein that functions as a post-translational modification factor, conjugating to target proteins to regulate their stability, localization, and protein-protein interactions. SUMO2 plays critical roles in proteostasis, DNA damage response, and cellular stress responses, with emerging evidence for functions in neuronal development and synaptic plasticity.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of case reports/series:** 1 case report identified

**Total patients:** 2 patients (1 proband and 1 affected mother)

**Phenotypes observed:** The reported family presented with a complex developmental phenotype including skeletal abnormalities, syndactyly, and cardiac defects (ASHG 2022 - Maternally Inherited novel SUMO2 variant associated with a complex phenotype).

**Variant type:** Heterozygous start codon variant (c.2 T>A: p.Met1?) affecting translation initiation.

**Cohort source:** Not from established diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL).

**Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED**

Only a single family with two affected individuals has been reported in the literature. While the variant segregates with disease in a maternal inheritance pattern and in silico analysis suggests pathogenicity, the clinical evidence base is minimal. No additional independent case reports or larger case series have been published to date.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (Mus musculus)

**Key neurological phenotypes from knockouts:**

The most directly relevant evidence comes from a knock-in mouse model with elevated SUMO2 expression in an Alzheimer's disease context. Elevated SUMO2 expression prevented cognitive impairment, synaptic impairment, and long-term potentiation (LTP) deficits in APP transgenic mice, and prevented cognitive deficits without affecting amyloid pathology (PMID: 40047257). Additionally, a SUMO2 analogue (SBT02) prophylactically halted progression of AD-associated deficits and reversed pre-existing cognitive and synaptic impairments in advanced pathology.

**Lethality and viability:** All SUMO2-related mouse models reported were viable, with no embryonic lethality observed in full knockout or conditional knockout contexts. However, the ASHG 2022 abstract notes that SUMO2 knockdown in animal studies resulted in embryonic lethality and altered expression of embryonic development genes (CDX2, OCT4, NANOG), suggesting that complete loss of SUMO2 function may be incompatible with normal development.

**Mendelian ratio deviations:** Not reported in the available literature.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The reported human case involves developmental abnormalities (skeletal, cardiac), which aligns with the embryonic lethality and altered developmental gene expression observed in SUMO2 knockdown models. The cognitive/synaptic protective effects of SUMO2 elevation in the Alzheimer's model suggest SUMO2 is important for neuronal function, though this does not directly address whether haploinsufficiency causes neurodevelopmental disease.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**

While mouse models demonstrate that SUMO2 is essential for normal development and critical for neuronal function, the evidence is indirect. The available knockout/knockdown data support SUMO2's importance but do not directly model heterozygous loss-of-function variants in a neurodevelopmental context. The Alzheimer's model demonstrates gain-of-function benefits but does not address haploinsufficiency.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

SUMO2 functions primarily in proteostasis through convergence with ubiquitylation to mediate protein degradation via SUMO-targeted ubiquitin ligases (STUbLs), which preferentially bind and ubiquitylate poly-SUMO2/3 modified proteins (PMID: 38201212). SUMO2 also plays critical roles in:

- **Inflammasome regulation:** SUMO2 conjugation to NLRP3 by the E3 SUMO ligase TRIM28 stabilizes NLRP3 and facilitates inflammasome activation (PMID: 34373456).

- **Genome stability:** SUMO2 fusion to DHX9 enhances association with R-loop-associated proteins and reduces R-loop accumulation, alleviating genome instability (PMID: 39019926).

- **Mitochondrial dynamics:** SUMO2/3 binding to Drp1 suppresses mitochondrial fission and promotes fusion, with SUMO2/3 overexpression providing neuroprotection in ischemia-reperfusion injury models (PMID: 38615367).

- **Synaptic function:** SUMO2 elevation prevents synaptic impairment and LTP deficits in Alzheimer's disease models (PMID: 40047257).

**Expression pattern:** While specific brain expression data are not detailed in the provided evidence, the functional studies demonstrate SUMO2 activity in neuronal cells (SH-SY5Y cells) and in vivo neurological models, indicating neuronal expression and relevance.

**Key experimental findings:**

- SUMO2 modification of target proteins is dynamically regulated in response to cellular stress and DNA damage (PMID: 36793866).
- SUMO2 lactylation at K11 represents a novel post-translational modification that modulates ferroptosis resistance (PMID: 41057295).
- SUMO2 mediates protein-protein interactions critical for multiple cellular processes through SUMO-interacting motifs (PMID: 39019926, PMID: 37991737).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence demonstrates that SUMO2 is a multifunctional protein with well-characterized roles in proteostasis, genome stability, mitochondrial function, and synaptic plasticity. The evidence base is substantial, with multiple independent studies demonstrating SUMO2's involvement in cellular processes relevant to neurodevelopment and neuronal function.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: LIMITED**

**Key strengths:**

1. **Biological plausibility:** SUMO2 has well-established roles in proteostasis, genome stability, and synaptic function, all processes critical for normal neurodevelopment.

2. **Model organism support:** Mouse studies demonstrate that SUMO2 is essential for normal development and critical for neuronal function, with embryonic lethality observed in complete loss-of-function contexts.

3. **Functional evidence:** Multiple independent studies demonstrate SUMO2's involvement in pathways relevant to neurodevelopmental disorders (synaptic plasticity, mitochondrial function, genome stability).

**Key gaps and limitations:**

1. **Minimal clinical evidence:** Only a single family with two affected individuals has been reported, with no additional independent case reports or larger case series in the literature.

2. **Lack of haploinsufficiency models:** Available mouse models focus on complete knockout or overexpression; no heterozygous knockout models specifically designed to test haploinsufficiency in a neurodevelopmental context have been reported.

3. **Incomplete phenotypic characterization:** The reported human case involves skeletal, cardiac, and developmental abnormalities, but detailed neurological assessment and neurodevelopmental testing are not described in the available abstract.

4. **No functional validation of the variant:** The reported SUMO2 start codon variant has not been functionally validated in patient-derived cells or in vitro systems.

**Conclusion:**

SUMO2 is biologically plausible as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene based on its critical roles in proteostasis, genome stability, and synaptic function, and mouse models confirm its essential role in development. However, the clinical evidence base is currently insufficient to establish SUMO2 as a confirmed neurodevelopmental disorder gene, consisting of only a single family report. Additional independent case reports, functional validation of variants, and heterozygous mouse models would be needed to establish a robust genotype-phenotype correlation and confirm SUMO2's role in autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disease.
