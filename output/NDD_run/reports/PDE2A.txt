# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: PDE2A AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

PDE2A (phosphodiesterase 2A) is a dual-substrate cyclic nucleotide phosphodiesterase that hydrolyzes both cAMP and cGMP, with particularly high expression in brain regions including cortex, hippocampus, and striatum (PMID: 27321640). PDE2A plays critical roles in regulating intraneuronal signaling, synaptic plasticity, and developmental processes including cell proliferation, apoptosis, and differentiation.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent case reports/series:** 5 publications describing PDE2A-associated disease

**Total patient count:** Approximately 16 affected individuals across multiple families
- 6 patients with homozygous c.1514T>C, p.(Phe505Ser) variant (PMID: 37317634)
- 6 patients with homozygous c.1490T>C, p.(Phe497Ser) variant (ASHG 2022)
- 3 patients with biallelic variants including compound heterozygotes (PMID: 32467598)
- 1 patient with homozygous c.1439A>G, p.(Asp480Gly) variant (PMID: 29392776)

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Paroxysmal dyskinesia (present in 4 of 5 studies)
- Cognitive impairment/intellectual disability (present in 4 of 5 studies)
- Seizures/drug-refractory seizures (present in 3 of 5 studies)
- Speech impairment (present in 2 of 5 studies)
- Developmental delay (present in 3 of 5 studies)
- Abnormal mitochondrial morphology (present in 2 of 5 studies)

**Predominant variant type:** Missense variants (4 of 5 studies report missense mutations as primary findings); loss-of-function variants also documented (nonsense, splice-site variants in PMID: 32467598)

**Cohort source:** No patients from established international cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL) identified. Two studies report Pakistani families with founder mutations identified through homozygosity mapping and haplotype analysis (PMID: 37317634; ASHG 2022), suggesting geographic clustering.

**Important caveat regarding heterozygous variants:** All published clinical cases involve **biallelic (homozygous or compound heterozygous) variants**, not heterozygous pathogenic variants. The clinical evidence does not currently support autosomal dominant inheritance with heterozygous pathogenic variants causing disease.

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) multiple independent families with segregating variants; (2) founder mutations with haplotype confirmation; (3) consistent phenotypic clustering (paroxysmal dyskinesia + intellectual disability); (4) functional characterization showing reduced enzymatic activity. However, evidence is limited by: (1) small patient numbers; (2) all cases biallelic rather than heterozygous; (3) limited ethnic diversity (predominantly Pakistani families); (4) lack of large cohort studies.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (primary), rat (secondary)

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

*Full knockout (homozygous null):*
- **Embryonic lethality** - Complete lethality observed in PDE2A-/- mice (PMID: 32326334; PMID: 29409032)
- **Liver development defects** - Severely reduced liver size at E14.5, loss of hematopoietic niche integrity, hepatoblast apoptosis (PMID: 32326334)
- **Cardiac developmental defects** - Cardiac enlargement, absent atrial trabeculation, interventricular septum defects, reduced cardiomyocyte proliferation, increased apoptosis in cardiac progenitors (PMID: 29409032)
- **Molecular mechanism** - Increased intracellular cAMP, upregulation of ICER, downregulation of anti-apoptotic genes (Bcl2) and cardiac transcription factors (Tbx family, Mef2c)

*Heterozygous knockout (Pde2a+/-):*
- **Viable but with neurobehavioral phenotypes** - Homozygous animals not viable; heterozygotes show sub-Mendelian ratios
- **Sex-dependent socio-cognitive deficits** - Males show severe deficits throughout life; females show reduced cognitive performance in adulthood (PMID: 38154608)
- **Neuroinflammatory changes** - Microglia activation in males, elevated glutathione levels
- **Synaptic dysfunction** - Increased GluR1 externalization in CA1, reduced mGluR-dependent long-term depression
- **Mendelian ratio deviation** - Sub-Mendelian ratios suggest partial embryonic lethality even in heterozygotes

**Consistency with human phenotype:**
The heterozygous mouse model shows cognitive and behavioral deficits relevant to neurodevelopmental disorders, but the human clinical cases all involve biallelic variants causing more severe phenotypes (paroxysmal dyskinesia, seizures, progressive deterioration). The embryonic lethality of homozygous PDE2A-/- mice contrasts with the viability of homozygous human patients, suggesting either: (1) human missense variants retain partial function; (2) species-specific developmental requirements differ; or (3) genetic background effects modulate severity.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**

Evidence is strengthened by: (1) consistent demonstration of PDE2A requirement for embryonic development; (2) clear molecular mechanism (cAMP/cGMP dysregulation); (3) sex-dependent phenotypes in heterozygotes relevant to neurodevelopmental disorders; (4) multiple independent knockout studies with consistent findings. Limitations include: (1) heterozygous mice do not fully recapitulate human paroxysmal dyskinesia phenotype; (2) homozygous lethality prevents direct modeling of human homozygous variants.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

PDE2A functions as a critical regulator of cAMP and cGMP signaling with multiple downstream effects:

1. **cAMP/cGMP regulation** - PDE2A is activated by cGMP and hydrolyzes both cAMP and cGMP, creating cross-talk between these signaling pathways (PMID: 40412662)

2. **Dopaminergic signaling** - PDE2A activation moderates pathologically elevated cAMP/PKA responses to dopamine D1 receptor stimulation in striatal neurons, with implications for L-DOPA-induced dyskinesia (PMID: 40412662)

3. **Wnt/β-catenin pathway** - PDE2A suppresses Wnt/β-catenin signaling by inhibiting cAMP accumulation and GSK-3β phosphorylation, modulating stem cell self-renewal (PMID: 34512162)

4. **AMPAR trafficking and synaptic plasticity** - PDE2A haploinsufficiency causes increased GluR1 externalization and reduced mGluR-dependent long-term depression, affecting synaptic transmission (PMID: 38154608)

5. **Cardiac cAMP compartmentation** - PDE2A3 isoform restores subcellular cAMP compartmentation in caveolin-rich microdomains, critical for cardiac function (PMID: 38776406)

**Expression pattern (brain/neurodevelopmental relevance):**

- **Brain-enriched expression** - Highest expression in cortex, hippocampus, and striatum (PMID: 27321640)
- **Disease-associated expression changes** - PDE2A mRNA is reduced in amygdala of patients with schizophrenia, major depressive disorder, and bipolar disorder; region-specific changes in frontal cortex (schizophrenia), caudal entorhinal cortex/hippocampus/striatum (bipolar disorder) (PMID: 32119913)
- **Developmental expression** - Distinct isoform expression patterns between neonatal and adult cardiomyocytes (PMID: 37296663), suggesting developmental stage-specific functions
- **Endothelial expression** - Expressed in venous and capillary endothelial cells in brain, kidney, liver, and cardiac tissue, with potential role in blood-brain barrier function (PMID: 10375378)

**Key experimental findings:**

1. **Neuronal growth and recovery** - PDE2A inhibition enhances motor recovery after stroke, increases axonal projections in peri-infarct cortex, and enhances functional connectivity through CREB-dependent neuronal growth responses (PMID: 36163142)

2. **Fragile X Syndrome connection** - PDE2A translation is negatively modulated by FMRP, linking PDE2A dysfunction to Fragile X pathophysiology (PMID: 38154608)

3. **Mitochondrial dysfunction** - Patients with PDE2A variants show abnormal mitochondrial morphology in fibroblasts (PMID: 37317634), and PDE2A knockdown in lung tissue improves mitochondrial-related outcomes in acute lung injury (PMID: 21571906)

4. **Isoform-specific functions** - Three PDE2A isoforms (A1, A2, A3) show distinct tissue distribution and functional roles, with A3 particularly important for cardiac cAMP dynamics (PMID: 37296663)

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

Evidence is strengthened by: (1) well-characterized dual-substrate enzymatic function; (2) multiple independent pathways identified (dopaminergic, Wnt/β-catenin, AMPAR trafficking, cAMP compartmentation); (3) consistent molecular mechanism across studies (cAMP/cGMP dysregulation); (4) brain-enriched expression in regions relevant to cognition and movement control; (5) disease-associated expression changes in psychiatric disorders. Limitations include: (1) most mechanistic studies focus on loss-of-function rather than specific missense variants; (2) limited functional characterization of the specific human variants identified in patients.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths of the evidence:**

1. **Consistent phenotypic clustering** - Multiple independent families show paroxysmal dyskinesia + intellectual disability + seizures, suggesting a recognizable disease entity (IDDPADS phenotype)

2. **Founder mutations with genetic confirmation** - Pakistani families with shared haplotypes and homozygosity mapping provide strong genetic evidence for causation

3. **Robust biological mechanism** - Well-characterized cAMP/cGMP regulatory function with multiple downstream pathways relevant to neurodevelopment and movement control

4. **Model organism support** - Heterozygous mice show neurobehavioral deficits; homozygous knockouts show embryonic lethality with clear molecular mechanism

5. **Functional validation** - Missense variants shown to reduce enzymatic activity; splice-site variants predicted to produce non-functional transcripts

**Key gaps and limitations:**

1. **Biallelic inheritance only** - All published clinical cases involve homozygous or compound heterozygous variants; no evidence currently supports autosomal dominant inheritance with heterozygous pathogenic variants causing disease. The report title requesting evidence for "heterozygous (autosomal dominant) pathogenic variants" cannot be supported by current literature.

2. **Small patient cohort** - Only ~16 affected individuals across all studies; limited ethnic diversity (predominantly Pakistani families)

3. **Lack of large-scale sequencing data** - No evidence from exome/genome sequencing cohorts (gnomAD, UK Biobank, etc.) regarding frequency of heterozygous variants in general population or in neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts

4. **Incomplete functional characterization** - Limited in vitro or in vivo functional studies of the specific human missense variants identified in patients

5. **Heterozygous mouse phenotype mismatch** - Pde2a+/- mice show cognitive/behavioral deficits but not the prominent paroxysmal dyskinesia seen in human patients, suggesting incomplete phenotypic correlation

6. **Developmental lethality in mice** - Homozygous PDE2A-/- mice are embryonic lethal, yet homozygous human patients are viable, indicating species differences or functional compensation not yet understood

**Conclusion:**

PDE2A is established as a gene associated with autosomal recessive neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by paroxysmal dyskinesia, intellectual disability, and seizures (IDDPADS), with strong evidence from multiple independent families, founder mutations, and consistent molecular mechanisms. However, current published evidence does not support PDE2A as a gene for autosomal dominant disease caused by heterozygous pathogenic variants. The heterozygous knockout mouse model shows neurobehavioral phenotypes relevant to neurodevelopmental disorders, but human heterozygous carriers have not been systematically studied or reported in the literature. Future work should include: (1) systematic screening of neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts for heterozygous PDE2A variants; (2) functional characterization of identified variants; (3) investigation of potential dominant-negative or haploinsufficiency mechanisms in human carriers.
