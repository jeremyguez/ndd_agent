# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: ZNF644 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

ZNF644 is a zinc finger protein that functions as a core regulatory component of the G9a/GLP histone methyltransferase complex, mediating H3K9 methylation and gene repression during neuronal development. The protein contains multiple zinc finger motifs that recognize consensus DNA sequences and target the G9a/GLP complex to chromatin for transcriptional silencing (PMID: 25789554).

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent studies:** 2 publications

**Total patient numbers:** Specific patient counts are not provided in the extracted data; both studies identified 5'UTR mutations in ZNF644 from patient cohorts with neurodevelopmental disorders.

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Neurodevelopmental disorder (both studies)
- Autism (PMID: 37961498)

**Predominant variant type:** 5'UTR mutations (non-coding regulatory variants affecting translation)

**Variant mechanism:** Both studies identified functional 5'UTR mutations that alter translational activity and reduce protein production in neurons. The first study (PMID: 37961498) used massively parallel reporter assays and patient-derived cell lines to confirm altered protein production. The second study (PMID: 41344325) employed polysome-MPRA and Cre-dependent MPRA assays to identify mutations from neurodevelopmental disorder families.

**Known cohorts:** No specific mention of recruitment from established cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL).

**Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED**

The clinical evidence is limited by: (1) lack of specific patient numbers reported; (2) only two publications identified; (3) focus on 5'UTR variants rather than more traditionally pathogenic coding variants; (4) no detailed phenotypic characterization or segregation data provided in the extracted summaries.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:**
- Zebrafish (knockdown studies)
- Mouse (knock-in models)

**Key phenotypes from model organisms:**

*Zebrafish knockdown (PMID: 27546533):*
- Altered G9a/H3K9me2-mediated gene silencing during neurogenesis
- Defects in retinal differentiation
- Impaired transition of proliferating progenitor cells toward differentiation
- Complementary roles of znf644a and znf644b orthologs in regulating progenitor gene expression

*Mouse knock-in models (PMID: 30834109):*
- Increased vitreous chamber depth
- Altered lens diameter
- High myopia phenotype
- Preserved retinal physiological function
- Viable animals (no lethality observed)
- Significant differences in vitreous chamber depth and lens diameter

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The zebrafish data demonstrate that ZNF644 is critical for neurogenesis and retinal differentiation, which is consistent with neurodevelopmental disorder phenotypes. However, the mouse models primarily demonstrate myopia rather than neurodevelopmental phenotypes, suggesting tissue-specific or allele-specific effects.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**

The zebrafish knockdown data provide strong support for ZNF644's role in neurogenesis and developmental processes. However, the mouse models focus on ocular phenotypes rather than neurodevelopmental outcomes, and specific sample sizes and statistical significance measures are not provided in the extracted data.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

ZNF644 functions as a core component of the G9a/GLP histone methyltransferase complex. It physically associates with G9a and GLP proteins (PMID: 25789554, PMID: 27546533) and serves as a critical co-regulator of H3K9 methylation-mediated gene silencing. The protein contains zinc finger motifs that enable direct DNA binding and chromatin targeting.

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**

The biological evidence demonstrates particular relevance to neurogenesis and retinal development. ZNF644 orthologs (znf644a and znf644b) have complementary roles in regulating progenitor gene expression during neurogenesis in zebrafish (PMID: 27546533), indicating developmental stage-specific and tissue-specific functions.

**Key experimental findings:**

- Protein interaction studies confirm ZNF644 as a core G9a/GLP complex subunit (PMID: 25789554)
- Zebrafish studies demonstrate essential roles in neurogenesis and retinal differentiation (PMID: 27546533)
- Multiple GWAS studies identify ZNF644 variants in high myopia cohorts: Chinese cohort (PMID: 35002215), US cohort (PMID: 22539872), and Chinese early-onset high myopia families (PMID: 36964802)
- Circular RNA derived from ZNF644 (circ-ZNF644) is dysregulated in sepsis-induced acute kidney injury and modulates inflammatory responses (PMID: 36052886, PMID: 35976517)
- ZNF644 is a direct target of miR-362 in Sertoli cells and regulates cell proliferation and apoptosis (PMID: 30021720)

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is strong, with well-characterized protein interactions, demonstrated roles in critical developmental pathways (neurogenesis, retinal differentiation), and multiple independent studies confirming functional relevance across different biological contexts.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths:**
1. Well-characterized molecular function as a G9a/GLP complex component with clear roles in histone methylation and gene repression
2. Functional validation in model organisms (zebrafish) demonstrating essential roles in neurogenesis and retinal differentiation
3. Multiple independent clinical studies identifying pathogenic 5'UTR mutations in neurodevelopmental disorder patients with functional validation through reporter assays and patient-derived cells
4. Consistent biological evidence across multiple experimental systems supporting developmental relevance

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. Limited clinical evidence with only two publications and unspecified patient numbers
2. Predominant focus on 5'UTR variants rather than coding variants, which may represent a specialized subset of pathogenic mechanisms
3. Lack of detailed clinical phenotyping, segregation analysis, or de novo variant confirmation in the available summaries
4. Mouse models primarily demonstrate myopia rather than neurodevelopmental phenotypes, limiting direct phenotypic correlation
5. No reported recruitment from established diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD)
6. Absence of population frequency data or comparison to control cohorts for the identified 5'UTR variants

**Conclusion:**

ZNF644 demonstrates moderate evidence as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene, supported by functional 5'UTR mutations identified in patient cohorts with altered translational activity, combined with strong biological evidence for roles in neurogenesis and developmental gene regulation. However, the clinical evidence base remains limited by small patient numbers and lack of detailed phenotypic characterization. Additional studies with larger patient cohorts, comprehensive phenotyping, and segregation analysis would strengthen the evidence for ZNF644 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with autosomal dominant inheritance.
