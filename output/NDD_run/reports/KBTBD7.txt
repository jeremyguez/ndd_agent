# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: KBTBD7 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

KBTBD7 is a Kelch repeat and BTB (POZ) domain-containing protein that functions as a substrate adaptor for CUL3-based E3 ubiquitin ligase complexes. It mediates ubiquitin-dependent proteasomal degradation of multiple substrates including DRD2, PTEN, TIAM1, Vangl, KLF15, and FOXA1, thereby regulating diverse cellular pathways including dopamine signaling, cell polarity, inflammatory responses, and ferroptosis.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse and zebrafish

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

Mouse studies demonstrated that KBTBD7 full knockout is viable and does not result in lethality. The primary phenotypes observed were:
- Elevated static levels of DRD2 protein in pituitary gland, thalamus, and heart (PMID: 32572597)
- Enhanced p38 and NF-κB signaling activation in cardiac tissue post-myocardial infarction, with promoted DAMP-triggered inflammatory responses in macrophages (PMID: 29991775)

Zebrafish knockdown studies revealed:
- Defective convergent extension during gastrulation (PMID: 33990333)
- Impaired Wnt/planar cell polarity (Wnt/PCP) signaling (PMID: 33990333)
- Rescue of vascular malformation phenotype in rnf213a mutants by kbtbd7 mRNA, indicating functional relevance to vascular development (PMID: 32248732)

**Consistency with human phenotype:** No human phenotype data exists for direct comparison. However, the zebrafish gastrulation and Wnt/PCP signaling defects suggest potential relevance to neurodevelopmental processes, as convergent extension and planar cell polarity signaling are critical for early embryonic development and neural tube formation.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**

While multiple model organisms demonstrate functional roles for KBTBD7 in developmental and physiological processes, the evidence is limited by: (1) absence of heterozygous knockout data specifically testing dominant-negative or haploinsufficiency mechanisms; (2) lack of neurological phenotyping in mouse knockouts; (3) no reported Mendelian ratio deviations or lethality data that would suggest developmental sensitivity; and (4) absence of spontaneous or induced mutations in model organisms that would parallel human disease.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

KBTBD7 functions as a substrate adaptor for CUL3-RING ubiquitin ligase complexes and mediates degradation of multiple functionally distinct substrates:

1. **DRD2 regulation:** KBTBD7 directly interacts with and ubiquitinates DRD2 at five specific lysine residues (K221, K226, K241, K251, K258), promoting proteasomal degradation. KBTBD7 expression negatively correlates with DRD2 in human pituitary tumors and is highly expressed in dopamine-resistant prolactinomas (PMID: 32572597).

2. **Wnt/PCP signaling:** KBTBD7 is recruited by p97/VCP to promote Vangl ubiquitination and endoplasmic reticulum-associated degradation (ERAD). Wnt5a/CK1 signaling prevents Vangl ubiquitination by inducing phosphorylation, facilitating ER export and pathway activation (PMID: 33990333).

3. **RAC1 signaling:** KBTBD7 mediates ubiquitylation and proteasomal degradation of TIAM1 (a RAC1-specific GEF) through a CUL3 complex. KBTBD7 employs ATG8-interacting motifs to bind GABARAP proteins, and TIAM1 ubiquitylation depends on GABARAP binding (PMID: 25684205).

4. **Inflammatory signaling:** KBTBD7 interacts with MKK3/6 and promotes their activation, enhancing p38 and NF-κB signaling. miR-21 directly targets KBTBD7 to suppress inflammatory responses post-myocardial infarction (PMID: 29991775). KBTBD7 also promotes KLF15 ubiquitination and degradation, leading to NLRP3 inflammasome and NF-κB activation in septic brain injury (PMID: 39489209).

5. **Ferroptosis and mitochondrial function:** KBTBD7 interacts with FOXA1, promotes FOXA1 expression, and indirectly inhibits SLC7A11 transcription, affecting ferroptosis regulation in septic lung injury (PMID: 38652964).

6. **Oncogenic signaling:** KBTBD7 enhances ubiquitin-dependent degradation of PTEN, activating EGFR/PI3K/AKT signaling in non-small cell lung carcinoma (PMID: 35499228).

**Expression pattern:**

KBTBD7 is a TFII-I transcription factor target gene with a promoter containing well-conserved TFII-I-binding consensus sites. Microarray analysis revealed KBTBD7 was dramatically downregulated in Gtf2i and Gtf2ird1 mutant embryos, suggesting a specific role in brain development and function (PMID: 19527686). KBTBD7 expression is upregulated in inflammatory conditions including LPS-induced sepsis, myocardial infarction, and septic brain injury, indicating context-dependent regulation.

**Key experimental findings:**

- KBTBD7 knockout mice show elevated DRD2 protein levels across multiple tissues (PMID: 32572597)
- KBTBD7 knockdown in zebrafish causes defective convergent extension, a critical developmental process (PMID: 33990333)
- KBTBD7 functions as a tumor suppressor in breast cancer through TIAM1/RAC1 regulation (PMID: 25684205)
- miR-21 knockout mice show significantly higher inflammatory cytokine levels and worse cardiac outcomes post-MI, mediated through KBTBD7 (PMID: 29991775)
- KBTBD7 knockdown reduces inflammatory responses, ROS production, and ferroptosis in septic lung injury models (PMID: 38652964)

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is robust, with multiple independent studies demonstrating KBTBD7's role in ubiquitin-mediated protein degradation, developmental signaling pathways (Wnt/PCP, RAC1), inflammatory responses, and cellular stress responses. The evidence spans multiple cell types, tissues, and experimental approaches (yeast two-hybrid, co-immunoprecipitation, luciferase reporter assays, in vivo models). However, the evidence is primarily derived from loss-of-function studies and does not directly address potential pathogenic mechanisms of heterozygous missense or haploinsufficient variants.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: LIMITED**

**Key strengths:**

1. **Robust biological characterization:** KBTBD7 has well-established molecular functions as a ubiquitin ligase adaptor with multiple validated substrates and clear mechanistic pathways.

2. **Developmental relevance:** Zebrafish knockdown studies demonstrate critical roles in convergent extension and Wnt/PCP signaling, processes fundamental to neurodevelopment.

3. **Multi-system involvement:** Evidence spans neurological (brain development, dopamine signaling), cardiac, inflammatory, and vascular systems, suggesting pleiotropic effects consistent with neurodevelopmental disorder genes.

4. **Regulatory complexity:** KBTBD7 is a TFII-I target gene potentially relevant to Williams syndrome etiology, and its expression is tightly regulated by miR-21 and other mechanisms.

**Key gaps and limitations:**

1. **Complete absence of clinical data:** No human patients with KBTBD7 variants have been reported, making it impossible to establish clinical-genetic correlation or determine whether variants cause disease.

2. **Lack of heterozygous variant studies:** All model organism evidence derives from complete knockouts or knockdowns. No studies examine heterozygous mice or zebrafish, which would be critical for assessing dominant-negative or haploinsufficiency mechanisms.

3. **No neurological phenotyping in mice:** While zebrafish show developmental defects, mouse KBTBD7 knockouts were not assessed for behavioral, cognitive, or neurological abnormalities.

4. **Absence of spontaneous mutations:** No naturally occurring KBTBD7 mutations in model organisms or human populations have been identified and characterized.

5. **Unclear pathogenic mechanism:** The biological evidence does not clarify whether heterozygous loss-of-function variants would cause disease through haploinsufficiency, or whether specific missense variants might cause gain-of-function or dominant-negative effects.

**Conclusion:**

KBTBD7 is a biologically well-characterized ubiquitin ligase adaptor with established roles in developmental signaling, inflammatory responses, and cellular homeostasis. However, there is currently **no clinical evidence** linking KBTBD7 variants to neurodevelopmental or other human disorders. While the gene's functions in Wnt/PCP signaling and brain development are suggestive of potential neurodevelopmental relevance, the complete absence of patient data, combined with the lack of heterozygous variant studies in model organisms, prevents classification as a confirmed neurodevelopmental disorder gene. Future studies should prioritize: (1) systematic screening of neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts for KBTBD7 variants; (2) generation and characterization of heterozygous KBTBD7 knockout mice with comprehensive neurological and behavioral assessment; and (3) functional studies of candidate pathogenic variants to establish disease mechanisms.
