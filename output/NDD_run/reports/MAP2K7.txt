# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: MAP2K7 AND NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDERS

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

MAP2K7 (mitogen-activated protein kinase kinase 7), also known as MEK7 or MKK7, is a serine/threonine kinase that activates c-Jun NH2-terminal protein kinase (JNK) in response to stress signals including inflammatory cytokines, osmotic stress, and genomic damage (PMID: 39717752). The gene is highly conserved across species and plays critical roles in immune regulation, neuronal development, and cellular stress responses.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent studies:** 2 studies with clinical relevance to neurodevelopmental phenotypes

**Total patients:** Specific patient numbers not provided; evidence is primarily from functional/mechanistic studies rather than case series

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Altered prenatal immune response and cytokine dysregulation (PMID: 30691477)
- Schizophrenia-associated neurodevelopmental abnormalities (PMID: 30691477)
- Cerebral cavernous malformations (PMID: 33891857)

**Predominant variant type:** Loss-of-function (haploinsufficiency documented in maternal immune activation model); somatic mutations also reported in CCM lesions

**Known cohorts:** No mention of recruitment from established cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL)

**Key findings:** The primary clinical evidence comes from a mechanistic study demonstrating that Map2k7 haploinsufficiency in mice alters the maternal immune response to viral mimetics (polyI:C), with enhanced induction of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IL-2, IL-10, IL-12, TNF-α, CXCL3) and dysregulated chemokine responses. These alterations are proposed to contribute to schizophrenia-associated neurodevelopmental abnormalities (PMID: 30691477). A second study identified MAP2K7 somatic mutations in cerebral cavernous malformation lesions, though this represents somatic rather than germline pathology (PMID: 33891857).

**Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED**

The clinical evidence is limited by the absence of large case series or cohort studies documenting heterozygous MAP2K7 variants in patients with neurodevelopmental disorders. Evidence is primarily derived from functional studies in animal models rather than direct human genetic or clinical data.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (5 studies) and zebrafish (1 study)

**Key neurological phenotypes from knockouts:**

*Heterozygous knockout (mouse):* Map2k7 haploinsufficient mice demonstrate significant cognitive and attentional deficits including impaired attention, increased missed responses in the 5-choice serial reaction time task (5-CSRTT), pronounced performance decrement over time, elevated intra-individual reaction time variability, and inability to sustain attention (PMID: 27774567). These phenotypes are directly relevant to neurodevelopmental and neuropsychiatric disorders.

*Full knockout (mouse):* Reduced JNK phosphorylation in neurons, impaired axon outgrowth, and reduced tubulin acetylation in axons, indicating disrupted neuronal development and cytoskeletal dynamics (PMID: 40008675).

*Heterozygous knockout (zebrafish):* Higher lethality during viral infection, more pronounced tissue damage, and reduced antiviral capacity (PMID: 37367226). Homozygous Map2k7 knockout is embryonic lethal in zebrafish, indicating essential developmental function.

**Mendelian ratios and lethality:**
- Zebrafish: Homozygous knockout is embryonic lethal (sub-Mendelian ratio); heterozygotes are viable but show increased vulnerability
- Mouse: Heterozygous knockouts are viable with Mendelian inheritance ratios, but demonstrate behavioral and cognitive phenotypes
- Mouse conditional knockouts in other tissues (testis, immune cells, cancer models) are viable, suggesting tissue-specific or context-dependent lethality

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The attentional deficits and cognitive processing abnormalities observed in Map2k7 haploinsufficient mice are consistent with neurodevelopmental and neuropsychiatric phenotypes (particularly schizophrenia-related cognitive dysfunction) proposed in human studies. The embryonic lethality of homozygous knockout in zebrafish suggests that complete loss of MAP2K7 function is incompatible with normal development, supporting a haploinsufficiency mechanism for heterozygous pathogenic variants.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**

Multiple independent mouse studies demonstrate consistent neurodevelopmental and cognitive phenotypes associated with Map2k7 haploinsufficiency. The behavioral deficits in heterozygous mice directly parallel proposed human neurodevelopmental phenotypes, and the embryonic lethality of homozygous knockout in zebrafish indicates essential developmental function.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

MAP2K7 functions as a key node in the JNK/MAPK signaling cascade, activated by upstream kinases (including MAP3K5 and MAP3K3) in response to stress signals. The kinase phosphorylates and activates JNK (MAPK8/MAPK9), which regulates gene expression, cell proliferation, apoptosis, and inflammatory responses (PMID: 39717752). MAP2K7 is also regulated post-translationally through methylation (by EEF1AKMT3 at K296), which decreases phosphorylation and protein degradation (PMID: 35753528).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**

MAP2K7 is expressed in brain tissues and is particularly important during neuronal development. The gene shows altered expression in lung adenocarcinoma and colorectal cancer (PMID: 39920481, PMID: 41515982), and is involved in axon outgrowth and neuronal cytoskeletal dynamics through the PAK2-JNK pathway (PMID: 40008675). A testis-specific promoter has evolved within the Map2k7 gene, indicating tissue-specific regulatory complexity (PMID: 35069705).

**Key experimental findings:**

1. **Immune regulation:** MAP2K7 mediates JNK signaling in maternal immune responses; haploinsufficiency alters cytokine and chemokine production in response to viral mimetics, with potential consequences for fetal brain development (PMID: 30691477).

2. **Neuronal development:** MAP2K7 is required for normal axon outgrowth and microtubule dynamics through the PAK2-JNK pathway; its loss impairs phosphorylation of neuronal substrates (SCG10, MAP1B) and reduces tubulin acetylation (PMID: 40008675).

3. **Therapeutic targeting:** MAP2K7 is a validated therapeutic target in T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia; small-molecule inhibitors (5Z-7-oxozeaenol) induce cytotoxicity through pathway inhibition (PMID: 34504651). Rational design of selective covalent MAP2K7 inhibitors has been achieved (PMID: 37197477).

4. **Pathway integration:** MAP2K7 is suppressed by cardioprotective miRNAs (miR-125b-5p, miR-128-3p, miR-30d-5p) delivered via extracellular vesicles, and is targeted by miR-128-3p in cancer contexts (PMID: 35387487, PMID: 39476205).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

Extensive biochemical and molecular evidence demonstrates MAP2K7's critical roles in JNK signaling, immune regulation, and neuronal development. The kinase is well-characterized functionally, with multiple validated substrates and regulatory mechanisms. Expression studies confirm relevance to disease pathways, and the gene is an established therapeutic target with selective inhibitors in development.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths:**
1. Strong model organism evidence: Multiple independent mouse studies demonstrate consistent neurodevelopmental and cognitive phenotypes in Map2k7 haploinsufficient animals, with direct relevance to proposed human phenotypes (attention deficits, cognitive dysfunction)
2. Robust biological evidence: MAP2K7 is a well-characterized kinase with established roles in JNK signaling, immune regulation, and neuronal development; multiple pathways and substrates are validated
3. Functional evidence for haploinsufficiency: Zebrafish homozygous knockout is embryonic lethal, and heterozygous mice show Mendelian inheritance with behavioral phenotypes, supporting a haploinsufficiency mechanism
4. Mechanistic plausibility: Altered maternal immune responses and impaired neuronal development are biologically plausible mechanisms linking MAP2K7 dysfunction to neurodevelopmental disorders

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. **Absence of human genetic data:** No published case reports, case series, or cohort studies documenting heterozygous MAP2K7 variants in patients with neurodevelopmental disorders
2. **Limited clinical phenotyping:** Clinical evidence is restricted to functional studies in animal models; no direct human clinical data on patients with MAP2K7 variants
3. **No recruitment from established cohorts:** Studies do not reference recruitment from large genomic databases (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, GEL) that would provide systematic ascertainment
4. **Somatic vs. germline distinction:** The CCM evidence involves somatic mutations, not germline variants, limiting relevance to inherited neurodevelopmental disorders

**Conclusion:**

MAP2K7 demonstrates strong biological and model organism evidence supporting a role in neurodevelopmental processes, particularly in attention, cognitive function, and neuronal development. Heterozygous loss-of-function variants in Map2k7 produce consistent cognitive and attentional deficits in mice that align with proposed human neurodevelopmental phenotypes. However, the evidence for MAP2K7 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene remains MODERATE due to the complete absence of published human genetic data documenting pathogenic variants in affected individuals. Systematic screening of neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts and functional validation of identified variants would be necessary to establish MAP2K7 as a confirmed disease gene.
