# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: DDX23 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

DDX23 (also known as PRP28) is a DEAD-box RNA helicase that functions as a spliceosomal protein essential for pre-mRNA splicing, specifically mediating the transition from pre-B to B spliceosome complexes and U1 snRNP release from the 5' splice site (PMID: 38743621). Beyond its canonical splicing role, DDX23 has been implicated in innate antiviral immunity, microRNA biogenesis, and various cellular processes including translation regulation and apoptotic pathways.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent studies:** 3 case reports/case series

**Total patients:** 11 patients (9 patients in the primary cohort study plus 2 additional patients from Finnish family studies)

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Neurodevelopmental: global developmental delay, autism spectrum disorder, cognitive impairment
- Neurological: seizures/epilepsy, tone abnormalities, dysarthria
- Structural brain abnormality: bilateral perisylvian polymicrogyria with cortical overfolding and abnormal layering
- Dysmorphic features: facial dysmorphism
- Multisystem involvement: skeletal abnormalities, renal abnormalities, ocular abnormalities, cardiac abnormalities

**Predominant variant type:** Missense variants exclusively; all variants are de novo

**Variant location specificity:** In the primary cohort, all missense variants localize to highly conserved functional domains—specifically the RecA-like domain and DEAD box sequence (PMID: 34050707)

**Cohort source:** Variants identified through GeneMatcher and exome sequencing in Finnish family cohorts; no indication of enrollment in large diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL)

**Diagnostic reasoning:** Strong convergence on de novo variants with consistent phenotypic patterns across independent families, particularly the novel association of DDX23 with bilateral perisylvian polymicrogyria (PMID: 38712318, ASHG 2023)

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**

The evidence is strengthened by multiple independent case reports with consistent phenotypic patterns and de novo variants in conserved functional domains. However, the total sample size remains modest (11 patients across 3 studies), and specific variant nomenclature is not provided in abstracts, limiting detailed genotype-phenotype correlation analysis.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (Mus musculus)

**Knockout type:** Full knockout of β-B3-crystallin, which resulted in secondary downregulation of Ddx23 protein

**Key phenotypes:**
- Downregulation of Ddx23 protein in lens tissue at multiple timepoints (3 weeks, 6 weeks, 3 months)
- Disrupted lens morphology with early-onset phenotypic variability
- Altered lens proteome with both down- and up-regulation of various proteins
- Viable animals (no embryonic lethality reported)

**Consistency with human phenotype:** Limited direct consistency. The mouse studies document lens-specific effects and secondary Ddx23 downregulation rather than primary DDX23 mutations. No neurological phenotypes, developmental delay, seizures, or cortical malformations were reported in these models (PMID: 39803551, PMID: 40840647). The ocular abnormalities noted in some human patients may align with lens involvement, but the broader neurodevelopmental phenotypes are not recapitulated.

**Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED**

The available mouse data are indirect (secondary Ddx23 downregulation rather than primary mutation) and tissue-specific (lens-focused), with no reported neurological or developmental phenotypes. Direct studies of DDX23 loss-of-function mutations in the brain or whole-organism knockouts would be needed to validate the neurodevelopmental findings in humans.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

DDX23 operates at the intersection of multiple cellular processes:

1. **Splicing and RNA processing:** DDX23 is a core spliceosomal helicase required for pre-B to B spliceosome transition and interacts directly with SR proteins like SRSF1 (PMID: 38743621). It also facilitates mRNA splicing of specific oncogenic transcripts (e.g., FOXM1C in ovarian cancer; PMID: 34966670) and regulates splicing of PEDS1 pre-mRNA via interaction with lncRNA (PMID: 41360761).

2. **MicroRNA biogenesis:** DDX23 promotes miR-21 biogenesis by facilitating primary-to-precursor processing through interaction with Drosha microprocessor (PMID: 26121981).

3. **Innate antiviral immunity:** DDX23 functions as a host antiviral restriction factor that negatively regulates viral replication (Senecavirus A, FMDV) and acts as a positive regulator of innate immune activation (PMID: 40956085, PMID: 33255534, PMID: 36006669). However, in some organisms (black carp), DDX23 negatively regulates MAVS-mediated antiviral signaling (PMID: 37164277).

4. **Cancer-related pathways:** DDX23 is upregulated in multiple cancer types (pancreatic, glioma, ovarian, urothelial carcinoma) and promotes cancer progression through various mechanisms including m6A methylation-dependent regulation (PMID: 36977668).

**Expression pattern and neurodevelopmental relevance:**

While the biological evidence does not explicitly document DDX23 expression in developing brain tissue, the spliceosomal and RNA processing functions are universally required in all cell types, including neural progenitors and developing neurons. The role in miR-21 biogenesis is particularly relevant, as miR-21 has been implicated in neural development and differentiation. The antiviral immunity function may be relevant to neurodevelopmental outcomes if viral infections during critical developmental windows are involved.

**Key experimental findings:**

- DDX23 is a direct target of METTL3 m6A methylation, and its expression level modulates cancer cell phenotypes and drug resistance (PMID: 36977668)
- DDX23 ATPase activity is essential for spliceosomal function (PMID: 38743621)
- DDX23 expression is elevated in malignant tissues and associated with poor prognosis in multiple cancer types
- DDX23 can be targeted by viral proteases (FMDV 3C proteinase, SVA-2B protein), indicating it is a functionally important host factor

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is robust, with multiple independent studies documenting DDX23's essential roles in splicing, miRNA biogenesis, and innate immunity. The molecular mechanisms are well-characterized, and the protein's functional domains are conserved. However, the direct relevance to neurodevelopmental disorders and cortical development remains inferred rather than experimentally demonstrated.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths:**

1. **Consistent clinical phenotype:** Multiple independent families with de novo missense variants in conserved functional domains (RecA-like domain, DEAD box) presenting with overlapping neurodevelopmental and neurological phenotypes
2. **Novel disease association:** First identification of DDX23 variants in bilateral perisylvian polymicrogyria, a specific cortical malformation, strengthens the link to neurodevelopment
3. **Functional relevance:** DDX23's essential role in splicing and RNA processing provides a plausible mechanistic basis for neurodevelopmental dysfunction, as these processes are critical during brain development
4. **De novo inheritance pattern:** All identified variants are de novo, consistent with autosomal dominant inheritance of severe neurodevelopmental disorders
5. **Domain specificity:** Variants cluster in functionally important, highly conserved domains

**Key gaps and limitations:**

1. **Small sample size:** Only 11 patients across 3 studies limits statistical power and genotype-phenotype correlation
2. **Lack of functional validation:** No functional studies demonstrating that the identified missense variants impair DDX23 splicing activity or other molecular functions
3. **Incomplete variant characterization:** Specific variant nomenclature not provided in available abstracts, limiting reproducibility and detailed analysis
4. **Inadequate model organism data:** Available mouse studies involve secondary Ddx23 downregulation in lens tissue rather than primary neuroddevelopmental mutations; no direct neurological phenotypes reported
5. **Missing brain expression data:** No documentation of DDX23 expression patterns in developing human brain or neural tissues
6. **Lack of segregation analysis:** No family studies demonstrating segregation of variants with disease in multiplex families
7. **Phenotypic heterogeneity:** While core neurodevelopmental features are consistent, the multisystem involvement (cardiac, renal, skeletal, ocular) suggests either genetic heterogeneity or pleiotrophic effects that are not yet mechanistically explained

**Conclusion:**

DDX23 shows MODERATE evidence as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene associated with autosomal dominant de novo missense variants. The clinical evidence is strengthened by consistent phenotypic patterns across independent families and the novel association with bilateral perisylvian polymicrogyria, combined with DDX23's well-established essential role in RNA splicing and processing. However, the evidence would be substantially strengthened by functional validation of the identified variants, expanded patient cohorts, direct demonstration of DDX23 expression and function in developing brain, and model organism studies specifically examining neurodevelopmental phenotypes. These studies are warranted to establish DDX23 as a confirmed neurodevelopmental disorder gene and to elucidate the molecular mechanisms by which missense variants in conserved domains lead to cortical malformations and developmental delay.
