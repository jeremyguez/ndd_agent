# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: ANK3 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

ANK3 encodes ankyrin-G, a scaffolding protein that localizes to the axon initial segment (AIS), nodes of Ranvier, and dendritic compartments where it organizes neuronal membrane proteins including ion channels, cell adhesion molecules, and cytoskeletal components. The gene produces three isoforms through alternative splicing with distinct expression patterns and domain compositions, enabling isoform-specific functions in neuronal development and signaling (PMID: 38988293, PMID: 35794211).

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of Studies and Patient Cohorts:**
Eight independent clinical studies were identified, including two peer-reviewed publications and six conference abstracts. The peer-reviewed literature includes 20 patients across two studies (PMID: 38988293 with 16 patients; PMID: 34218362 with 4 patients). Conference abstracts report an additional 133+ patients from larger cohorts (ASHG 2023 India cohort: 15 patients; ASHG 2022 Qatar cohort: 107 patients; ASHG 2018 AnkyrinG interactome study: 1,009 patient-parent trios; ASHG 2013 UDN case: 1 patient). Total approximate patient count: 150+ individuals with ANK3 variants.

**Most Common Phenotypes:**
Across all studies, the most frequently observed phenotypes in heterozygous carriers include:
- Autism spectrum disorder (reported in 6/8 studies)
- Intellectual disability / developmental delay (reported in 6/8 studies)
- Language delay (reported in 2 peer-reviewed studies)
- Motor delay / hypotonia (reported in 2 peer-reviewed studies)
- Epilepsy / seizures (reported in 3 studies including ASHG 2013 UDN case)
- Attention deficit disorder / ADHD (reported in 1 peer-reviewed study)
- Sleep disturbances (reported in 1 peer-reviewed study)
- Behavioral problems / aggressivity (reported in 2 studies)

**Predominant Variant Types:**
Loss-of-function (LoF) variants predominate in the clinical literature. The largest peer-reviewed study (PMID: 38988293) reports that the majority of monoallelic variants are predicted to result in truncated protein (frameshift, nonsense, splice site variants). Missense variants are also reported but appear less common. The second peer-reviewed study (PMID: 34218362) specifically describes heterozygous frameshift or nonsense variants affecting all ANK3 transcripts, supporting an autosomal-dominant inheritance pattern with isoform-dependent pathogenicity.

**Cohort Representation:**
One patient was identified through the Undiagnosed Diseases Network (UDN) with compound heterozygous mutations and severe intractable seizures (ASHG 2013). The remaining patients come from research cohorts in India, Qatar, and international WES/WGS studies, but are not from established diagnostic cohorts (DDD, GREGoR, RUMC, GEL).

**Strength of Clinical Evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) consistent phenotypic spectrum across multiple independent studies; (2) segregation analysis and predicted loss-of-function variants supporting pathogenicity; (3) identification of de novo variants in large family-based cohorts (ASHG 2018 AnkyrinG interactome study with p=0.007 for overrepresentation); (4) recognition of a distinct biallelic phenotype with cerebellar ataxia (PMID: 40879451), supporting isoform-specific mechanisms. Limitations include: (1) relatively small sample sizes in peer-reviewed publications; (2) lack of functional validation in most cases; (3) limited detailed phenotypic characterization in some conference abstracts.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms Studied:**
Nine independent model organism studies were identified, predominantly using mice (8 studies) and one zebrafish study.

**Key Phenotypes from Knockouts:**

*Mouse heterozygous knockouts (Ank3+/-)* consistently demonstrate behavioral and neurobiological abnormalities relevant to neurodevelopmental and psychiatric disorders:
- Increased anxiety-related behaviors in multiple behavioral tests (elevated plus maze, open field, social interaction) (PMID: 28411148)
- Altered psychiatric-related behaviors including increased anxiety in novelty-suppressed feeding and impaired passive avoidance (PMID: 28109561)
- Structural brain abnormalities including smaller cingulate cortex, retrosplenial cortex, motor cortex, and hippocampal fimbria (PMID: 28411148)
- Altered microtubule dynamics with increased GSK3 activity and elevated CRMP2 phosphorylation (PMID: 30046097)
- Impaired axonal transport and altered hippocampal proteome (PMID: 28109561)

*Mouse full knockouts* reveal more severe phenotypes:
- Absence epilepsy with increased slow gamma power (25-45 Hz) (PMID: 38123552)
- Abnormal sleep-wake patterns with decreased REM sleep and seizures more common during REM sleep (PMID: 38123552)
- Hyperactivity and repetitive behavior phenotype (PMID: 38123552)
- Increased excitability in interneurons with increased somatic Ca2+ activity (PMID: 41688438)
- Altered AnkG interaction with inositol trisphosphate receptor complex (PMID: 41688438)

*Mouse knock-in model* (BD-associated p.W1989R variant):
- Disrupted ankyrin-G/GABARAP interaction (PMID: 40849089)
- Inhibitory deficits with reduced presynaptic GABAergic neurotransmission (PMID: 40849089)
- Cortical pyramidal neuron hyperexcitability with shortened AIS length (PMID: 40849089)

*Mouse conditional knockout* (oligodendrocyte-specific):
- Age-dependent destabilization of axoglial interactions in aged but not young mice (PMID: 39738113)
- Histological, electrophysiological, and behavioral pathophysiologies (PMID: 39738113)

*Zebrafish knockdown*:
- Coloboma and microphthalmia phenotypes, establishing ANK3's role in optic fissure morphogenesis (PMID: 35034853)

**Lethality and Mendelian Ratios:**
All mouse models are viable with no reported lethality or infertility, indicating that ANK3 disruption is compatible with survival. No Mendelian ratio deviations are reported, consistent with autosomal-dominant inheritance in humans.

**Consistency with Human Phenotype:**
Model organism evidence shows strong consistency with human neurodevelopmental phenotypes. Mouse heterozygous knockouts recapitulate anxiety, behavioral abnormalities, and structural brain changes observed in human patients. The epilepsy phenotype in full knockouts aligns with seizures reported in human cases. The behavioral and neurobiological abnormalities in mice support the neurodevelopmental and psychiatric manifestations observed clinically.

**Strength of Model Organism Evidence: STRONG**

Strengths include: (1) multiple independent mouse models with consistent phenotypic findings; (2) dose-dependent effects (heterozygous vs. full knockout); (3) mechanistic insights into molecular pathways (GSK3/CRMP2 signaling, GABARAP interaction, microtubule dynamics); (4) demonstration of lithium responsiveness in behavioral phenotypes, providing therapeutic relevance (PMID: 28109561, PMID: 40849089); (5) cross-species validation in zebrafish. Limitations include: (1) most studies lack detailed sample size reporting; (2) limited investigation of isoform-specific effects despite known isoform diversity in humans.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known Pathways and Molecular Function:**

ANK3 encodes ankyrin-G, a critical scaffolding protein with multiple established functions:

1. **Axon Initial Segment (AIS) Organization:** Ankyrin-G localizes to the AIS where it anchors the spectrin cytoskeleton and organizes membrane proteins including voltage-gated sodium channels (Nav), potassium channels, and cell adhesion molecules (PMID: 35794211). This organization is essential for action potential initiation and neuronal excitability.

2. **Dendritic Compartmentalization:** Ankyrin-G localizes to dendritic shafts and spines where it regulates calcium signaling and synaptic function through interaction with inositol trisphosphate receptor complexes (PMID: 41688438).

3. **GABAergic Synapse Stabilization:** Ankyrin-G interacts with GABARAP to stabilize inhibitory synapses and presynaptic GABAergic neurotransmission (PMID: 40849089). Disruption of this interaction leads to inhibitory deficits and neuronal hyperexcitability.

4. **Axonal Transport:** ANK3 dysfunction impairs the kinesin cargo system and neuronal ion channel/glutamate receptor transport, affecting hippocampal proteome composition (PMID: 28109561).

5. **Microtubule Dynamics:** ANK3 loss enhances microtubule dynamics through GSK3/CRMP2 signaling, affecting cytoskeletal stability and neuronal morphology (PMID: 30046097).

**Expression Pattern:**

ANK3 shows prominent expression in the developing and adult nervous system with isoform-specific patterns:
- Three isoforms with distinct domain compositions and expression patterns across brain developmental stages (PMID: 38988293)
- Localized expression in the optic fissure, periocular mesenchyme, and ciliary marginal zone during eye development (PMID: 35034853)
- Differential expression in ASD brain post-mortem tissues (ASHG 2023 - Investigating Immune Functions)
- Expression modulation by childhood trauma in bipolar disorder and schizophrenia (PMID: 37620394)
- Genotype-dependent expression differences in brain tissue with relevance to Alzheimer's disease (ASHG 2022 - Genome-wide meta-analysis)

**Key Experimental Findings:**

1. **Isoform-Specific Pathology:** Biallelic ANK3 variants cause more severe phenotypes than monoallelic variants, with cerebellar ataxia appearing exclusively in biallelic cases and correlating with cerebellar atrophy on brain MRI (PMID: 40879451). This suggests isoform-specific pathological mechanisms, with the cerebellar isoform being particularly vulnerable to biallelic loss.

2. **Protein-Protein Interaction Network:** De novo mutations in the ANK3 protein-protein interaction network are significantly overrepresented in neurodevelopmental disorders (p=0.007), indicating that ANK3 and its interacting partners form a critical functional module (ASHG 2018 - Genetic variation in the AnkyrinG interactome).

3. **Cognitive Function Association:** ANK3 variants (rs2393640) are significantly associated with attention and memory impairment in bipolar disorder patients (P=1.50×10⁻⁴, FDR=0.0353), with epistatic interactions with CACNA1C (PMID: 38657895).

4. **Neuro-Immune Function:** ANK3 is identified as a neuro-immune gene with prominent immune functions in autism spectrum disorder, showing differential expression in ASD brain tissues and carrying deleterious variations in ASD cohorts (ASHG 2023 - Investigating Immune Functions).

5. **Kidney Function Association:** Novel GWAS evidence identifies ANK3 SNP rs59288815 as associated with cystatin-C estimated glomerular filtration rate in continental Africans (P<5×10⁻⁸, posterior probability of causality >99%), suggesting broader pleiotropic effects (ASHG 2023 - First GWAS of cystatin-C kidney function).

6. **Alternative Splicing in Psychiatric Disorders:** ANK3 shows differentially expressed novel isoforms in dorsolateral prefrontal cortex of bipolar disorder cases, with alternative splicing patterns including intron retention and alternative 5'/3' splicing sites (ASHG 2015 - RNA-sequencing identifies differentially expressed novel gene isoforms).

**Strength of Biological Evidence: STRONG**

Strengths include: (1) well-characterized molecular function with multiple validated protein interactions; (2) clear mechanistic links between ANK3 disruption and neuronal dysfunction (excitability, synaptic transmission, cytoskeletal organization); (3) isoform-specific pathology explaining phenotypic variation; (4) GWAS evidence supporting association with multiple neuropsychiatric and neurological phenotypes; (5) expression studies demonstrating relevance to disease pathogenesis. Limitations include: (1) limited functional validation of specific patient variants; (2) incomplete understanding of how different ANK3 isoforms contribute to distinct phenotypes; (3) relatively few studies examining posttranslational modifications affecting ANK3 function.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall Evidence Level: STRONG**

ANK3 is supported by strong, multi-faceted evidence as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous (autosomal-dominant) pathogenic variants.

**Key Strengths of the Evidence:**

1. **Consistent Clinical Phenotype:** Multiple independent studies identify a recognizable neurodevelopmental phenotype including autism spectrum disorder, intellectual disability, developmental delay, language delay, motor delay, and epilepsy. The phenotypic consistency across diverse populations (India, Qatar, international cohorts) strengthens the evidence.

2. **Mechanistic Coherence:** Model organism studies and molecular biology experiments provide clear mechanistic explanations for how ANK3 loss-of-function causes neurodevelopmental dysfunction through disruption of AIS organization, GABAergic synapse stabilization, axonal transport, and microtubule dynamics.

3. **Isoform-Specific Pathology:** The distinction between monoallelic and biallelic phenotypes, with cerebellar ataxia appearing exclusively in biallelic cases, demonstrates sophisticated understanding of ANK3's isoform-dependent functions and provides a framework for explaining phenotypic variation.

4. **Genetic Evidence:** Significant overrepresentation of de novo mutations in the ANK3 protein-protein interaction network (p=0.007) in neurodevelopmental disorders provides population-level genetic support for pathogenicity.

5. **Therapeutic Responsiveness:** Demonstration that lithium reverses behavioral and neurobiological abnormalities in ANK3-disrupted mice provides functional validation and therapeutic relevance.

**Key Gaps and Limitations:**

1. **Limited Functional Validation:** Most human cases lack functional studies demonstrating that specific variants impair ANK3 function. Only a few studies (e.g., PMID: 40849089) provide detailed mechanistic validation of patient variants.

2. **Small Peer-Reviewed Sample Size:** The two peer-reviewed clinical studies include only 20 patients total, limiting statistical power. Larger peer-reviewed case series would strengthen the evidence.

3. **Incomplete Phenotypic Characterization:** Some conference abstracts provide limited clinical detail. Standardized phenotypic assessment across studies would improve evidence quality.

4. **Isoform-Specific Mechanisms Incompletely Understood:** While isoform-specific pathology is recognized, the molecular basis for why different isoforms cause distinct phenotypes (e.g., cerebellar ataxia in biallelic cases) remains incompletely characterized.

5. **Limited Investigation of Missense Variants:** While LoF variants predominate, the pathogenic mechanisms of missense variants are less well understood. The BD-associated p.W1989R variant provides one example, but more detailed characterization of missense variants would be valuable.

---

## CONCLUSION

ANK3 is a well-established neurodevelopmental disorder gene with strong evidence supporting pathogenicity of heterozygous loss-of-function variants. The clinical evidence demonstrates a consistent neurodevelopmental phenotype across multiple independent cohorts, the model organism evidence shows robust and mechanistically coherent phenotypes, and the biological evidence reveals clear molecular pathways linking ANK3 disruption to neuronal dysfunction. The recognition of isoform-specific pathology and the demonstration of therapeutic responsiveness to lithium further support ANK3's role in neurodevelopmental and psychiatric disorders. Future work should focus on functional validation of patient variants, larger peer-reviewed case series, and detailed characterization of isoform-specific mechanisms to fully elucidate ANK3's contribution to neurodevelopmental disease.
