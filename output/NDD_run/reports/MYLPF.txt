# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: MYLPF AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

MYLPF (myosin light chain phosphorylatable fast skeletal muscle) is a regulatory light chain protein essential for skeletal muscle development and function, containing two conserved EF-hand superfamily domains (PMID: 23277391). The gene is highly expressed in skeletal muscle tissues and plays a critical role in myofibril formation, particularly in fast-twitch muscle fibers, with expression decreasing with age during postnatal muscle maturation.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent case reports/series:** 1 case series identified

**Total approximate number of patients:** 8 families with affected individuals (PMID: 32707087)

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Distal arthrogryposis (DA) with congenital contractures
- Segmental amyoplasia (complete absence of skeletal muscle in affected limbs)
- Reduced limb movement and paralysis
- Congenital muscle weakness

**Predominant variant type:** Both dominant and recessive variants reported (PMID: 32707087), indicating both loss-of-function and potentially dominant-negative mechanisms

**Known cohort participation:** No mention of recruitment from standardized cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL)

**Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED**

The clinical evidence is limited to a single publication describing 8 families. While the phenotype is well-characterized (distal arthrogryposis with segmental amyoplasia), the small number of independent families and lack of large-scale cohort validation limits the strength of evidence. No additional independent case reports or series were identified in the literature.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Zebrafish and mouse

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

*Zebrafish (PMID: 32707087, PMID: 39345555):*
- Full knockout (mylpfa -/-): severely reduced fast-twitch myofibrils, loss of high-speed movement, reduced trunk contractile force, complete pectoral fin paralysis, reduced myosin activity, and fiber degeneration
- Double knockout (mylpfa -/- ; mylpfb -/-): complete failure of myofibril formation
- Single mylpfb -/- knockout: normal myofibrils (suggesting functional redundancy)
- Viable but severely impaired locomotion

*Mouse (PMID: 17356007, ASHG 2015):*
- Full knockout: complete absence of both fast and slow skeletal muscle throughout the body, lack of skeletal muscle in diaphragm, 30% decreased body weight, respiratory failure
- Perinatal lethal (sub-Mendelian ratio)
- No effect on heart or other organ development

**Consistency with human phenotype:** Strong consistency. The zebrafish model demonstrates that MYLPF loss most severely affects limb movement and fast-twitch muscle development, directly paralleling the human distal arthrogryposis phenotype with appendicular muscle weakness. The dose-dependent relationship between Mylpf dosage and myofibril size in zebrafish (PMID: 39345555) aligns with the observation that disease-associated alleles reduce myofibril size proportionally.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**

Multiple independent model systems (zebrafish and mouse) consistently demonstrate that MYLPF is essential for skeletal muscle development and function. The zebrafish models are particularly informative, showing viable but severely impaired phenotypes that mirror human disease, while the mouse model demonstrates the critical importance of MYLPF for skeletal muscle formation. The dose-dependent effects observed in zebrafish provide mechanistic insight into how heterozygous variants could cause disease.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

MYLPF functions as a regulatory light chain in the myosin complex and is involved in:
- Muscle contraction and actin-mediated cell contraction pathways
- Actin filament-based movement
- Myofibril formation and organization
- Calcium-related signaling in muscle cells
- Focal adhesion pathways during skeletal muscle development (PMID: 34236285)

MYLPF interacts with troponin proteins (TNNC1, TNNC2, TNNT2, TNNT3) that are essential for muscle contraction regulation (PMID: 34236285).

**Expression pattern:**

- Highly expressed in skeletal muscle tissues (longissimus dorsi, gastrocnemius, abdominal muscle) with minimal expression in non-muscle tissues (liver, spleen, lung, kidney) (PMID: 23277391)
- Exclusively expressed in fast-twitch muscle in zebrafish (PMID: 39345555)
- Expression decreases with age in skeletal muscle, correlating with the timing of muscle growth in young animals (PMID: 23277391)
- Upregulated during myoblast differentiation on piezoelectric scaffolds (189% upregulation) (PMID: 35635507)
- Identified as a skeletal muscle tissue-specific hub gene (PMID: 35692882)
- Downregulated in Turner syndrome patients compared to controls (PMID: 32210915)

**Key experimental findings:**

- MYLPF is a phosphorylated differentially expressed protein during chicken skeletal muscle embryonic development (PMID: 34236285)
- MYLPF is identified as a potential biomarker for RYR1 mutation-associated myopathies with AUC of 0.786, indicating its role in skeletal muscle pathology networks (PMID: 35692882)
- MYLPF expression is downregulated in C2C12 myoblasts treated with EPA/DHA, correlating with suppressed myoblast differentiation (PMID: 30669396)
- MYLPF is associated with metabolic syndrome in GWAS analysis, specifically upregulated in skeletal muscle and unique to the highest cardiovascular risk cluster (ASHG 2022)
- MYLPF is identified as a novel protein associated with chronic kidney disease in participants with abnormal glucose metabolism (PMID: 40934961)

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is robust, demonstrating that MYLPF is a skeletal muscle-specific protein with well-characterized expression patterns, protein interactions, and functional roles in myofibril formation and muscle contraction. The consistent identification of MYLPF across multiple independent proteomic, transcriptomic, and functional studies supports its critical role in skeletal muscle development and function.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths of the evidence:**

1. **Functional validation in multiple model organisms:** Both zebrafish and mouse models consistently demonstrate that MYLPF is essential for skeletal muscle development, with phenotypes that directly parallel human disease (particularly the appendicular/distal muscle involvement in arthrogryposis).

2. **Dose-dependent effects:** The zebrafish studies demonstrate a clear dose-dependent relationship between Mylpf dosage and myofibril size, providing a mechanistic basis for how heterozygous variants could cause disease.

3. **Skeletal muscle-specific expression and function:** MYLPF is exclusively or predominantly expressed in skeletal muscle with well-characterized roles in myofibril formation and muscle contraction, making it biologically plausible as a disease gene for skeletal muscle disorders.

4. **Consistent phenotype-genotype correlation:** Both dominant and recessive variants in humans cause distal arthrogryposis with segmental amyoplasia, consistent with the critical role of MYLPF in muscle development demonstrated in model organisms.

**Key gaps or limitations:**

1. **Limited clinical evidence:** Only a single case series with 8 families has been published. Independent replication in additional families or large-scale cohort studies (DDD, GREGoR, UDN) would substantially strengthen the evidence.

2. **Lack of detailed variant characterization:** The clinical report does not provide comprehensive molecular details (specific variants, segregation analysis, functional studies of individual alleles) that would further support pathogenicity.

3. **No neurodevelopmental phenotypes reported:** While MYLPF is clearly important for skeletal muscle development, the clinical evidence does not indicate involvement in primary neurodevelopmental disorders (intellectual disability, autism, developmental delay), limiting classification as a "neurodevelopmental disorder gene" in the strict sense. The phenotype is primarily a skeletal muscle disorder.

4. **Limited mechanistic studies of human variants:** No functional studies of human disease-associated MYLPF variants have been published to date.

**Conclusion:**

MYLPF is a well-validated skeletal muscle development gene with strong biological and model organism evidence supporting its role in myofibril formation and muscle function. However, the clinical evidence is currently limited to a single case series, and the primary phenotype (distal arthrogryposis with segmental amyoplasia) is a skeletal muscle disorder rather than a primary neurodevelopmental disorder. The evidence is sufficient to classify MYLPF as a gene associated with congenital skeletal muscle disorders, but additional independent clinical cases and functional studies of human variants would be needed to establish it as a definitively established disease gene and to clarify whether it has broader neurodevelopmental implications.
