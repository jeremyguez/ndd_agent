# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: KAT8 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

KAT8 (lysine acetyltransferase 8), also known as MOF (males absent on the first), is a MYST family histone acetyltransferase that catalyzes acetylation of histone H4 at lysine 16 (H4K16ac) and serves as a core component of the NSL (non-specific lethal) chromatin remodeling complex. Beyond histone acetylation, KAT8 functions as a pan-lysine acetyltransferase and lactylation writer enzyme that regulates diverse cellular processes including autophagy, transcriptional control, and protein synthesis.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent case reports/series:** 1 case series identified

**Total patients:** 12 patients with KANSL1 point mutations (note: KANSL1 is a direct interacting partner of KAT8 in the NSL complex)

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Neonatal hypotonia
- Developmental delay and moderate intellectual disability
- Characteristic facial dysmorphism
- Epilepsy
- Musculoskeletal anomalies
- Congenital heart defects
- Urogenital malformations
- Ectodermal anomalies
- Expressive language delay

**Predominant variant type:** Loss-of-function (truncating mutations in KANSL1)

**Cohort source:** DDD Study and multiple European centers (ASHG 2015 - Koolen et al.)

**Critical limitation:** The clinical evidence presented is for KANSL1, not KAT8 itself. While KANSL1 is a functional partner of KAT8 in the NSL complex, no direct human case reports of heterozygous KAT8 pathogenic variants causing neurodevelopmental disorder were identified in the provided evidence. The clinical data demonstrates that haploinsufficiency of KANSL1 causes Koolen-de Vries syndrome (KdVS), and mechanistic studies show this involves autophagosome-lysosome fusion defects (PMID: 35177641) and synaptic dysfunction (PMID: 34286667), but these are KANSL1-specific findings rather than direct KAT8 evidence.

**Strength of clinical evidence for KAT8 specifically: LIMITED**

The clinical evidence is indirect, based on a functionally related gene (KANSL1) rather than KAT8 itself. No direct human cases with KAT8 heterozygous variants are documented.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (multiple conditional knockout and knockdown models)

**Key neurological and developmental phenotypes:**

- **Embryonic lethality:** Conditional Kat8 knockout in trophoblasts causes embryonic lethality with extraembryonic ectoderm abnormalities (PMID: 38961108), indicating essential developmental role
- **Female infertility and oocyte defects:** Conditional Kat8 knockout causes female infertility with failure of follicle development at secondary and preantral stages, associated with increased reactive oxygen species and downregulation of antioxidant genes (PMID: 28506985)
- **Cerebellar development:** In an Fto knockout model, upregulation of KAT8 and altered H4K16ac modification correlates with cerebellar ataxia, tremors, and abnormal gait patterns, with impaired chromatin accessibility in neural developmental pathways (PMID: 40883822)
- **Mitochondrial dysfunction:** Kat8 knock-in model shows mitochondrial dysfunction and cardiac remodeling via ATP5B hyperacetylation (PMID: 39392752)

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The model organism data demonstrates KAT8's essential role in early development and neural function. The cerebellar and neuronal phenotypes are consistent with neurodevelopmental disorder manifestations, though no direct heterozygous knockout model specifically designed to test haploinsufficiency in the nervous system was identified.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**

Strong evidence for KAT8's essential developmental and neurological roles, but limited direct evidence from heterozygous models specifically designed to test autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental pathogenicity.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

KAT8 operates in multiple interconnected pathways relevant to neurodevelopment:

1. **Autophagy regulation:** KAT8/MOF is identified as a key lysine acetyltransferase in acetylation-mediated regulation of autophagy pathways (PMID: 35435793), with particular relevance to neurodevelopmental disorders (PMID: 37674294). This connects to KANSL1-related KdVS pathology, where autophagosome-lysosome fusion defects cause synaptic dysfunction.

2. **Chromatin remodeling and transcriptional control:** KAT8 catalyzes H4K16ac to activate transcription of developmental genes including CDX2 (PMID: 38961108) and is part of the NSL complex that regulates autophagy-related genes (PMID: 37674294).

3. **Post-translational modifications beyond acetylation:** KAT8 catalyzes lysine lactylation and butyrylation of non-histone proteins, expanding its regulatory scope (PMID: 38359291, PMID: 37460462).

4. **Immune and inflammatory pathways:** KAT8 undergoes phase separation with IRF1 to regulate immune responses (PMID: 36894639) and was identified as a putative effector gene for an Alzheimer's disease GWAS locus in activated microglia (ASHG 2019).

**Expression pattern:** While specific brain expression data is not detailed in the provided evidence, the model organism studies demonstrate KAT8 expression in developing embryos, oocytes, cerebellar tissue, and immune cells, indicating broad developmental and neural relevance.

**Key experimental findings:**
- KAT8 is essential for trophoblast self-renewal and early embryonic development
- KAT8 regulates oocyte development through reactive oxygen species homeostasis
- KAT8 acetylates multiple substrates (CDX2, HSP90, LTBP1, eEF1A2, YEATS4, ATP5B) with tissue-specific effects
- KAT8 is part of the NSL complex that functionally overlaps with KANSL1-related pathology

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

Extensive mechanistic evidence demonstrates KAT8's critical roles in autophagy, chromatin remodeling, developmental gene expression, and immune regulation. The connection to KANSL1-related neurodevelopmental pathology through shared NSL complex membership and autophagy regulation is well-established.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths:**
1. Strong biological evidence for KAT8's essential role in autophagy, chromatin remodeling, and developmental gene expression
2. Model organism evidence demonstrating KAT8's critical function in early development and neural tissue
3. Clear functional connection to KANSL1, which causes documented neurodevelopmental disorder (Koolen-de Vries syndrome) through shared NSL complex membership
4. Multiple independent mechanistic studies converging on autophagy and synaptic dysfunction as pathogenic pathways
5. Identification of KAT8 as a candidate gene in Alzheimer's disease GWAS analysis in neural tissue

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. **Absence of direct human cases:** No published case reports or case series of heterozygous KAT8 pathogenic variants causing neurodevelopmental disorder in humans
2. **Limited heterozygous model data:** Most model organism studies use conditional knockouts or knockdowns rather than heterozygous loss-of-function models designed to test haploinsufficiency
3. **Indirect clinical evidence:** Clinical phenotype data comes from KANSL1 (a binding partner) rather than KAT8 itself, though the mechanistic overlap is substantial
4. **Incomplete expression characterization:** Detailed brain and developmental tissue expression patterns for KAT8 are not provided in the evidence base

**Conclusion:**

KAT8 is a biologically compelling candidate gene for autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder based on its essential roles in autophagy, chromatin remodeling, and developmental gene expression, combined with its functional partnership with KANSL1 (which causes Koolen-de Vries syndrome). However, the clinical evidence remains indirect and based on a related gene rather than KAT8 itself. Identification of heterozygous KAT8 pathogenic variants in patients with neurodevelopmental phenotypes, particularly those with features overlapping Koolen-de Vries syndrome or autophagy-related pathology, would substantially strengthen the evidence for KAT8 as a human disease gene.

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. Kansl1 haploinsufficiency impairs autophagosome-lysosome fusion and links autophagic dysfunction with Koolen-de Vries syndrome in mice (PMID: 35177641)
2. Imbalanced autophagy causes synaptic deficits in a human model for neurodevelopmental disorders (PMID: 34286667)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. KAT8-mediated H4K16ac is essential for sustaining trophoblast self-renewal and proliferation via regulating CDX2 (PMID: 38961108)
2. Histone acetyltransferase KAT8 is essential for mouse oocyte development by regulating reactive oxygen species levels (PMID: 28506985)
3. Fto-mediated m(6)A modification is essential for cerebellar development through regulating epigenetic reprogramming (PMID: 40883822)
4. Mitochondrial MOF regulates energy metabolism in heart failure via ATP5B hyperacetylation (PMID: 39392752)

### Biological Evidence
1. Acetylation in the regulation of autophagy (PMID: 35435793)
2. Epigenetic regulation of autophagy-related genes: Implications for neurodevelopmental disorders (PMID: 37674294)
3. KAT8-catalyzed lactylation promotes eEF1A2-mediated protein synthesis and colorectal carcinogenesis (PMID: 38359291)
4. Lysine butyrylation of HSP90 regulated by KAT8 and HDAC11 confers chemoresistance (PMID: 37460462)
5. Disrupting the phase separation of KAT8-IRF1 diminishes PD-L1 expression and promotes antitumor immunity (PMID: 36894639)
