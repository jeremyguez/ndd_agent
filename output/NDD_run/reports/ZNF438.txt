# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: ZNF438 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

ZNF438 is a C2H2-type zinc finger protein containing five zinc finger domains that functions as a transcriptional repressor (PMID: 17669267). The gene is ubiquitously expressed across human tissues with nuclear localization, suggesting a broad regulatory role in gene expression.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent case reports/series:** 1 case report

**Total patients:** 1 patient

**Phenotypes observed:** The single reported patient presented with intellectual disability, agenesis of corpus callosum, and congenital heart disease (PMID: 41622991).

**Variant type:** Chromosome 10p11.23-p11.21 microdeletion (~4.2 Mb) including ZNF438 and multiple other genes

**Cohort source:** Not specified as part of a known diagnostic cohort

**Critical limitation:** The clinical evidence is substantially weakened by the fact that ZNF438 was included in a large microdeletion affecting multiple genes (ZEB1, EPC1, KIF5B, NRP1). The diagnostic report explicitly attributes the patient's phenotype to haploinsufficiency of these other genes rather than ZNF438 specifically. No heterozygous point mutations or smaller deletions affecting ZNF438 alone have been reported in association with neurodevelopmental phenotypes.

**Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED**

The single case involves a large multi-gene deletion where ZNF438 is not implicated as the primary disease gene, providing minimal specific evidence for ZNF438 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** None identified

**Key findings:** No model organism studies were identified in the available evidence.

**Strength of model organism evidence: NONE**

The absence of knockout or knockdown studies in model organisms represents a significant gap in the evidence base for understanding ZNF438's role in neurodevelopment.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Molecular function and pathways:**

ZNF438 functions as a transcriptional repressor with demonstrated activity in dual luciferase assays (PMID: 17669267). The gene has been proposed as a candidate transcriptional repressor of ZEB1 in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma cells based on chromatin conformation analysis (PMID: 41391024). Additionally, ZNF438 is a negatively regulated target of the androgen receptor in prostate cancer cells, with ChIP-PCR confirming AR binding and DHT-mediated downregulation (PMID: 27122218).

**Expression pattern:**

ZNF438 is ubiquitously expressed across 18 human adult tissues (PMID: 17669267), but notably, the available evidence does not specifically document expression in developing brain or neural tissues, which would be particularly relevant for neurodevelopmental assessment.

**Key experimental findings:**

- Transcriptional repression activity confirmed in mammalian cell transfection assays (PMID: 17669267)
- Nuclear localization when exogenously expressed (PMID: 17669267)
- DNA methylation at CpG site cg01656216 near ZNF438 associated with cardiovascular disease and epigenetic age across 87,827 individuals (PMID: 36991458)
- Identified as a shared genetic risk factor between rheumatoid arthritis and stroke (p = 0.0015) through GWAS and transcriptome-wide association analysis (PMID: 39953635)
- Lower ZNF438 expression correlated with worse overall survival in osteosarcoma and altered immune cell populations (PMID: 35281811)
- Genome-wide significant DNA methylation changes (>10%) following glucocorticoid exposure (PMID: 30821055)
- Identified in co-expression networks associated with pediatric sepsis and neutrophil degranulation pathways (PMID: 33274532)

**Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE**

While ZNF438 has well-characterized transcriptional repressor function and documented involvement in multiple disease-relevant pathways (immune-inflammatory, cardiovascular, cancer), the biological evidence does not specifically establish a mechanistic link to neurodevelopmental processes. The ubiquitous expression pattern is consistent with a broad regulatory role, but brain-specific expression data and functional studies in neural contexts are lacking.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: LIMITED**

**Key strengths:**
- ZNF438 has well-established molecular function as a transcriptional repressor
- The gene is ubiquitously expressed and involved in multiple biological pathways
- One clinical case involves a patient with neurodevelopmental phenotypes in a region containing ZNF438

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- Only one clinical case, and that case involves a large multi-gene deletion where ZNF438 is not identified as the primary disease gene
- No heterozygous point mutations or smaller deletions affecting ZNF438 alone have been reported in neurodevelopmental disorder patients
- No model organism studies demonstrating neurodevelopmental phenotypes
- No brain-specific expression data or functional studies in neural contexts
- The biological evidence, while substantial, primarily links ZNF438 to immune-inflammatory, cardiovascular, and cancer pathways rather than neurodevelopment

**Conclusion:**

Current evidence does not support ZNF438 as a well-established neurodevelopmental disorder gene. While the single reported case involves a patient with developmental delay and congenital anomalies, ZNF438 was included in a large microdeletion where other genes are implicated as the primary disease contributors. To establish ZNF438 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene, additional independent cases with heterozygous ZNF438-specific variants (point mutations or small deletions) would be needed, ideally supported by model organism studies demonstrating neurodevelopmental phenotypes and functional studies characterizing ZNF438's role in neural development.
