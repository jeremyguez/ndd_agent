# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: RAB14 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

RAB14 is a small GTPase belonging to the Rab family of proteins that functions as a key regulator of intracellular membrane trafficking and vesicular transport. RAB14 has been implicated in multiple cellular processes including autophagosome maturation, cytokinesis, endosomal targeting, mitophagy, and lipid homeostasis, with emerging roles in developmental signaling pathways.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of case reports/series:** 0

**Total patients:** 0

**Most common phenotypes:** Not applicable

**Predominant variant type:** Not applicable

**Known cohorts:** Not applicable

**Strength of clinical evidence: NONE**

No clinical case reports, case series, or patient cohorts with RAB14 pathogenic variants have been identified in the current literature. There is a complete absence of human genetic evidence linking RAB14 variants to neurodevelopmental or any other human disease phenotypes.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (Mus musculus)

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

Five independent mouse knockout studies have characterized RAB14 function:

1. **Embryonic lethality with developmental failure:** RAB14 full knockout mice are embryonic lethal with failure to develop epiblast and primitive endoderm lineages, impaired FGFR transport, and defective FGF signaling (PMID: 21238925). This demonstrates RAB14 is essential for early embryonic development.

2. **Autophagy defects:** RAB14 knockout mice show altered MAP1LC3/LC3-II levels and impaired autophagosome maturation, with a mild autophagy-defective phenotype in single knockouts that becomes severe in RAB2/RAB14 double knockouts (PMID: 38953305).

3. **Cytokinesis failure:** RAB14 depletion causes cytokinesis failure or significantly prolonged division time with inhibition of actin clearance at the abscission site and impaired recruitment of Rab11-endosomes to intracellular bridge microtubules (PMID: 33566684).

4. **Intestinal inflammation and barrier dysfunction:** Rab11FIP1-deficient mice (which interact with RAB14) develop spontaneous inflammation with abnormal accumulation of subapical vesicles in colonocytes, rectal prolapse, reduced goblet cell populations, and increased susceptibility to DSS-induced colon damage (PMID: 35819177). This study demonstrates RAB14's role in epithelial homeostasis.

5. **Female infertility:** RAB14 knockout mice show impaired follicle development, increased granulosa cell apoptosis, and significantly reduced lipid droplet content in ovaries, resulting in decreased litter size (PMID: 41553083).

**Consistency with human neurodevelopmental phenotype:** The embryonic lethality and early developmental failure observed in RAB14 knockout mice are consistent with a critical role in neurodevelopment, as FGF signaling is essential for epiblast and neural tissue development. However, no human neurodevelopmental cases have been reported to validate this connection.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**

While mouse knockouts demonstrate RAB14 is essential for early development and has multiple cellular functions, the evidence is limited by: (1) lack of heterozygous knockout phenotype characterization, (2) absence of any reported human cases to validate that haploinsufficiency causes neurodevelopmental disease, and (3) no specific neurological phenotypes documented in the available knockout studies.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

RAB14 participates in multiple cellular pathways:

- **Vesicular trafficking and endosomal dynamics:** RAB14 localizes to autophagosomes, early endosomes, and Golgi-endosome contacts, where it interacts with HOPS complex subunits (VPS39, VPS41) and retromer components (Vps26B) to regulate membrane bud formation and cargo transport (PMID: 38953305, PMID: 38565878).

- **Mitophagy:** RAB14 promotes Parkin-dependent mitophagy through TGN-derived vesicles, with RAB14 overexpression increasing mitophagy activity and knockdown resulting in elongated mitochondrial morphology (PMID: 40737294).

- **Lipid metabolism:** RAB14 mediates lipophagy in granulosa cells and facilitates fatty acid trafficking from lipid droplets to mitochondria through interaction with CPT1A (PMID: 41553083, PMID: 29721083).

- **Cytokinesis:** RAB14 binds to the MACF2/CAMSAP3 complex and is essential for endosome targeting during cell division (PMID: 33566684).

- **Developmental signaling:** RAB14 is critical for FGFR transport and FGF signaling during early embryogenesis (PMID: 21238925).

**Expression pattern:**

RAB14 is expressed broadly across tissues. While specific brain expression data are limited in the provided evidence, the embryonic lethality phenotype and role in FGF signaling suggest expression in developing neural tissues. RAB14 has also been identified as a hub gene in shared pathophysiology of Alzheimer's disease and type 2 diabetes mellitus, implicating it in neurological processes (PMID: 36923118).

**Key experimental findings:**

- RAB14 localizes to multiple membrane compartments including autophagosomes, endosomes, peroxisomes, and the trans-Golgi network (PMID: 38953305, PMID: 26775587).
- RAB14 interacts with multiple protein complexes (HOPS, retromer, MACF2, CPT1A, Parkin) indicating it serves as a hub protein in cellular trafficking.
- RAB14 is frequently overexpressed in multiple cancer types (lung, gastric, ovarian, breast, pancreatic), where it promotes proliferation through YAP, AKT, and Wnt signaling pathways (PMID: 32982313, PMID: 28107526, PMID: 27718127, PMID: 27994670, PMID: 30267303).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is robust, with well-characterized molecular interactions, multiple validated cellular functions, and consistent findings across independent studies. However, the evidence is primarily focused on cancer biology and cellular trafficking rather than neurodevelopmental processes.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
- Robust biological evidence demonstrating RAB14's critical role in multiple cellular processes essential for development
- Mouse knockout studies showing embryonic lethality and developmental failure
- Multiple independent studies confirming RAB14's role in FGF signaling, autophagy, and cellular trafficking
- Identification of RAB14 as a hub gene in neurological disease pathways

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- **Complete absence of clinical evidence:** No human cases with RAB14 variants have been reported in any disease context, let alone neurodevelopmental disorders
- **No heterozygous knockout characterization:** All mouse studies describe full knockouts; haploinsufficiency effects are unknown
- **Limited neurodevelopmental specificity:** While RAB14 is essential for early development, no specific neurological phenotypes have been documented in animal models
- **No functional studies of putative pathogenic variants:** No missense or loss-of-function variants have been functionally characterized
- **Lack of expression data in developing brain:** Specific RAB14 expression patterns in neural tissues are not documented in the provided evidence

**Conclusion:**

While RAB14 is a biologically important gene with critical roles in cellular trafficking, autophagy, and early embryonic development, there is currently INSUFFICIENT evidence to classify it as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene. The complete absence of clinical case reports, combined with the lack of characterization of heterozygous effects in model organisms, prevents any meaningful assessment of RAB14's role in human neurodevelopmental disease. Future studies identifying RAB14 variants in patients with neurodevelopmental phenotypes, combined with functional characterization of haploinsufficiency in animal models, would be necessary to establish RAB14 as a disease gene.
