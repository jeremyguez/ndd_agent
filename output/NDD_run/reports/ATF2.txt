# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: ATF2 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

ATF2 (Activating Transcription Factor 2) is a transcription factor that integrates extracellular and intracellular stress signals through phosphorylation by JNK/p38 and ATM/ATR kinases, functioning as a multifaceted regulator of gene expression with both transcriptional and post-translational regulatory mechanisms (PMID: 28212892). ATF2 exhibits tissue- and context-dependent roles, functioning as both an oncogenic and tumor suppressor protein depending on cellular context and subcellular localization (PMID: 18677098).

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of case reports/series:** 0

**Total patients:** 0

**Most common phenotypes:** Not applicable

**Predominant variant type:** Not applicable

**Known cohort sources:** Not applicable

**Strength of clinical evidence: NONE**

No independent case reports, case series, or patient cohorts with ATF2 pathogenic variants were identified in the literature. There is a complete absence of clinical evidence linking ATF2 variants to neurodevelopmental or any other human disease phenotypes in the available literature.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (7 studies)

**Key neurological phenotypes from genetic manipulation:**

Mouse studies demonstrate ATF2's involvement in multiple neurological processes:

- **Epilepsy and neuroinflammation:** ATF2 knockdown in a kainic acid-induced epilepsy model reduced M1 polarization of microglia, enhanced M2 polarization, relieved neuroinflammation, and reduced TNF-α and IL-6 expression while preserving hippocampal structure (PMID: 40595935).

- **Sepsis-associated encephalopathy:** OGT-mediated O-GlcNAcylation of ATF2 protected against sepsis-associated encephalopathy by inhibiting microglial pyroptosis; conversely, OGT deficiency augmented microglial pyroptosis, exacerbated secondary neuronal injury, and impaired cognitive function in healthy mice (PMID: 40411666).

- **Dopamine signaling and synaptic plasticity:** In A53T-alpha-synuclein transgenic mice, ATF2 expression was reduced as a dopamine-inducible gene, correlating with progressive reduction in spontaneous movement, altered dopamine signaling, and impaired synaptic plasticity including absent long-term depression in corticostriatal slices (ASHG 2010 - A53T-alpha-synuclein overexpression impairs dopamine signaling).

- **Metabolic and immune regulation:** ATF2/p38 knockout in T cells protected against diet-induced obesity through enhanced thermogenesis and increased IL-35 levels, demonstrating ATF2's role in metabolic homeostasis (PMID: 38730210).

- **Vascular endothelial barrier integrity:** ATF2 knockout impaired endothelial barrier integrity and vascular function through reduced PRKCD expression and altered nucleoplasmic translocation (PMID: 40810740).

**Lethality and viability:** All ATF2 knockout/knockdown mice were viable, with no reported embryonic lethality or perinatal death.

**Mendelian ratio deviations:** Not reported in any study.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** No human phenotypes are available for comparison. However, the neuroinflammatory, cognitive, and synaptic plasticity findings suggest potential relevance to neurodevelopmental processes.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**

While multiple mouse studies demonstrate ATF2's functional importance in neurological processes including epilepsy, encephalopathy, dopamine signaling, and synaptic plasticity, the evidence is limited by: (1) lack of heterozygous dominant-negative studies; (2) reliance on knockdown/knockout rather than pathogenic missense variants; (3) absence of developmental timing studies; and (4) no direct demonstration that ATF2 haploinsufficiency causes neurodevelopmental phenotypes.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

ATF2 functions as a stress-responsive transcription factor in multiple critical pathways:

- **MAPK signaling:** ATF2 is a downstream effector of p38 MAPK and JNK signaling, activated by phosphorylation in response to cellular stress (PMID: 28212892). It integrates signals from MAP4K4-MAPK (JNK/ERK/p38) and WT1 signaling to regulate target gene expression (PMID: 38383842).

- **Inflammatory regulation:** ATF2 acts as a transcriptional regulator of pro-inflammatory genes including TLR4 (PMID: 40107350) and adhesion molecules (VCAM-1, ICAM-1) (PMID: 33133688). ATF2 phosphorylation upregulates E-selectin (CD62E) expression during vascular inflammation (PMID: 40151887).

- **Ferroptosis regulation:** ATF2 transcriptionally regulates ferroptosis-inhibitory genes including HSPH1/SLC7A11 (PMID: 36473315), CHAC1 (PMID: 38393971), and CENPT/GCLC/GSH pathway components (PMID: 40651948).

- **Mechanotransduction:** ATF2 functions downstream of p38 MAPK signaling to regulate IL-1α-induced Piezo1 expression in chondrocytes, with CREBP1 (ATF2/CREBP1) directly binding the PIEZO1 promoter (PMID: 33758095).

- **Hepatic fibrosis:** ATF2 is a downstream transcription factor in p38 MAPK/ATF2 signaling activated by MASP1, promoting hepatic stellate cell activation (PMID: 35849032).

**Expression pattern and brain relevance:**

ATF2 is expressed in multiple tissues including brain, with evidence of dopamine-inducible expression in striatum (ASHG 2010). The gene is involved in stress responses and inflammatory signaling relevant to neuroinflammation and neuronal function. However, specific developmental expression patterns in the brain have not been characterized in the available literature.

**Key experimental findings:**

- ATF2 is subject to multiple levels of regulation including phosphorylation by kinases (p38, JNK, ATM/ATR), ubiquitination (by MG53 and other E3 ligases), and post-translational modifications (O-GlcNAcylation by OGT, lysine-demyristoylation by SIRT6) (PMID: 28212892, PMID: 40107350, PMID: 40411666, PMID: 40810740).

- ATF2 exhibits context-dependent oncogenic or tumor suppressor activity depending on tissue type and genetic background (PMID: 18677098).

- ATF2 protein stability and subcellular localization are dynamically regulated and associated with disease phenotypes (PMID: 28212892).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

ATF2's molecular function is well-characterized across multiple biological systems with clear mechanistic pathways identified. However, the strength is limited by lack of specific evidence for neurodevelopmental roles and absence of studies examining heterozygous pathogenic variants.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
1. Multiple mouse studies demonstrate ATF2's functional importance in neurological processes including epilepsy, encephalopathy, dopamine signaling, and synaptic plasticity
2. Well-characterized molecular function as a stress-responsive transcription factor with multiple regulatory mechanisms
3. Evidence of involvement in inflammatory and immune pathways relevant to neurodevelopmental disorders

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. **Complete absence of clinical evidence:** No case reports, case series, or patient cohorts with ATF2 variants have been reported
2. **No heterozygous studies:** All model organism evidence derives from complete knockouts or knockdowns, not heterozygous dominant-negative variants
3. **No developmental studies:** Lack of evidence examining ATF2's role during critical neurodevelopmental windows
4. **No variant-specific evidence:** No studies examining pathogenic missense or loss-of-function variants in humans or their functional consequences
5. **Indirect neurological evidence:** Mouse studies focus on acute disease models (epilepsy, sepsis) rather than developmental phenotypes

**Conclusion:**

While ATF2 is a well-characterized transcription factor with demonstrated roles in neuroinflammation, synaptic plasticity, and dopamine signaling in mouse models, there is currently insufficient evidence to classify it as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene. The complete absence of human clinical data, combined with the lack of heterozygous variant studies, prevents any assessment of ATF2's pathogenic potential in autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorders. Future studies would require identification of de novo or inherited ATF2 variants in patients with neurodevelopmental phenotypes, functional characterization of these variants, and targeted investigation of ATF2's role during critical developmental periods.
