# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: ABI2 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

ABI2 (Abl-interactor 2) is an adaptor protein containing a homeobox homology domain, proline-rich region, and SH3 domain that interacts with Abl-family tyrosine kinases and functions as a core component of the wave regulatory complex (WRC) involved in actin cytoskeleton reorganization and cell migration (PMID: 12011975). The gene plays critical roles in cell migration, dendritic spine morphogenesis, and neuronal development, with emerging evidence for involvement in neurodevelopmental disorders.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent studies:** 2 case series/reports

**Total patients:** Approximately 8+ patients with heterozygous missense variants; additional patients with homozygous loss-of-function variants in autosomal recessive families (exact number not specified in the recessive study).

**Most common phenotypes observed (heterozygous missense variants):**
- Moderate to severe motor delay
- Absent or delayed expressive language
- Intellectual disability
- Seizures
- Autistic traits
- Macrocephaly
- Thinning of the corpus callosum
- White matter signal abnormalities

**Predominant variant type:** Missense variants in heterozygous state (dominant-acting). The recurrent p.Tyr491Cys variant was identified in 6 of 8 patients (75%) in a highly conserved SH3 domain, strongly suggesting a recurrent pathogenic mutation (PMID: 40475134). Loss-of-function mutations were identified in autosomal recessive families with non-syndromic intellectual disability (PMID: 28397838).

**Cohort source:** International collaborations (PMID: 40475134); consanguineous families from Pakistan and Iran (PMID: 28397838). No indication of recruitment from major diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL).

**Diagnostic reasoning:** The heterozygous missense variants were identified as de novo mutations in unrelated individuals with severe neurodevelopmental phenotypes. The high recurrence of p.Tyr491Cys in the SH3 domain, combined with de novo status and consistent severe phenotype across patients, provides strong evidence for pathogenicity. The identification of loss-of-function mutations in homozygous state in consanguineous families with intellectual disability further supports ABI2's role in neurodevelopment.

**Strength of clinical evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) recurrent de novo missense variant in a conserved domain; (2) consistent severe neurodevelopmental phenotype across unrelated patients; (3) independent confirmation through loss-of-function mutations in recessive families; (4) high expression in prenatal brain tissue. Limitations include: relatively small number of reported cases and lack of large-scale cohort validation.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (Mus musculus)

**Key neurological phenotypes from knockouts:**

Full knockout mice exhibit severe neurodevelopmental and neurological abnormalities directly relevant to the human phenotype:
- Defective cell migration in neocortex and hippocampus
- Abnormal dendritic spine morphology and density
- Severe deficits in short-term memory
- Severe deficits in long-term memory
(PMID: 15572692)

Additionally, ABI2-deficient mice show ocular abnormalities, renal abnormalities, and reproductive trait abnormalities with histological evidence of ciliopathy phenotypes (PMID: 36456625).

**Lethality and developmental impact:** A conditional knockout study of the closely related ABI1 gene (which shares functional redundancy with ABI2) revealed embryonic lethality at approximately embryonic day 11.5 with malformations in developing heart and brain, demonstrating the critical importance of ABI family proteins in early neurodevelopment (PMID: 21482783). This suggests that complete loss of ABI2 function may also have severe developmental consequences.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The mouse knockout phenotypes show strong consistency with human clinical findings. Defective cell migration and abnormal dendritic spine morphology directly correlate with the intellectual disability, motor delay, and white matter abnormalities observed in patients. Memory deficits in mice align with cognitive impairment in humans. The corpus callosum thinning and white matter abnormalities in patients are consistent with disrupted neuronal migration and connectivity observed in ABI2-deficient mice.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) multiple independent knockout studies showing consistent neurological phenotypes; (2) direct relevance of observed defects (migration, spine morphology, memory) to human neurodevelopmental pathology; (3) demonstration of critical developmental role through conditional knockout lethality. Limitations include: lack of specific studies modeling the recurrent p.Tyr491Cys missense variant or heterozygous knockouts that would more directly parallel the human dominant-acting mutations.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

ABI2 functions as a core component of the wave regulatory complex (WRC) that regulates actin cytoskeleton dynamics and cell migration (PMID: 12011975). The protein is involved in:

1. **Cytoskeletal reorganization:** ABI2 interacts with Abl-family tyrosine kinases and promotes membrane ruffling, lamellipodia formation, and directional cell migration (PMID: 12011975, PMID: 37042842).

2. **Signaling pathway regulation:** ABI2 is phosphorylated by kinases (PIM1 at Ser183 in cancer cells) to enhance WRC formation and promote cell protrusion (PMID: 37042842). In tumor suppression contexts, ABI2 interacts with RAC1 to inhibit PI3K/Akt signaling (PMID: 38937761).

3. **Transcriptional co-activation:** ABI2 serves as a co-activator of transcription factor HHEX in hepatocellular carcinoma, promoting cancer stem cell properties (PMID: 38844969).

**Expression pattern:** The biological evidence indicates high expression in prenatal brain tissue (PMID: 28397838), consistent with a critical role in neurodevelopment. The mouse knockout studies demonstrate essential functions in developing brain and eye tissues.

**Key experimental findings relevant to neurodevelopment:**

- ABI2-deficient mice show defective secondary lens fiber orientation and migration in the eye, and defective cell migration in neocortex and hippocampus (PMID: 15572692), directly implicating ABI2 in neuronal migration during brain development.

- The SH3 domain, where the recurrent p.Tyr491Cys variant is located, is critical for protein-protein interactions and is highly conserved, suggesting that missense mutations in this domain disrupt essential binding interactions.

- Conditional knockout of the related ABI1 gene causes embryonic lethality with brain malformations and defective actin cytoskeleton regulation in mouse embryonic fibroblasts, demonstrating the critical role of ABI family proteins in developmental processes (PMID: 21482783).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) well-characterized molecular function in actin dynamics and cell migration, processes essential for neurodevelopment; (2) demonstrated expression in prenatal brain; (3) multiple independent studies confirming WRC involvement and signaling interactions; (4) clear mechanistic link between ABI2 dysfunction and neuronal migration defects observed in patients. Limitations include: limited direct evidence for how specific missense variants (particularly p.Tyr491Cys) affect protein function or domain interactions.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: STRONG**

**Key strengths of the evidence:**

1. **Recurrent de novo missense variant:** The p.Tyr491Cys variant identified in 6 of 8 patients (75%) in a highly conserved SH3 domain provides compelling evidence for a recurrent pathogenic mutation with dominant-acting effects (PMID: 40475134).

2. **Consistent clinical phenotype:** All reported patients with heterozygous missense variants share a severe, recognizable neurodevelopmental phenotype including intellectual disability, motor delay, seizures, and characteristic brain imaging findings (corpus callosum thinning, white matter abnormalities).

3. **Functional validation through model organisms:** ABI2-deficient mice exhibit neurological phenotypes (defective migration, abnormal dendritic spines, memory deficits) that directly parallel human clinical findings, providing strong functional support for pathogenicity.

4. **Biologically plausible mechanism:** ABI2's well-characterized role in actin cytoskeleton regulation and cell migration provides a clear mechanistic link to the observed neurodevelopmental pathology, particularly neuronal migration defects and white matter abnormalities.

5. **Independent confirmation in recessive families:** Loss-of-function mutations in homozygous state in consanguineous families with intellectual disability independently confirm ABI2's critical role in neurodevelopment.

**Key gaps or limitations:**

1. **Limited case numbers:** Only 8 patients with heterozygous missense variants have been reported, limiting statistical power and generalizability.

2. **Lack of large-scale cohort validation:** No evidence of systematic screening in major diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, GEL) that would establish prevalence and refine the phenotypic spectrum.

3. **Mechanistic gap for missense variants:** While the biological function of ABI2 is well-characterized, specific functional consequences of the p.Tyr491Cys variant or other missense mutations have not been experimentally demonstrated.

4. **Incomplete phenotypic characterization:** The full spectrum of clinical features and natural history remain incompletely defined with only one primary case series.

**Conclusion:**

ABI2 is a strong candidate gene for autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by intellectual disability, motor delay, seizures, and white matter abnormalities. The evidence is supported by a recurrent de novo missense variant in a conserved domain, consistent clinical phenotype across unrelated patients, and compelling functional validation through mouse models showing defective neuronal migration and memory deficits. While the current evidence base is strong, expansion through systematic cohort screening and functional studies of specific variants would further establish ABI2's role in neurodevelopmental disease and refine genotype-phenotype correlations.

## REFERENCES


### Clinical Evidence
1. Missense ABI2 variants linked to a neurodevelopmental disorder with intellectual disability, epilepsy, hypoplasia of the corpus callosum, and white matter abnormalities (PMID: 40475134)
2. Mapping autosomal recessive intellectual disability: combined microarray and exome sequencing identifies 26 novel candidate genes in 192 consanguineous families (PMID: 28397838)

### Model Organism Evidence
1. ABI2-deficient mice exhibit defective cell migration, aberrant dendritic spine morphogenesis, and deficits in learning and memory (PMID: 15572692)
2. Analysis of genome-wide knockout mouse database identifies candidate ciliopathy genes (PMID: 36456625)
3. Essential role for Abi1 in embryonic survival and WAVE2 complex integrity (PMID: 21482783)

### Biological Evidence
1. In search of a function for the E3B1/Abi2/Argbp1/NESH family (PMID: 12011975)
2. PIM1 phosphorylates ABI2 to enhance actin dynamics and promote tumor invasion (PMID: 37042842)

4. The HHEX-ABI2/SLC17A9 axis induces cancer stem cell-like properties and tumorigenesis in HCC (PMID: 38844969)
