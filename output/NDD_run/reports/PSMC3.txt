# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: PSMC3 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

PSMC3 (proteasome 26S subunit, ATPase 3) is a core AAA-ATPase component of the 26S proteasome regulatory particle that mediates substrate unfolding and translocation into the catalytic core. PSMC3 plays essential roles in protein homeostasis and has recently been implicated in regulating type I interferon signaling and RNAi pathways through protein-protein interactions.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of Independent Studies:** Four primary clinical studies were identified (two peer-reviewed publications and two ASHG 2022 conference abstracts).

**Total Patient Count:** Approximately 24-25 patients with confirmed PSMC3 variants across published studies, plus additional patients from CNV-based cohort analyses.

**Most Common Phenotypes Observed:**
- Neurodevelopmental delay and intellectual disability (primary phenotype across multiple studies)
- Autism spectrum disorder with severe social communication deficits
- Dysregulated type I interferon signaling
- Neurosensory manifestations (deafness, cataracts) in specific cases
- Altered dendrite development and impaired reversal learning

**Predominant Variant Types:**
- De novo missense variants (15 variants in 23 patients; PMID: 37256937)
- Deep intronic homozygous variants causing cryptic exon transcription (1 patient; PMID: 32500975)
- Copy number variations (duplications associated with ASD; PMID: 36959829)

**Cohort Affiliation:** No patients from major international cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL) were explicitly mentioned. Studies originated from Bangladesh-based cohorts and international research collaborations (France).

**Diagnostic Reasoning:** The strongest clinical evidence comes from the de novo missense variant study (PMID: 37256937), which identified 15 independent de novo variants in 23 unrelated patients with consistent neurodevelopmental phenotypes, supported by functional evidence of proteasome dysfunction and dysregulated interferon signaling in patient-derived T cells. The zebrafish model study (PMID: 32500975) provided experimental validation through whole-genome sequencing and phenotypic rescue in model organisms.

**Strength of Clinical Evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) multiple independent de novo variants in unaffected parents, supporting autosomal dominant inheritance; (2) consistent phenotypic clustering across patients; (3) functional validation in patient cells demonstrating proteotoxic stress and interferon dysregulation; and (4) model organism validation. However, the total patient number remains modest (~24 published cases), and recruitment from non-Western populations may limit generalizability.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms Studied:**
- Mouse (Mus musculus)
- Zebrafish (Danio rerio)
- Drosophila melanogaster

**Key Phenotypes from Knockouts:**

*Mouse:* Full knockout of Psmc3 resulted in embryonic lethality before implantation with defective blastocyst development (PMID: 10945464), indicating essential developmental function. This severe phenotype suggests that heterozygous variants in humans may cause hypomorphic effects rather than complete loss of function.

*Zebrafish:* Knockout of PSMC3 reproduced human neurosensory phenotypes including inner ear development anomalies and cataracts, with evidence of impaired protein homeostasis and ubiquitinated protein accumulation (PMID: 32500975). This directly validated the proteotoxic stress mechanism observed in human patients.

*Drosophila:* Deletion of the PSMC3 ortholog Rpt5 impaired reversal learning capabilities, demonstrating a specific role in cognitive function consistent with the neurodevelopmental delay phenotype in humans (PMID: 37256937).

**Consistency with Human Phenotype:** Excellent consistency. The zebrafish model directly recapitulated the deafness and cataract phenotypes from the human patient, and the Drosophila model demonstrated cognitive impairment paralleling human intellectual disability. The mouse embryonic lethality suggests that heterozygous missense variants in humans may cause partial loss of function or dominant-negative effects rather than haploinsufficiency.

**Strength of Model Organism Evidence: STRONG**

Multiple independent model systems consistently demonstrate neurodevelopmental and sensory phenotypes aligned with human disease. The zebrafish study provided particularly strong evidence through direct phenotypic rescue and mechanistic validation of proteotoxic stress.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known Pathways and Molecular Function:**

PSMC3 functions as a core component of the 26S proteasome regulatory particle with multiple established roles:

1. **Proteasome-mediated protein degradation:** PSMC3 mediates substrate unfolding and translocation. Pathogenic variants impair this function, leading to accumulation of ubiquitinated proteins and proteotoxic stress (PMID: 37256937).

2. **Type I interferon signaling regulation:** PSMC3 variants dysregulate type I interferon production through PKR activation in patient-derived T cells, suggesting a role in innate immune homeostasis (PMID: 37256937).

3. **RNAi pathway regulation:** PSMC3 stabilizes AGO2 protein through USP14-mediated deubiquitination, promoting RNAi activity. PSMC3 depletion increases AGO2 ubiquitination and degradation (PMID: 36528617).

4. **Protein-protein interactions:** PSMC3 interacts with FRA1 in cancer cell apoptosis regulation (PMID: 38011562) and with HBx protein in hepatitis B virus pathogenesis (PMID: 35695579), indicating broader roles in protein stability regulation.

**Expression Pattern and Neurodevelopmental Relevance:**

While specific brain expression data were not detailed in the provided evidence, the consistent neurodevelopmental phenotypes across multiple independent patients and model organisms strongly suggest PSMC3 expression in developing neural tissues. Recent neuroimaging genetics analysis implicated PSMC3 in Alzheimer's disease pathophysiology through effects on frontal cortex thickness and volume (PMID: 41001458), indicating relevance to brain development and neuronal homeostasis.

**Key Experimental Findings:**

- Patient-derived T cells from PSMC3 variant carriers show impaired proteasome function with accumulation of ubiquitinated proteins and dysregulated interferon signaling (PMID: 37256937)
- PSMC3 was identified as a top-tier therapeutic target in Alzheimer's disease models through siRNA perturbation and quantitative proteomics (PMID: 41388808)
- PSMC3 is part of a genome-wide significant locus (SPI1-SLC39A13-PSMC3-RAPSN) associated with lacunar stroke in large meta-analyses (7,338 cases; 254,798 controls; PMID: 33773637)
- PSMC3 is downregulated in ischemic stroke and identified as a PANoptosis regulator (PMID: 40786625)

**Strength of Biological Evidence: STRONG**

Multiple independent lines of evidence demonstrate PSMC3's role in protein homeostasis, interferon signaling, and RNAi pathways. Functional studies in patient cells directly show proteotoxic stress and interferon dysregulation. The identification of PSMC3 in genome-wide significant loci for stroke and its implication in Alzheimer's disease suggest broader neurological relevance. However, the specific molecular mechanisms by which heterozygous missense variants cause neurodevelopmental delay (versus complete loss of function) require further characterization.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall Evidence Level: STRONG**

**Key Strengths:**

1. **Multiple independent de novo variants in unaffected parents:** The identification of 15 de novo missense variants in 23 unrelated patients (PMID: 37256937) provides compelling evidence for autosomal dominant inheritance with high penetrance.

2. **Functional validation in patient cells:** Direct demonstration of proteotoxic stress, impaired proteasome function, and dysregulated type I interferon signaling in patient-derived T cells mechanistically links PSMC3 variants to disease pathogenesis.

3. **Model organism validation across multiple species:** Zebrafish, Drosophila, and mouse studies consistently demonstrate neurodevelopmental and sensory phenotypes aligned with human disease, with the zebrafish model directly recapitulating the human phenotype.

4. **Consistent phenotypic clustering:** Neurodevelopmental delay, intellectual disability, and autism spectrum disorder appear consistently across independent patient cohorts, suggesting a core disease mechanism.

5. **Biological plausibility:** PSMC3's established roles in protein homeostasis, interferon signaling, and RNAi pathways provide mechanistic explanations for neurodevelopmental dysfunction.

**Key Gaps and Limitations:**

1. **Limited patient cohort size:** Approximately 24 published patients with point variants is modest compared to other neurodevelopmental disorder genes. Recruitment from non-Western populations (Bangladesh) may limit generalizability to other populations.

2. **Incomplete mechanistic understanding:** While proteotoxic stress and interferon dysregulation are demonstrated, the specific molecular mechanisms by which heterozygous missense variants cause neurodevelopmental delay (versus haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative effects) remain incompletely characterized.

3. **Lack of large-scale population data:** No large-scale exome or genome sequencing studies (gnomAD, UK Biobank) were cited to establish the frequency of PSMC3 variants in unaffected populations or to assess constraint metrics.

4. **Limited functional characterization of individual variants:** While the 15 de novo variants were identified, detailed functional studies of individual variants were not described.

5. **Absence from major international cohorts:** No patients from established neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD) were reported, limiting integration with broader disease databases.

**Conclusion:**

PSMC3 is a strong candidate gene for autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder caused by heterozygous missense variants. The evidence is supported by multiple independent de novo variants in unaffected parents, consistent neurodevelopmental phenotypes across patients, functional validation demonstrating proteotoxic stress and interferon dysregulation in patient cells, and consistent phenotypic validation across multiple model organisms. Future studies should focus on expanding the patient cohort through international collaboration, characterizing individual variant effects, and elucidating the specific molecular mechanisms linking PSMC3 dysfunction to neurodevelopmental pathology.
