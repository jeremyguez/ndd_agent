# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: KIAA0100 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

KIAA0100, also known as BLTP2 (bridge-like lipid transfer protein 2), is a large protein (~254 kDa) that functions as a lipid transfer protein mediating bulk lipid transport between the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) and plasma membrane (PM). The protein localizes to ER-PM contact sites through interactions with phosphoinositides, adaptor proteins (FAM102A/FAM102B), and N-BAR domain proteins, where it plays a critical role in controlling plasma membrane dynamics and cellular lipid homeostasis (PMID: 40899996, PMID: 39974967).

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

KIAA0100 functions as a bridge-like lipid transfer protein that tethers the ER to tubular endosomes and macropinosomes, mediating bulk lipid transport between the ER and PM (PMID: 40899996). The protein contains four FMP27 domains, a signal peptide, and transmembrane regions consistent with localization to the secretory pathway (PMID: 29404013). At the molecular level, KIAA0100 interacts with phosphoinositides and adaptor proteins to localize at ER-PM contacts and control PM dynamics (PMID: 39974967).

**Expression pattern:**

Northern blot analysis in U937 cells revealed two mRNA products (6000-10000 bp and 5000-6000 bp) and two protein products (254 kDa and <250 kDa), indicating alternative splicing or post-translational modification (PMID: 29404037). Expression has been documented in cancer cell lines and tumor tissues, but no systematic analysis of expression in developing brain or neurodevelopmental tissues was identified in the available literature.

**Key experimental findings:**

- KIAA0100 silencing in MDA-MB-231 breast cancer cells reduced cell aggregation, metastasis, and invasion, and sensitized cells to anoikis; the protein stabilizes microtubule structure and interacts with heat shock protein HSPA1A (PMID: 29867023).

- CRISPR/Cas9-mediated downregulation in U937 cells inhibited proliferation and promoted apoptosis, suggesting a role as an anti-apoptotic factor (PMID: 29404013).

- KIAA0100 is upregulated in breast carcinoma and lung cancer through regulatory pathways (circ_0000520/miR-512-5p axis in lung cancer; copy-number amplification at 17q11.2 in breast cancer), and elevated expression correlates with aggressive phenotypes and poor prognosis (PMID: 35866672, PMID: 37145128, PMID: 39724502).

**Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE**

While KIAA0100 has well-characterized molecular functions in lipid transport and membrane dynamics, and documented roles in cancer biology, there is no evidence specifically linking the protein to neurodevelopmental processes, neuronal function, or brain development. The biological evidence is robust for cancer-related functions but lacks neurodevelopmental relevance.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
- Well-characterized molecular function in lipid transport and membrane dynamics
- Multiple independent studies confirming protein interactions and cellular localization
- Evidence of functional importance in cellular processes (proliferation, apoptosis, migration)

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- Complete absence of clinical evidence: no reported cases of KIAA0100 pathogenic variants in patients with neurodevelopmental disorders
- No model organism studies examining neurodevelopmental phenotypes
- No expression data from developing brain or neurodevelopmental tissues
- All functional evidence derives from cancer cell lines and tumor tissues, not neuronal systems
- No evidence that loss-of-function or gain-of-function variants cause disease in humans
- The gene is primarily characterized in the context of cancer biology, not neurodevelopmental disease

**Conclusion:**

Based on the available literature, there is currently INSUFFICIENT evidence to support KIAA0100 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene associated with heterozygous pathogenic variants. While KIAA0100 has important cellular functions in lipid transport and membrane dynamics, and is implicated in cancer biology, no clinical cases, model organism evidence, or neurodevelopmental expression data support a role in neurodevelopmental disease. To establish KIAA0100 as a disease gene, clinical case reports of patients with KIAA0100 variants and neurodevelopmental phenotypes, along with functional studies in neuronal systems, would be required.
