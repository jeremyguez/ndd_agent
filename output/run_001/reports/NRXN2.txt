# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: NRXN2 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

NRXN2 (Neurexin-2) is a presynaptic cell adhesion molecule that functions as a context-dependent specifier of synapse properties and critical modulator of excitatory/inhibitory (E/I) balance in the central nervous system (PMID: 36923655). NRXN2 regulates synapse density, neurotransmitter release probability, and axon growth through interactions with postsynaptic binding partners and alternative splicing-dependent mechanisms (PMID: 36961922).

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Case Reports and Case Series:**
Seven independent clinical studies were identified reporting NRXN2 variants in neurodevelopmental disorders, including 6 case reports/small case series and 1 case-control association study.

**Patient Numbers:**
Approximately 7-8 individual patients with NRXN2 variants across case reports, plus 529 ASD cases in the association study (PMID: 29045040).

**Most Common Phenotypes Observed:**
- Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) and developmental delay (reported in 3 studies)
- Epilepsy/seizures and intellectual disability (reported in 3 studies)
- Speech delay and global developmental delay (reported in 2 studies)
- Behavioral inflexibility and social interaction deficits (reported in 1 study)

**Predominant Variant Types:**
- Loss-of-function (LoF) variants: 2 cases (truncating mutation, c.808dup frameshift)
- Missense variants: 4 cases (c.3176G>A p.Arg1059Gln; c.1475T>G p.Leu492Arg homozygous in 2 siblings; c.1901G>T; rare damaging missense SNV)
- Common variant association: 1 study (rs12273892 polymorphism)

**Notable Case Details:**
A homozygous missense variant (c.1475T>G, p.Leu492Arg) was identified in two affected siblings from a consanguineous family with global developmental delay, severe intellectual disability, epilepsy, and speech delay; classified as "Likely Pathogenic" by ACMG/AMP criteria with in-silico prediction of detrimental effects (PMID: 39748282). A digenic case involving NRXN2 c.3176G>A (p.Arg1059Gln) and NRXN1 mutation presented with early infantile epileptic encephalopathy, developmental delay, respiratory depression, and arcuate nucleus hypoplasia (PMID: 30709877). A truncating NRXN2 mutation failed to promote synaptic differentiation and bind postsynaptic partners LRRTM2 and NLGN2 in functional assays (PMID: 21424692).

**Cohort Membership:**
One study identified NRXN2 variants in patients from iTARGET Autism and MSSNG cohorts (ASHG 2018 - Whole genome sequencing analysis identifies multiple candidate variants in a family with autism spectrum disorder). Most other studies were single-center case reports without explicit cohort designation.

**Strength of Clinical Evidence: MODERATE**

The evidence is strengthened by functional validation (failed synaptic differentiation, impaired binding assays), segregation analysis in families, and ACMG/AMP classification. However, the total number of independent patients is small (7-8 case reports), and most are single cases rather than large cohort studies. The association study (PMID: 29045040) provides population-level evidence but is limited to a Chinese population and involves a common variant rather than rare pathogenic variants.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms Studied:**
Mouse (primary model, 8 studies) and zebrafish (2 studies).

**Key Phenotypes from Knockouts:**

*Full Knockout Mice:*
- Social interaction deficits and increased anxiety-like behavior (PMID: 25745399, PMID: 25423136)
- Reduced spontaneous transmitter release at excitatory synapses and altered NMDAR-mediated responses (PMID: 25745399)
- Atypical structural connectivity in socially relevant brain regions (amygdala, anterior cingulate cortex, orbitofrontal cortex, hippocampus) with increased fractional anisotropy and altered axonal orientation (PMID: 30858964)
- Deficits in sociability, social memory, and anxiety-like phenotype with decreased presynaptic vesicular release (PMID: 25423136)
- Severe breathing abnormalities in double neurexin knockout models (PMID: 30709877)

*Conditional Knockout Mice:*
- Behavioral inflexibility, stereotyped behavior patterns, and reversal learning deficits (PMID: 38702381)
- Spontaneous unprovoked seizures (both electrographic and behavioral) with increased hippocampal CA3→CA1 network activity (PMID: 36941261)
- Reduced social preference and increased nestlet shredding behavior (PMID: 36941261)

*Heterozygous Knockout Mice:*
- Lack of social memory for familiar versus novel conspecifics and deficits in long-term object discrimination (PMID: 26595880)

*Zebrafish Models:*
- Knockdown of nrxn2a isoforms phenocopied SMN-deficient fish with reduced motor axon excitability (PMID: 24218366)

**Lethality and Viability:**
Full and conditional NRXN2 knockout mice are viable, indicating that complete loss of NRXN2 is not embryonic lethal. No Mendelian ratio deviations or infertility reported.

**Consistency with Human Phenotype:**
Model organism phenotypes show strong consistency with human clinical presentations: social deficits and behavioral inflexibility align with ASD; seizures align with epilepsy cases; anxiety-like behavior aligns with neurodevelopmental presentations. The respiratory dysfunction in double neurexin knockouts is particularly relevant to the human case with respiratory depression (PMID: 30709877).

**Strength of Model Organism Evidence: STRONG**

Multiple independent mouse models consistently demonstrate neurological and behavioral phenotypes relevant to human neurodevelopmental disorders. The use of both full and conditional knockouts, combined with detailed electrophysiological and structural analyses, provides robust mechanistic insight. Zebrafish models add evolutionary conservation evidence.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known Pathways and Molecular Function:**

NRXN2 functions as a presynaptic cell adhesion molecule that:
- Restricts synapse numbers and restrains presynaptic release probability through alternative splicing-dependent mechanisms (PMID: 36961922)
- Modulates excitatory/inhibitory (E/I) balance, a hallmark disrupted in autism spectrum disorder (PMID: 36923655)
- Acts as a receptor for cerebellin-1 (Cbln1) in mediating axon growth and guidance during early development (PMID: 36395107)
- Interacts with postsynaptic binding partners including LRRTM2 and NLGN2 (PMID: 21424692)

**Expression Pattern:**
NRXN2 is expressed in developing and mature brain, with particular relevance to hippocampus, neocortex, cerebellum, and spinal cord. Expression is dynamically regulated during neurodevelopment and in response to stress (PMID: 36805521 - upregulated nrxn2 in zebrafish larvae in response to cold stress).

**Key Experimental Findings:**

*Splice Variant Specificity:*
In cultured hippocampal neurons, deletion of Nrxn2 caused significant increase in synapse density and release probability. Rescue experiments revealed that specific Nrxn2β splice variants (Nrxn2β-SS4+/SS5- and Nrxn2β-SS4+/SS5+) reversed increased synapse density, while only Nrxn2β-SS4+/SS5+ normalized release probability, demonstrating splice variant-specific synaptic restriction functions (PMID: 36961922).

*Regulatory Mechanisms:*
SNW1 depletion in human embryonic stem cell-derived cerebral organoids resulted in altered NRXN2 expression and disrupted alternative splicing patterns (PMID: 40608414). Autism-associated miR-873 variants showed 20-30% inhibition/dysregulation effects on NRXN2 expression in dual-luciferase assays, with functional consequences in hippocampal neurons (PMID: 33262327).

*Synaptic Electrophysiology:*
Patch-clamp recordings in Nrxn2α knockout mice revealed reduced spontaneous transmitter release at excitatory synapses in neocortex, altered facilitation, and reduced NMDAR-dependent decay time and NMDAR-mediated responses (PMID: 25745399).

**Strength of Biological Evidence: STRONG**

Multiple complementary experimental approaches (cell culture, organoid models, electrophysiology, molecular assays) demonstrate NRXN2's critical roles in synapse development, neurotransmitter release, and E/I balance. The identification of splice variant-specific functions and regulatory mechanisms provides mechanistic depth. Expression studies confirm neurodevelopmental relevance.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall Evidence Level: MODERATE-TO-STRONG**

**Key Strengths:**
1. **Functional validation**: Multiple studies demonstrate that NRXN2 variants impair synaptic function, synapse density regulation, and neurotransmitter release through well-characterized mechanisms.
2. **Consistent phenotypes across evidence types**: Human cases with ASD, epilepsy, developmental delay, and behavioral inflexibility are recapitulated in multiple independent mouse models.
3. **Mechanistic understanding**: Detailed knowledge of NRXN2's role in E/I balance, alternative splicing regulation, and synaptic organization provides biological plausibility for pathogenicity.
4. **Evolutionary conservation**: Phenotypes are conserved across mammalian and non-mammalian models (zebrafish).
5. **Molecular evidence**: Functional assays demonstrate that pathogenic variants fail to bind postsynaptic partners or promote synaptic differentiation.

**Key Gaps and Limitations:**
1. **Limited case series**: Only 7-8 individual patients with rare NRXN2 variants reported; most are single cases rather than large cohort studies. No large-scale exome/genome sequencing studies systematically quantifying NRXN2 variant frequency in neurodevelopmental disorder cohorts.
2. **Heterogeneous phenotypes**: Clinical presentations vary considerably (ASD vs. epilepsy vs. developmental delay), making it difficult to define a specific NRXN2-associated syndrome. The homozygous case (PMID: 39748282) may represent a distinct phenotype from heterozygous cases.
3. **Limited heterozygous data**: Most model organism studies use full or conditional knockouts; heterozygous knockout data are limited to one study (PMID: 26595880).
4. **Incomplete variant characterization**: Several case reports lack detailed variant notation or functional validation. The common variant association study (PMID: 29045040) involves a polymorphism rather than rare pathogenic variants.
5. **Digenic complexity**: The most severe case (PMID: 30709877) involves both NRXN1 and NRXN2 variants, complicating attribution of phenotype to NRXN2 alone.

**Conclusion:**

NRXN2 is a well-characterized neurodevelopmental gene with strong biological evidence for pathogenicity of loss-of-function and missense variants. Clinical evidence from case reports and functional studies demonstrates that NRXN2 variants cause autism spectrum disorder, epilepsy, developmental delay, and behavioral inflexibility through disruption of synaptic function and E/I balance. However, the limited number of reported patients and heterogeneous clinical presentations prevent definitive classification as a major monogenic neurodevelopmental disorder gene at this time. Systematic screening of large neurodevelopmental cohorts and additional functional characterization of identified variants would strengthen the evidence base.
