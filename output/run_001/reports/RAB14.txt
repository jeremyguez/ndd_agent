# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: RAB14 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

RAB14 is a small GTPase belonging to the Rab family of proteins that functions as a key regulator of intracellular membrane trafficking and vesicular transport. RAB14 localizes to multiple compartments including the trans-Golgi network, endosomes, autophagosomes, and mitochondria, where it coordinates diverse cellular processes including autophagy, cytokinesis, endosomal recycling, and mitophagy.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (primary), Xenopus (secondary)

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

Mouse studies reveal multiple critical functions of RAB14 with significant developmental and cellular consequences:

- **Embryonic lethality:** RAB14 full knockout mice are embryonic lethal at the peri-implantation stage, failing to develop epiblast and primitive endoderm lineages due to impaired FGF receptor transport and defective FGF signaling (PMID: 21238925). This demonstrates RAB14 is essential for early embryonic development.

- **Autophagy dysfunction:** RAB14 knockout cells show altered MAP1LC3/LC3-II levels and impaired autophagosome maturation, with a mild autophagy-defective phenotype in single knockouts that becomes severe in RAB2/RAB14 double knockouts (PMID: 38953305). RAB14 localizes to autophagosomes and interacts with HOPS complex subunits.

- **Cytokinesis failure:** RAB14 depletion causes cytokinesis failure or significantly prolonged division time through impaired recruitment of Rab11-endosomes to intracellular bridge microtubules and inhibition of actin clearance at the abscission site (PMID: 33566684).

- **Female infertility:** RAB14 knockout mice show impaired follicle development, increased granulosa cell apoptosis, and significantly reduced litter size through disrupted lipophagy and lipid homeostasis in ovarian cells (PMID: 41553083).

- **Neurodegeneration:** In cathepsin B/L-deficient mice, RAB14 protein accumulates more than 10-fold in brain lysosomes and specifically stains swollen axons, associated with early-onset neurodegeneration reminiscent of neuronal ceroid lipofuscinoses (PMID: 17765022). This suggests RAB14 dysfunction contributes to lysosomal storage disease pathology.

- **Intestinal inflammation:** Rab11FIP1-deficient mice (which affects RAB14-dependent pathways) develop spontaneous colon inflammation with abnormal accumulation of subapical vesicles in colonocytes and internalization of transmembrane mucin MUC13 with RAB14, showing greater susceptibility to DSS-induced mucosal damage (PMID: 35819177).

**Consistency with human neurodevelopmental phenotype:** The embryonic lethality and autophagy dysfunction observed in RAB14 knockouts are consistent with severe neurodevelopmental disorders, though no human cases have been reported. The neurodegeneration phenotype in the lysosomal storage disease model is particularly relevant to potential neurological manifestations.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strong because multiple independent mouse knockout studies demonstrate essential roles for RAB14 in early development, autophagy, and neuronal function. However, the strength is limited by the absence of any human clinical correlation.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

RAB14 participates in multiple critical cellular pathways:

- **Autophagy and mitophagy:** RAB14 promotes Parkin-mediated mitophagy through TBK1 and PI3K activity, with TGN-derived RAB14 vesicles mediating mitophagy through contact site formation with mitochondria (PMID: 40737294). RAB14 also regulates autophagosome maturation through HOPS complex interactions.

- **Endosomal trafficking and recycling:** RAB14 and its GEF FAM116A define an intermediate recycling compartment in the transferrin-recycling pathway, regulating ADAM10 cell-surface trafficking and adherens junction stability during cell migration (PMID: 22595670). RAB14 also regulates formation of RAB14+ endosome buds at Golgi-endosome contacts through the RhoBTB3-SHIP164-Vps26B complex (PMID: 38565878).

- **Fatty acid metabolism:** RAB14 facilitates fatty acid trafficking from lipid droplets to mitochondria through interaction with CPT1A (PMID: 29721083) and regulates long-chain fatty acid uptake into skeletal muscle via SLC27A4/FATP4 (PMID: 32868338).

- **Cytokinesis:** RAB14 binds to the MACF2/CAMSAP3 complex and is essential for endosome targeting to intracellular bridge microtubules during cell division.

- **Erythropoiesis:** RAB14 is a direct target of miR-144 and miR-451 and acts as a physiological inhibitor of human erythropoiesis (PMID: 25312678).

**Expression pattern:** While the biological evidence does not explicitly detail brain-specific expression patterns, RAB14's presence in neuronal lysosomes (PMID: 17765022) and its critical role in autophagy and mitochondrial function suggest significant neuronal expression. RAB14 is also expressed in multiple tissues including cancer cell lines, chondrocytes, and erythroid cells.

**Key experimental findings:**

- RAB14 is upregulated in multiple cancer types (lung, ovarian, breast, pancreatic, bladder) and promotes proliferation, invasion, and drug resistance through various signaling pathways (YAP, Wnt/β-catenin, PI3K/AKT).

- RAB14 interacts with multiple protein partners including CPT1A, CCN2, FAM116A, HOPS complex subunits, and retromer components, indicating a hub role in cellular trafficking.

- RAB14 preferentially binds to the GDP-bound inactive form in some contexts (UT-A1 regulation) while functioning as an active GTPase effector in others (Vps26B interaction).

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is strong, with multiple independent studies demonstrating RAB14's essential roles in autophagy, mitochondrial function, endosomal trafficking, and cell division. However, direct evidence linking RAB14 dysfunction to neurodevelopmental pathways remains limited.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: LIMITED**

**Key strengths:**

1. **Essential developmental role:** RAB14 knockout mice are embryonic lethal, demonstrating the gene is essential for early development and survival.

2. **Neurological relevance:** RAB14 accumulates in brain lysosomes and is implicated in neurodegeneration in lysosomal storage disease models, suggesting potential neurological consequences of dysfunction.

3. **Autophagy dysfunction:** RAB14's critical role in autophagosome maturation and mitophagy is well-established and relevant to neurodevelopmental and neurodegenerative disorders.

4. **Multiple cellular functions:** RAB14's involvement in cytokinesis, endosomal trafficking, and mitochondrial function suggests broad cellular consequences of pathogenic variants.

**Key gaps and limitations:**

1. **Complete absence of clinical evidence:** No human cases with RAB14 pathogenic variants have been reported in any disease context, let alone neurodevelopmental disorders. This is the most critical limitation.

2. **No heterozygous variant studies:** All model organism evidence derives from complete knockouts. No studies examine the effects of heterozygous loss-of-function or dominant-negative variants, which would be more relevant to autosomal dominant inheritance.

3. **No brain-specific functional studies:** While RAB14 is present in brain tissue, no studies specifically examine its role in neuronal development, synaptogenesis, or neurodevelopmental processes.

4. **Embryonic lethality confounds interpretation:** The embryonic lethality of RAB14 knockouts makes it difficult to assess postnatal neurological phenotypes without conditional knockout approaches.

5. **Cancer-focused biological evidence:** The majority of biological evidence focuses on RAB14's role in cancer cell proliferation and signaling, with limited direct relevance to neurodevelopmental function.

**Conclusion:**

While RAB14 is clearly an essential gene with critical roles in autophagy, mitochondrial function, and early development based on strong model organism and biological evidence, there is currently **insufficient evidence** to establish RAB14 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene. The complete absence of clinical case reports or patient cohorts with RAB14 variants is the primary limitation. To establish RAB14 as a disease gene, human genetic studies identifying pathogenic variants in patients with neurodevelopmental phenotypes would be required, along with functional studies of heterozygous variants in relevant cellular and animal models.
