# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: SECISBP2L AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

SECISBP2L (SECIS-binding protein 2-like) is a paralogue of SECIS-binding protein 2 (SBP2) that functions as a critical regulator of selenoprotein synthesis, particularly through its role in directing the translation of selenoproteins containing selenocysteine (Sec) residues. The gene has emerged as a key player in oligodendrocyte differentiation and myelination through its regulation of deiodinase 2 (DIO2) and thyroid hormone metabolism.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of case reports/series:** 0

**Total patients:** 0

**Most common phenotypes:** Not applicable

**Predominant variant type:** Not applicable

**Known cohort sources:** None identified

**Strength of clinical evidence: NONE**

No independent case reports, case series, or clinical cohorts with SECISBP2L pathogenic variants have been identified in the literature. This represents a critical gap between the strong biological and model organism evidence and the complete absence of human clinical data linking SECISBP2L variants to neurodevelopmental or other human disorders.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Zebrafish and mouse

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

In zebrafish (PMID: 35210313), full knockout of secisbp2l alone did not produce obvious developmental phenotypes, as secisbp2l -/- embryos retained a full complement of selenoproteins. However, the double mutant (sbp2 -/- secisbp2l -/-) completely prevented selenoprotein synthesis and resulted in juvenile lethality at 14 days post-fertilization. Single sbp2 -/- mutants showed selective loss of selenoproteins and increased sensitivity to oxidative stress with elevated Vtg1 stress marker expression, demonstrating functional redundancy between the two paralogues.

In mice, conditional knockout of Secisbp2l in the oligodendrocyte lineage (PMID: 35760530) produced significant neurodevelopmental phenotypes including decreased DIO2 protein levels, reduced T3 (active thyroid hormone) levels, impaired oligodendrocyte differentiation, hypomyelination, and motor deficits in both sexes. Notably, the defective oligodendrocyte differentiation was rescued by T3 or T3 analogues but not T4, establishing a specific pathway requirement.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The mouse conditional knockout phenotype (hypomyelination, motor deficits) is consistent with expected neurodevelopmental manifestations, though no human cases have been reported to validate this correlation.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**

The model organism evidence is robust, demonstrating clear gene function in selenoprotein synthesis and a specific, well-characterized role in oligodendrocyte differentiation and myelination through the DIO2-T3 pathway.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

SECISBP2L functions as a SECIS-binding protein required for selenoprotein synthesis. The gene is a paralogue of SBP2 with functional redundancy, as demonstrated by the zebrafish double mutant studies (PMID: 35210313). A key established pathway is the SECISBP2L-DIO2-T3 axis in oligodendrocyte differentiation (PMID: 35760530), where SECISBP2L is required for DIO2 translation, which in turn regulates local T3 (triiodothyronine) production essential for myelin development.

**Expression pattern:**

SECISBP2L is highly expressed in differentiating oligodendrocytes (PMID: 35760530). Beyond the CNS, GWAS and eQTL studies have identified SECISBP2L in normal lung tissue with cis-acting effects on gene expression (PMID: 28604730, ASHG 2016). The gene has also been identified in hippocampal expression screens related to neuroinflammation and neurogenesis (PMID: 37739108).

**Key experimental findings:**

- SECISBP2L is identified as a tier 1 therapeutic target for lung adenocarcinoma with strong causal evidence through Mendelian randomization (PMID: 39092217)
- SECISBP2L functions as a regulatory hub in lung squamous cell carcinoma, controlling multiple target RNAs involved in angiogenesis and signaling pathways (PMID: 34931870)
- SECISBP2L is a highly interconnected hub gene in acute myeloid leukemia treatment response networks with prognostic significance (hazard ratio 4.12) (PMID: 38069522)
- Cross-trait GWAS analysis identified SECISBP2L in pleiotropic loci shared between headache and thyroid function (free thyroxine), with genome-wide significance (pFisher's combined p-value < 2.09 × 10⁻⁶) (PMID: 36672757)

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is comprehensive, demonstrating clear molecular function in selenoprotein synthesis, a well-characterized pathway in oligodendrocyte biology, and consistent identification across multiple independent genomic and expression studies.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE**

**Key strengths:**

1. **Strong functional characterization:** SECISBP2L has well-established roles in selenoprotein synthesis and oligodendrocyte differentiation with clear mechanistic pathways defined in model organisms.

2. **Robust model organism evidence:** Both zebrafish and mouse studies demonstrate specific neurodevelopmental phenotypes (hypomyelination, motor deficits) consistent with a neurodevelopmental disorder gene, with clear genotype-phenotype correlations.

3. **Biological plausibility:** Multiple independent lines of evidence (GWAS, eQTL, expression studies, pathway analysis) support SECISBP2L's involvement in neural development and thyroid hormone regulation, both critical for neurodevelopment.

4. **Functional redundancy established:** The zebrafish double mutant studies clearly demonstrate that SECISBP2L and SBP2 have overlapping functions, which could explain why heterozygous variants might produce phenotypes through haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative mechanisms.

**Key gaps and limitations:**

1. **Complete absence of human clinical data:** No case reports, case series, or identified patients with SECISBP2L pathogenic variants exist in the literature. This is the most critical limitation preventing classification as a definitively established neurodevelopmental disorder gene.

2. **No evidence for heterozygous pathogenicity:** All model organism studies examined homozygous knockouts or conditional knockouts. There is no experimental evidence that heterozygous variants would produce human disease, which is essential for establishing autosomal dominant inheritance.

3. **Limited CNS expression data:** While SECISBP2L is known to be highly expressed in oligodendrocytes, comprehensive expression profiling across other CNS cell types and developmental stages is not detailed in the available literature.

4. **No human variant studies:** There are no studies examining the functional consequences of specific human variants in SECISBP2L or their segregation in families.

**Conclusion:**

SECISBP2L has strong biological and model organism evidence supporting a role in neurodevelopment, particularly through its regulation of oligodendrocyte differentiation and myelination via the DIO2-T3 pathway. However, the complete absence of human clinical cases with SECISBP2L pathogenic variants, combined with the lack of evidence for heterozygous pathogenicity, prevents definitive classification as an established neurodevelopmental disorder gene at this time. Future clinical identification of patients with SECISBP2L variants and functional studies of heterozygous effects would be necessary to establish this gene's role in human neurodevelopmental disease.
