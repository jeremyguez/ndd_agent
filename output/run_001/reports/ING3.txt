# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: ING3 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

ING3 (Inhibitor of Growth 3) is an epigenetic reader protein that recognizes the H3K4me3 histone mark and recruits histone acetyltransferase (HAT) or histone deacetylase (HDAC) complexes to regulate chromatin structure and gene expression. ING3 functions as a stoichiometric member of the NuA4 HAT complex and plays essential roles in cellular growth, proliferation, and embryonic development (PMID: 31752342, PMID: 31905726).

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Case Reports/Series:** One independent case report was identified.

**Total Patients:** 1 patient

**Phenotypes Observed:** The single reported patient with a 22.3-Mb interstitial duplication of chromosome 7q31.2→q33 (including ING3) presented with:
- Growth restriction and short stature
- Speech delay
- Intellectual disability
- Inguinal hernia
- Strabismus

(PMID: 22303804)

**Variant Type:** Duplication (gene dosage increase); not a loss-of-function variant

**Cohort Source:** Not from known diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL)

**Diagnostic Reasoning:** The authors speculated that gene dosage increase of ING3 (along with LEP) may be partially responsible for the growth restriction and short stature phenotype, though this was a large multi-gene duplication making attribution to ING3 alone uncertain.

**Strength of Clinical Evidence: LIMITED**

The clinical evidence is severely limited by: (1) only a single patient case; (2) a large multi-gene duplication rather than an isolated ING3 variant; (3) a gain-of-function mechanism (duplication) rather than loss-of-function; (4) no independent replication; and (5) speculative attribution of phenotype to ING3 specifically. This case does not provide strong support for ING3 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous loss-of-function variants.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms Studied:** Mouse and zebrafish

**Key Knockout Phenotypes:**

*Mouse:*
- Full ING3 knockout results in embryonic lethality (PMID: 31752342, PMID: 31905726, PMID: 40149370)
- Homozygous null mice show embryonic lethality at E10.5 with growth retardation and neural tube closure defects (PMID: 31905726)
- Conditional prostate-specific ING3 knockout shows increased DNA damage markers (γH2AX and 53BP1) but no gross anatomical abnormalities or prostate intraepithelial neoplasia (PMID: 40149370)
- Full knockout mice also demonstrate altered bone morphology and effects on osteoblast differentiation (PMID: 36943599)

*Zebrafish:*
- ING3 knockdown increases tfpia mRNA levels and prolongs thrombus formation time (PMID: 34315984), though this is not directly neurodevelopmental

**Mendelian Ratio Deviations:** Homozygous null mice are embryonic lethal, indicating essential gene function

**Consistency with Human Phenotype:** The embryonic lethality and neural tube closure defects in homozygous knockout mice are consistent with ING3 being essential for early development. However, the single human case involved a duplication (gain-of-function) rather than loss-of-function, making direct phenotypic correlation difficult. The mouse data do not directly support heterozygous loss-of-function causing neurodevelopmental disorder in humans.

**Strength of Model Organism Evidence: MODERATE**

The mouse data strongly demonstrate that ING3 is essential for embryonic development and neural tube formation, supporting a critical developmental role. However, the evidence is limited by: (1) lack of heterozygous knockout phenotype data; (2) embryonic lethality preventing assessment of postnatal neurodevelopmental effects; and (3) no specific neurodevelopmental disorder phenotypes reported in viable animals.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known Pathways and Molecular Function:**

ING3 functions as an epigenetic regulator through multiple mechanisms:
- Histone mark reader: Recognizes H3K4me3 and recruits HAT/HDAC complexes to regulate chromatin accessibility (PMID: 31752342)
- NuA4 HAT complex member: Functions with EAF6, EPC1, and TIP60 to acetylate histones and promote gene expression (PMID: 31905726)
- Cell cycle regulation: Controls expression of cell cycle genes by associating with chromatin modifiers at transcriptional start sites (PMID: 29381681)
- Androgen receptor signaling: Acts as a scaffold to enhance TIP60-AR interaction and promote prostate cancer growth (PMID: 28511652)
- Tumor suppressor: Undergoes ubiquitin-proteasome degradation via SCF(Skp2) E3 ligase; stabilization enhances tumor-suppressive functions including G1 arrest and apoptosis (PMID: 19935701)

**Expression Pattern:**

ING3 is most highly expressed in tissues with rapid cell proliferation and renewal:
- Small intestine, bone marrow, and epidermis show highest expression (PMID: 25819753)
- Expression is significantly higher in proliferating versus quiescent epithelial cells (PMID: 25819753)
- Notably, no specific data on ING3 expression in brain or neural tissues during development were identified in the provided evidence

**Key Experimental Findings:**

- ING3 knockdown in primary human MSC-derived osteoblasts inhibits osteoblastogenesis with decreased alkaline phosphatase and absent mineral deposition (ASHG 2018 - Genome-scale capture C promoter interaction analysis)
- ING3 overexpression restrains proliferation, migration, and invasion in lung adenocarcinoma and breast cancer cells (PMID: 38281617, PMID: 34007308)
- Reduced nuclear ING3 expression in melanomas correlates with poor prognosis (5-year survival 93% with strong staining vs. 44% with negative-to-moderate staining) (PMID: 17634537)
- ING3 stimulates cellular proliferation in benign prostate hyperplasia tissues and transforms normal human dermal fibroblasts (PMID: 29381681)

**Strength of Biological Evidence: STRONG**

The biological evidence is strong regarding ING3's role as an epigenetic regulator essential for cellular growth and proliferation. However, the evidence has a critical gap: there is minimal direct evidence linking ING3 to neurodevelopmental processes specifically. While embryonic lethality and neural tube defects in knockout mice suggest developmental importance, the biological studies focus primarily on cancer biology, bone development, and general cell proliferation rather than neurogenesis, neural differentiation, or brain development.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall Evidence Level: LIMITED**

**Key Strengths:**
1. Strong biological evidence that ING3 is essential for development and cellular proliferation
2. Mouse knockout data demonstrating embryonic lethality and neural tube closure defects, indicating critical developmental role
3. ING3 functions as an epigenetic regulator controlling gene expression through histone modifications, a mechanism relevant to neurodevelopment

**Key Gaps and Limitations:**
1. **Minimal clinical evidence:** Only one case report with a multi-gene duplication (gain-of-function), not a loss-of-function variant
2. **No heterozygous human data:** No reported cases of heterozygous ING3 loss-of-function variants in humans with neurodevelopmental phenotypes
3. **Lack of brain-specific expression data:** No evidence provided on ING3 expression in developing brain or neural tissues
4. **No neurodevelopmental-specific biological studies:** Biological evidence focuses on cancer, bone, and general proliferation rather than neurogenesis or neural differentiation
5. **Embryonic lethality confound:** Homozygous knockout lethality prevents assessment of postnatal neurodevelopmental phenotypes in viable animals
6. **Mechanism mismatch:** The single human case involved duplication (increased dosage), opposite to the loss-of-function mechanism expected for autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorders

**Conclusion:**

While ING3 is clearly essential for embryonic development based on mouse knockout data and functions as an important epigenetic regulator, the current evidence does NOT support ING3 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous loss-of-function variants. The single clinical case involves a gain-of-function duplication rather than loss-of-function, and there is insufficient evidence linking ING3 specifically to neurodevelopmental processes or demonstrating that heterozygous ING3 variants cause neurodevelopmental phenotypes in humans. Additional clinical cases with isolated ING3 loss-of-function variants and brain-specific functional studies would be needed to establish this gene-disease relationship.

8. Prognostic significance of nuclear ING3 expression in human cutaneous melanoma (PMID: 17634537)
