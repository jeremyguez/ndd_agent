# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: NUP85 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

NUP85 is a nucleoporin subunit of the Y-complex of the nuclear pore complex (NPC) that mediates nuclear-cytoplasmic transport and couples transcription to mRNA quality control and export (PMID: 39138317). Beyond its canonical role in nuclear transport, NUP85 has emerged as a multifunctional protein involved in lipid metabolism, inflammation, and neurodevelopmental processes.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent case reports/series:** 4 independent publications describing NUP85-associated disease

**Total patients:** Approximately 4 individual patients with detailed clinical characterization (3 case reports with single patients; 1 case series reference without specific patient count)

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome (SRNS) with focal segmental glomerulosclerosis: present in 3 of 4 case reports
- Neurodevelopmental impairment: present in 2 of 4 reports, including microcephaly, axial hypotonia, lack of motor milestones, refractory seizures, and structural brain abnormalities (corpus callosum hypoplasia, simplified gyration)
- Cleft lip-palate and mild intellectual disability: reported in 1 case (PMID: 39949197)
- Primary autosomal recessive microcephaly without SRNS: reported in 1 case (PMID: 36846113)

**Predominant variant type:** Missense variants exclusively; no loss-of-function variants (frameshift, nonsense, or splice site) reported in the clinical evidence

**Specific variants identified:**
- c.611T>A, p.Val204Glu (compound heterozygous)
- c.1904T>G, p.Leu635Arg (compound heterozygous)
- c.1379G>A, p.Arg460Gln (homozygous)
- Compound heterozygous missense variants (unspecified) in MCPH case

**Cohort source:** No patients from established research cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL) explicitly mentioned; cases appear to be from clinical diagnostic centers

**Inheritance pattern:** All reported cases show autosomal recessive inheritance (compound heterozygous or homozygous), NOT autosomal dominant. This is a critical limitation for the stated objective of assessing heterozygous pathogenic variants.

**Strength of clinical evidence: LIMITED**

The clinical evidence is limited by: (1) very small sample size (4 patients total across 4 publications); (2) all cases demonstrate autosomal recessive inheritance, not heterozygous dominant inheritance; (3) significant phenotypic heterogeneity even among recessive cases (ranging from isolated MCPH to severe multisystem disease); (4) lack of functional validation in most cases; (5) no population-based prevalence data or cohort studies establishing disease frequency.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Xenopus, zebrafish, and mouse

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:**

- **Xenopus (morpholino knockdown):** Disrupted glomerulogenesis, consistent with renal phenotype in humans (PMID: 30179222)
- **Xenopus (immunodepletion of NPC subcomplex containing NUP85):** Severe defects in NLS-mediated nuclear import, DNA replication defects, and formation of pore-free nuclei (PMID: 12718872)
- **Zebrafish (CRISPR/Cas9 knockout):** Developmental anomalies and embryonic lethality (PMID: 30179222)
- **Mouse (podocyte-specific conditional knockout of Nup160, a related NPC component):** Progressive proteinuria, fusion of podocyte foot processes, and dysregulation of Cdc42 signaling (PMID: 40298220)

**Consistency with human phenotype:** The model organism data demonstrates that disruption of NPC components causes glomerular dysfunction and developmental abnormalities, consistent with the renal and neurodevelopmental phenotypes observed in human patients. However, no organism-specific studies of NUP85 heterozygous mutations were identified.

**Neurological phenotypes in models:** The zebrafish knockout shows developmental anomalies and lethality, but specific neurological phenotypes (seizures, microcephaly, structural brain abnormalities) are not detailed. The mouse study focused on podocyte-specific knockout and did not assess neurodevelopmental outcomes.

**Strength of model organism evidence: MODERATE**

The evidence is moderate because: (1) multiple model systems demonstrate that NPC disruption causes relevant phenotypes (glomerulonephritis, developmental defects); (2) however, no studies specifically examined heterozygous NUP85 mutations; (3) no organism-specific studies of NUP85 itself (only related nucleoporins); (4) neurological phenotypes are not well-characterized in the available models; (5) no Mendelian ratio data provided for any crosses.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

NUP85 functions in multiple biological contexts:

1. **Nuclear pore complex structure and function:** NUP85 is a core component of the Y-complex that interacts with basket subunit Nup60 through tripartite junctions involving short linear motifs, coupling transcription to mRNA quality control and export (PMID: 39138317)

2. **Lipid metabolism and inflammation:** NUP85 regulates the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway and interacts with CCR2 to modulate lipid metabolism and inflammation (PMID: 38617542). NUP85 promotes lipid accumulation via the SLC27A1/PPAR-γ pathway (PMID: 41413582)

3. **Endoplasmic reticulum stress and fibrosis:** NUP85 regulates the USP47/ASK1 signaling pathway, controlling deubiquitination of ASK1 and exacerbating ER stress (PMID: 41903125)

4. **Macrophage autophagy:** NUP85 functions as a substrate for chaperone-mediated autophagy in macrophages; impaired degradation contributes to liver inflammation (PMID: 36912786)

5. **Viral pathogenesis:** NUP85 interacts with influenza A virus polymerase subunits to facilitate nuclear import of viral ribonucleoprotein (PMID: 36051755)

6. **Stress response:** In plants, NUP85 associates with the nup107-160 complex and mediator complex to regulate stress-responsive gene expression (PMID: 29232718)

**Expression pattern:** NUP85 mRNA and protein are upregulated in hepatocellular carcinoma and highly expressed in immune cells (macrophages, B cells, T cells) (PMID: 38952090). Expression in brain tissue and during neurodevelopment is not explicitly characterized in the provided evidence.

**Key experimental findings relevant to neurodevelopmental disease:**

- Patient-derived fibroblasts from MCPH cases showed reduced cell viability, and structural simulations predicted altered NUP85 structure and interactions with neighboring nucleoporins (PMID: 36846113)
- Molecular dynamics simulations of the p.Arg460Gln variant revealed destabilization of protein helix bundle (PMID: 39949197)
- Coimmunoprecipitation studies demonstrated that certain pathogenic alleles weaken interaction between neighboring NPC subunits (PMID: 30179222)

**Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE**

The evidence is moderate because: (1) NUP85's role in nuclear transport and NPC assembly is well-established; (2) multiple signaling pathways have been identified; (3) however, the specific mechanisms linking NUP85 dysfunction to neurodevelopmental phenotypes (microcephaly, seizures, cortical malformations) are not well-characterized; (4) brain-specific expression and function of NUP85 are not detailed; (5) most pathway studies focus on hepatic and immune contexts rather than neurodevelopmental tissues.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: LIMITED**

**Key strengths:**
1. NUP85 is a well-characterized structural component of the nuclear pore complex with established roles in nuclear transport
2. Multiple independent case reports document pathogenic missense variants causing severe phenotypes including neurodevelopmental impairment
3. Model organism studies confirm that NPC disruption causes relevant developmental and renal phenotypes
4. Molecular studies demonstrate that pathogenic variants alter protein structure and NPC subunit interactions
5. The phenotypic spectrum is expanding, with recent reports linking NUP85 variants to microcephaly, seizures, and cleft palate

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. **Critical limitation for stated objective:** All reported clinical cases show autosomal recessive inheritance (compound heterozygous or homozygous variants), NOT heterozygous autosomal dominant inheritance. The clinical evidence does not support the premise of heterozygous pathogenic variants causing neurodevelopmental disease.
2. Extremely small sample size (4 patients total) limits statistical power and generalizability
3. No functional studies demonstrating that heterozygous carriers of these variants have disease phenotypes
4. Significant phenotypic heterogeneity even among recessive cases suggests genetic or environmental modifiers
5. No brain-specific expression data or neurodevelopmental pathway analysis
6. Model organism studies do not specifically examine heterozygous NUP85 mutations
7. Lack of population-based prevalence data or large cohort studies
8. No studies of heterozygous family members to assess whether carriers are phenotypically normal or affected

**Conclusion:**

While NUP85 is emerging as a gene associated with severe multisystem disease including neurodevelopmental impairment, the current evidence base is limited and does NOT support autosomal dominant inheritance with heterozygous pathogenic variants. All published clinical cases demonstrate autosomal recessive inheritance with compound heterozygous or homozygous missense variants. To establish NUP85 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous pathogenic variants, studies documenting affected individuals carrying single heterozygous variants, functional evidence that heterozygous variants cause haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative effects, and characterization of NUP85 expression and function in developing brain tissue would be required.
