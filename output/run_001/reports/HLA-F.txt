# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: HLA-F AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

HLA-F is a non-classical, oligomorphic HLA Class Ib molecule primarily expressed on trophoblasts at the maternal-fetal interface and on extravillous trophoblasts that have invaded the maternal decidua (PMID: 16487601). Its primary known functions include participation in immune tolerance at the maternal-fetal interface through KIR-HLA and ILT2/ILT4 receptor interactions with decidual NK cells, regulation of trophoblast proliferation via PKM2-dependent glycolysis, and modulation of immune responses through cytokine secretion and NK cell differentiation (PMID: 37854606, PMID: 40251569, PMID: 35610960).

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of case reports/series:** 0

**Total patients:** 0

**Most common phenotypes:** Not applicable

**Predominant variant type:** Not applicable

**Strength of clinical evidence: NONE**

No clinical case reports, case series, or patient cohorts with HLA-F pathogenic variants and neurodevelopmental phenotypes were identified in the literature. The clinical evidence section returned no results, indicating an absence of published clinical data linking HLA-F variants to neurodevelopmental disorders in humans.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Mouse (Mus musculus)

**Key phenotypes from knockouts:** Two independent mouse knockout studies were identified. The first demonstrated that FAT10 gene knockout (note: FAT10 is the mouse ortholog of human HLA-F) resulted in viable, fertile mice with extended median lifespan (+20%), reduced adiposity, elevated metabolic rate, enhanced insulin sensitivity, and increased AMP kinase activity in skeletal muscle (PMID: 24706839). The second study showed that FAT10 knockout had no effect on MHC-I antigen presentation, surface expression, or cytotoxic T cell responses to viruses (PMID: 40821782).

**Neurological phenotypes:** Neither knockout study reported neurological, behavioral, or neurodevelopmental phenotypes. No lethality or Mendelian ratio deviations were observed.

**Consistency with human phenotype:** Not applicable, as no human neurodevelopmental phenotypes have been reported.

**Strength of model organism evidence: LIMITED**

While mouse knockout studies demonstrate that HLA-F is not essential for viability or basic immune function, they provide no evidence of neurodevelopmental involvement. The absence of neurological phenotypes in knockout mice does not exclude a role in human neurodevelopment, particularly given species differences in brain development and immune function.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

HLA-F functions through multiple mechanisms:
- **Immune tolerance at maternal-fetal interface:** HLA-F participates in KIR-HLA alloreognition with decidual NK cells and binds inhibitory receptors ILT2 and ILT4 on uterine NK cells, contributing to immune tolerance of the semi-allogeneic fetus (PMID: 35610960, PMID: 35234898)
- **Trophoblast proliferation:** HLA-F regulates trophoblast proliferation through direct binding to the PKM2 promoter, enhancing PKM2-dependent glycolytic flux by downregulating lactylation at the K305 residue (PMID: 40251569)
- **NK cell modulation:** HLA-F overexpression in trophoblasts promotes secretion of immunoregulatory cytokines (CSF1, CCL22) and drives adaptive NKG2C+ NK cell transformation (PMID: 37854606)

**Expression pattern:**

HLA-F mRNA is detected in most cell types with protein localized in the endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus in resting cells (PMID: 37166140). Upon cell activation, HLA-F reaches the cell membrane. Expression is particularly notable at the maternal-fetal interface, where HLA-F is expressed on extravillous trophoblasts and fluctuates during the menstrual cycle with peak levels during the implantation window (PMID: 16487601, PMID: 35234898). No brain-specific or neurodevelopmental expression has been documented.

**Key experimental findings:**

- Single-cell RNA-seq analysis of 101,250 cells revealed HLA-F-mediated immune tolerance mechanisms at the maternal-fetal interface in preeclampsia (PMID: 37854606)
- HLA-F genetic polymorphisms (rs1362126, rs2523405, rs2523393) in the HLA-F-AS1 region show dose-dependent effects on HLA-F mRNA expression (PMID: 37166140)
- Two HLA-F SNPs (rs3129055 and rs9258122) demonstrated significant association with nasopharyngeal carcinoma predisposition (p = 7.36×10⁻¹¹ and 3.33×10⁻¹⁰) (ASHG 2010 - Identification of multiple genetic variants related to nasopharyngeal carcinoma predisposition)
- HLA-F polymorphisms are associated with alloimmunization susceptibility in sickle cell disease through LILRB1 receptor interactions (PMID: 37686397)

**Strength of biological evidence: MODERATE**

The biological evidence demonstrates robust molecular and cellular functions for HLA-F, particularly in immune tolerance and metabolic regulation. However, all characterized functions relate to reproductive immunology, trophoblast biology, and systemic immune responses. No evidence links HLA-F to neurodevelopmental pathways, neurogenesis, synaptic function, or brain development.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
- Well-characterized molecular functions and signaling pathways for HLA-F
- Multiple independent studies confirming immune tolerance roles at the maternal-fetal interface
- Demonstration of functional consequences of HLA-F polymorphisms in disease contexts
- Viable mouse knockouts indicating HLA-F is not essential for basic development

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- Complete absence of clinical case reports or patient data linking HLA-F variants to neurodevelopmental disorders
- No neurological phenotypes observed in animal models
- No documented expression or function in brain tissue or neurodevelopmental contexts
- All published evidence focuses on reproductive immunology and cancer predisposition, not neurodevelopment
- No functional studies of potential neurodevelopmental pathways

**Conclusion:**

There is currently insufficient evidence to support HLA-F as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene. While HLA-F has well-established roles in immune tolerance and trophoblast biology, no clinical cases, animal model evidence, or biological data link HLA-F variants to neurodevelopmental phenotypes. To establish HLA-F as a neurodevelopmental gene would require identification of patients with HLA-F variants and neurodevelopmental disorders, demonstration of neurodevelopmental phenotypes in animal models, and characterization of HLA-F function in brain development or neuronal biology.
