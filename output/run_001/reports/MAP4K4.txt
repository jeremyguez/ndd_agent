# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: MAP4K4 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

MAP4K4 (Mitogen-Activated Protein Kinase Kinase Kinase Kinase 4) is a serine/threonine kinase that functions as a central integrator of extracellular signals, with key roles in regulating RAS signaling, Hippo pathway mechanotransduction, and cytoskeletal dynamics. The gene is extremely intolerant to missense variation (Z-score 4.01, pLI=1), indicating strong selective constraint against heterozygous disruption.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Number of independent studies:** 5 publications (4 peer-reviewed articles + 1 conference abstract)

**Total patients:** Approximately 32 patients across all studies (21 patients from PMID: 37126546; 2 patients from PMID: 36469137; 1 patient from PMID: 38679877; 1 patient from PMID: 30120215; 7 patients from ASHG 2018 abstract)

**Most common phenotypes observed:**
- Neurodevelopmental differences/developmental delay (present in all studies)
- Cardiac anomalies including congenital heart defects, valve insufficiency, and septal defects (ASHG 2018; PMID: 38679877)
- Multiple congenital anomalies (PMID: 37126546; PMID: 38679877)
- Autism spectrum disorder (PMID: 36469137)
- Craniofacial dysmorphism and craniosynostosis (PMID: 38679877)
- Noonan-like syndrome phenotype (ASHG 2018)

**Predominant variant type:** Loss-of-function variants (frameshift, nonsense, truncating) are most common and well-characterized. Missense variants are also reported, particularly in conserved kinase domain regions (PMID: 38679877; ASHG 2018).

**Cohort information:** No patients identified as being from established diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD, RUMC, GEL). One study identified a patient from Korea (PMID: 38679877). Most studies do not specify recruitment source.

**Strength of clinical evidence: MODERATE**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) multiple independent publications identifying MAP4K4 variants in neurodevelopmental disorders; (2) de novo variants in several cases, supporting pathogenicity; (3) functional validation in zebrafish models (PMID: 37126546); (4) transcriptome analysis demonstrating deleterious effects on mRNA splicing (PMID: 36469137); and (5) location of missense variants in conserved kinase domains. However, the evidence is limited by: (1) relatively small total patient numbers; (2) lack of representation in large diagnostic cohorts; (3) incomplete variant specification in some publications; and (4) limited segregation data or family studies.

## 3. MODEL ORGANISM EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Organisms studied:** Zebrafish and mouse

**Zebrafish evidence:**
- Knockdown of Map4k4 in zebrafish embryos causes developmental defects, neurodevelopmental differences, and multiple congenital anomalies (PMID: 37126546)
- Phenotypes consistent with impaired RAS signaling regulation

**Mouse evidence:**
- Full knockout is embryonic lethal with neural tube malformations and impaired photoreceptor neurite formation (PMID: 37290629)
- Full knockout causes impaired somite development and embryonic lethality (PMID: 37223681)
- Conditional knockout in endothelial cells is viable and shows metabolic benefits (PMID: 28611026)
- Full knockout in CD8 T cell context is viable under homeostatic conditions (PMID: 32220977)
- Knockout shows reduced c-JUN phosphorylation and imbalance in neurogenesis-related factors (PMID: 37290629)

**Consistency with human phenotype:**
The embryonic lethality of complete MAP4K4 knockout in mice is consistent with the observation that human patients carry heterozygous (not homozygous) variants. The neural tube malformations and neurodevelopmental phenotypes in mouse knockouts align well with the neurodevelopmental differences observed in human patients. The tissue-specific viability of conditional knockouts suggests that haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative effects in specific tissues (particularly neural and cardiac) may underlie the human phenotype.

**Strength of model organism evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) consistent developmental and neurological phenotypes across multiple model systems; (2) demonstration of embryonic lethality with complete loss-of-function, supporting the pathogenicity of human variants; (3) specific neural and cardiac phenotypes relevant to human disease; and (4) mechanistic insights into MAP4K4 function in neurogenesis and cell differentiation. The evidence is not limited by significant gaps, though more detailed characterization of heterozygous mouse models would be valuable.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

**Known pathways and molecular function:**

MAP4K4 functions at the intersection of multiple signaling pathways:

1. **RAS signaling regulation:** MAP4K4 negatively regulates RAS signaling in early embryos; loss-of-function variants cause excessive RAS pathway activation (PMID: 37126546)

2. **Hippo pathway mechanotransduction:** MAP4K4 is activated by RAP2 GTPase in response to low extracellular matrix stiffness and phosphorylates LATS1/2 kinases to inhibit YAP/TAZ, integrating mechanical signals into the Hippo tumor suppressor pathway (PMID: 30135582)

3. **JNK/MLK3 signaling:** MAP4K4 activates pro-proliferative JNK and MLK3 pathways (PMID: 37223681)

4. **Cytoskeletal regulation:** MAP4K4 regulates moesin phosphorylation and controls focal adhesion dynamics and actomyosin remodeling (PMID: 37369604)

5. **STRIPAK complex regulation:** MAP4K4 is regulated within STRIPAK complexes through association with striatin family members and PP2A phosphatase (PMID: 34032269; PMID: 35941177)

**Expression pattern:**
- Broadly expressed, with particular relevance to neural development (PMID: 37290629)
- Expressed in retinal endothelial cells and upregulated in pathological conditions (PMID: 40072537)
- Expressed in cardiomyocytes with multiple isoforms (PMID: 34032269)
- Expressed in immune cells including CD8 T cells and neutrophils

**Key experimental findings:**
- Frameshift variants in MAP4K4 trigger nonsense-mediated decay and impair mRNA splicing (PMID: 36469137)
- Missense variants in the kinase domain are predicted to be loss-of-function or dominant-negative (PMID: 37126546; PMID: 38679877)
- MAP4K4 inhibition may be therapeutically beneficial, potentially mitigated by integrin α5β1 inhibition (ASHG 2018)
- MAP4K4 shows dichotomous functionality: activating growth-promoting JNK signaling while simultaneously activating growth-suppressing Hippo signaling (PMID: 36568222)

**Strength of biological evidence: STRONG**

The evidence is strengthened by: (1) well-characterized molecular pathways with clear mechanistic links to neurodevelopmental and cardiac phenotypes; (2) multiple independent functional studies demonstrating MAP4K4's role in RAS and Hippo signaling; (3) specific evidence for neural tube development and photoreceptor neurite formation; (4) demonstration that loss-of-function variants impair critical signaling functions; and (5) identification of potential therapeutic targets. The evidence is comprehensive and mechanistically coherent.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: MODERATE-TO-STRONG**

**Key strengths:**
1. Multiple independent publications from different research groups identifying MAP4K4 variants in neurodevelopmental disorders
2. Strong biological evidence demonstrating MAP4K4's critical role in RAS signaling, Hippo pathway mechanotransduction, and neural development
3. Robust model organism evidence showing embryonic lethality with complete knockout and specific neural/cardiac phenotypes consistent with human disease
4. Functional validation of pathogenic variants through transcriptome analysis and zebrafish models
5. Extreme intolerance to missense variation (Z-score 4.01, pLI=1) indicating strong selective constraint
6. De novo variants in multiple families supporting pathogenicity

**Key gaps and limitations:**
1. Relatively small total patient numbers (32 patients across all studies) limits statistical power
2. Lack of representation in large diagnostic cohorts (UDN, GREGoR, DDD) limits ascertainment assessment
3. Limited segregation data and family studies to confirm autosomal dominant inheritance
4. Incomplete variant specification in some publications limits detailed genotype-phenotype correlation
5. No large-scale population frequency data provided to assess variant rarity
6. Limited characterization of heterozygous mouse models to directly model human haploinsufficiency

**Conclusion:**

MAP4K4 is emerging as a credible neurodevelopmental disorder gene with heterozygous pathogenic variants causing a spectrum of phenotypes including developmental delay, autism spectrum disorder, congenital heart defects, and craniofacial anomalies. The combination of multiple independent clinical reports, strong biological evidence for MAP4K4's role in critical developmental pathways, and consistent model organism phenotypes provides moderate-to-strong support for pathogenicity. However, the relatively small patient cohort and lack of representation in large diagnostic databases suggest that additional case ascertainment through clinical exome/genome sequencing and systematic cohort studies would substantially strengthen the evidence base and enable more precise genotype-phenotype correlation.
