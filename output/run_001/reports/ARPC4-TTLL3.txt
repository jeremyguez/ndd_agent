# LITERATURE EVIDENCE REPORT: ARPC4-TTLL3 AS A NEURODEVELOPMENTAL DISORDER GENE

## 1. GENE SUMMARY

ARPC4-TTLL3 is a fusion gene product identified through systematic meta-analysis of osteoarthritis-associated proteins in human cartilage. The gene appears to be involved in cellular structural organization and protein modification, though its primary molecular function and neurodevelopmental relevance remain poorly characterized in the current literature.

## 2. CLINICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

- **Independent case reports/case series found:** 0
- **Total patients with neurodevelopmental phenotypes:** 0
- **Most common phenotypes:** Not applicable

- **Strength of clinical evidence: NONE**

No clinical evidence linking ARPC4-TTLL3 heterozygous variants to neurodevelopmental disorders was identified in the available literature. The gene has not been reported in association with any human disease phenotypes in clinical case studies or patient cohorts.

No model organism studies investigating ARPC4-TTLL3 function or pathogenicity were identified in the available literature.

## 4. BIOLOGICAL EVIDENCE SUMMARY

- **Known pathways:** ARPC4-TTLL3 was identified through co-expression network analysis in the context of osteoarthritis-associated genes and showed significant pathway enrichment overlap with cellular senescence drivers (p < 0.01) (PMID: 35457215).

- **Expression pattern:** The gene was detected via mass spectrometry analysis across different zones of human osteoarthritic cartilage (superficial, middle, and deep layers) compared to normal controls, indicating cartilage-specific expression. No brain or neurodevelopmental tissue expression data were reported.

- **Key experimental findings:** ARPC4-TTLL3 was identified as a novel osteoarthritis-associated protein through systematic meta-analysis of 1,689 differentially expressed genes in human osteoarthritic cartilage (PMID: 35457215). The evidence is limited to cartilage biology and cellular senescence pathways.

- **Strength of biological evidence: LIMITED**

The biological evidence is restricted to cartilage pathology and does not establish relevance to neurodevelopmental processes or brain function.

## 5. OVERALL ASSESSMENT

**Overall evidence level: INSUFFICIENT**

**Key strengths:**
- Gene identification through rigorous systematic meta-analysis and mass spectrometry validation in human tissue samples

**Key gaps and limitations:**
- Complete absence of clinical evidence linking ARPC4-TTLL3 to any human disease, including neurodevelopmental disorders
- No model organism studies to establish functional relevance
- Expression data limited to cartilage; no evidence of brain or neurodevelopmental tissue expression
- No mechanistic studies explaining how variants in this gene could cause neurodevelopmental phenotypes
- Current evidence base restricted to osteoarthritis biology, which is distinct from neurodevelopmental pathology

**Conclusion:**
There is currently insufficient evidence to support ARPC4-TTLL3 as a neurodevelopmental disorder gene. The available literature identifies this gene exclusively in the context of osteoarthritis-associated protein expression in cartilage tissue. To establish pathogenicity for neurodevelopmental disorders, clinical case reports with heterozygous variants, functional studies demonstrating neurodevelopmental relevance, and model organism evidence would be required.
